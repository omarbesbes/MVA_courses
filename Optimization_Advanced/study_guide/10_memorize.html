How to use this page

Memorizing works when you retrieve, not when you reread. Everything below asks you to produce an answer before you see it, and every answer points back to the card where it is proved.

The page follows the order in which memory is built. First the skeleton: the thirty statements that carry the course, in compressed form, plus the twelve formulas you must be able to write without thinking. Then the flashcards: one question per card, the answer on the back with a one-line reason and a chip to the card (hover the chip to see the full statement, click it to pin it). Then the two sections that sharpen the boundaries of each notion: minimal pairs (notions that look alike, with a test that tells them apart) and counterexamples (the examples that kill a false claim, each checked in one line). Finally three ways to test yourself: a graded quiz, six exam-style problems and an oral drill in which you explain one mechanism in 60 seconds.

A spaced-repetition plan (four weeks before the exam)

  • Day 1. Skeleton lines 1–13 (pages 1–3) read aloud; flashcards of pages 1–3; the first six formulas.
  • Day 2. Skeleton lines 14–24 (pages 4–5); flashcards of pages 4–5; formulas 4–8. Redo yesterday's cards that you got wrong.
  • Day 4. Skeleton lines 25–30 (pages 6–7) and the labs; flashcards of pages 6–8; the minimal pairs.
  • Day 7. The whole skeleton from memory, on paper; the whole deck shuffled with "only not known"; the counterexamples; quiz ★ and ★★.
  • Day 14. Quiz ★★★; two exam-style problems under time; ten random oral prompts.
  • Day 21 and day 28. All six problems; the full oral drill; the shuffled deck again. Untick "known" on any card you hesitated on, so that the filter brings it back.

The "known" boxes live on the lecture pages (top right of every card): tick one when you can restate the result and its proof idea from memory. Learn · the by-heart list

The course in 30 lines

One line per central result, in the compressed form you should be able to say without looking. The chip opens the card (hover for the full statement). If you can produce these thirty lines on a blank sheet, you know the skeleton of the exam.

The one line behind the thirty

Existence (lines 1–5) → Fermat's rule $0\in\partial F(\hat x)$ (6–10) → the resolvent $\prox_{\gamma f}=(\Id+\gamma\partial f)^{-1}$ turns the inclusion into a fixed-point equation (11–13) → Fejér monotonicity and averaging make the fixed-point iteration converge (14–21) → splitting and duality build the right averaged operator for each structure (22–28) → and when there is no prox, majorize instead (29–30).

Page 1 · Convexity and existence

  1. Weierstrass. $S$ nonempty compact, $f:S\to\,]-\infty,+\infty]$ proper lsc $\Rightarrow$ $\exists\,\hat x\in S$ with $f(\hat x)=\inf_S f$. Minimize, extract, lsc. Weierstrass
  2. Existence in finite dimension. $\H$ finite-dimensional, $f$ proper, lsc, coercive $\Rightarrow$ $\Argmin f$ is nonempty and compact. coercive existence
  3. Convex minimizers. $f$ proper convex with $\inf f\gt-\infty$: $\Argmin f$ is convex, every local minimizer is global, and strictly convex $\Rightarrow$ at most one minimizer. convex minimizers
  4. Existence over a closed convex set. $f\in\Gz{\H}$, $C$ closed convex, $\dom f\cap C\ne\emptyset$, $f$ coercive or $C$ bounded $\Rightarrow$ a minimizer over $C$ exists; unique if $f$ is strictly convex. existence over C
  5. Variational inequality. $\hat x$ local minimizer over a convex $C$ of $f$ Gâteaux differentiable at $\hat x$ $\Rightarrow$ $\ps{\nabla f(\hat x)}{y-\hat x}\ge0$ for all $y\in C$ (also sufficient when $f$ is convex). variational inequality

Page 2 · Subdifferential and conjugate

  1. Fermat. $f$ proper: $0\in\partial f(x)\iff x\in\Argmin f$, where $u\in\partial f(x)\iff f\ge f(x)+\ps{\cdot-x}{u}$ on all of $\H$. Fermat's rule
  2. Two subdifferentials to know. Convex and Gâteaux differentiable at $x$ $\Rightarrow$ $\partial f(x)=\{\nabla f(x)\}$; $\partial\ind_C=N_C$ with $N_C(x)=\{u:\ps{u}{y-x}\le0\ \forall y\in C\}$ for $x\in C$. smooth casenormal cone
  3. Sum rule. $\partial f+L^*\partial g\,L\subset\partial(f+g\circ L)$ whenever $\dom g\cap L(\dom f)\ne\emptyset$; equality for $f\in\Gz{\H}$, $g\in\Gz{\G}$ if $\inte(\dom g)\cap L(\dom f)\ne\emptyset$ or $\dom g\cap\inte L(\dom f)\ne\emptyset$. Moreau–Rockafellar
  4. Moreau–Fenchel. $f$ proper: $f$ lsc convex $\iff$ $f^{**}=f$; then $f^*\in\Gz{\H}$. Moreau–Fenchel
  5. Fenchel–Young. $f(x)+f^*(u)\ge\ps xu$, with equality $\iff u\in\partial f(x)$ $\iff$ (for $f\in\Gz{\H}$) $x\in\partial f^*(u)$: $\partial f^*=(\partial f)^{-1}$. Fenchel–Young

Page 3 · The proximity operator

  1. Prox is well defined. $f\in\Gz{\H}$, $\gamma\gt0$: $f+\frac1{2\gamma}\norm{\cdot-x}^2$ has exactly one minimizer, $\prox_{\gamma f}x$ (affine minorant + parabola: coercive and strictly convex). existence and uniqueness
  2. Characterization. $p=\prox_{\gamma f}x\iff\frac{x-p}{\gamma}\in\partial f(p)$, i.e. $\prox_{\gamma f}=(\Id+\gamma\partial f)^{-1}$. prox = resolvent
  3. Moreau decomposition. $\prox_{\gamma f^*}x=x-\gamma\prox_{\gamma^{-1}f}(\gamma^{-1}x)$; for $\gamma=1$, $\prox_f+\prox_{f^*}=\Id$. Moreau decomposition

Page 4 · Fixed points and Forward–Backward

  1. Picard. $T$ a strict contraction (constant $\rho\in[0,1[$) with fixed point $\hat x$ $\Rightarrow$ $x_{n+1}=Tx_n$ satisfies $\norm{x_n-\hat x}\le\rho^n\norm{x_0-\hat x}$. Picard
  2. Fejér convergence. Fejér-monotone with respect to $D$ and every weak sequential cluster point in $D$ $\Rightarrow$ $x_n\wto$ a point of $D$. Fejér convergence
  3. Demiclosedness. $T:C\to\H$ nonexpansive on a closed convex $C$, $x_n\wto\hat x$ and $Tx_n-x_n\to0$ $\Rightarrow$ $\hat x\in\Fix T$. demiclosedness
  4. Krasnosel'skii–Mann. $T:C\to C$ nonexpansive, $\Fix T\ne\emptyset$, $\lambda_n\in[0,1]$ with $\sum\lambda_n(1-\lambda_n)=+\infty$ $\Rightarrow$ $x_{n+1}=x_n+\lambda_n(Tx_n-x_n)$ is Fejér, $Tx_n-x_n\to0$, $x_n\wto$ a point of $\Fix T$. KM
  5. Averaged operators. $A=(1-\alpha)\Id+\alpha R$ with $R$ nonexpansive; $A_1\circ A_2$ is $\frac{\alpha_1+\alpha_2-2\alpha_1\alpha_2}{1-\alpha_1\alpha_2}$-averaged; an $\alpha$-averaged $T$ may be relaxed with $\lambda_n\in[0,1/\alpha]$, $\sum\lambda_n(1-\alpha\lambda_n)=+\infty$. compositionaveraged iteration
  6. The two bricks. $\prox_f$ is firmly nonexpansive ($\tfrac12$-averaged) for every $f\in\Gz{\H}$; $\Id-\gamma\nabla g$ is $\frac{\gamma\nu}{2}$-averaged for $\gamma\in\,]0,2/\nu[$ when $g\in\Gz{\H}$ has a $\nu$-Lipschitz gradient (Baillon–Haddad). prox firmly nonexpansivegradient step
  7. Forward–Backward. $\gamma\in\,]0,2/\nu[$, $\delta=2-\gamma\nu/2$, $\lambda_n\in[0,\delta]$ with $\sum\lambda_n(\delta-\lambda_n)=+\infty$, $\Argmin(f+g)\ne\emptyset$ $\Rightarrow$ $x_{n+1}=x_n+\lambda_n(\prox_{\gamma f}(x_n-\gamma\nabla g(x_n))-x_n)\wto$ a minimizer of $f+g$. Forward–Backward
  8. Proximal point. $\gamma_n\gt0$ with $\sum\gamma_n=+\infty$, $\Argmin f\ne\emptyset$ $\Rightarrow$ $x_{n+1}=\prox_{\gamma_nf}x_n\wto$ a minimizer of $f$. proximal point

Page 5 · Douglas–Rachford and PPXA+

  1. Reflect, then read the shadow. $\rprox_{\gamma f}=2\prox_{\gamma f}-\Id$ is nonexpansive, and $\zer(\partial f+\partial g)=\prox_{\gamma g}\big(\Fix(\rprox_{\gamma f}\circ\rprox_{\gamma g})\big)$. rproxDR dictionary
  2. Douglas–Rachford. Any $\gamma\gt0$, $\lambda_n\in[0,2]$ with $\sum\lambda_n(2-\lambda_n)=+\infty$, $\zer(\partial f+\partial g)\ne\emptyset$ $\Rightarrow$ $x_n\wto\hat x$, and the shadow $y_n=\prox_{\gamma g}x_n\wto\hat y=\prox_{\gamma g}\hat x\in\Argmin(f+g)$. Douglas–Rachford
  3. PPXA+. Parallel DR on $\H_1\times\dots\times\H_m$: $m$ proxes in parallel and one solve with $\sum_iL_i^*L_i$ (assumed an isomorphism) $\Rightarrow$ $v_n\wto\hat v\in\Argmin\sum_ig_i\circ L_i$. PPXA+

Page 6 · Duality, ADMM and primal-dual

  1. Duality. For proper $f,g$: $\mu=\inf f+g\circ L\ \ge\ -\mu^*$ with $\mu^*=\inf_v f^*(-L^*v)+g^*(v)$; for $f\in\Gz{\H}$, $g\in\Gz{\G}$ and the sum-rule qualification condition, $\mu=-\mu^*$ and the dual infimum is attained. weak dualitystrong duality
  2. Kuhn–Tucker points. $-L^*\hat v\in\partial f(\hat x)$ and $L\hat x\in\partial g^*(\hat v)$ $\Rightarrow$ $\hat x$ solves the primal and $\hat v$ the dual; under the qualification condition, saddle points of $\Lag(x,y,v)=f(x)+g(y)+\ps{v}{Lx-y}$ are exactly the KT points with $\hat y=L\hat x$. KT pointssaddle points
  3. ADMM. $L^*L$ an isomorphism, the qualification condition, $\Argmin(f+g\circ L)\ne\emptyset$, any $\gamma\gt0$ $\Rightarrow$ $x_n\wto\hat x\in\Argmin(f+g\circ L)$ and $\gamma z_n\wto\hat v$, a dual solution: ADMM is Douglas–Rachford on the dual. ADMMDR ↔ ADMM
  4. Primal-dual. $\tau^{-1}-\sigma\norm{L}^2\gt\beta/2$ and $\zer(\partial f+\nabla h+L^*\partial g\,L)\ne\emptyset$ $\Rightarrow$ $x_n\wto\hat x\in\Argmin(f+h+g\circ L)$ and $v_n\wto\hat v\in\Argmin\big((f+h)^*\circ(-L^*)+g^*\big)$. primal-dual

Page 7 · Majorization–Minimization

  1. MM and the half-quadratic majorant. $f(x_{n+1})\le h(x_{n+1},x_n)\le h(x_n,x_n)=f(x_n)$; if $\psi(\sqrt\cdot)$ is concave, $\dot\psi\ge0$ and $\omega(x)=\dot\psi(x)/x$ has a finite limit at $0^+$, then $\psi(\abs x)\le\psi(\abs y)+\dot f(y)(x-y)+\tfrac12\omega(\abs y)(x-y)^2$. MMhalf-quadratic
  2. MM quadratic. $x_{n+1}=x_n-\theta_nA(x_n)^{-1}\nabla f(x_n)$; $f$ coercive differentiable, $\nu\Id\preccurlyeq A(x_n)\preccurlyeq\bar\nu\Id$, $\theta_n\in[\underline\theta,2-\bar\theta]$ $\Rightarrow$ $f(x_n)-f(x_{n+1})\ge\mu_1\norm{x_{n+1}-x_n}^2\ge\mu_2\norm{\nabla f(x_n)}^2$, so (finite dimension) $\nabla f(x_n)\to0$, cluster points are stationary ($f\in C^1$), minimizers ($f$ convex). MM convergence

Statement · page 1

Weierstrass: hypotheses and conclusion?

click or Space to flip

Answer

$S$ nonempty compact, $f:S\to\,]-\infty,+\infty]$ proper and lsc $\Rightarrow$ some $\hat x\in S$ has $f(\hat x)=\inf_S f$.

Why: minimizing sequence, extract a convergent subsequence (compact), pass to the limit (lsc).

Weierstrass
Statement · page 1

No compact set in sight: what gives existence in $\R^N$?

click or Space to flip

Answer

$\H$ finite-dimensional, $f$ proper, lsc and coercive ($f(x)\to+\infty$ as $\norm x\to+\infty$) $\Rightarrow$ $\Argmin f$ is nonempty and compact.

Why: coercivity confines minimizing sequences to a closed ball, compact in finite dimension; then Weierstrass.

coercive existence
Proof idea · page 1

Why is "$f$ lsc" the same as "$\epi f$ closed"?

click or Space to flip

Answer

$\Rightarrow$: if $(x_n,\zeta_n)\in\epi f\to(x,\zeta)$, then $f(x)\le\liminf f(x_n)\le\lim\zeta_n=\zeta$. $\Leftarrow$: if $\liminf f(x_n)\lt\zeta\lt f(x)$, infinitely many $(x_n,\zeta)\in\epi f$ converge to $(x,\zeta)\notin\epi f$.

lsc ⇔ closed epigraph
Statement · page 1

Three facts about the minimizers of a convex function?

click or Space to flip

Answer

$f$ proper convex, $\inf f\gt-\infty$: $\Argmin f$ is convex; every local minimizer is global; if $f$ is strictly convex there is at most one minimizer.

Why: one word, the chord. Uniqueness comes from strict convexity, never existence.

convex minimizers
Statement · page 1

Existence and uniqueness of a minimizer over a closed convex set $C$?

click or Space to flip

Answer

$f\in\Gz{\H}$, $C$ closed convex, $\dom f\cap C\ne\emptyset$, and $f$ coercive or $C$ bounded $\Rightarrow$ a minimizer over $C$ exists; unique if $f$ is strictly convex.

Why: apply the existence theory to $f+\ind_C\in\Gz{\H}$.

existence over C
Statement · page 1

First-order condition at a local minimizer over a convex set $C$ ($f$ Gâteaux differentiable)?

click or Space to flip

Answer

$\ps{\nabla f(\hat x)}{y-\hat x}\ge0$ for every $y\in C$; if $\hat x\in\inte C$, $\nabla f(\hat x)=0$. Sufficient when $f$ is convex.

Why: compare with $\hat x+\alpha(y-\hat x)\in C$, divide by $\alpha$, let $\alpha\to0^+$ (chord argument).

variational inequality
Formula · page 1

Hessian test for convexity and for strict convexity?

click or Space to flip

Answer

On an open convex $\dom f$, $f$ twice differentiable: convex $\iff\nabla^2f(x)\succeq0$ for all $x$; $\nabla^2f\succ0$ everywhere $\Rightarrow$ strictly convex (not conversely: $x^4$).

Why: restrict to a line, $\varphi(t)=f(x+t(y-x))$, and use $\varphi''\ge0$.

Hessian test
Counterexample · page 1

A proper, lsc, strictly convex function on $\R$ with no minimizer?

click or Space to flip

Answer

$f(x)=e^x$: $\inf f=0$ is never attained. It is not coercive ($e^x\to0$ as $x\to-\infty$).

Why: the minimizing sequence $x_n=-n$ escapes to infinity; strict convexity buys uniqueness, not existence.

coercive
Statement · page 1

Strong versus weak convergence: definitions and the standard example?

click or Space to flip

Answer

$x_n\to\hat x$: $\norm{x_n-\hat x}\to0$. $x_n\wto\hat x$: $\ps{y}{x_n-\hat x}\to0$ for every $y$. Strong $\Rightarrow$ weak; equivalent in finite dimension; in $\ell^2$, $e_n\wto0$ while $\norm{e_n}=1$.

strong and weak
Trick · page 1

The indicator trick: what does a constraint become, and what do you get from $\ind_C$?

click or Space to flip

Answer

$\Argmin_{x\in C}f=\Argmin(f+\ind_C)$. Then $\partial\ind_C=N_C$, $\prox_{\gamma\ind_C}=P_C$ for every $\gamma$, $\ind_C^*=\sigma_C$; $\ind_C\in\Gz{\H}\iff C$ nonempty closed convex.

T01 indicator trick
Formula · page 1

Adjoint of the duplication $L:x\mapsto(x,\dots,x)\in\H^m$? And $L^*L$?

click or Space to flip

Answer

$L^*(y_1,\dots,y_m)=\sum_{i=1}^my_i$ and $L^*L=m\Id$; the projection onto $\ran L$ (the diagonal) is the average.

Why: $\ps{Lx}{y}=\sum_i\ps{x}{y_i}=\ps{x}{\sum_iy_i}$.

adjoint of duplication
Statement · page 2

Subgradient: definition and picture?

click or Space to flip

Answer

$u\in\partial f(x)\iff(\forall y\in\H)\ f(y)\ge f(x)+\ps{y-x}{u}$: $u$ is the slope of an affine minorant of $f$ that touches the graph at $x$. A global inequality.

Picture: the fan of supporting lines through a kink.

subdifferential
Statement · page 2

Fermat's rule, exactly: which hypothesis, which conclusion?

click or Space to flip

Answer

$f$ proper (nothing else): $0\in\partial f(x)\iff x\in\Argmin f$.

Why: the horizontal line through $(x,f(x))$ supports the graph exactly when $f(x)$ is the lowest value. No convexity needed; it speaks of global minimizers only.

Fermat's rule
Formula · page 2

$\partial\abs{\cdot}(x)$ for every $x\in\R$?

click or Space to flip

Answer

$\{-1\}$ for $x\lt0$, $[-1,1]$ at $x=0$, $\{1\}$ for $x\gt0$.

Why: every line through the origin with slope in $[-1,1]$ stays below $\abs\cdot$; Fermat then says $0$ minimizes $\abs\cdot$ although it has no gradient there.

subdifferential
Proof idea · page 2

Why is $\partial f$ monotone?

click or Space to flip

Answer

Write $f(x_2)\ge f(x_1)+\ps{x_2-x_1}{u_1}$ and $f(x_1)\ge f(x_2)+\ps{x_1-x_2}{u_2}$, add: the values cancel and $\ps{u_1-u_2}{x_1-x_2}\ge0$.

monotoneT06 twice and add
Statement · page 2

Normal cone: definition and its value at an interior point, on a subspace, on the sphere of a ball?

click or Space to flip

Answer

$\partial\ind_C(x)=N_C(x)=\{u:\ps{u}{y-x}\le0\ \forall y\in C\}$ for $x\in C$, $\emptyset$ outside. Interior: $\{0\}$; vector space: $C^\perp$; $\norm{x-c}=\rho$ on $\overline B(c,\rho)$: $\R_+(x-c)$.

normal cone
Statement · page 2

Sum rule for $f+g\circ L$: what is free, what needs a condition?

click or Space to flip

Answer

Free (domains meet): $\partial f+L^*\partial g\,L\subset\partial(f+g\circ L)$. Equality for $f\in\Gz{\H}$, $g\in\Gz{\G}$ if $\inte(\dom g)\cap L(\dom f)\ne\emptyset$ or $\dom g\cap\inte L(\dom f)\ne\emptyset$; automatic if $g$ is finite-valued.

Moreau–Rockafellar
Counterexample · page 2

A sum rule with a strict inclusion?

click or Space to flip

Answer

On $\R$: $f(x)=-\sqrt x$ for $x\ge0$ ($+\infty$ else), $g=\ind_{]-\infty,0]}$. Then $\partial f(0)=\emptyset$, but $f+g=\ind_{\{0\}}$ and $\partial(f+g)(0)=\R$.

Why: the domains only touch at $0$; no interior point, no equality.

sum rule
Formula · page 2

The conjugates to know cold?

click or Space to flip

Answer

$(\tfrac12\norm\cdot^2)^*=\tfrac12\norm\cdot^2$; $\norm\cdot^*=\ind_{\overline B(0,1)}$; $\ind_C^*=\sigma_C$; $(\tfrac1q\norm\cdot_q^q)^*=\tfrac1{q^*}\norm\cdot_{q^*}^{q^*}$ with $\tfrac1q+\tfrac1{q^*}=1$; $\ps{c}{\cdot}^*=\ind_{\{c\}}$.

conjugates
Statement · page 2

Fenchel–Young: inequality, equality case, and the inversion?

click or Space to flip

Answer

$f$ proper: $f(x)+f^*(u)\ge\ps xu$, with equality $\iff u\in\partial f(x)$. For $f\in\Gz{\H}$: $u\in\partial f(x)\iff x\in\partial f^*(u)$, i.e. $\partial f^*=(\partial f)^{-1}$.

Fenchel–YoungT10
Statement · page 2

When is $f^{**}=f$?

click or Space to flip

Answer

For proper $f$: $f^{**}=f\iff f$ is lsc and convex (Moreau–Fenchel). Then $f^*$ is proper too, so $f^*\in\Gz{\H}$.

Why: a closed convex function is the supremum of its affine minorants, and $f^{**}$ is exactly that supremum.

Moreau–Fenchel
Statement · page 2

Which $\Gz{}$ functions are support functions? Which set for $\norm\cdot_q$?

click or Space to flip

Answer

Exactly the positively homogeneous ones: $f=\sigma_C$ with $C=\partial f(0)$ nonempty closed convex and $f^*=\ind_C$. $\norm\cdot_q=\sigma_{\{\norm y_{q^*}\le1\}}$, $\norm\cdot_1=\sigma_{[-1,1]^N}$.

support functions
Statement · page 3

Definition of $\prox_{\gamma f}$, and why it exists and is unique?

click or Space to flip

Answer

$\prox_{\gamma f}x=\argmin_y f(y)+\frac1{2\gamma}\norm{y-x}^2$, $f\in\Gz{\H}$, $\gamma\gt0$.
