## Key Question 1 — Judge Decoding (Bachmann et al., ICLR 2025): the "alignment versus correctness" argument, the judge, the results, the limitations

### Takeaway
Judge Decoding shows that the standard acceptance test measures agreement with the target, not quality: GPT-4o answers and human-written Wikipedia text get only about 2–3 tokens accepted by Llama-3.1-405B before the first rejection. It adds a linear "judge" (16.4k parameters, trained in under 1.5 h on 500 hand-annotated question/correct/wrong triples) on the target's last hidden state and accepts a draft token if either the standard test or the judge accepts it; with Llama-3.1-8B drafting it reports about 19–20 tokens accepted per verification, 129 tokens/s for 405B (8 H100) and near-target benchmark scores, at the price of losing the distribution guarantee and of needing in-domain annotation (HumanEval drops from 86.6 to 80.4 when code examples are removed from the judge's training set).

**(b) Problem, in the authors' framing (the alignment-versus-correctness argument)**
- [VERIFIED] "While this approach guarantees to reproduce the target output, it incurs a substantial penalty: many high-quality draft tokens are rejected, even when they represent objectively valid continuations. Indeed, we show that even powerful draft models such as GPT-4o, as well as human text cannot achieve high acceptance rates under the standard verification scheme." — [arXiv abstract page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19309)
- [VERIFIED] "Relying on target probabilities guarantees the same output, but as a consequence, a token is solely judged based on its alignment with the target model, and not by its inherent contextual quality." The research question is printed in a box: "Can we adapt verification to assess token quality rather than alignment?" — [arXiv HTML, Sec. 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19309v1)
- [VERIFIED] Standard rule the paper starts from (its Eq. 4), for draft tokens $c_i$ with draft probabilities $\bm q_i$ and target probabilities $\bm p_i$: $\text{Accept } c_i \text{ if all previous tokens are accepted and } \epsilon_i < \frac{\bm p_i[c_i]}{\bm q_i[c_i]} \text{ for } \epsilon_i \sim \mathcal U([0,1])$. — [arXiv HTML, Sec. 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19309v1)
- [VERIFIED] Saturation with draft length (Llama-3.1-8B draft, Llama-3.1-405B target, MT-Bench and GSM8K): "We observe that the number of accepted tokens quickly saturates as a function of $M$ and the acceptance rates thus decrease rapidly. ... This is the reason why prior work is limited largely to $M\leq 7$." — [arXiv HTML, Sec. 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19309v1)
- [VERIFIED-FIG] Fig. 2 (left), read off the curves (approximate): with standard SD the number of accepted tokens goes from about 3.5 at $M{=}5$ to about 11 at $M{=}50$; with judge verification it grows almost linearly to about 38 at $M{=}50$ (a dashed "85% rate" line is drawn for reference). — [Fig. 2 image](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19309v1/draft_exps.png)
- [VERIFIED] Evidence 1, a much stronger drafter: "we use GPT-4o as draft model for the target Llama-405B. We generate full answers with GPT-4o on MT-Bench, GSM8K and HumanEval and simply check how many tokens the target accepts under greedy decoding before the first rejection, as there is no way to properly perform SD with closed-source models. ... Counter-intuitively, we find that the target model does not reward the higher quality of tokens, accepting only roughly two before encountering the first rejection." — [arXiv HTML, Sec. 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19309v1)
- [VERIFIED-FIG] Fig. 3 (right), accepted tokens before first rejection, GPT-4o text verified by Llama-405B, standard vs judge verification: MT-Bench 2.3 vs 25.8; GSM8K 2.1 vs 22.3; HumanEval 2.6 vs 27.1. — [Fig. 3 image](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19309v1/gpt_drafter.png)

**(c) Mechanism**
- [VERIFIED] Why embeddings: "our experiments show that last hidden layer embeddings of erroneous tokens effectively "flag" errors and contradictions, prompting the model to generate subsequent tokens that attempt to correct the mistake. ... when forced to start with the incorrect statement "The capital of France is Berlin", the model continues with "… just kidding, it's actually Paris"." — [arXiv HTML, Sec. 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19309v1)
- [VERIFIED] Signal read: the target model's hidden state at the position of the draft token itself (not the logits that predicted it): "we thus stick to using the last embedding of the target before the RMS normalization (Zhang & Sennrich, 2019) and the language modelling (LM) head." Deeper layers "perform best with only insignificant differences, while too shallow layers are clearly worse". — [arXiv HTML, Sec. 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19309v1)
- [VERIFIED] Judge = one linear layer (logistic regression): "While we experimented with more complex architectures, including MLPs and shallow Transformer networks, a simple linear head proved most effective ... we train only 16.4k parameters on just 30k tokens in less than 1.5 hours, with all target model parameters remaining frozen." Size per model: "for Llama-405B, our linear head has dimension $16,384$ while for Llama-70B it has $8,192$. Our linear head can be viewed as an additional entry in the vocabulary $\mathcal V$, reducing its inference overhead thus to practically zero." — [arXiv HTML, Sec. 4.1 and App. B.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19309v1)
- [VERIFIED] Decision rule: accept $c_i$ (embedding $\bm e_i \in \mathbb R^D$) "if $\sigma\left(f_{\text{judge}}(\bm e_i)\right) > \delta$ for $\delta \in [0.5, 1]$, where $\sigma$ is the sigmoid function. ... In practice we observed that there is no need to tune this value to ensure quality and leave it at the natural value $\delta = 0.5$." — [arXiv HTML, Sec. 4.1 "Inference"](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19309v1)
- [VERIFIED] Combination with the standard test, a logical OR of two masks: "we thus get two accept/reject masks from the target model: $\bm z_{\text{stand}} \in \{0,1\}^M$ as in standard SD verification and $\bm z_{\text{judge}} \in \{0,1\}^M$ from the judge head. We take the logical OR between the two, $\bm z = \bm z_{\text{stand}} \vee \bm z_{\text{judge}}$, since when the judge rejects and standard SD accepts, the corrected token according to the target will exactly be the same token. ... Note that $\delta = 1$ reduces to SD." Fig. 7 caption: "Once both methods disagree, subsequent tokens get rejected automatically as usual, even if they were individually accepted." — [arXiv HTML, Sec. 4.1 and App. B.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19309v1)
- [VERIFIED] The judge is biased towards rejection: "it can occasionally occur as the judge was tuned to rather reject than accept when in doubt to avoid false positives." — [arXiv HTML, App. B.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19309v1)
- [VERIFIED] Training data and labels (manual annotation): "The set of input prompts are a mixture of newly-created questions and two public datasets that we heavily filtered (Alpaca (Taori et al., 2023) and ARC (Clark et al., 2018)). Importantly, we only use the input questions and none of the answers. We leverage several models to produce a diverse set of correct and wrong answers, including Mistral-Large-2, Llama-8B and Llama-405B ... All answers were manually reviewed and corrected by the authors, who also annotated the precise location of errors in wrong answers ... In total we collected $500$ high-quality question, correct answer, wrong answer tuples. For training, we label every token from the correct answer as positive, every token from the wrong answer up until the point of mistake as positive, and finally every mistaken token as negative. ... the dataset exhibits a strong imbalance, leading to roughly $20\times$ more positive than negative examples." Footnote: "Using LLMs to that end proved to be too imprecise". — [arXiv HTML, Sec. 4.1 "Dataset curation"](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19309v1)
- [VERIFIED] Loss and hyper-parameters: "we train a linear head $f_{\text{judge}}$ on top of the target embeddings, using a weighted cross entropy loss as the objective to counter the imbalance in the dataset. We place larger weight on the negative examples ... and perform early-stopping to reduce overfitting. We tune all hyperparameters on a small test split." "AdamW optimizer ... with learning rate $\eta = 0.0001$, weight decay $0.1$ and batch size $128$." The class weight value is not given (not found). — [arXiv HTML, Sec. 4.1 and App. B.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19309v1)
- [VERIFIED] Draft lengths used: "We show top-$K$ decoding, standard SD for $M=10$ and our judge decoding for $M=25$ (striped)." — [arXiv HTML, Fig. 6 caption](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19309v1)
- [VERIFIED] Training-free baseline it is compared with: "top-K verification, where a candidate token $c_i$ is accepted, if it is among the $K$ highest valued probabilities $\bm p_i$ ... Setting $K=V$ reduces to running just the fast draft model, while $K=1$ results in standard SD." — [arXiv HTML, Sec. 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19309v1)

**(d) In what sense lossy; guarantees**
- [VERIFIED] "Our approach however also comes with a drawback; the mathematical guarantee to maintain target quality is lost by relying on the judge." What is kept: the standard mask (tokens the target would have produced are always accepted) and the target's own correction at the first rejected position ("if $c_i$ is the first rejected token, one can sample a correct token from $\bm p_i$"). No bound on the deviation is given. — [arXiv HTML, Sec. 6 and Sec. 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19309v1)

**(g) Costs, caveats, limitations**
- [VERIFIED] Out-of-distribution judge: "we filter our dataset by removing all coding examples, train the verification head for Llama-405B on this reduced set and then evaluate on the coding task HumanEval. While we do observe a drop in performance from $86.6$ to $80.4\%$, the performance is still significantly better than the draft model at $71.3\%$ ... Nevertheless, our approach is not a silver bullet and to maintain target quality it is required to train the judge on data of similar nature." — [arXiv HTML, Sec. 5.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19309v1)
- [VERIFIED] Authors' limitation list (Appendix A), verbatim: (1) "An obvious limitation is the loss of the mathematical guarantee to match target quality. While we perform extensive experiments and show that quality is maintained, there is no certainty for novel tasks." (2) "The draft model needs to be of high-quality, otherwise our approach naturally does not prove beneficial and too many tokens end up being rejected. Self-speculation and small drafters in the spirit of Medusa or Eagle are thus not ideal since their generations quickly deteriorate when drafting too far into the future." (3) "the target model needs to be of sufficient size to be able to provide accurate judgements. Speedups for smaller models such as Llama-8B are hence tougher to achieve." (4) "new tasks do require careful annotation of data to maintain quality. The required amount on the other hand turns out to be small in our case." (5) "If the draft model has safety issues, the target model could potentially accept safety-critical tokens through the judge, even if the target would otherwise never produce such outputs. We have not observed such issues in our experiments but have also not thoroughly investigated this problem". — [arXiv HTML, App. A](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19309v1)
- [VERIFIED] With HuggingFace (unoptimised) the 8B/70B judge is slower than Eagle-2 (2× vs 3.3×): "When drafting with Llama-8B for Llama-70B with batch size $1$ in simple frameworks, the latency delta between the two models is relatively small, limiting the speed-ups of judge decoding." — [arXiv HTML, Sec. 5.2 and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19309v1)

**(h) Predecessors and successors**
- [VERIFIED] Declared inspiration: "We draw inspiration from the LLM-as-a-judge framework, where LLMs are used to judge the quality of other model responses to user questions (Zheng et al., 2023)", but "using LLM-judges directly is not feasible because (a) they require lengthy system prompts and often chain-of-thought reasoning, slowing inference, and (b) they evaluate full answers, whereas SD requires evaluating short, sometime partial continuations." Latent-knowledge probes are cited as support (Burns et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2023a; von Rütte et al., 2024). — [arXiv HTML, Sec. 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19309v1)
- [VERIFIED] Positioning against earlier acceptance-rate work: "All these methods improve acceptance rates by either encouraging better alignment with more information or producing more guesses in parallel. This is different from our work, which seeks to change the verification scheme itself." — [arXiv HTML, Sec. 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19309v1)

## Key Question 2 — AutoJudge (Garipov et al., NeurIPS 2025): automatic mining of "important" mismatches, the classifier, the results

### Takeaway
AutoJudge keeps Judge Decoding's inference rule but replaces manual annotation by a semi-greedy counterfactual search: starting from the target's answer, each draft/target mismatch is swapped in turn and the target regenerates the rest; a mismatch is "important" only if the final answer (GSM8K number, LiveCodeBench test outcome) changes. A logistic regression on the hidden states of the draft token then overrides rejections of "unimportant" mismatches. It reports over 40 accepted tokens per target pass on GSM8K (8B/70B) at ≤1% accuracy change, and in vLLM 1.1–1.5× over well-tuned lossless SD for a 0–2-point loss (up to ≈2× when both use a 32-token window or with RAM offloading); the judge is task-specific (GSM8K-trained judges fail on code and on harder maths) and the mining is expensive (≈700 s per GSM8K example for 8B/70B).

**(b) Problem, in the authors' framing**
- [VERIFIED] "Intuitively, if a model generates a reasoning chain, not all mismatching tokens are equally important: errors in derivation should be fixed, while minor word choices should not. Judge Decoding (Bachmann et al., 2025) takes advantage of this by labeling which tokens are important for reasoning ... However, their approach relies on human annotators to determine which tokens are important for reasoning. This complicates adoption and can be prone to human errors, particularly if the task requires expert knowledge". — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20039v4)
- [VERIFIED] Why per-task training: "Intuitively, different tasks (such as creative writing, math, or programming) have different criteria for which parts of the generated response matter most. Hence, it is best to train the important token classifier for the exact task at hand. However, doing so with Judge Decoding would require relabeling the data by human annotators, which can be costly and time-consuming in specialized domains such as medicine or law." — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20039v4)

**(c) Mechanism**
- [VERIFIED] Definition of importance: "A token $y_t \in y_{target}$ is unimportant if swapping that token for the draft model's output results in an equivalent answer. Likewise, if replacing $y_i$ (and continuing target generation from there) results in a different answer, then the original token was "important" and the token should be generated with $\theta_{target}$." Mismatch set: $\mathcal I(x)=\{t\in[1,T): \arg\max_{y_{next}} P(y_{next}\mid x,y_{1:t},\theta_{target}) \neq \arg\max_{y_{next}} P(y_{next}\mid x,y_{1:t},\theta_{draft})\}$, found "by re-encoding the target model response with the draft model". — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20039v4)
- [VERIFIED] The search procedure (Algorithm 1, "Search for Important Tokens"), in the paper's notation: $y \leftarrow \textsc{generate}(x,\theta_{target})$; $\alpha \leftarrow \textsc{extractAnswer}(y)$; $\widetilde y \leftarrow$ draft arg-max predictions on $x \oplus y$; $\mathcal I \leftarrow \{i \mid y_i \neq \widetilde y_i\}$; while $\mathcal I \neq \emptyset$: $t \leftarrow \min(\mathcal I)$; $\hat y = y_{1:t} \oplus \widetilde y_t \oplus \textsc{generate}(x \oplus y_{1:t} \oplus \widetilde y_t, \theta_{target})$; $\hat\alpha \leftarrow \textsc{extractAnswer}(\hat y)$; if $\alpha \equiv \hat\alpha$: record $(t, y_t, \widetilde y_t, \texttt{False})$, set $y \leftarrow \hat y$ and recompute $\widetilde y$ ("Equivalent answer, token $y_t$ is not important", "Continue search from the new response"); else record $(t, y_t, \widetilde y_t, \texttt{True})$ ("Different answer, token $y_t$ is important, keep it"); then $\mathcal I \leftarrow \{i \mid y_i \neq \widetilde y_i \cap i > t\}$. — [arXiv HTML v4, Algorithm 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20039v4)
- [VERIFIED] Why sequential and "semi-greedy": "we reframe our task from detecting individual important tokens to finding combinations of tokens that jointly affect the final answer ... the exact solution to this problem requires a tree search over possible token assignments ... we opt instead for a simpler, semi-greedy search that starts from the target model response and iteratively tries to replace mismatching target model tokens with their draft counterparts. ... That way, we guarantee that the search algorithm is aligned with what happens during inference". With the naive per-token variant, "fewer than 1% of the tokens were labeled as important" (Llama-3.1-70B target, Llama-3.2-1B draft) because "a sufficiently strong target model might recover from even a low-quality token". Guarantee stated: "whenever draft and target models produce different (non-equivalent) answers to a given prompt, our algorithm will find at least one important token". — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 3.1 and App. A](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20039v4)
- [VERIFIED] Answer equivalence is task-defined: for GSM8K "the final answer is literally whatever the model puts after "the final answer (is)""; for code "the output from the testing system given the generated code"; "in math problems, $\texttt{1.5}{\equiv}\texttt{3/2}$"; for tasks without formal evaluation "we can define $\textsc{extractAnswer}(y)=y$ and detect if two answers are equivalent using an LLM or human judges." Sampling is handled by fixing the randomness with the Gumbel-max trick: $y_{next} = \arg\max_i \log P(i \mid x \oplus y_{1:t}, \theta) + \textsc{GumbelPRNG}(s \oplus x \oplus y_{1:t})$. — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 3.1 and App. A](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20039v4)
- [VERIFIED] Classifier and features: "we train a simple logistic regression to detect important tokens"; features are hidden states that *encode the mismatching draft token* ("using the latter representations results in substantially greater classifier accuracy"), from the last layer; "We train a classifier on the last hidden state embeddings from both draft and target models (concatenated) for encoded draft tokens"; "using draft model representations alone is substantially worse"; "the AUC difference is within $1\%$" between target-only and concatenated features, and the vLLM implementation "predicts important tokens using only the hidden state of the target model". The last drafted position needs either one extra encoded token or automatic rejection. — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 3.2, Sec. 4.1, App. B, App. E](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20039v4)
- [VERIFIED] Training details: scikit-learn v1.4.2 logistic regression, `max_iter` 500, "$L_2$ regularization coefficient tuned individually for each setup (for instance $10^{-4}$ for 8B/70B), found by grid search over a logarithmic grid between $10^{0},\dots,10^{-7}$"; 90%/10% split at the sample level for model selection; alternatives tried: "a Random Forest with 128 trees and a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) with a single hidden layer consisting of 128 hidden units" — "the classifier quality is fairly robust to the choice of the regularization hyperparameter [and] of the classifier architecture". The number of classifier parameters is not stated (it equals the feature dimension plus one). — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 4.1 and App. B](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20039v4)
- [VERIFIED] Training-set sizes: GSM8K "≈7.47K training samples and ≈1.32K test samples"; "The training dataset contains ≈130K mismatches, about $20\%$ of which are deemed important." LiveCodeBench (`code_generation_lite`, `release_v5`, "880 programming tasks", 5-fold out-of-fold evaluation): "we only have ≈27K mismatching tokens to train the classifier ... only ≈5% of the mismatching tokens were deemed to affect the output quality." — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 4.1 and 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20039v4)
- [VERIFIED] Inference rule: "During said verification stage, the classifier is called when the original algorithm would reject a token. If the would-be-rejected token is deemed to be unimportant, i.e. not to affect the response quality, then we override the verification procedure and accept the token instead, proceeding to test subsequent tokens (if any) as per the original algorithm." Threshold: "we select a decision threshold that achieves a high recall (≥90%) in order to retain quality". Draft window: "we use the increased draft window size of $W{=}64$ tokens for all evaluations" (accepted-token experiments); vLLM windows are tuned per method (see (f)). — [arXiv HTML v4, Sec. 3.3 and 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20039v4)
- [VERIFIED] Cost of mining: "1B draft / 8B target model pair takes, on average, 65.6 seconds to process a GSM8K example, whereas the 8B draft / 70B target takes up an average of 706.4 seconds per sample. On LiveCodeBench, the same 8B draft / 70B target model takes up 449 seconds per sample." For the 405B target the mining ran through the Together API: "The experiment required ≈ $800 in API credits." Hardware: "A100-SXM4 GPUs with 80GB DRAM on servers with dual Epyc 7742 CPU and 1TiB RAM". — [arXiv HTML v4, App. P and R](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20039v4)
- [VERIFIED] The judge is not a confidence heuristic in disguise: the correlation "between the Llama 3.2 1B Instruct draft model's probability of the chosen draft token and the corresponding AutoJudge classifier probability ... varies within approximately $\pm 0.3$ per generation, and the full-sample covariance is $-0.073$." — [arXiv HTML v4, App. O](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20039v4)

- [VERIFIED] Authors' limitations: "Our approach assumes that the downstream task has a way to determine whether or not two solutions are equivalent. ## Key Question 3 — SelfJudge (Yoon et al., ICML 2026): the self-supervision signal, the rule, the results

### Takeaway
SelfJudge (arXiv 2510.02329; the title and ID in the brief are correct, venue ICML 2026) labels each draft/target mismatch without ground truth or rollouts: it substitutes the draft token into the target's own response and measures how much the target's likelihood of that token *and of the next 20 tokens of its original continuation* changes; mismatches whose "semantic preservation score" exceeds a threshold are labelled acceptable and a single logistic-regression judge on target hidden states is trained for all tasks. Gains are modest but broad: on Llama-3.1-8B/3.2-1B it adds 0.8–2.2 accepted tokens per cycle for 0.5–1.6 points of average task loss and 23–46% more tokens/s than lossless SD in vLLM; on 70B/8B, 10–23% more tokens/s with GSM8K accuracy unchanged and MMLU within ±0.8 points.

- [VERIFIED] Inference rule (same as Judge Decoding): with target hidden states $(h_{t+1},\ldots,h_{t+\gamma})=\mathcal M_{\text{target}}([\mathbf x_{\leq t}; d_{t+1},\ldots,d_{t+\gamma}])$, $\mathsf{Verifier}(h_t) > \theta \implies \text{Accept } d_t$, "where $\mathsf{Verifier}$ is a learned binary classifier (we use logistic regression for simplicity), and $\theta$ is a hyperparameter determined via holdout validation. ... - [VERIFIED] Step 1, which tokens are labelled: "we generate response $y$ using the target model given an instruction. We then feed response $y$ to the draft model ... - [VERIFIED] Step 2, the self-supervision signal ("semantic preservation score") for the draft alternative $z_i$: $s(y, z_i) = \log P(z_i \mid y_{<i}, y_{>i}) - \log P(y_i \mid y_{<i}, y_{>i})$, rewritten with autoregressive quantities as $s(y,z_i) = \underbrace{\log P(z_i \mid y_{<i}) - \log P(y_i \mid y_{<i})}_{s_{\text{prefix}}} + \underbrace{\log P(y_{>i} \mid y_{<i}, z_i) - \log P(y_{>i} \mid y_{\leq i})}_{\text{suffix likelihood}}$, i.e. $s = s_{\text{prefix}} + \log\frac{P(y_{>i}\mid z_i, y_{<i})}{P(y_{>i}\mid y_i, y_{<i})}$ ("Log Bayes Factor"). "In practice, our semantic preservation score uses $N$ future tokens as opposed to using all future tokens." "we obtain the probabilities conditioned on the replaced token by directly feeding the token-substituted response, i.e., $[y_{<i}, z_i, y_{>i}]$, into the target model." ### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The self-supervision signal is a *local* consistency test: a draft token is acceptable if the target, after reading it, still finds its own next 20 tokens about as likely as before. It is the offline, likelihood-based analogue of FLy's online test (does the target keep agreeing with the draft for the next W tokens), and of the "self-correction" observation of Judge Decoding.
- [INFERENCE] SelfJudge is not fully free of outcome supervision: its label threshold $\tau$ is set from AutoJudge labels on 100 GSM8K questions, so one answer-verifiable task is still needed for calibration.
- [INFERENCE] The claim that the OR rule preserves distributional guarantees is incorrect as written: accepting a token that the rejection-sampling test would have rejected changes the output distribution (this is exactly what the 2026 analysis in "Revisiting Lossy Verification" formalises for judge-based relaxation).
- [INFERENCE] Relative speedups over lossless SD in vLLM are 1.10–1.23× on 70B/8B and 1.23–1.46× on 8B/1B — the smallest in the family, in exchange for the smallest accuracy movement and one judge for all tasks. Table 6 (Pass@1 of 8.6% for the lossless baseline) is too weak a setting to conclude anything about code beyond "AutoJudge's general judge collapses there".

## Key Question 4 — Training-free loose verification: FLy (Li et al., ICLR 2026)

### Takeaway
FLy (arXiv 2511.22972, "Training-Free Loosely Speculative Decoding: Accepting Semantically Correct Drafts Beyond Exact Match", ICLR 2026; title and ID in the brief are correct) needs no judge: at a draft/target mismatch it first checks the target's normalised entropy (below θ = 0.3 → reject as usual), otherwise provisionally accepts the draft token and looks at the next W = 6 drafted positions; if the target's arg-max agrees with the draft at all of them the mismatch is kept, and any further mismatch is read as the target "correcting" an error and triggers rejection. With Llama-3.1-8B drafting 15 (70B) or 25 (405B) tokens it reports 2.74–2.88× over autoregressive decoding for 70B and 4.80–5.34× for 405B at temperature 0 (against 1.83–2.04× and 2.82–2.83× for lossless SD with the same drafter) with ≥99% of the target's accuracy; the rule presupposes long, high-quality draft chains and exact match on the following tokens.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Identity**
- [VERIFIED] Exact title "Training-Free Loosely Speculative Decoding: Accepting Semantically Correct Drafts Beyond Exact Match"; authors "Jinze Li, Yixing Xu, Guanchen Li, Shuo Yang, Jinfeng Xu, Xuanwu Yin, Dong Li, Edith C.H.Ngai, Emad Barsoum"; arXiv:2511.22972 [cs.CL], v1 28 Nov 2025, v2 5 Dec 2025, v3 29 Apr 2026; comment "Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2026". — [arXiv abstract page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22972)
- [VERIFIED] First author Jinze Li: "Advanced Micro Devices, Inc., Beijing, China" and "Department of Electrical and Electronic Engineering, The University of Hong Kong". — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22972v3)
- [VERIFIED] Code: "Our code is available at https://github.com/AMD-AGI/FLy"; the README titles the project "FLy: Training-Free Loosely Speculative Decoding ... (ICLR 2026)" and builds on EleutherAI's lm-evaluation-harness. — [GitHub repository](https://github.com/AMD-AGI/FLy)

**(b) Problem, in the authors' framing**
- [VERIFIED] "standard SPD is fundamentally constrained by its exact-match rule: the target accepts a draft token only if it is identical to its own generation. This rigid requirement forces the rejection of many plausible continuations, even those semantically aligned ... Recent work (Bachmann et al., 2025) shows that even high-quality drafts (e.g., human-written text) achieve low acceptance under this scheme." Against trained judges: "this approach requires carefully curated training data and training, incurring high annotation costs. Moreover, the supervisively trained classifier often fails to generalize across multiple domains or tasks". — [arXiv HTML v3, Sec. 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22972v3)
- [VERIFIED] Core insight: "LLMs tend to exhibit self-corrective behavior when conditioned on genuinely erroneous tokens, but not when faced with merely worded differently yet semantically valid alternatives (Pan et al., 2023; Bachmann et al., 2025). Building on this property, FLy leverages the target model's own behavior to distinguish harmful mismatches from semantically equivalent continuations." — [arXiv HTML v3, Sec. 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22972v3)

**(c) Mechanism (two tiers)**
- [VERIFIED] Notation: $K$ draft tokens $\hat y_i$ per round, target arg-max tokens $y_i$, match indicator $\Delta_i = \mathds 1[\hat y_i = y_i]$, mismatch set $J = \{j \mid \Delta_j = 0\}$. Standard rule: accept up to the first mismatch. — [arXiv HTML v3, Sec. 2.1–2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22972v3)
- [VERIFIED] Tier 1, entropy-level gate, computed from the target logits at the mismatch position: $h_j = \frac{-\sum_{v\in\mathcal V} p_{\mathcal M_T,j}(v)\,\log p_{\mathcal M_T,j}(v)}{\log|\mathcal V|} \in [0,1]$; $\textsf{Gate}(j) = \textsf{Strict}$ if $h_j < \theta$, $\textsf{Defer}$ if $h_j \geq \theta$. "If the target model is confident (i.e., $h_j<\theta$), indicating a strong preference for its top-rated token such as digits in arithmetic, we fall back to the standard SPD rule and reject all tokens starting from the mismatch position". — [arXiv HTML v3, Eqs. 5–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22972v3)
- [VERIFIED] Tier 2, token-level deferred window: $N_W(j) = \sum_{i=j+1}^{j+W} (1-\Delta_i)$ and $\textsf{DeferDecide}(j) = \textsf{Accept}$ if $h_j \geq \theta$, $N_W(j) = 0$ and $j + W \leq K$; $\textsf{Reject}$ otherwise. "if the target model can continue generating from the mismatched token without further divergence, the first divergent token is likely semantically correct and can be accepted. Otherwise, if the target keeps disagreeing, attempting to course-correct the mismatch and thereby causing another divergence, we reject the mismatch at $j$ and the following draft tokens." Boundary case: when $j+W>K$ "we immediately reject this mismatch at position $j$ since the rest of the tokens are not enough to judge the semantic correctness". Accepted length per round: $s_{\rm FLy} = \min\{s_{\rm gate}, s_{\rm defer}\}$. — [arXiv HTML v3, Eqs. 8–11](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22972v3)
- [VERIFIED] No extra forward pass: "FLy introduces no additional forward passes. Instead, it computes per-token entropy directly from the already-available logits." Measured cost per round (Table 1): "Gate & Window activation" 0.45 ms (70B) and 0.57 ms (405B), against target verification 58.62 ms and 200.97 ms and draft time 245.51 → 197.45 ms (70B, without → with MLA) and 428.30 → 363.01 ms (405B). — [arXiv HTML v3, Sec. 2.2–2.3, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22972v3)
- [VERIFIED] Hyper-parameters: "We set the deferred window length to $W=6$ and the entropy gate threshold to $\theta=0.3$ for all experiments. The draft token number $K$ at each round is set to 15 for the 70B target model and 25 for the 405B target model." — [arXiv HTML v3, Sec. 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22972v3)
- [VERIFIED] Third component, multi-level acceleration (MLA): because accepted runs are long, "the draft model's auto-regressive cost [becomes] the dominant runtime bottleneck", so the 8B drafter is itself accelerated with Prompt Lookup Decoding (n-gram retrieval); "Enabling MLA improves speedup from 2.69× to 2.86×". - [INFERENCE] Speedup over lossless SD with the same 8B drafter (SpS rows, temperature 0): 70B 2.88 / 1.83 = 1.57× in-domain and 2.74 / 2.04 = 1.34× out-of-domain; 405B 5.34 / 2.82 = 1.89× and 4.80 / 2.83 = 1.70×. - [INFERENCE] The window test is itself an exact-match test on the *following* tokens, so FLy needs K ≥ W + 1 drafted tokens and a drafter whose continuations the target usually reproduces verbatim; that is why it is evaluated with an 8B drafter and K = 15–25, and why it does not transfer to shallow EAGLE-style trees (AdaptiveSpec's criticism). The "Secure SD" study used speculation length 5, for which W = 6 can never accept, so their FLy numbers necessarily come from other (W, θ) settings.
- [INFERENCE] How the method operates at temperature 1 is not specified: the algorithm box defines matches against the target arg-max.

## Key Question 5 — "Think Before You Accept: Semantic Reflective Verification for Faster Speculative Decoding" (Wang et al., arXiv 2505.18629)

### Takeaway
The paper exists exactly under the title and ID given in the brief (Harbin Institute of Technology; arXiv preprint of 24 May 2025, no published venue found). Its "Reflective Verification" is training-free: the verification input is extended to [draft ‖ reflection prompt ‖ short prefix ‖ draft again], so one target forward pass yields the usual logits and a second, "reflective" set of logits for the same positions; the two are mixed with weight α = 0.3 and any ordinary acceptance test is then run on the mixed distribution. It adds about one accepted token per round and 5–15% throughput on top of exact-match, speculative-sampling or typical-acceptance verification (Llama-3 1B→8B and 8B→70B), with benchmark scores within noise; it changes the target distribution and roughly triples the number of tokens fed to the verifier.

- [VERIFIED] "existing verification methods rely heavily on distributional consistency while overlooking semantic correctness, thereby limiting the potential speedup of speculative decoding. **(c) Mechanism**
- [VERIFIED] Verification input: $\mathrm{Draft_{final}} = \mathrm{Concat}(\mathrm{Draft_{ori}} \| \mathrm{Prompt_{reflection}} \| \mathrm{Prefix_{position}} \| \mathrm{Draft_{ori}})$, "where $\mathrm{Draft_{ori}}$ denotes the candidate tokens generated by the draft model, $\mathrm{Prompt_{refection}}$ is a probe designed to prompt the model to reflect, and $\mathrm{Prefix_{position}}$ refers to the tokens preceding the current draft candidate within the context, serving to help the model locate the position for regeneration." "Benefiting from the unidirectional attention mechanism, the subsequent template leaves the verification of the initial draft tokens unaffected." The extra positions are discarded afterwards: "They are pruned after each forward pass". — [arXiv HTML, Sec. 3.1, Eq. 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18629v1)
- [VERIFIED] Decision signal and rule: logits at position $i$ of the first copy ("consistency") and at the corresponding position of the second copy ("semantic correctness") are fused, $Prob_{mix}[i] = \mathrm{Softmax}((1-\alpha)\cdot Logits[i] + \alpha \cdot Logits[i+\mathrm{shift\_len}])$, "$\mathrm{shift\_len}$ denotes the number of tokens occupied by the designed prompt and the first draft segment, and $\alpha$ is a hyper-parameter that controls the weight of the reflective logits." The fused $p_i$ then replaces the target distribution in whatever test is used; for speculative sampling: accept while $r_i \le \frac{p_i(x)}{q_i(x)}$, $r_i \sim U(0,1)$, and resample from $norm(\max(0, p_{n+1}(x) - q_{n+1}(x)))$ (Algorithm 1); exact-match and typical-acceptance variants are Algorithms 2–3. "This method merely produces an output distribution with a higher acceptance rate and does not involve any specific verification mechanism." — [arXiv HTML, Sec. 3.2–3.3, Eq. 2, App. A](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18629v1)
- [VERIFIED] Hyper-parameters (Table 4): $\alpha = 0.3$ everywhere ("Based on the ablation results, we set the hyper-parameter to 0.3"); prefix length 4; draft length $K$ = 5 (1B&8B, MT-Bench), 8 (1B&8B, GSM8K and HumanEval), 8 (8B&70B, MT-Bench), 10 (8B&70B, GSM8K and HumanEval); temperature 0.8 on MT-Bench and 0.2 on GSM8K and HumanEval ("For different draft models and datasets, we select the optimal draft length K and temperature"). Reflection prompts tested (Table 2): "${draft} Oh! I made a mistake! The correct answer is: ${prefix} ${draft}", "${draft} Let me correct myself, the right response is: ${prefix} ${draft}", "${draft} [BACK] ${prefix} ${draft}", and an empty prompt "${draft} ${prefix} ${draft}". — [arXiv HTML, App. B, Table 4 and Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18629v1)
- No judge, no labels, no training ("a training-free and semantics-aware approach").

**(d) Lossy in what sense; guarantees**
- [VERIFIED] The verified distribution is no longer the target's; with speculative sampling the output follows the fused distribution, not $p$. The authors acknowledge the reflective logits cannot be trusted alone: "fully substituting the output distribution with reflective logits leads to performance degradation. Since the method is training-free, the reflective ability of LLMs is not reliable enough to maintain consistency with the original distribution." (R1) "The proposed method, while empirically shown to be useful, is technically no longer a speculative algorithm and lacks a theoretical explanation"; "In Appendix D, the authors mention "For different draft models and datasets, we select the optimal temperature". This is similar to "training on the test set""; "state-of-the-art tree-based verification - such as EAGLE-2 ... is not discussed in the paper"; "The so-called "speculative decoding with exact match" is only correct in greedy decoding". (R2) "By the nature of LLMs, it seems the target model is heavily incentivized to output semantic logits that agree with the drafted tokens as the position prefix encourages the model to continue repeating by repeating the draft tokens. (R3) "The paper does not clearly define core terms like "semantic correctness,"". — [HF mirror of ICLR 2026 submissions, row GkMxEQlWyU](https://huggingface.co/datasets/davidheineman/iclr-2026)

**(h) Relation to predecessors and successors**
- [VERIFIED] Positioning: Judge Decoding "trains a classifier on human-annotated data"; reward-guided SD "introduces an auxiliary reward model to evaluate drafts, allowing for step-level discrimination"; "Although some methods (Bachmann et al., 2025; Liao et al., 2025) leverage models with deep representations, they typically require additional models and training. We achieve a favorable balance between semantic-level validation and plug-and-play applicability." It is cited by FLy as the training-free route ("leveraging the reflective capacity of LLMs to semantically probe draft correctness") and by CoSpec among loosely verified methods. — [arXiv HTML, Sec. 1 and 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18629v1); [FLy HTML, Sec. 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22972v3)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Throughput gain of the reflective step over the same base rule, from Table 1: exact match 58.84 / 51.02 = +15.3% (1B→8B) and 20.00 / 18.09 = +10.6% (8B→70B); speculative sampling +15.1% and +7.5%; typical acceptance +7.6% and +6.0%. This is the smallest gain in the family, because the base rule is kept and only the reference distribution is nudged.
- [INFERENCE] Score differences are small and of both signs (e.g. MT-Bench 7.44 → 7.37 on 8B, GSM8K 77.63 → 77.41, HumanEval 65.85 → 68.90); without confidence intervals (164 HumanEval problems) the reported "improvements" cannot be separated from sampling noise, as a reviewer notes.
- [INFERENCE] The empty-prompt row of Table 2 (repeat the draft without any reflective instruction: #MAT 7.45 vs 6.39, Pass@1 64.63 vs 65.85) suggests that a large part of the acceptance gain comes from the target copying the first copy of the draft, while the instruction mainly protects accuracy — consistent with the reviewer's "repetition" concern.

## Key Question 6 — "Alignment-Augmented Speculative Decoding with Alignment Sampling and Conditional Verification" (AASD; Wang et al., EMNLP 2025)

### Takeaway
The paper exists under the title and ID given (arXiv 2505.13204, EMNLP 2025 main conference; Soochow University and Huawei Cloud). It is a retrieval-based, single-model method for long-context generation: drafts are n-grams copied from the prompt, widened into a small tree with tokens the model itself preferred during prefilling ("alignment sampling"), and verified with an entropy-dependent probability threshold ("conditional verification") instead of exact match. The acceptance rule is a statistical threshold, not a correctness judge, so the paper sits on the boundary of this family; it reports a mean acceptance length up to 2.39, speedups of 1.6–2.2× over autoregressive decoding (LLaMA-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-32B) and task scores that *increase* on average (44.69 → 47.98 for LLaMA-3.1-8B) because the relaxed rule lets the model copy from the context.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Identity**
- [VERIFIED] Exact title "Alignment-Augmented Speculative Decoding with Alignment Sampling and Conditional Verification"; authors "Jikai Wang, Zhenxu Tian, Juntao Li, Qingrong Xia, Xinyu Duan, Zhefeng Wang, Baoxing Huai, Min Zhang"; arXiv:2505.13204 [cs.CL], v1 19 May 2025, v2 12 Sep 2025; comment "Accepted at EMNLP 2025 Main". First author: "Soochow University, China" / "Key Laboratory of Data Intelligence and Advanced Computing, Soochow University"; co-authors from "Huawei Cloud, China". Code: not found. ACL Anthology identifier: not found. — [arXiv abstract page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13204); [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13204v2)

**(b) Problem, in the authors' framing**
- [VERIFIED] "two issues have been largely overlooked in previous work on retrieval-based speculative decoding. First, poor alignment between the retrieved drafts and the model's output distribution leads to a low acceptance rate. Second, in many generation scenarios, the input context and the generated content have strong correlation. As a result, drafts retrieved from the input context tend to be of higher quality and do not require strict verification". They cite Judge Decoding for the premise: "Recent research (Bachmann et al., 2025) shows that even a high-quality draft will have a low acceptance rate if it does not align with the model output distribution" and "They trained an additional module on carefully designed data to judge whether to accept draft tokens. However, for prompt-based speculative decoding, as the draft source is highly relevant to the answer, a training-free verification strategy can be designed". Own measurement (100 NQ samples, LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct): "About 45% retrieved draft tokens are misaligned with the model output. The acceptance rate of misaligned tokens is much lower than that of aligned tokens." — [arXiv HTML v2, Sec. 1 and 2.3–2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13204v2)

**(c) Mechanism**
- [VERIFIED] Drafting: a "draft pool" indexing n-grams of the input context (and of newly generated tokens), queried with the last tokens of the sequence ("We use 6-grams and set the maximum length of the key for retrieval to 6"). Alignment sampling: at positions where the retrieved token was not the model's top prediction during prefilling, the higher-ranked tokens are added as siblings ("Each position can be extended by at most two tokens"), giving a draft tree verified with a tree attention mask. — [arXiv HTML v2, Sec. 3.1–3.2 and 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13204v2)
- [VERIFIED] Conditional verification: a draft token $\hat x_i^j$ is accepted if $p_M(\hat x_i^j \mid \mathbb F(\hat x_i^j)) \geqslant \delta_i^j$ with $\delta_i^j = \min\left(-\alpha \sum P(\hat x_i^j)\log P(\hat x_i^j) + \beta,\ \Delta\right)$ and $\Delta = \max_{\hat x_i^j} P(\hat x_i^j)$; "$\alpha$ ... is the factor affecting the threshold value due to information entropy. $\beta$ is the benchmark value of the threshold. The function of $\Delta$ is to ensure that the token with the highest probability will be accepted during verification." Direction of the adaptation: "Low-confidence tokens can be processed with stricter thresholds to maintain precision, while high-confidence tokens benefit from relaxed thresholds to ensure recall." Scope: "We only perform conditional verification on the draft token retrieved from the input context and selected by alignment sampling. Strict verification is still applied for tokens retrieved from generated context. We accept the longest draft candidate that passes verification." Values: "$\alpha$ is set to 0.1 and 0.2 for LLaMA3.1 and Qwen2.5, respectively. $\beta$ is set to 0.1." — [arXiv HTML v2, Sec. 3.3, Eqs. 4–6, and Sec. 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13204v2)
- No judge, no training: "It is a plug-and-play speculative decoding algorithm that does not require additional modules or training."

**(d) Lossy in what sense; guarantees**
- [VERIFIED] The authors present the rule as a new sampling method rather than as an approximation of the target: "Equipped with conditional verification, AASD introduces a novel sampling method, which enhances access to input fragments." No guarantee; the case study shows what happens when the threshold is too loose: with a fixed threshold $\delta$ = 1e-7 "The prediction is completely messy", and "the threshold should be large enough to keep the model output logical and fluent." — [arXiv HTML v2, Sec. 4.1 and 4.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13204v2)

**(e) Experimental setup**
- [VERIFIED] Models "LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct ... and Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct" (no separate draft model); tasks: QA (NQ — 300 sampled validation queries —, TriviaQA, 2WikiMQA, HotpotQA; F1), summarisation (Multi-News, GovReport; ROUGE-L), code completion (RepoBench-P, LCC; Edit Sim), using LongBench subsets, plus SpecBench for speed. Quality baselines are decoding strategies (greedy, top-k with k = 50, nucleus with p = 0.8, beam search with width 10); speed baselines are autoregressive decoding, REST and PLD. "We use 4 A100-PCIE-40GB GPUs for all experiments." — [arXiv HTML v2, Sec. 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13204v2)

**(f) Headline results**
- [VERIFIED] Abstract: "our approach increases the average generation score by 3.3 points for the LLaMA3 model. Our method achieves a mean acceptance length up to 2.39 and speed up generation by 2.23×." Table 1 averages over the 8 datasets: LLaMA3.1-8B greedy 44.69, top-k 40.52, nucleus 41.35, beam 44.16, AASD 47.98 (largest gains on RepoBench-P 48.11 → 58.84 and LCC 66.05 → 71.03; TQA 92.13 → 91.56); Qwen2.5-32B greedy 39.72, AASD 40.60 (code tasks slightly lower: RepoBench-P 37.72 → 37.23, LCC 60.85 → 58.65). — [arXiv HTML v2, abstract and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13204v2)
- [VERIFIED] Table 2 (mean acceptance length MAL / tokens per second / speedup over autoregressive greedy decoding; 2WikiMQA, GovReport, LCC). LLaMA3.1-8B: REST 1.08 / 18.26 / 0.91, 1.27 / 21.20 / 1.05, 1.26 / 26.09 / 1.07; PLD 1.93 / 32.77 / 1.63, 1.42 / 21.33 / 1.06, 2.06 / 36.52 / 1.77; AASD 2.37 / 37.18 / 1.85, 1.97 / 34.00 / 1.69, 2.39 / 46.20 / 2.23. Qwen2.5-32B: PLD 2.13 / 14.04 / 1.74, 1.51 / 10.01 / 1.23, 1.46 / 8.72 / 1.19; AASD 2.14 / 13.87 / 1.72, 2.18 / 14.20 / 1.74, 1.89 / 11.80 / 1.61. — [arXiv HTML v2, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13204v2)

Quality ablation (Table 4, average score): AASD 47.98; fixed threshold 0.1 instead of the adaptive one 46.80; top-k (k = 5) verification 35.66. — [arXiv HTML v2, Tables 4–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13204v2)

**(g) Costs, caveats, limitations**
- [VERIFIED] "The performance drops rapidly for more complex tasks such as code completion" when the threshold is lowered (App. A); for Qwen2.5-32B AASD "does not show an advantage in code-related tasks, possibly because the model's inherent code capabilities are relatively weak, making it unable to effectively reject the wrong draft tokens." Safety: "too relaxed verification may affect the model's ability to defend against attack inputs. If the input context contain harmful information, using AASD may lead the model to incorporate it into its responses. Therefore, in practical applications, AASD requires additional safety alignment". — [arXiv HTML v2, Sec. 4.2, 4.6, Limitations and App. A](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13204v2)
- [VERIFIED] In a later comparison by another group (LLaMA-3.3-70B, T = 0, mean of GSM8K / HumanEval / MBPP) AASD gives 1.61× (τ 1.90) with score 90.64 vs 90.05 for the target; on Qwen3-32B 1.54× with 83.88 vs 83.84. — [CoSpec HTML, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24793)

**(h) Relation to predecessors and successors**
- [VERIFIED] Builds on prompt-lookup / retrieval drafting (PLD, LLMA, REST) and takes from Judge Decoding only the premise that "correct but non-aligned draft candidates can be accepted". FLy classifies it among loose methods ("couple this with flexible thresholding strategies that adaptively accept high-quality but imperfect candidates"); CoSpec uses it as the "utility-improving" baseline. — [arXiv HTML v2, Sec. 2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13204v2); [FLy HTML, Sec. 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22972v3); [CoSpec HTML, Sec. 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24793)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The acceptance rule is a per-token probability threshold whose strictness *increases* with the entropy of the verifying distribution — the opposite direction to typical acceptance and to FLy's entropy gate (which relax at high entropy). It trusts the copied token when the model is confident and the token still has probability ≥ ~0.1.
- [INFERENCE] The reported accuracy gain is not evidence that relaxed verification is harmless in general: it comes from long-context tasks where copying from the prompt is usually right, and it is compared with greedy decoding of the same model, not with a larger target. There is no small-draft/large-target pair in the main experiments.
- [INFERENCE] The relaxed rule itself contributes little speed (1.85 vs 1.78, 1.69 vs 1.62, 2.23 vs 1.89 in Table 5); most of the acceleration is the retrieval drafting.

- EMNLP proceedings page and code not located. No experiment at 70B or above in the main tables (only a LLaMA-2 70B comparison with small-model SD on NQ: AASD 1.53× vs 1.28× for a 7B drafter).
