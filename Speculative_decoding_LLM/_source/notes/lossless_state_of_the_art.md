## 1. Independent draft models and how to train them (DistillSpec, Online SD, direct alignment, FastDraft; acceptance rates of common pairs)

### Takeaway
A separate small autoregressive drafter is the simplest lossless design; the research question is alignment (distil the drafter toward the target, offline or online) versus drafter latency, and the verified evidence says latency matters as much as accuracy. Verified acceptance rates for off-the-shelf pairs are moderate (token acceptance roughly 0.35–0.8 depending on pair, task and temperature), which is why trained/aligned drafters and target-conditioned heads took over.

### Cited Findings

**DistillSpec** — VERIFIED metadata: "DistillSpec: Improving Speculative Decoding via Knowledge Distillation", first author Yongchao Zhou (Google Research / University of Toronto, student researcher at Google), arXiv 2310.08461 (v2 2024-03-31). Venue: ICLR 2024 per Semantic Scholar/DBLP key `conf/iclr/ZhouLRMRKKA24` (secondary; arXiv metadata has no venue). Code: not found.
- (b) Problem. VERIFIED: "identifying a compact draft model that is well-aligned with the target model is challenging."
- (c) Mechanism. VERIFIED: "uses knowledge distillation to better align the draft model with the target model, before applying SD. DistillSpec makes two key design choices...: utilizing on-policy data generation from the draft model, and tailoring the divergence function to the task and decoding strategy." Divergences studied (VERIFIED, §5.2): "1) forward KL (FKL), 2) Jenson-Shannon divergence (JSD), 3) reverse KL (RKL), and 4) total variation distance (TVD)". Link to acceptance (VERIFIED): the token-level acceptance rate "satisfies β(x, y<t) = 1 − D_TVD(p(y_t), q(y_t))" (citing Leviathan Cor. 3.6).
- Which divergence wins (VERIFIED, §5.2): "on XSum with greedy decoding, using data generated from both M_q and M_p leads to the best performance, with JSD slightly outperforming the other divergences. However, on GSM8K with greedy decoding, FKL with only draft-generated data emerges as the best setup. In contrast, with temperature sampling (at T=1)... RKL combined with data generated by M_p is the most effective setup." And: "although TVD is the objective we should aim to optimize for SD (cf. Eq. 3), its direct optimization does not yield the best performance in most of the settings explored. - (d) Losslessness: the distilled drafter is used inside standard SD, so the main results are strictly lossless (same distribution). One-line lossy note: the paper also combines DistillSpec "with lossy SD to achieve fine-grained control over the latency vs. task performance trade-off" (VERIFIED abstract).
- (e) Setup (VERIFIED, §5): "GPT-like decoder-only Transformer models trained on LM1B..., where the target and draft models have 234M and 33M parameters, respectively; and... ### Inferences
- Divergence choice is empirical: TVD is the quantity that equals the rejection rate, yet DistillSpec finds JSD/FKL/RKL each win in some setting; later 2026 work (LK losses, TVP, REAL, verification-aware training; section 7) revisits exactly this gap with acceptance-length objectives.
- For small targets (≤8B) a separate drafter costs a large share of step time (47% for Qwen3-8B at bs 1 in vLLM), so the independent-drafter design is mostly attractive for 70B-class targets; for small targets, head-based or parallel drafters dominate.
- Reported token acceptance for off-the-shelf small→large pairs clusters around 0.4–0.75 at low temperature; with the capped-geometric formula this gives roughly 1.6–3.5 tokens per target call, consistent with the 1.5×–2.5× end-to-end numbers seen in the framework reports above.

### Gaps
- No primary source found that tabulates the per-token acceptance rate α specifically for Llama-3.2-1B/3B → Llama-3.x-70B or Qwen small → Qwen large; what is verified is end-to-end speedup (NVIDIA: 3.55×/3.16×/2.63× for 1B/3B/8B drafters on one H200; vLLM: 1.5× with a 0.5B drafter on 4×H100) and the vLLM-study chain statistics (accepted length ≈2.3, acceptance rate ≈0.42–0.43 per proposed token for a short chain).
- DistillSpec hardware and code: not found. Direct-alignment hardware: not found.
- Venue of DistillSpec (ICLR 2024), OSD (ICML 2024) confirmed only through Semantic Scholar, not through OpenReview/proceedings pages.

## 2. Self-drafting with extra heads or layers (Medusa, Hydra, EAGLE line, HASS, Falcon, GRIFFIN, CORAL, PARD, Kangaroo, LayerSkip, Draft & Verify, SWIFT, CLaSp, ReDrafter)

### Takeaway
Drafters that reuse the target's hidden states (Medusa heads → Hydra → EAGLE's feature-level autoregression → EAGLE-2 dynamic trees → EAGLE-3 token-level training with multi-layer fusion) became the dominant lossless family in 2024–2025: on Vicuna-13B at T=0, batch 1, the authors' own tables go 2.07× (Medusa) → 3.07× (EAGLE) → 4.26× (EAGLE-2) → 5.58× (EAGLE-3) on MT-bench. Medusa's headline 2.3–2.8× is not a strictly lossless number (Medusa-2 fine-tunes the backbone and the paper's sampling rule is the lossy "typical acceptance"); third-party greedy numbers for Medusa are 1.4–2.1×. Layer-skipping self-drafters (Draft & Verify, SWIFT, CLaSp, Kangaroo, LayerSkip) need no or little training but report 1.3–2.2×.

### Cited Findings

**Medusa** — VERIFIED: "Medusa: Simple LLM Inference Acceleration Framework with Multiple Decoding Heads", Tianle Cai (Princeton University / Together AI), arXiv 2401.10774; venue ICML 2024 per Semantic Scholar (secondary; the HTML carries ICML keywords). Code: https://github.com/FasterDecoding/Medusa (arXiv comment).
- (b) VERIFIED abstract: speculative decoding's "implementation is impeded by the challenges associated with acquiring and maintaining a separate draft model."
- (c) VERIFIED: "adding extra decoding heads to predict multiple subsequent tokens in parallel. Using a tree-based attention mechanism, Medusa constructs multiple candidate continuations and verifies them simultaneously in each decoding step." Heads sit on the last hidden state; the k-th head predicts position k+1 independently of the other drafted tokens (Hydra's description: existing heads are "sequentially independent"). Two training modes (VERIFIED abstract): "Medusa-1: Medusa is directly fine-tuned on top of a frozen backbone LLM, enabling lossless inference acceleration. Medusa-2: Medusa is fine-tuned together with the backbone LLM, enabling better prediction accuracy of Medusa heads and higher speedup but needing a special training recipe that preserves the backbone model's capabilities."
- Typical acceptance (VERIFIED, §2.3.1): accept a candidate token when "p_original(x_{n+k} | x_1..x_{n+k−1}) > min(ε, δ exp(−H(p_original(· | x_1..x_{n+k−1}))))", with "greedy decoding for the first token". The paper states: "we can reuse the rejection sampling scheme as used in speculative decoding... to generate consistent responses with the same distribution as the original model. However, it cannot further enhance the acceleration rate. Alternatively, we introduce a typical acceptance scheme" and (appendix) "we do not insist on an exact correspondence between the output and language model distribution." Repo README: "Relaxing the requirement of matching the distribution of the original model makes the non-greedy generation even faster than greedy decoding."
- (d) Classification: Medusa-1 + greedy verification = lossless for greedy decoding (frozen backbone). Medusa-1/2 + typical acceptance = lossy (output distribution is not the target's). Medusa-2 = lossy with respect to the original model even under greedy, because the backbone weights are changed (the paper reports quality deltas, Table 1: "6.18 (+0.01) | 7.25 (-0.07) | 6.43 (-0.14) | 7.18 (+0.05)" GPT-4-judged MT-Bench scores).
- (e) Setup VERIFIED: Vicuna-7B/13B (v1.5), Vicuna-33B, Zephyr-7B; MT-Bench; "The baseline is the default Huggingface implementation"; training "5 hours for Medusa-1 on Vicuna 7B model with a single NVIDIA A100 PCIE GPU to train on 60k ShareGPT samples". The acceptance rule and temperature used for the headline Fig. 3/Table 1 numbers: not found in the main text (the typical-acceptance ablation is run "with a fixed temperature of 0.7").
- (f) Results VERIFIED: "Medusa-1 shows a 2.18× speedup, while Medusa-2 further improves this to a 2.83×" (Vicuna-7B); "13B model, Medusa-1 results in a 2.33× speed increase, while Medusa-2... 2.83×"; Table 1 (Medusa-2): acceleration rate 3.47/3.14/3.51/3.01 tokens per step and speedup 2.83/2.66/2.83/2.35 for Vicuna-7B/Zephyr-7B/Vicuna-13B/Vicuna-33B; Table 3: "Medusa-1 heads without tree attention ∼1.5x | Adding tree attention ∼1.9x | Using optimized tree configuration ∼2.2x | Training heads with Medusa-2 ∼2.8x". Gemma 4 uses a separate assistant checkpoint for MTP decoding"). DeepSeek's own production baseline before 2026 was "MTP-1" (DSpark paper, section 7).

### Inferences
- The EAGLE line's gains come from three separable levers, each verified above: target-feature conditioning (EAGLE), calibrated-confidence tree shaping at no training cost (EAGLE-2, +20–40%), and training the head the way it is used (HASS/CORAL/GRIFFIN, then EAGLE-3's token-level multi-step training with more data, another ~1.4×).
- Medusa's widely quoted 2.2–2.8× (or "3.6×" in the listing abstract) should not be cited as a lossless speedup: the paper itself separates the distribution-preserving rule from typical acceptance and reports quality scores rather than exactness; under greedy third-party tests it is 1.4–2.1×.
- Training-free layer-skipping is capped around 1.3–2× in every verified report because the "drafter" still costs a large fraction of a full forward pass (Kangaroo states this explicitly).
- Head-based drafters report much lower speedups when re-measured by others on newer targets (EAGLE-3: 5.5× on Vicuna-13B in its own paper vs about 1.8–2.5× on Qwen3-4B/8B in the DFlash and JetSpec tables, sections 6–7), so per-paper numbers should be read with their target model.

## 3. Retrieval, n-gram and copy-based drafting (REST, Prompt Lookup Decoding, LLMA, SuffixDecoding, Token Recycling, SAM-Decoding, ANPD; OpenAI Predicted Outputs)

### Takeaway
Model-free drafters cost almost nothing per step, are trivially lossless (the target verifies every token) and shine when output repeats the prompt, earlier outputs or a corpus: 1.2–2.7× on general benchmarks, but up to 5.3× on agentic/repetitive workloads (SuffixDecoding) and "2x-4x" on input-grounded tasks (PLD). They are the variants most widely shipped (vLLM n-gram/suffix, HF prompt lookup, TGI n-gram, llama.cpp n-gram family, TensorRT-LLM suffix automaton) and are routinely combined with neural drafters.

### Inferences
- Copy-based drafting has zero drafter latency, so its speedup equals (tokens accepted per target call) minus verification overhead; that is why it wins on edit/agent workloads with very long matches and loses on free-form chat (PLD 1.15× on translation).
- The same lookup idea appears under four product names (prompt lookup, n-gram, predicted outputs, speculative edits); only the source of the guess differs (prompt, user-supplied prediction, original file).
- Because acceptance for copied text is near 1 on matched spans and near 0 elsewhere, hybrids that switch per step (SAM-Decoding+EAGLE-2, TensorRT-LLM "SA enhancement", 2026 "Hybrid Spark") are the natural end state.

## 4. Trees and multiple candidates (SpecInfer, Sequoia, MCSD, RSD, OPT-Tree, DySpec, SpecHub, traversal verification) and the verification rules that keep them lossless

### Takeaway
Verifying a tree of candidates in one target pass (tree attention) raises accepted tokens per step, and every method in this family keeps the exact target distribution by using a multi-candidate generalisation of rejection sampling (SpecInfer's multi-step speculative sampling, SpecTr's optimal-transport draft selection, recursive rejection sampling with or without replacement, or sequence-level rules such as block/traversal verification). What each paper adds is either a better tree shape (static optimal: Sequoia; per-step dynamic: EAGLE-2, OPT-Tree, DySpec), a better way to draw siblings (without replacement: Sequoia, RSD, MCSD), or a better acceptance rule (SpecHub, block verification, traversal verification, 2026 hierarchical/OT-on-trees rules). Gains from better verification alone are single-digit to low-double-digit percent.

**Theory of multi-draft acceptance (verified abstracts):**
- "Multi-Draft Speculative Sampling: Canonical Decomposition and Theoretical Limits", Ashish Khisti (Qualcomm AI Research / University of Toronto), ICLR 2025 spotlight, arXiv 2410.18234: "the optimal scheme can be decomposed into a two-step solution: in the first step an importance sampling (IS) type scheme is used to select one intermediate token; in the second step (single-draft) speculative sampling is applied".
- "Towards Optimal Multi-draft Speculative Decoding", Zhengmian Hu (University of Maryland / Adobe Research), arXiv 2502.18779: "sampling without replacement outperforming sampling with replacement. Additionally, existing verification algorithms do not reach the theoretical upper bound".
- "A Theoretical Perspective for Speculative Decoding Algorithm", Ming Yin (Princeton), NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2411.00841: speculative decoding's rejection count "indicates its optimality among the class, and having fewer rejections needs to suffer quality loss or requires extra information."
- 2026 additions: lossless Hierarchical Speculative Decoding (arXiv 2601.05724; listed under ICLR 2026 by a community list, venue UNVERIFIED): "a provably lossless verification method... integrating HSD into EAGLE-3 yields over a 12% performance gain"; UniVer (arXiv 2605.04543): tree verification as conditional OT, "improves acceptance length by 4.2% to 8.5% over standard recursive rejection sampling without replacement, while maintaining exact distributional alignment"; SpecTr-GBV (arXiv 2604.25925): multi-draft + greedy block verification; RheoSampling (arXiv 2609.21827): "the first dynamic-tree method with both context-aware top-K construction and stochastic sampling while maintaining losslessness"; Dynamic Delayed Tree Expansion (arXiv 2602.16994): "allows OT-based methods like SpecInfer to outperform Traversal Verification for the first time, achieving 5% higher average throughput".

### Inferences
- Is each still lossless? Yes for all eight named methods as published (each uses or proves a distribution-preserving acceptance rule); the practical caveat is that several evaluate only under greedy decoding (OPT-Tree, Token Recycling, SAM-Decoding), where losslessness reduces to exact-match of the argmax and tree width simply means "is the target's argmax among my candidates".
- A structural tension is documented by RheoSampling's framing and by vLLM's defaults (`draft_sample_method` default `greedy`): dynamic top-K trees are deterministic in the draft, so under sampling they need special treatment to stay exactly lossless; stochastic trees (SpecInfer/Sequoia/RSD style) are lossless by construction but waste budget at low temperature.
- Verification-rule improvements are worth ~5–12% (block verification 5–8%, traversal 2.2–5.7% acceptance length, HSD 12% on EAGLE-3, UniVer 4.2–8.5%), far less than drafter improvements.

### Gaps
- MCSD and RSD headline speedups: abstracts give no single number; per-table values not extracted.
- RSD venue: not found in primary metadata (its HTML uses the ICML template, which is not proof of acceptance). MCSD venue only via Semantic Scholar (NLPCC 2024).

## 5. Adaptive draft length, scheduling and parallelism (SpecDec++, AdaEDL, SVIP, dynamic lookahead / HF heuristic, PEARL, AMUSD, cascades, staged, TriForce, MagicDec, SPIN, asynchronous and "speculative speculative" variants)

### Takeaway
Three orthogonal system levers sit on top of any drafter: (i) stop drafting when the next token is likely to be rejected (learned head: SpecDec++, DISCO; entropy rules: AdaEDL, SVIP; confidence threshold: Kangaroo, Hugging Face default), typically worth 7–22% over the best fixed length (AdaEDL reports up to 57% where drafting is expensive); (ii) cascade drafters (staged SD, CS Drafting, TriForce, hierarchical SD) so the drafter itself is speculated; (iii) overlap drafting and verification in time on spare compute (PEARL, AMUSD, DSI, PipeSpec, SwiftSpec, SpecBranch, Mirror-SD, and "Speculative Speculative Decoding"/Saguaro at ICLR 2026), worth roughly 1.3–1.9× over sequential SD at the cost of extra devices. Batch-aware controllers (TurboSpec, MagicDec, TETRIS, Nightjar) decide when to speculate at all under load. All are lossless because they change when/what is drafted, not how tokens are accepted.

### Inferences
- Training-free entropy/confidence stopping rules give most of the benefit of learned stop heads (AdaEDL/SVIP 10–22% vs SpecDec++ 7–11% and DISCO 10% over the best static length), which explains why the shipped default (Hugging Face) is a threshold heuristic.
- Parallel/asynchronous variants do not raise acceptance; they hide drafter latency behind verification. Their ceiling is therefore the "free drafter" limit of the same acceptance profile, and they consume a second device (SSD uses an extra H100; AMUSD/PipeSpec multiple GPUs; Mirror-SD NPUs).
- DSI's result is from simulation and PEARL/AMUSD/SSD are batch-1 results; none of the verified sources shows these gains at high concurrency except MineDraft/FASER/ForeSpark, which are vLLM-based serving studies.

### Gaps
- PEARL inference hardware: not found in the text searched. CS Drafting's results are on FLAN-T5 targets only (no modern chat LLM).
- SPIN's explicit losslessness claim: not found.
- Whether any production stack runs SSD/Saguaro-style pre-speculation: not found.

## 6. Benchmarks: what Spec-Bench measures, and the latest comparative numbers that could be verified

### Takeaway
Spec-Bench measures wall-clock speedup over autoregressive decoding and mean accepted tokens per step on 480 prompts (6 task types × 80), with Vicuna targets, greedy decoding, FP16 and batch size 1 on one GPU; its public leaderboard was last led by SAM-Decoding+EAGLE-2 (Vicuna-7B: 2.38× on an RTX 3090, 2.73× on an A100) and by EAGLE-3 on Vicuna-13B (3.02× on an A100), and it contains none of the 2026 parallel drafters. ### Inferences
- Spec-Bench is now a legacy yardstick (Vicuna, batch 1, PyTorch); the field moved to Qwen3-family targets, serving engines (SGLang/vLLM/TensorRT-LLM) and concurrency sweeps, with SPEED-Bench the closest thing to a successor.
- Every independent measurement lands well below the corresponding authors' table: EAGLE-3 5.5× (own, Vicuna-13B) vs 3.0× (Spec-Bench) vs 1.3–1.9× (SPEED-Bench, batch 32) vs about 1.8–2.5× as a baseline in the DFlash/JetSpec tables. Ratios between methods are more trustworthy than absolute speedups, and even those shrink under matched training (4.60 → 1.53 spread).
- Math/code benchmarks with long predictable spans drive the record numbers (6–10×); on open-ended chat the same drafters give 2.7–4.6× at batch 1.

### Gaps
- No third-party leaderboard that includes DFlash, DSpark, JetSpec, Domino or PARD-2 was found; Spec-Bench's board stops at EAGLE-3.
- The Spec-Bench leaderboard file carries no "last updated" date in the text read; values are as retrieved on 2026-11-22.
- MMSpec (VLM benchmark, arXiv 2603.14989) and the energy benchmark were not read.

## 7. New in 2026: lossless papers that set speedup records or change the design space

### Takeaway
2026 changed the drafter, not the verifier: single-pass "parallel" drafters conditioned on target features (block diffusion: DFlash, ICML 2026) displaced autoregressive EAGLE-style heads, lifting batch-1 speedups on Qwen3 from about 2× (EAGLE-3 as re-measured) to about 5–6×, and a dense follow-up line added intra-block causality (Domino, DSpark, JetSpec, DFlash2), trees over diffusion marginals (DDTree, Bastion, DForkTree), capacity (DFlare) and looping (DLoop), with authors' records of 9.64× (JetSpec) and, with system co-design, 10.87× (SpecPilot). The second 2026 theme is training drafters for acceptance length rather than KL (LK losses, VSD, PARD-2, VAT, TVP, REAL). Third: parallelising speculation with verification (SSD/Saguaro, MineDraft). Fourth: production-scale evidence (DSpark in DeepSeek-V4; SPEED-Bench; the vLLM study) and the first careful audits of what "lossless" means under finite precision. All records are on math/code-heavy suites at batch size 1.

### Cited Findings

**DFlash** — VERIFIED: "DFlash: Block Diffusion for Flash Speculative Decoding", Jian Chen (UC San Diego), ICML 2026 (arXiv comment: "Accepted at ICML 2026. Camera-ready version"), arXiv 2602.06036 (v1 2026-02-05). Code: https://github.com/z-lab/dflash.
- (b) VERIFIED: "existing methods still rely on autoregressive drafting, which remains sequential and limits practical speedups"; small AR drafters hit "a speedup ceiling of approximately 3×", large ones "a modest 3−4×".
- (c) VERIFIED: "employs a lightweight block diffusion model for parallel drafting. By generating draft tokens in a single forward pass and conditioning the draft model on context features extracted from the target model"; "Hidden context features extracted from the target model are fused and injected into each draft layer's Key-Value cache"; features come "from a fixed set of layers uniformly sampled from shallow to deep". llama.cpp's description: "Unlike EAGLE-3 (a single-layer autoregressive draft), the DFlash draft uses several transformer layers but emits a whole block per draft step."
- (d) Lossless as claimed ("over 6× lossless acceleration"); results are given for T=0 and T=1. In vLLM the default is greedy drafting with target-only acceptance, with a probabilistic mode for ratio-based acceptance ("`greedy` uses target-only acceptance for random requests; `probabilistic` retains the full draft distribution for probability-ratio acceptance").
- (e) Qwen3-4B/8B "with thinking mode disabled and a maximum of 2048 generated tokens"; GSM8K, MATH-500, AIME25, HumanEval, MBPP, LiveCodeBench, MT-Bench; "all experiments on NVIDIA H200 GPUs unless otherwise specified"; training data "around 800K samples from NVIDIA Nemotron Post-Training Dataset V2... and CodeAlpaca", "trained on target-model outputs".

**DSpark (DeepSeek)** — VERIFIED: "DSpark: Confidence-Scheduled Speculative Decoding with Semi-Autoregressive Generation", Xin Cheng (Peking University / DeepSeek-AI), arXiv 2607.05147 (2026-07-06; technical report, no venue). Code/checkpoints: DeepSpec repo and `deepseek-ai/dspark_qwen3_4b_block7` (VERIFIED via the DeepSpec README and llama.cpp docs).
- (b) VERIFIED: parallel drafters "suffer from rapid acceptance decay due to a lack of inter-token dependencies. - Batch-SD correctness study with code at github.com/eBay/spec_dec ("Correctness Forensics for Batch Speculative Decoding: Diagnosing the Ragged Tensor Problem", arXiv 2510.22876; a Findings paper — the venue string in the arXiv comment was truncated in my listing): "EXSPEC reaches 3× throughput at batch size 8 with 95% exact match to standard decoding; residual divergence traces to floating-point non-determinism, not synchronization error."
- llama.cpp documentation makes the same operational point: "stochastic CPU and backend sampling can still select different tokens because floating-point operations can differ between implementations and devices. Use greedy sampling when exact output matching is required." — [llama.cpp docs](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/speculative.md)

### Inferences
- The design-space change is "one drafter forward pass per round, conditioned on target hidden states": this removes the drafter-latency term that capped EAGLE-style heads, so acceptance length (τ ≈ 6–11) translates almost directly into speedup at batch 1.
- Pure block-parallel drafting loses left-to-right dependency inside the block; the entire second half of 2026 (Domino, DSpark, JetSpec, DFlash2, TreeFlash, LiLiCorr) is about restoring it cheaply. This is the same sequential-dependence lesson as Medusa → Hydra → EAGLE, replayed one level up.
- Records (9.6×, 10.9×) come from math/code suites, batch 1, Qwen3-family targets with thinking disabled, and authors' own measurements; under concurrency the same systems report 2–3× (DFlash at c=32: 2.9×; AngelSpec at c=4–64: 1.98–2.40×).
- "Lossless" is increasingly qualified: exact in exact arithmetic, but bf16/FP8 engines, CUDA-graph sampling variants and batching make token-identical trajectories a property that must be tested, not assumed.

### Gaps
- No independent replication of DFlash/JetSpec/PARD-2/SpecPilot numbers on a common third-party benchmark was found (the DCore study covers six drafter recipes on Qwen3-4B/8B under one SGLang protocol and reports 2.7–3.3× at concurrency 1 for its best configuration, but its full per-method table was not extracted).
- Whether DFlash-style drafters keep exact-distribution guarantees under sampling in every engine (greedy-draft + target-only acceptance vs probability-ratio acceptance) was verified only from vLLM's option descriptions, not from proofs in the papers.
- Venues for JetSpec, DSpark, PARD-2, Domino, DDTree, DFlare: none stated (arXiv only as of 2026-11-22).

## 9. The ceiling: what limits lossless methods

### Takeaway
Lossless speedup is bounded three ways, each stated in primary sources. Statistically, a single-draft token is accepted with probability at most 1 − TV(p, q) and no unbiased rejection rule does better, so accepted tokens per target call follow a capped geometric law; with trees, the expected accepted length grows only logarithmically in the verification budget and inversely with the target's entropy. Economically, the gain is divided by (γc + 1): a slow or deep drafter erases it, and speculative decoding can be slower than plain decoding. Systemically, the "free" parallel capacity that verification consumes disappears as batch size or load grows, so batch-1 speedups of 4–6× become 1.1–2× in loaded production engines. Going beyond these limits requires either changing the output distribution (lossy methods) or lowering TV and drafter cost, which is what the 2025–2026 target-conditioned one-pass drafters did.

### Cited Findings

**Acceptance is bounded by distribution overlap:**
- Leviathan et al.: "β = 1 − D_LK(p,q)" with "D_LK(p,q) = 1 − Σ_x min(p(x), q(x))"; "E(# generated tokens) = (1 − α^(γ+1)) / (1 − α)"; "D_LK(p,q) = 1 ⇔ p and q have disjoint support". — [arXiv 2211.17192](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.17192)
- Yin et al. (NeurIPS 2024): "the accelerate rate for SD is T / Σ_{n=1}^{T} E[TV(p_n, q_n)(·|x_{1:n−1})]"; "We prove in Theorem 2 that any unbiased algorithm cannot have fewer rejections than Speculative Decoding. This indicates its optimality among the class, and having fewer rejections needs to suffer quality loss or requires extra information." — [arXiv 2411.00841](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00841)
- Block Verification shows token-level verification is slightly sub-optimal at block level, but the recoverable amount is small: "modest but consistent wall-clock speedups... of 5%-8%"; "optimal in the expected number of tokens produced each iteration". — [arXiv 2403.10444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10444)
- Multi-draft: "existing verification algorithms do not reach the theoretical upper bound for both without replacement and with replacement sampling" (Hu et al., arXiv 2502.18779); SpecTr's efficient selection is "(1−1/e)-optimal multiplicatively" (arXiv 2310.15141).
- DySpec abstract: "the speedup and scalability are strongly bounded by the token acceptance rate." — [arXiv 2410.11744](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11744)
- Temperature lowers overlap in practice: T5-small → T5-XXL α 0.75 (T=0) vs 0.62 (T=1) on EnDe (Leviathan Table 2); EAGLE on LLaMA2-Chat 13B "at temperature=0, the speedup ratios range from 3.01x to 3.76x, while at temperature=1, they range from 2.66x to 2.89x"; DFlash 4.9× (T=0) vs 4.1× (T=1) average on Qwen3. Medusa's authors on rejection sampling: "this sampling strategy results in diminished efficiency as the sampling temperature increases... Even if their distributions align perfectly, the output of the draft model may still be rejected."

**Tree budget buys only logarithmic gains:**
- "Speculative Decoding Speed-of-Light: Optimal Lower Bounds via Branching Random Walks", Sergey Pankratov and Dan Alistarh (ISTA; Red Hat AI), arXiv 2512.11718 (EACL 2026 per DBLP key, secondary): "we establish the first "tight" lower bounds on the runtime of any deterministic speculative generation algorithm... the expected number of tokens successfully predicted per speculative iteration is bounded as E[X] ≤ (μ + μ_(2)) log(P)/μ² + O(1), where P is the verifier's capacity, μ is the expected entropy of the verifier's output distribution, and μ_(2) is the expected second log-moment"; "the achievable speedup under this approach scales logarithmically with the parallel capacity P, and roughly inversely with the expected entropy μ, highlighting the diminishing returns of increased parallelism and the inherent difficulty of speculating when the model's output is highly variable." — [arXiv 2512.11718](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11718)
- Sequoia: "the number of tokens generated by verifying Sequoia trees scales nearly logarithmically in the size of the tree, while existing tree structures asymptote in the number of tokens they can generate." — [arXiv 2402.12374](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12374)
- SpecExec: "the number of tokens accepted by the target model is empirically upper-bounded (approximately by 10 for this model and dataset combination) regardless of the number of speculated tokens." — [arXiv 2406.02532](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02532)
- JetSpec: SD "faces a scaling limitation: increasing the draft budget improves speed only when acceptance remains high and drafting overhead stays low." — [arXiv 2606.18394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18394)
- vLLM study: larger trees raise accepted length (2.25 → 2.92) while the per-token acceptance rate falls (0.415 → 0.095), and "By batch size 64, the k=21 tree falls below 1× speedup on all workloads". — [arXiv 2601.11580](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11580)

**Drafter cost:**
- Leviathan Theorem 3.8: improvement factor "(1 − α^(γ+1)) / ((1 − α)(γc + 1))"; "If α > c, there exists γ for which we'll get an improvement, and the improvement factor will be at least (1+α)/(1+c)"; it also "assume[s] that we have enough compute resources to support the increased concurrency".
- DSI: "We identify a gap where SI can be slower than non-SI if drafters are too slow or inaccurate." — [arXiv 2405.14105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14105)
- "Decoding Speculative Decoding": "the performance of speculative decoding depends heavily on the latency of the draft model, and the draft model's capability in language modeling does not correlate strongly with its performance in speculative decoding"; "As model size increases, throughput decreases due to higher inference latency despite consistent increases in TAR." — [arXiv 2402.01528](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.01528)
- EAGLE: "the bottleneck for speculative sampling performance lies in the high overhead of the draft model." Kangaroo: "the inference latency of the self-draft model may no longer be negligible compared to the large model". DFlash: small autoregressive drafters have "a speedup ceiling of approximately 3×"; large ones are limited "to a modest 3−4×". Mirror-SD: "gains are capped by the cost of autoregressive draft generation: increasing draft size elevates acceptance rates but introduces additional latency overhead".
- Consumer hardware: "three of five configurations decelerate, either because the draft fails to out-speed a small target or because the quantized Metal backend executes "parallel" verification serially" (arXiv 2607.17283). AWS/Trainium: a 0.6B drafter's "acceptance rate was roughly 60% lower, enough to cancel out the compute savings."

**Batch size, load and memory:**
- EAGLE §4.4: "As the batch size increases, the available computational capacity of the GPU decreases, leading to a reduction in the acceleration effect." EAGLE-3: "As batch sizes increase, this redundancy decreases, reducing the effectiveness of speculative sampling"; in SGLang on an H100, EAGLE drops below 1× from batch 24 (0.93×) while EAGLE-3 keeps 1.3–1.4× up to batch 64.
- vLLM study: "Increasing batch size improves absolute throughput but systematically reduces the relative speedup of speculative decoding, an effect that is amplified for larger models" (EAGLE on Llama3.1-8B/GSM8K: 1.73× → 1.21× from batch 1 to 128); "verification by the target model dominates the execution"; "when the acceptance rate is low, repeatedly running the large model on rejected tokens incurs substantial runtime cost"; an oracle that always picks the better of EAGLE and n-gram per position "exposes substantial additional headroom, achieving up to a 2.2× further speedup".
- TurboSpec: "speculative decoding may degrade LLM serving performance if added naively without tuning to the incoming requests and the speculation method"; "computation inefficiencies due to incorrect token speculations... become prohibitive if the acceptance rate drops or the request rate to the system increases." — [arXiv 2406.14066](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.14066)
- Nightjar: SD "improves throughput in low-load, memory-bound systems but degrades performance in high-load, compute-bound environments due to verification overhead... retaining the draft model reduces KV cache capacity, limiting batch size". — [arXiv 2512.22420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22420)
- MagicDec (the nuance): "the conventional wisdom suggests that its efficacy is limited to small batch sizes... surprisingly SD can achieve speedup even for a high throughput inference regime for moderate to long sequences", because with long contexts KV loading keeps inference memory-bound ("the KV loading time increases much more... for larger batches"). — [arXiv 2408.11049](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11049)
- SPEED-Bench at batch 32 on production engines: mean speedups 1.06–1.90× for neural drafters and below 1× for n-gram (section 6). DFlash under SGLang: 4.8× at concurrency 1 → 2.9× at concurrency 32. PayPal: +49% at concurrency 1 → +22–29% at 8–32. Engine docs say the same: vLLM "medium-to-low QPS... memory-bound workloads"; TensorRT-LLM "at low batch sizes"; SambaNova "the overhead of running the draft model can outweigh any speedup".
- MoE targets add an input-dependent verification cost (expert loading): "The Limits of Speculation: Bounding Speculative Decoding in Mixture-of-Experts" (arXiv 2609.22156); "Why Speculative Decoding Works Better Than Predicted on Sparse MoE Models" (NeurIPS 2026, arXiv 2610.38981). Not read beyond abstracts.

**Exactness itself has an implementation limit:** bf16 inference gave exact trajectory match on only 45% of prompts in one audit, FP32 on all (arXiv 2609.15504); llama.cpp advises greedy sampling "when exact output matching is required" (section 7).

### Inferences
- With acceptance rate α and a free drafter, tokens per target call are at most 1/(1 − α): α = 0.8 gives 5, α = 0.9 gives 10. Reported τ of 6–11 for 2026 drafters therefore implies effective per-token agreement near 0.85–0.9 on math/code text, close to what the target's own entropy permits (the speed-of-light bound's 1/μ dependence); on high-entropy chat the same drafters sit at τ ≈ 4–6.
- Under exact verification, the remaining levers are all visible in 2026 work: reduce c to one cheap forward pass per round (parallel drafters), reduce TV by conditioning on target features and training for acceptance length, hide the remaining drafter time behind verification (SSD, MineDraft), and spend verification only where survival probability is high (DSpark/ForeSpark/adaptive verification). None of them moves the 1 − TV bound.
- Anything claiming acceptance above the overlap bound at a fixed drafter must relax the distribution (typical acceptance, FLy, ensemble verification, judge-style acceptance): that is the boundary between this note and the lossy guide.

### Gaps
- No source gives a model-independent numeric ceiling; the speed-of-light bound's constants (μ, μ_(2)) for specific models and its measured tightness figures were not extracted.
- The bound in arXiv 2512.11718 is for deterministic draft-tree algorithms; an equivalent tight bound for stochastic (sampling-based) verification was not found.
- Quantitative memory overhead of 2026 multi-layer drafters (DFlash/DSpark) versus EAGLE-3 ("below 10%" in the vLLM study) : not found.
