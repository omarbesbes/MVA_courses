# Lossy speculative decoding: beyond text, evaluation practice, deployment reality and risks (state of the field on 22–23 November 2026)

## 1. Beyond text: where is relaxed acceptance natural (images, vision-language, robot actions, speech), and what do LANTERN, SJD, Spec-VLA, VADUSA and their 2026 successors actually do?

### Takeaway
Whenever the output vocabulary is a learned codebook or a discretised continuous quantity (VQ image codes, speech codec tokens, 256-bin robot actions), exact-match speculative decoding gives about 1.0–1.3x or even a slowdown (EAGLE-2 on LlamaGen: 0.93x at temperature 1; standard SD on a speech LLM: 0.98x), and every reported gain above roughly 2x comes from a lossy acceptance rule validated only by task metrics (FID/CLIP, WER/MOS, success rate), not by distributional equality. Text-output vision-language models were handled losslessly through 2025 (ViSpec, SpecVLM, MASSV, MMSpec benchmark) and the first loosened rule for video LLMs (LVSpec) only appeared in 2026; by late 2026 the image line has moved from heuristic relaxations (LANTERN) to rules with explicit total-variation analysis (COOL-SD) and to non-exact spatial/parallel correction (Spatial SD, MuLo-SD, CSD).

### Cited Findings

#### 1.1 LANTERN (the reference lossy method for images)
- **[V] Identity.** "LANTERN: Accelerating Visual Autoregressive Models with Relaxed Speculative Decoding"; Doohyuk Jang, Sihwan Park, June Yong Yang, Yeonsung Jung, Jihun Yun, Souvik Kundu, Sung-Yub Kim, Eunho Yang; affiliations listed: KAIST, Intel Labs, AITRICS; arXiv 2410.03355 (v1 4 Oct 2024, v3 2 Mar 2025); arXiv comment: "30 pages, 13 figures, Accepted to ICLR 2025 (poster)"; code https://github.com/jadohu/LANTERN. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03355)
- **[V] Problem ("token selection ambiguity").** Naive SD loses "a $41\%$ to $58\%$ reduction in mean accepted length" on LlamaGen-3B versus Vicuna-7B; cause: "visual AR models present substantially lower average top-$1$ and top-$10$ probabilities in next token predictive distributions compared to language models". Consequence for the standard rule: "the EAGLE-2 drafter exhibits an average accept probability of 0.0402, meaning only 4% of drafts are accepted when applied to visual AR models." The authors' explanation is a hypothesis, stated as such: "We hypothesize that this issue arises from fundamental differences between image and language data." — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03355)
- **[V] Enabling observation (latent proximity).** "after each token is sampled, it is re-sampled uniformly from the 100 closest tokens in latent space. Figure 3 reveals that the images generated by this procedure closely resemble those produced using the original sampling method." — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03355)
- **[V] Mechanism and exact rule** ($p$ = drafter, $q$ = target, $\widetilde{x}\sim p(\cdot|s)$, $B_k(\widetilde{x})$ = the $k$ nearest codebook tokens to $\widetilde{x}$ in latent space including itself, $\ell_2$ distance). First relaxation: accept with probability $\min\left(1,\frac{\sum_{x\in B_{k}(\widetilde{x})}q(x|s)}{p(\widetilde{x}|s)}\right)$ (their Eq. 1). This corresponds to the distorted target $q_{k}(x|s,D=\widetilde{x})=\sum_{x\in B_{k}(\widetilde{x})}q(x|s)$ if $x=\widetilde{x}$; $0$ if $x\in B_{k}(\widetilde{x}),x\neq\widetilde{x}$; $q(x|s)$ otherwise. TV-bounded neighbourhood: "$A_{k,\delta}(\widetilde{x})$ is the largest subset of $B_{k}(\widetilde{x})$ such that ... $D_{TV}(q_{k,\delta}(x|s,D=\widetilde{x}),q(x|s))<\delta$", built "by incrementally adding tokens from $B_{k}(\widetilde{x})$ ... starting with the closest ones to $\widetilde{x}$, and stopping when adding another token would exceed the TVD threshold $\delta$". Final rule: accept $\widetilde{x}$ with probability $\min\left(1,\frac{\sum_{x\in A_{k,\delta}(\widetilde{x})}q(x|s)}{p(\widetilde{x}|s)}\right)$, else "re-sample $x\sim[q_{k,\delta}(x|s,D=\widetilde{x})-p(x|s)]_{+}$". Greedy case: accept $\widetilde{x}$ if $\widetilde{x}=\arg\max_{x}q_{k,\delta}(x|s,D=\widetilde{x})$. Hyper-parameters: $k$ (100, 300, 1000 tested) and $\delta$ (0.05–0.4). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03355)
- **[V] Lossy; what is guaranteed.** The paper says the relaxation "inevitably distorts the target distribution"; the only guarantee is the per-step, per-draft-token bound $D_{TV}<\delta$ between the distorted and original next-token distributions. No bound on the distribution of the whole image is stated in the paper (COOL-SD later criticises exactly this, see 1.4). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03355)
- **[V] Setup.** Target: text-conditioned LlamaGen-XL Stage I (main), Stage II and Anole (appendix E); base SD method: EAGLE-2 with a trained drafter; MS-COCO validation captions; speed on 1000 captions; quality (FID, CLIP score, Precision/Recall, HPS v2) "with 30K samples"; "The actual speed-up is measured on a single RTX 3090"; EAGLE-2 quality not measured: "we do not evaluate image quality on EAGLE-2 since it theoretically guarantees exact distribution matching with the target model." — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03355)
- **[V] Headline results (Table 2, LlamaGen-XL Stage I, RTX 3090).** Greedy ($\tau=0$): vanilla 1.00x, FID 28.63, CLIP 0.3169, precision 0.4232, recall 0.3517, HPSv2 23.18; EAGLE-2 1.29x (MAL 1.60); LANTERN ($\delta=0.05,k=1000$) 1.56x, MAL 2.02, FID 29.77, CLIP 0.3164, P 0.4484, R 0.3158, HPSv2 22.62; LANTERN ($\delta=0.2,k=1000$) 2.26x, MAL 2.89, FID 30.78, CLIP 0.3154, P 0.4771, R 0.2773, HPSv2 21.69. Sampling ($\tau=1$): vanilla FID 15.22, CLIP 0.3203, P 0.4781, R 0.5633, HPSv2 24.11; EAGLE-2 0.93x (MAL 1.20); LANTERN ($\delta=0.1$) 1.13x, MAL 1.75, FID 16.17, R 0.5172; LANTERN ($\delta=0.4$) 1.69x, MAL 2.40, FID 18.76, CLIP 0.3206, P 0.4909, R 0.4497, HPSv2 23.22. Table 1: mean accept probability of the first draft token rises from 0.0402 (EAGLE-2) to 0.0725–0.3657 depending on $(k,\delta)$. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03355)
- **[V] How the abstract numbers relate to the table.** The abstract's "increases speed-ups by $\mathbf{1.75}\times$ and $\mathbf{1.82}\times$" is relative to naive EAGLE-2 (2.26/1.29 and 1.69/0.93); the absolute speed-ups over autoregressive decoding are 2.26x and 1.69x: "improving from $1.29\times$ to $\mathbf{2.26}\times$ in greedy decoding and from $0.93\times$ to $\mathbf{1.69}\times$ in random sampling". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03355)
- **[V] Caveats stated or visible in the paper.** Recall (diversity) drops, which the authors acknowledge: "recall shows a modest decline, indicating a slight reduction in image diversity"; random neighbourhoods do not work (Table 3: random selection MAL 1.26 versus 2.09 for $\ell_2$ at $\delta=0.2$); hardware sensitivity: on Intel Gaudi 2, "EAGLE-2 achieves only 0.80$\times$ speed-up at $\tau=0$ and 0.55$\times$ at $\tau=1$, LANTERN improves these figures to 1.32$\times$ and 1.02$\times$", with the comment that SD is "particularly sensitive to low-level implementation details, which can vary across different accelerator architectures"; other models: LlamaGen-XL Stage II 1.64x (MAL 2.24) versus EAGLE-2 0.96x; Anole 1.17x (MAL 1.83) versus 0.73x. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03355)
- **[V] How later papers describe LANTERN.** GSD: LANTERN "increases only the numerator, it shares the same drawbacks as naive lossy methods—bias and the exploding problem—achieving only a 1.6× speed-up" and "requir[es] training". COOL-SD: LANTERN and LANTERN++ "rely on heuristic, empirically driven designs and lack any mathematical account of their divergence from the target distribution". PCG (speech): LANTERN "relaxes acceptance by aggregating probability over latent neighbors and bounding distortion via total variation distance". — [Source GSD](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07747); [Source COOL-SD](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09212); [Source PCG](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13732)

#### 1.2 LANTERN++
- **[V] Identity.** "LANTERN++: Enhancing Relaxed Speculative Decoding with Static Tree Drafting for Visual Auto-regressive Models"; Sihwan Park, Doohyuk Jang, Sungyub Kim, Souvik Kundu, Eunho Yang (KAIST, AITRICS, Intel); arXiv 2502.06352 (v1 10 Feb 2025, v2 27 Mar 2025); arXiv comment: "ICLR 2025 Workshop at SCOPE (Oral), 16 pages, 5 figures, short paper"; same code repository. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06352)
- **[V] Problem.** Dynamic (EAGLE-2) trees fail in images for two reasons: shallow trees (products of low confidences decay) and deterministic draft selection, "effectively setting $p(\widehat{x}|s)=1$. This reduces the acceptance probability to $q(\widehat{x}|s)$", while "even the most likely token often receiv[es] a probability below 0.2". Table 1: step compression EAGLE-1 (static) x2.49 versus EAGLE-2 (dynamic) x2.11 on LlamaGen-3B, the reverse of Vicuna-7B (x3.94 versus x4.98). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06352)
- **[V] Rule.** Static tree drafting plus a multiplicative bound replacing LANTERN's additive one: LANTERN: $\sum_{x\in A_{k,\delta}(\widehat{x})}q(x|s)<\delta\ \rightarrow$ LANTERN++: $\sum_{x\in A_{k,\lambda}(\widehat{x})}q(x|s)<\lambda\,q(\widehat{x}|s)$, with $\lambda>1$; acceptance probability $\min\left(1,\sum_{x\in A_{k,\lambda}(\widehat{x})}\frac{q(x|s)}{p(\widehat{x}|s)}\right)$. Guarantee as stated: the relaxed accept probability "does not exceed $\lambda$ times the original accept probability". Lossy. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06352)
- **[V] Setup and results (Table 2, MS-COCO 2017 val, "batch size $1$ on a single A100 PCIe 80GB").** Lumina-mGPT: target FID 28.93, CLIP 0.3333; EAGLE-1 steps x2.94, latency x2.10, FID 29.22; LANTERN++ ($k=10,\lambda=2$) x3.19 / x2.28, FID 30.11, CLIP 0.3296; ($k=10,\lambda=3$) x3.63 / x2.56, FID 33.91, CLIP 0.3267. Anole: EAGLE-1 x2.68 / x1.75 (FID 20.22 versus target 20.28); $\lambda=3$: x3.41 / x2.21, FID 25.48. LlamaGen-XL Stage I: EAGLE-1 x2.50 / x1.90; $\lambda=3$: x2.98 / x2.15, FID 23.89 versus 23.64. Stage II: EAGLE-1 x2.38 / x1.83; $\lambda=3$: x2.86 / x2.14, FID 39.80 versus 40.52. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06352)
- **[V] Caveat stated.** "tuning the multiplicative bound remains crucial for balancing acceleration and image quality." Note also that the headline "up to x2.56" coincides with the largest FID increase (28.93 to 33.91). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06352)

#### 1.3 Speculative Jacobi Decoding and successors: lossless or lossy?
- **[V] SJD identity.** "Accelerating Auto-regressive Text-to-Image Generation with Training-free Speculative Jacobi Decoding"; Yao Teng, Han Shi, Xian Liu, Xuefei Ning, Guohao Dai, Yu Wang, Zhenguo Li, Xihui Liu (The University of Hong Kong, Huawei Noah's Ark Lab, CUHK, Tsinghua, SJTU, Infinigence AI); arXiv 2410.01699; arXiv comment "ICLR 2025"; code https://github.com/tyshiwo1/Accelerating-T2I-AR-with-SJD/. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01699)
- **[V] SJD mechanism.** No draft model: the model's own predictions from the previous Jacobi iteration are the draft. Acceptance of token $x_i^{(j)}$: $x_{i}^{(j+1)}\leftarrow x_{i}^{(j)}$ if $r\sim\mathcal{U}[0,1],\ r<\min\left(1,\frac{p_{\theta}(x_{i}^{(j)}|\boldsymbol{x}_{1:i-1}^{(j)})}{p_{\theta}(x_{i}^{(j)}|\boldsymbol{x}_{1:i-1}^{(j-1)})}\right)$; on rejection resample from $\frac{\max(0,p_{\theta}(x|\boldsymbol{x}_{1:i-1}^{(j)})-p_{\theta}(x|\boldsymbol{x}_{1:i-1}^{(j-1)}))}{\sum_{x}\max(0,\cdot)}$; later positions are re-drawn from the current-iteration conditionals; fixed Jacobi window $W$; optional spatial-prior initialisation. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01699)
- **[V] SJD is claimed lossless in distribution.** "Theorem 1 (The correctness of speculative Jacobi decoding) The token sampled in each speculative Jacobi iteration satisfies $p_{\theta}(x|\boldsymbol{x}_{1:i-1}^{(j)})$". Later papers treat it as the lossless baseline: GSD lists "(B) Lossless methods, including Jacobi Decoding [18] and SJD [22]"; COOL-SD: "SJD serves as a lossless acceleration method"; PathRelax: "By construction, SJD preserves exact sampling from $p_{\theta}$". — [Source SJD](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01699); [Source GSD](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07747); [Source COOL-SD](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09212); [Source PathRelax](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10492)
- **[V] SJD results.** Table 1 (MS-COCO 2017 val, A100): Lumina-mGPT 87.23 s to 42.73 s (latency 2.04x, steps 2.22x), FID 30.76 to 30.85, CLIP-Score 31.29 to 31.35; with spatial-prior init 2.05x / 2.23x, FID 31.13; Anole 48.96 s to 26.18 s (1.87x / 1.97x), FID 28.87 to 29.14. Table 2 (Parti-prompt, RTX 4090): Lumina-mGPT 100.69 s to 47.52 s (2.12x / 2.26x), CLIP 32.13 to 32.13. The wording in the text is "nearly without sacrificing visual qualities", and a failure mode is shown: "In complex image scenarios, our method generates some continuous tokens that result in artifacts". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01699)
- **[V] GSD (lossy, on top of SJD).** "Grouped Speculative Decoding for Autoregressive Image Generation"; Junhyuk So, Juncheol Shin, Hyunho Kook, Eunhyeok Park (POSTECH); arXiv 2508.07747; "Accepted to the ICCV 2025". Rule ($p$ = expert, $q$ = draft): partition the vocabulary into clusters $C_i$, $p'(C_i)=\sum_{x\in C_i}p(x)$, $q'(C_i)=\sum_{x\in C_i}q(x)$, accept $x$ if $\min\Bigl(1,\frac{p'(C(x))}{q'(C(x))}\Bigr)\geq r,\ r\sim\mathcal{U}[0,1]$. Clusters are built dynamically per step from the expert's sorted probabilities ($G$ nearest tokens, thresholds $d$ on embedding distance and $\delta$ on probability difference; $d=0.5$, $\delta=0.15$, $L=16$). Theory: $\mathrm{TV}(p',q')\leq\mathrm{TV}(p,q)$, so the acceptance rate cannot decrease. Finding against static clustering: "clustering solely based on static embedding distance fails to achieve speedup". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07747)
- **[V] GSD results (Table 1, Lumina-mGPT 7B at 768x768, top-$K$=2000, $\tau=1$, 8x RTX 3090 server).** Parti-prompt: SJD 2.15x latency / 2.31x NFE, CLIP 32.090 (target 32.091); GSD $G=3$ 2.38x / 2.66x, CLIP 32.125; $G=50$ 4.65x / 3.76x, CLIP 32.075; $G=100$ 4.88x / 3.91x, CLIP 31.975. MS-COCO 2017: target FID 30.79; SJD 2.22x / 2.25x, FID 30.78; GSD $G=3$ 2.54x / 2.57x, FID 31.50; $G=10$ 3.62x / 3.39x, FID 33.12; $G=25$ 3.77x / 3.53x, FID 33.55. Naive lossy baselines: "Amplify $\left(\frac{k\cdot p}{q}\right)$" with $k=4$ gives FID 40.05; "Addition $\left(\frac{p+\epsilon}{q}\right)$" with $\epsilon=0.3$ gives FID 40.20. No greedy results: "We did not conduct experiments with the Greedy setting ($\tau=0$), as it significantly degrades the quality of generated images". Headline numbers differ within the paper: abstract "an average of 3.7x", introduction "an average 3.8× speedup", Figure 1 caption "up to 4.3x". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07747); [Source abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07747)
- **[V] SJD2 (needs fine-tuning; not presented as lossless).** "Speculative Jacobi-Denoising Decoding for Accelerating Autoregressive Text-to-image Generation"; Yao Teng et al.; arXiv 2510.08994; NeurIPS 2025 poster. "we ... fine-tune the autoregressive model to adapt to the noise perturbation"; result: "reduces the number of forward passes by about $4\times$ on Lumina-mGPT and more than $5\times$ on Emu3 and thus achieves latency speedup by more than $2\times$". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08994); [Source venue](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/115190)
- **[V, abstract only] SJD++.** arXiv 2512.07503 (Teng et al., 8 Dec 2025): "SJD++ reuses high-confidence draft tokens after each verification phase instead of resampling them all"; "$2\times$ to $3\times$ inference latency reduction and $2\times$ to $7\times$ step compression, while preserving visual quality with no observable degradation". Whether it is lossless: not found (abstract makes no exactness claim). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07503)
- **[V] PathRelax (2026).** arXiv 2606.10492 (Lei, Wang, Dai, Zhou; Southeast University; 9 Jun 2026). Tree of parallel Jacobi paths: "PathExplore is a lossless method" (appendix "Proof of the Lossless Guarantees of PathExplore"); "PathRelax" adds "cross-path relaxed verification" (lossy). Abstract: speed-ups "4.14$\times$, 3.95$\times$, and 4.18$\times$" on Parti-Prompts, MSCOCO2017, T2ICompBench; "PathExplore, without any relaxed sampling, outperforms relaxed sampling methods in the speedup ratio, such as GSD and LANTERN". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10492)

#### 1.4 Other image and video generation work, 2025–2026
- **[V] COOL-SD (theory for relaxed SD in images).** "Annealed Relaxation of Speculative Decoding for Faster Autoregressive Image Generation"; Xingyao Li, Fengzhuo Zhang, Cunxiao Du, Hui Ji (acknowledges Sea AI Lab and the Singapore Ministry of Education; affiliations not shown in the HTML header: not found); arXiv 2601.09212 (14 Jan 2026); "Accepted to AAAI 2026". Rule ($P$ = target, $Q$ = draft): $f_{i}=\min\left\{1,\frac{\omega_{i}\cdot P(\tilde{x}_{i}|\texttt{pt},\tilde{x}_{1:i-1})}{Q(\tilde{x}_{i}|\texttt{pt},\tilde{x}_{1:i-1})}\right\}$ with annealed $\omega_{i}=\delta\cdot\exp(-\nu\cdot i-\mu)$ ($\nu>0$, $\mu$ chosen so that $\sum_{i=1}^{L}\exp(-\nu i-\mu)=L$; uniform variant $\omega_i=\delta$ called UniformRSD). Theorem 2 gives the expected accepted length $\mathbb{E}[\tau+1]=1+\sum_{i=1}^{L}\sum_{\tilde{x}_{1:i}}Q(\tilde{x}_{1:i})\prod_{j=1}^{i}f_{j}$, an upper bound on $\mathrm{TV}(\hat{P}_{X_{1:L+1}},P_{X_{1:L+1}})$, and the bound-minimising resampling distribution $G_{i+1}^{*}=\mathrm{Norm}\bigl([P(\cdot|\tilde{x}_{1:i})-Q(\cdot|\tilde{x}_{1:i})f_{i+1}((\tilde{x}_{1:i},\cdot),P,Q)]_{+}\bigr)$. Key statement: "any relaxation of the acceptance criterion inevitably introduces bias that cannot be perfectly corrected by adjusting the resampling distributions." Annealing result: "increasing relaxation at an earlier position while reducing it at a later one ... leads to lower distributional bias than the reverse". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09212)
- **[V] COOL-SD results (Table 1, single A100-SXM4-40GB).** Lumina-mGPT: target CLIP 0.3330, FID 28.99, latency 170.14 s; Eagle-1 (lossless) accepted length 2.76, 2.37x, FID 29.05; LANTERN++ ($\lambda=2,k=10$) 2.99, 2.48x, FID 30.31; Cool-SD ($\delta=1.1$) 3.11, 2.69x, FID 30.30. LlamaGen-XL: Eagle-1 2.03x (FID 20.97 versus target 21.08); LANTERN++ 2.15x (21.17); Cool-SD ($\delta=1.1$) 2.27x (21.02); Cool-SD ($\delta=2$) 2.72x (21.20). Qualitative claim: "Cool-SD preserves image fidelity comparable to the lossless baseline up to a $\times 3.10$ speed-up, whereas LANTERN++ begins to show visible artifacts". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09212)
- **[V] VVS (CVPR 2026).** arXiv 2511.13587 (Dong et al., Tsinghua SIGS / CUHK): "VVS: Accelerating Speculative Decoding for Visual Autoregressive Generation via Partial Verification Skipping"; skips some verification passes: "reduces the number of target model forward passes by $2.8\times$ relative to vanilla AR decoding while maintaining competitive generation quality". It states plainly: "Employing the relaxed acceptance of SD for image AR generation introduces a lossy approximation." — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13587)
- **[V] Spatially Speculative Decoding (2026).** arXiv 2606.20543 (Xiang, Zhang, Yu, Mao; Rutgers; v3 27 Sep 2026): up to "11.03x in wall-clock time while maintaining generation quality on DPG-Bench and GenEval"; limitation section: "its relaxed parallel correction is not exactness-preserving ... SSD does not sample from the exact autoregressive distribution"; it also reports that in discrete token space "exact-match acceptance rates fall below $5\%$ (So et al., 2025)". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20543)
- **[V, abstract only] CSD (ICML 2026), CASCADE, MuLo-SD (CVPR 2026), SDVG.** CSD, arXiv 2606.27829 (East China Normal University / Huawei): "directly relaxing its criteria leads to degradation in image quality"; "entropy-based probability relaxation mechanism with an optimal resampling strategy" plus "a distribution alignment filter". CASCADE, arXiv 2605.07230: training-free "acceptance relaxation" from "semantic interchangeability and convergence" in tree SD, "up to 3.6x acceleration". MuLo-SD, arXiv 2601.05149 (Qualcomm AI Research; "Accepted at CVPR 2026"): low-resolution drafter, "local rejection and resampling", "up to $\mathbf{5\times}$". SDVG, arXiv 2604.17397 (video): no token distribution exists, so verification is "an image-quality router" (ImageReward, worst-frame aggregation, threshold $\tau$); "retains 98.1% of target-only VisionReward quality (0.0773 vs. 0.0788) at a 1.59x speedup with tau=-0.7, and reaches 2.09x at 95.7% quality retention". CSD's reported "4.33×" on Janus-Pro is from a search snippet only [U]. — [Source CSD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27829); [Source CSD venue](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/61522); [Source CASCADE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07230); [Source MuLo-SD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05149); [Source SDVG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17397)
- **[V, abstract only] Continuous tokens.** "Continuous Speculative Decoding for Autoregressive Image Generation" (Wang et al., arXiv 2411.11925; arXiv comment "ECCV 2026"): for continuous AR models it uses "an approximated criterion", "denoising trajectory alignment" and "acceptance-rejection sampling ... with an appropriate upper bound"; "over 2x wall-time speedup while preserving the image generation quality". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11925)

#### 1.5 Vision-language and video-language models (text output): lossless until 2026
- **[V] Gagrani et al. 2024.** "On Speculative Decoding for Multimodal Large Language Models"; Mukul Gagrani et al. (Qualcomm AI Research); arXiv 2404.08856; "Accepted as a spotlight paper to ELVM workshop at CVPR 2024". Lossless (greedy only): "We use greedy decoding for all experiments so that the SPD generation is identical to the target model's autoregressive generation." Result: "memory-bound speedup of up to 2.37$\times$ using a 115M parameter language model" for LLaVA 7B; a text-only drafter suffices. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08856)
- **[V] ViSpec (NeurIPS 2025).** arXiv 2509.15235 (Kang, Shu, Li, Zhai, Chen; Peking University and Huawei Noah's Ark Lab). Treats SD as "a lossless acceleration technique"; results: "at temperature 0 with LLaVA-v1.6-Vicuna-7B, ViSpec achieves a speedup of $2.90\times$ on TextVQA, surpassing EAGLE-2 ($1.25\times$) and Medusa ($1.46\times$)", "speedups of up to 3.22$\times$". It deliberately removes Medusa's relaxed rule: "For a fair comparison, we do not relax the draft token acceptance condition of Medusa under non-greedy settings as proposed in the original paper; instead, we adopt the same acceptance condition as EAGLE-2." — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15235)
- **[V] SpecVLM (AMD, images).** arXiv 2509.11815 (Huang et al.; Xi'an Jiaotong University and Advanced Micro Devices; no venue in arXiv comments): "2.5--2.9x end-to-end speedups within 5 epochs across LLaVA and MMMU ... while preserving the target model's output distribution (lossless decoding)"; "As our framework preserves the target distribution (lossless decoding), we do not perform additional output-fidelity evaluation." — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.11815)

#### 1.6 Robot actions (vision-language-action models)
- **[V] Spec-VLA identity (ID and venue confirmed).** "Spec-VLA: Speculative Decoding for Vision-Language-Action Models with Relaxed Acceptance"; Songsheng Wang, Rucheng Yu, Zhihang Yuan, Chao Yu, Feng Gao, Yu Wang, Derek F. - **[V] Problem.** OpenVLA decodes greedily, so exact match is required; "the draft generator models fail to predict the initial draft tokens in about half of the samples"; direct EAGLE-style SD "yields only minor speed improvements, from $1.08\times$ to $1.15\times$". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22424)
- **[V] Rule.** Strict: accept iff $a_i == \hat{a}_i$. Relaxed (their Eq. 5): accept if $D(a_{i},\hat{a}_{i})\leq r$, otherwise "Resample $\hat{a}_{i}=a_{i}$", where $a_i$ is the verifier's greedy token, $\hat a_i$ the draft token and $r$ the "Relaxation Threshold". Distance is free to compute because "VLA models, notably OpenVLA and RT-2, discretize continuous dimensions into 256 bins ... the distance between tokens can be directly inferred from the absolute difference between bin IDs". Settings: $r=9$ for LIBERO-Goal/Object/Spatial, $r=5$ for LIBERO-Long; EAGLE-2 tree with "maximum nodes to 50, tree depth to 4, and ... the top 8 tokens". Lossy; no formal guarantee, quality is the task success rate. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22424)
- **[V] Setup.** Verifier: fine-tuned OpenVLA; drafter: one Llama decoder layer (EAGLE style), trained "in 6 hours using $4\times$ Tesla A100 (80G) GPU"; LIBERO simulation, four suites with 10 tasks each, "50 trials on each task". Inference hardware for the speed-up: not found in the passages read. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22424)
- **[V] Results (Table 1; SR = success rate; Length = tokens per verifier forward pass).** LIBERO-Goal: AR SR 78.0%; strict Spec-VLA length 2.04, 1.09x, SR 74.2%; relaxed 2.94, 1.42x, SR 74.4%. #### 1.7 Speech
- **[V] VADUSA (tolerance by repeated sampling).** "Fast and High-Quality Auto-Regressive Speech Synthesis via Speculative Decoding"; Bohan Li, Hankun Wang, Situo Zhang, Yiwei Guo, Kai Yu (X-LANCE Lab, Shanghai Jiao Tong University); arXiv 2410.21951; "Accepted by ICASSP 2025". - **[V] PCG (group-exact acceptance, Apple).** "Principled Coarse-Grained Acceptance for Speculative Decoding in Speech"; Moran Yanuka, Paul Dixon, Eyal Finkelshtein, Daniel Rotman, Raja Giryes (Apple, Tel-Aviv University); arXiv 2511.13732 (v4 22 Jan 2026); "Accepted to ICASSP 2026". Acoustic Similarity Groups: $\mathcal{G}(t):=\{t'\in\mathcal{V}:\cos(\text{Emb}_{q}(t),\text{Emb}_{q}(t'))>\theta\}$ (overlapping; "$\theta\in[0.38,0.45]$ optimal"). ### Inferences
- The three families of relaxed rules outside text share one idea, replacing token identity by membership of an equivalence class, but differ in where the class comes from: a fixed metric on the codebook (LANTERN's $k$-NN in latent space, Spec-VLA's bin distance, PCG's cosine groups), the target's own next-token distribution at that step (GSD's dynamic clusters), or nothing at all (additive/multiplicative boosts: Speech-SD's $+\beta$, COOL-SD's $\omega_i$, VADUSA's repeated sampling). GSD's and PCG's own ablations suggest that fixed-metric classes work for speech and actions but are weak for image codebooks (GSD: static embedding clustering "fails to achieve speedup"), which is a writer-relevant nuance rather than a settled fact.
- The only rules with a formal statement about the output distribution are LANTERN (per-step TV $<\delta$ conditional on the draft token), COOL-SD (sequence-level TV upper bound and optimal resampling), PCG (exact at group level) and SJD/PathExplore (exact). All others rest on task metrics.
- Claims that quality is preserved often hide a measurable shift on the diversity/intelligibility axis: LANTERN recall 0.5633 to 0.4497 at 1.69x; LANTERN++ FID 28.93 to 33.91 at 2.56x; GSD FID 30.79 to 33.55 at 3.77x; Speech-SD WER 3.67% to 5.70%; PCG WER 11.1 to 13.8. A fair summary: the shift is small on prompt-alignment metrics (CLIP score) and visible on FID, recall and WER.
- In these modalities the lossless baseline is frequently not a speed-up at all (0.93x, 0.98x, LM-RTF 0.509 versus 0.504), so the usual text-world framing (lossy acceptance buys some extra speed on top of an already useful lossless speed-up) does not transfer: here relaxation is what makes SD usable.

### Gaps
- Inference hardware used for Spec-VLA's and VADUSA's speed-up figures: not found in the text read.
- SJD++ and MC-SJD ("Speculative coupled decoding for training-free lossless acceleration of autoregressive visual generation", cited by PathRelax and the 2026 survey): exactness status not verified from primary text; arXiv ID of MC-SJD not confirmed.
- CSD's acceptance rule in LaTeX and its exact numbers were not extracted (abstract only); the "4.33×" figure is from a search snippet.
- No paper was found that applies a human preference study at scale to relaxed image SD; evidence is automatic metrics plus small MOS panels in speech (PCG: "Four participants each rate 85 randomly selected samples").
- KERV, HeiSD, MuLo-SD, CASCADE, SDVG and Uni-LaSpec were read at abstract level only.

## 2. Evaluation: which metrics are used, how Spec-Bench is built, what the documented pitfalls are, and how lossy methods are (and should be) evaluated

### Takeaway
Speed-up numbers are only comparable when hardware, numeric precision, batch size, temperature, draft length, output length and the serving stack are all held fixed; primary sources show each of these moving the result by tens of percent or flipping method rankings, and the standard benchmarks (Spec-Bench, SPEED-Bench, MMSpec) report efficiency only, with no quality column. For lossy methods three 2026 studies converge on the same criticism: accepted tokens per round is not speed (completions get longer), comparisons are often made against the wrong baseline or on easy benchmarks, and the relaxation knob does not transfer across tasks; no benchmark or leaderboard dedicated to lossy speculative decoding was found as of November 2026.

### Cited Findings

#### 2.1 Metric definitions (with the source that defines them)
- **[V] Acceptance rate $\beta$, $\alpha$ (Leviathan et al., arXiv 2211.17192; $p$ = target, $q$ = draft).** "The acceptance rate $\beta_{x_{<t}}$, given a prefix $x_{<t}$, is the probability of accepting $x_{t}\sim q(x_{t}|x_{<t})$ by speculative sampling"; $\alpha=E(\beta)$; "$\beta=1-D_{LK}(p,q)$" with $D_{LK}(p,q)=1-\sum_{x}\min(p(x),q(x))$, hence "$\alpha=1-E(D_{LK}(p,q))=E(\min(p,q))$". Under an i.i.d. assumption, "$E(\#\ generated\ tokens)=\frac{1-\alpha^{\gamma+1}}{1-\alpha}$" per target call for draft length $\gamma$. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.17192)
- **[V] Cost coefficient and walltime improvement factor.** "Let $c$, the cost coefficient, be the ratio between the time for a single run of $M_{q}$ and the time for a single run of $M_{p}$"; "unlike $\alpha$ which is an intrinsic property of the models and the task, the value of $c$ depends on the hardware configuration and software implementation details"; "The expected improvement factor in total walltime by Algorithm 1 is $\frac{1-\alpha^{\gamma+1}}{(1-\alpha)({\gamma}c+1)}$", under the assumption "that we can run $\gamma+1$ concurrent evaluations of $M_{p}$ in parallel without increasing the walltime." — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.17192)
- **[V] Block efficiency (SpecTr, arXiv 2310.15141).** Block efficiency $:=\frac{\text{Total number of decoded tokens}}{\text{Number of serial calls to }\mathcal{M}_{b}}$, presented as "the theoretical speedup" under two assumptions (negligible small-model time; free parallelism); "the actual wall clock speedup is smaller than the theoretical speedup of block efficiency". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.15141)
- **[V] $\tau$, MBSU, token rate (Gagrani et al., arXiv 2404.08856).** "1) block efficiency ($\tau$), the average number of tokens generated per block (or target model run), for a block of size $\gamma$ and input $x$, the maximum value of $\tau(x)$ can be $\gamma+1$ ...; 2) memory-bound speedup (MBSU), the hypothetical speedup achieved by SPD for a given block efficiency $\tau(x)$ and a relative latency $c$ defined as ratio between number of parameters of draft to target model, i.e., $\mathrm{MBSU}(x)=\frac{c\tau(x)}{c\gamma+1}$; 3) token rate, the total number of tokens generated divided by the total time of generation". Caution: as printed, with $c$ = draft/target size ratio, this formula does not reduce to Leviathan's $\tau/(\gamma c+1)$; treat the printed formula as possibly a typo and MBSU as a speed-up predicted from $\tau$ and a parameter-count cost ratio, not a measured one. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08856)
- **[V] Mean accepted tokens (#MAT, Spec-Bench).** The leaderboard reports "the mean speedup over 3 different runs and #mean accepted tokens per decoding step (which is `1.00` for vanilla autoregressive decoding)". Synonyms seen in primary sources: "mean accepted length ... the average number of tokens accepted in each forward step of the target model" (LANTERN, MAL); "Average Accepted Tokens Per Step (AATPS)" (Hu and Huang); "average acceptance lengths $\tau$" (ViSpec); "average accepted length $\tau$, i.e., how many draft tokens survive each verification step" (Mistletoe abstract). Whether the count includes the "bonus"/correction token differs by paper (Spec-VLA analyses lengths "produced by the draft model" only, so "the minimum accept length becomes 0"). — [Source Spec-Bench](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hemingkx/Spec-Bench/main/Leaderboard.md); [Source LANTERN](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03355); [Source Hu-Huang](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.20418); [Source ViSpec](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15235); [Source Mistletoe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14005); [Source Spec-VLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22424)
- **[V] Step compression ratio and NFE (images).** "$\mathcal{S}=\frac{\text{\# generated tokens}}{\text{\# decoding steps}}$" (SJD, LANTERN++); GSD reports "the number of function evaluations (NFE), which indicates the number of model forward passes performed" plus latency. — [Source SJD](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01699); [Source LANTERN++](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06352); [Source GSD](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07747)
- **[V] Conditional acceptance rate, acceptance length, Output TPS and User TPS (SPEED-Bench, ICML 2026).** "conditional Acceptance Rate (AR) is defined as the probability of accepting draft token $x_{i}$ given the draft prefix $x_{<i}$ was accepted"; "$\text{AL}=\mathbb{E}[L_{t}]=1+\sum_{i=1}^{\gamma}\prod_{j=1}^{i}\text{AR}_{j}$" (the count includes the verification token); "Throughput (Output TPS), defined as the total tokens generated across all concurrent requests per second, and User TPS, defined as tokens generated per second for a single request." — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09557)
- **[V] Goodput (SmartSpec/TurboSpec, arXiv 2406.14066).** "The goodput represents the number of correct tokens the system generates per unit time, i.e., it does not include the incorrect tokens generated by speculative decoding. Note that in the absence of speculative decoding, the goodput and throughput are the same." — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.14066)
- **[V] "TAR" is used with a different meaning by Yan et al.** "tokens accepted per iteration (also known as TAR) have a linear effect on speculative decoding throughput"; metrics: "throughput (tokens generated per second) and TAR". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.01528)
- **[V] Speed-up as throughput ratio (and why).** Liu et al. 2026: "To account for fluctuations in output length, we use token throughput, i.e., the number of generated tokens per second, as the performance metric ... Speedup is then defined as the ratio between the throughput with speculative decoding (SD) and the throughput without it." Xia, Ribar and Balanca 2026 model it as $S\approx\frac{(\bar{l}_{\text{accept}}+1)C_{\text{AR}}}{N_{\text{draft}}C_{\text{drafter}}+C_{\text{verify}}}\approx\frac{\bar{l}_{\text{accept}}+1}{1+N_{\text{draft}}c_{\text{rel}}}$ with $\Pr(\text{accept draft token})=1-\text{TV}(q,p)$. — [Source Liu](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11580); [Source Xia](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08690)
- **[V] Metrics exposed by production software.** Fireworks: "`speculation-acceptance`: acceptance rate by proposed-token position", with the warning "A high acceptance rate alone does not guarantee a speedup because the drafter also consumes compute." llama.cpp: Prometheus counters `llamacpp:spec_decode_num_draft_tokens_total`, `..._num_accepted_tokens_total`, `..._num_drafts_total`, `..._num_accepted_tokens_per_pos_total`, and a log line "draft acceptance rate = 0.57576 ( 171 accepted / 297 generated)" (here "acceptance rate" = accepted/drafted tokens). vLLM documents "Per-Request Acceptance Metrics". — [Source Fireworks](https://docs.fireworks.ai/deployments/speculative-decoding.md); [Source llama.cpp README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/server/README.md); [Source llama.cpp doc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/docs/speculative.md); [Source vLLM](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/speculative_decoding/)
- **[V] Metrics specific to lossy methods.** Performance Retention Rate: "$\mathrm{PRR}=\frac{x-x_{\text{draft}}}{x_{\text{target}}-x_{\text{draft}}}$ ... $\mathrm{PRR}=0$ matches the draft model, and $\mathrm{PRR}=1$ matches the target model" (Zhang et al., IEEE S&P 2027). Length-corrected speed-up: $S_{\text{relax}}\approx\frac{\bar{L}_{\text{AR}}}{\bar{L}_{\text{relax}}}\frac{\bar{l}_{\text{accept}}+1}{1+N_{\text{draft}}c_{\text{rel}}}$ (Xia et al. 2026, their Eq. 4, intermediate expression omitted). Length inflation "$\lambda=\bar{L}_{\mathrm{relax}}/\bar{L}_{\mathrm{strict}}$" (Wang et al. 2026). Iso-quality and iso-latency reporting: "Reduction in latency from different methods ($T=1,\gamma=5$) when matching the quality of the large model (cols 2–7), and the best quality metric when matching each method yields without exceeding the latency of the large model (cols 8–13)" (Narasimhan et al., ICLR 2025, Table 2). "accuracy retention" relative to target-only (vLLM FLy docs). — [Source Zhang](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08678); [Source Xia](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08690); [Source Wang](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.23818); [Source Narasimhan](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19261); [Source vLLM FLy](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/speculative_decoding/fly/)

#### 2.4 Documented pitfalls when reading reported speed-ups
- **[V] Hardware.** Xia et al.: "the acceleration effect of most Speculative Decoding methods is notably enhanced when employed on high-performance GPUs ... the speedup ratio for Medusa escalates from 1.48$\times$ to 2.42$\times$, and for Lookahead, it rises from 1.11$\times$ to 1.77$\times$." LANTERN: EAGLE-2 on LlamaGen is 1.29x on RTX 3090 but 0.80x on Intel Gaudi 2 at $\tau=0$. — [Source Xia](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.07851); [Source LANTERN](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03355)
- **[V] Numeric precision.** Xia et al.: "The experimental results reveal a noticeable reduction in speedup for all methods under FP32 precision. Specifically, PLD ... achieves merely 1.01 $\times$ speedup in FP32 precision, and the acceleration effect of EAGLE ... also diminishes, with its speedup falling from 2.39 $\times$ to 1.74 $\times$ ... we advocate for future studies to report speedup metrics across both precision settings." — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.07851)
- **[V] Temperature.** Leviathan et al.: "speedups of 2.6X (temp=1) and 3.4X (temp=0) on the translation task and ... 2.3X (temp=1) and 3.1X (temp=0) for the summarization task" (T5-XXL, "batch size of 1 on a single TPU-v4"). Xia et al.: "the acceleration effect of all methods decreases with an increase in sampling temperature." Ouyang et al. (UIUC and Microsoft; arXiv 2410.10141; Findings of EMNLP 2024): "we observe a consistent trend of diminishing speedup as the decoding temperature increases from $0$ to $1$ ... The effect was most pronounced when the KD temperature was set to $0$, leading to a relative speedup difference of $31\%$ and $29\%$ for offline distillation and online distillation, respectively"; on out-of-domain GSM8K "this value increases to $42\%$"; distilling the drafter at the decoding temperature helps ("configurations along the diagonals ... typically yield the most accelerated decoding speeds"). — [Source Leviathan](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.17192); [Source Xia](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.07851); [Source Ouyang](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10141); [Source Ouyang venue](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.767/)
- **[V] Batch size and prototypes versus serving engines.** Liu et al. 2026: "Prior studies only used prototype implementations rather than production-level systems ... these evaluations are often conducted with a batch size of one ... an unrealistic configuration"; "for the Llama3.1-8B model on the GSM8K dataset, the speedup of EAGLE over the non-SD baseline decreases from 1.73$\times$ to 1.21$\times$ as the batch size increases from 1 to 128"; trees: "By batch size 64, the $k{=}21$ tree falls below 1$\times$ speedup on all workloads for both models, whereas the chain remains above 1$\times$ throughout"; "the verification stage generally takes the largest execution time, ranging from 42% to 95%". SPEED-Bench: "as the batch size increases and the system approaches the compute-bound regime, the cost of verifying additional tokens may outweigh the gains, and $DL=1$ is preferred." TurboSpec: "speculative decoding may degrade LLM serving performance if added naively without tuning to the incoming requests and the speculation method." Xia et al. 2024 list batched inference as an open problem ("the inference latency of a batch depends on the slowest sample in the batch"). — [Source Liu](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11580); [Source SPEED-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09557); [Source TurboSpec](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.14066); [Source Xia](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.07851)
- **[V] Sequence length (the counter-case).** MagicDec (Sadhukhan et al., arXiv 2408.11049, ICLR 2025 poster): "SD Can Improve Throughput Only Beyond a Critical Sequence Length: While existing research ... suggests that SD is inefficient for large batches due to high verification costs, this limitation only applies to very short sequences ... once sequences exceed a certain critical length (which varies based on the model and hardware), the KV loading cost becomes the dominant factor, even for large batches"; headline "up to 2.51x speedup for Llama3.1-8B when serving batch sizes ranging from 32 to 256". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11049); [Source venue](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/30513)
- **[V] Drafter latency matters more than drafter quality.** Yan, Agarwal, Venkataraman (NAACL 2025; arXiv 2402.01528; "over 350 experiments with LLaMA-65B and OPT-66B", 4x A100 80GB): "the performance of speculative decoding depends heavily on the latency of the draft model, and the draft model's capability in language modeling does not correlate strongly with its performance in speculative decoding"; "As model size increases, throughput decreases due to higher inference latency despite consistent increases in TAR." — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.01528)
- **[V] Implementation overheads and engine choice.** SPEED-Bench: "TensorRT-LLM achieves higher peak throughput by leveraging a unified CUDA graph for the entire draft-verification loop. In contrast, vLLM's multi-engine design incurs slight host communication overheads". Xia et al. 2026: "we find that for separate draft models, vLLM has $c_{\text{rel}}$ much higher than what could be ideally achieved" (measured on "vLLM 0.20.1"). Old vLLM docs (v0.6.6): "speculative decoding in vLLM is not yet optimized and does not usually yield inter-token latency reductions for all prompt datasets or sampling parameters." — [Source SPEED-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09557); [Source Xia](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08690); [Source vLLM v0.6.6](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/v0.6.6/usage/spec_decode.html)
- **[V] Synthetic prompts inflate results.** SPEED-Bench: "With SD enabled, synthetic benchmarking overestimates throughput by an average of 23% compared to SPEED-Bench"; random tokens cause "Trivial Response" (inflated acceptance) or "Topic Latching". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09557)
- **[V] Task and data dependence.** Spec-Bench: "most methods achieve a suboptimal speedup on the translation subtask"; PLD 2.46x on summarisation versus 1.15x on translation (3090 board). Sandler, Üstün, Romanelli, Hooker, Fioretto (University of Virginia, Cohere, Hofstra; arXiv 2510.02128): "speed-up gained from speculative decoding is not uniformly distributed across tasks, consistently diminishing for under-fit, and often underrepresented tasks." — [Source Xia](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.07851); [Source leaderboard](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hemingkx/Spec-Bench/main/Leaderboard.md); [Source Sandler](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02128)
- **[V] Draft length and drafter cost interact with any claimed gain.** Xia et al. 2026: "an increase in $\bar{l}_{\text{accept}}$ may be measured in a paper for a specific $N_{\text{draft}}$, however, real-world $C_{\text{drafter}}$ may lead a practitioner to choose a lower optimal $N_{\text{draft}}$ where real speed-up improvements are much lower." — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08690)

#### 2.5 How lossy methods are evaluated, which baselines are fair, and published criticism
- **[V] What "quality" means differs by modality.** Images: FID, CLIP score, precision/recall, HPSv2, ImageReward (LANTERN, COOL-SD). Speech: WER/CER, speaker similarity, MOS panels (Speech-SD, PCG). Robots: task success rate (Spec-VLA). Text: task accuracy retention (FLy docs: "Relative to Target-only, the minimum measured accuracy retention was 96.43% on AMD and 96.30% on NVIDIA; both corresponded to one fewer correct answer on AIME25"), PRR against both draft and target (Zhang et al.), LLM-as-judge for attack success ("we follow the StrongREJECT ... setting and use GPT-4o-mini as an LLM-as-a-judge"). — [Source LANTERN](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03355); [Source PCG](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13732); [Source Spec-VLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22424); [Source vLLM FLy](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/speculative_decoding/fly/); [Source Zhang](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08678)
- **[V] Critique 1: relaxation is not a drop-in replacement (Xia, Ribar, Balanca; Graphcore / Imperial College; arXiv 2607.08690, 9 Jul 2026, "preprint").** "a practitioner should consider the cost of evaluation when deciding whether to choose to deploy relaxed speculative decoding ... the cost of evaluating the capability of a frontier generalist model across all intended deployment scenarios may be considerable ... Crucially, strict spec-dec doesn't require any capability re-evaluation (in theory)". Criticism of the literature: "existing works generally present narrow evaluations in terms of $N_{\text{draft}}$ and real-world drafter cost $C_{\text{drafter}}$, and they often neglect the impact of overall generation length on speed-up. Experiments are often performed on out-of-date benchmarks such as CNN/Daily Mail ... and XSum ... that may not accurately reflect real-world applications." Findings (AIME 2024, GPQA Diamond, LiveCodeBench; Qwen3.5 MTP+27B, Qwen3 0.6B+32B, Qwen3 8B+32B; about $10^3$ queries per point): "varying draft length has a considerable impact on achievable speed-up, having comparable or greater impact than relaxation, whilst not requiring any additional capability evaluation"; lightweight MTP drafters "can't be relied on for relaxation"; a small LM drafter "still shows consistent clear capability degradation, a far cry from the near-lossless capability demonstrated in many relaxed spec-dec papers"; "relaxing into a drafter that is a weak language model may ... induce generation pathologies (e.g. rambling, repetition loops) that lead to longer generations"; "the calibration of $\alpha$ can be quite different between tasks". Conclusion: relaxation "should be treated by practitioners as a deployment-specific optimisation potentially requiring further capability evaluation and tuning, rather than a drop-in replacement for strict standard speculative decoding." — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08690)
- **[V] Critique 2: wrong baselines and easy benchmarks (Wang, Zhou, Li, Wang, Xiao, Zheng, Shang; Baidu, Zhejiang University, Shandong University, independent; arXiv 2607.26627, v2 4 Sep 2026, no venue).** "existing work overstates their advantages under curated settings (selective hyperparameters or easy benchmarks)". For truncation-based rules (typical acceptance and similar): "the seemingly comparable performance reported in prior work Cai et al. (2024); Narasimhan et al. (2024) largely arises from improved baseline performance induced by their adopted truncation sampling methods"; the right comparison is "directly using the same truncation sampling strategies for the target model" (their "True baseline"); "Accuracy gap between the lossless baseline and truncation-based verification widens sharply with task difficulty, increasing from +0.38 pp on GSM8K to +6.67 pp on AIME"; "easy tasks such as GSM8K mask the distortions introduced by lossy verification". For "collaborative" rules: "overshoot suppression, rather than interpolation, [is] the key design principle" (overshoot = tokens with $q(x)>p(x)$, draft over-estimation). Benchmarks: MATH, MBPP+, INCLUDE, BFCL; metrics "Block Efficiency (BE) ... and Decoding Speed (DS), tokens per second". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26627)
- **[V] Critique 3: accepted tokens per round is not speed (Wang, Xu, Gu, Zhang; University of Waterloo; arXiv 2610.23818, 16 Oct 2026, "Submitted for NAACL 2027, under review").** Five training-free rules (CACTUS, mentored decoding, reducible fuzzy SD, two speculative-cascade rules), six benchmarks, twelve target-drafter pairs, "vLLM 0.26.0 on a single NVIDIA H100 with $N_{\mathrm{draft}}=6$", 360 cells: "At each rule's most relaxed evaluated setting, relaxed verification on average accepts $1.34\times$ as many tokens per round but produces $1.54\times$ longer completions"; "For rules that reduce verifier rounds, accuracy typically drops substantially"; "accepted tokens per round alone is an unreliable measure of decoding speed under relaxed verification. Completion length and verifier rounds must also be reported". Method note: "Because $\alpha$ is not comparable across methods ..., we compare methods at matched $\bar{\ell}$." — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.23818); [Source abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23818)
- **[V] Critique 4: utility benchmarks miss safety (Zhang, Wang, Gong, Yang; Penn State and Duke; arXiv 2610.08678; "accepted by IEEE S&P 2027").** Lossy methods "are still primarily evaluated through the efficiency–utility trade-off, using metrics such as speedup, average acceptance length, perplexity, task accuracy, or human preference"; their measurement adds jailbreak and prompt-injection benchmarks (details in section 5). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08678)
- **[V] Fair-baseline practices found in primary sources.** (a) Compare against naive relaxations at equal speed: GSD's "Amplify" and "Addition" baselines; PCG versus Speech-SD "for a fixed speedup factor". (b) Compare against lossless SD on the same pair and prompts (Wang et al. 2026; vLLM FLy docs report "x vs Standard SD"). (c) Compare against the small model and the large model as the two anchors (PRR; Narasimhan Table 2 "when matching the quality of the large model"). (d) Compare against cascades: Narasimhan et al. state "empirically, cascades offer better cost-quality trade-offs, often even outperforming the large model, while theoretically, speculative decoding offers a guarantee of quality-neutrality", and Google's blog shows speculative decoding as a single point and cascades as a curve between the small and large model. (e) Do not mix lossy and lossless methods in one speed table: ViSpec explicitly de-relaxes Medusa "For a fair comparison". — [Source GSD](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07747); [Source PCG](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13732); [Source Narasimhan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19261); [Source Google blog](https://research.google/blog/speculative-cascades-a-hybrid-approach-for-smarter-faster-llm-inference/); [Source ViSpec](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15235)
- **[V] Lossless and lossy rules are mixed in common comparisons.** Jones, Yuan, Maffeis (Imperial College London, arXiv 2610.10929) note for the standard public checkpoints: "When combined with sampling, SpS and the EAGLE family use exact speculative sampling, which preserves the target distribution. Medusa and Hydra use typical acceptance, which accepts any proposal to which the target assigns sufficient probability and does not preserve the target distribution." Medusa's README: "Relaxing the requirement of matching the distribution of the original model makes the non-greedy generation even faster than greedy decoding." (Spec-Bench's public boards are greedy-only, where this difference does not arise.) — [Source Jones](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10929); [Source Medusa](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FasterDecoding/Medusa/main/README.md)
- **[V] Benchmarks that skip quality because the method is lossless.** "Output quality is not evaluated since our method is lossless" (SpecVLM video); "we do not perform additional output-fidelity evaluation" (SpecVLM AMD); "we do not evaluate image quality on EAGLE-2 since it theoretically guarantees exact distribution matching" (LANTERN). Section 3 shows why a small sanity check is still advisable. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.16201); [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.11815); [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03355)

#### 2.6 Is there a benchmark or leaderboard dedicated to lossy speculative decoding?
- **[V] Not found.** The benchmarks located (Spec-Bench, SPEED-Bench, MMSpec, SpecDecode-Bench code) contain lossless methods and efficiency metrics only; MMSpec says so explicitly ("Since the selected speculative decoding methods are all lossless ... our benchmark only contains two key efficiency metrics"). The closest things are the three 2026 comparison studies in 2.5 (arXiv 2607.08690, 2607.26627, 2610.23818), each with its own models, tasks and metrics, and the security measurement of arXiv 2610.08678. The 2026 multimodal survey still calls for one: "A shared benchmark should span architectures, modalities, input lengths, task uncertainty, output lengths, batch sizes, hardware, and backends. It should report candidate and task quality, stage latency, throughput, memory, energy, and both decode-only and end-to-end speedup." — [Source MMSpec](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14989); [Source survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20743)

### Inferences
- A defensible reading rule for any speed-up claim: note (1) device, (2) precision, (3) batch size/concurrency, (4) temperature and truncation sampler, (5) draft length and whether it was tuned for the lossless baseline too, (6) engine (research prototype versus vLLM/TensorRT-LLM/SGLang), (7) whether speed is tokens per second or end-to-end time for the same task. For lossy methods add (8) completion length relative to the lossless run and (9) quality relative to both the target and the draft model on a hard benchmark.
- "Acceptance rate" has at least three incompatible meanings in the sources (per-token probability $\alpha$; accepted/drafted token ratio; tokens accepted per round), and "mean accepted tokens" sometimes includes the bonus token and sometimes not. Numbers should never be compared across papers without checking the definition.
- Because the lossless baseline's own speed-up depends on draft length, a lossy method evaluated at a long draft length (where strict SD wastes most drafts) will look better than at the draft length a practitioner would actually choose; Xia et al. 2026 make this point explicitly and it applies equally to the image papers (LANTERN++ and COOL-SD use fixed static trees).
- For lossy methods the only honest summary statistic is a curve (quality versus end-to-end time), ideally with the small model, the target model, lossless SD and a cascade drawn on the same axes; single-number claims (a speed-up factor at a stated accuracy retention) are tied to one benchmark and one relaxation setting that the 2026 studies show does not transfer.

### Gaps
- No leaderboard or community benchmark for lossy SD found; no evidence of Spec-Bench being extended with a quality column (its last logged update is April 2025).
- No peer-reviewed human-preference or large LLM-judge study comparing several lossy SD rules on open-ended generation was found; arXiv 2607.26627 explicitly restricts itself to reasoning, code, multilingual and function-calling tasks.
- Venue status of arXiv 2607.08690, 2607.26627 and 2610.23818 is preprint/under review as of 22 Nov 2026.
- The exact README wording of Spec-Bench's equality check was obtained through an automated page summary, not the raw file.

## 3. Is "lossless" exact in practice?

### Takeaway
No: every primary source that tested it reports that theoretically lossless speculative decoding can produce outputs different from plain decoding, because of floating-point and batching effects rather than algorithmic error; the guarantee that survives is distributional, up to numerics. This matters for evaluation (lossless methods are routinely exempted from quality checks) and for calibration of claims about lossy methods (differences of a few points can be noise).

### Cited Findings
- **[V] vLLM documentation, section "Lossless guarantees of Speculative Decoding" (page dated November 19, 2026).** "Speculative decoding sampling is theoretically lossless up to the precision limits of hardware numerics. Floating-point errors might cause slight variations in output distributions"; "vLLM's implementation of speculative decoding is algorithmically validated to be lossless. Key validation tests include: Rejection Sampler Convergence ... Greedy Sampling Equality: Confirms that greedy sampling with speculative decoding matches greedy sampling without it"; "vLLM does not currently guarantee stable token log probabilities (logprobs). This can result in different outputs for the same request across runs"; "variations in generated outputs with and without speculative decoding can occur due to following factors: Floating-Point Precision: Differences in hardware numerical precision may lead to slight discrepancies in the output distribution. Batch Size and Numerical Stability: Changes in batch size may cause variations in logprobs and output probabilities, potentially due to non-deterministic behavior in batched operations or numerical instability." The same text is present in the v0.6.6 docs. New in 2026: "These guarantees apply to the standard rejection sampler. FLy deliberately relaxes verification and is not distribution preserving." — [Source latest](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/speculative_decoding/); [Source v0.6.6](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/v0.6.6/usage/spec_decode.html)
- **[V] Spec-Bench paper, appendix D.3.** "the outputs generated by Speculative Decoding in FP16 precision may not consistently align with those derived from autoregressive decoding. This divergence stems from the accumulation of floating-point errors inherent in FP16 computations, which can result in discrepancies between the outputs of the two decoding methods, particularly in the context of longer sequences. In FP32 precision, the outputs of Speculative Decoding are guaranteed to be exactly the same as autoregressive decoding." (The benchmark itself runs at FP16.) — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.07851)
- **[V] Measured on a production engine (Liu et al. 2026, vLLM, H100).** "Although in theory SD should preserve the same token distribution as standard decoding, in practice we observe discrepancies in the generated outputs—-even under greedy decoding." Table 1 (ShareGPT, Llama3.1-8B, mean generation length): batch 1: 737 without SD, 750 n-gram, 731 EAGLE, 736 EAGLE-3; batch 32: 674, 697, 693, 706; "even without speculative decoding ..., generation length differs across different batch sizes ... these differences are not consistently longer or shorter than the baseline, suggesting that they stem from numerical nondeterminism rather than implementation bugs." — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11580)
- **[V] llama.cpp documentation.** "A fixed seed produces repeatable random draws, but stochastic CPU and backend sampling can still select different tokens because floating-point operations can differ between implementations and devices. Use greedy sampling when exact output matching is required." — [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/docs/speculative.md)
- **[V] Greedy decoding itself is not precision-invariant (context).** Du et al., "Greedy Decoding Is Not Precision-Invariant: Cross-Precision Output Divergence in LLM Inference" (arXiv 2609.26621; "Accepted by Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR), 2026"): "the same model, prompt, and decoding algorithm produce different outputs in BF16 versus FP16 on identical hardware. Across ... six models ... and three benchmarks, 49-100% of prompts diverge; a single token flip often cascades into trajectory-level divergence"; the outcome "depends primarily on the top-two logit margin at the LM head". (Abstract only; the paper is not about speculative decoding.) — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26621)
- **[V] A 2026 practitioner paper keeps a caveat on "lossless".** "strict spec-dec doesn't require any capability re-evaluation (in theory) ... There still may be numerical effects that lead to deviations, so small-scale sanity checks may still be necessary." It also notes sampling noise in its own results: "There is variance in capability evaluation due to random sampling of responses, which can be seen in the differences between lossless configurations". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08690)
- **[V] Lossless baselines differ from the target in published tables (noise or numerics, not explained by the authors).** Spec-VLA: strict (exact-match, greedy) Spec-VLA success rate 74.2% versus 78.0% for autoregressive OpenVLA on LIBERO-Goal, 83.8% versus 85.0% on Spatial, 50.8% versus 52.0% on Long (500 episodes per suite). LANTERN++: lossless EAGLE-1 FID 29.22 versus 28.93 (Lumina-mGPT), 40.71 versus 40.52 (LlamaGen Stage II). COOL-SD: Eagle-1 FID 29.05 versus 28.99. Speech-SD: $\beta=0$ gives WER 6.34% versus 3.67% for the target decoded autoregressively. — [Source Spec-VLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22424); [Source LANTERN++](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06352); [Source COOL-SD](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09212); [Source Speech-SD](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15380)
- **[V] Lossless SD does not reproduce the target's benchmark scores exactly in a 2026 security measurement.** In Table I of Zhang et al. (batch size 32, temperature 0), lossless SD has performance-retention rates of 1.019, .915, .947, 1.000 (Qwen3-0.6B/8B on Jailbreak-SD, OpenPromptInjection, HumanEval, GSM8K) and .909, .983, .933, .976 (Llama3-1B/8B), where 1 means equal to the target and 0 equal to the draft model. The paper does not discuss the cause. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08678)
- **[V] Official documentation that states exactness without a numeric caveat.** Hugging Face Transformers: "Standard speculative decoding is lossless — it guarantees the output distribution matches the target model exactly." Hugging Face Whisper post (models loaded in float16): "mathematically ensuring exactly the same outputs are achieved", and after the benchmark "3.5% WER again, as we have identical outputs to using the main model standalone." Google Research: "the method guarantees an identical output distribution." EAGLE README: "provably maintaining the consistency with vanilla decoding in the distribution of generated texts." — [Source HF docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/main/en/assisted_decoding); [Source HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/whisper-speculative-decoding); [Source Google](https://research.google/blog/looking-back-at-speculative-decoding/); [Source EAGLE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SafeAILab/EAGLE/main/README.md)
- **[V] Losslessness is conditional on design choices that implementations expose as options.** Hugging Face code: sampling with draft logits uses the Leviathan rule (`probability_ratio = p_i / q_i`, residual `torch.clamp((p_n_plus_1 - q_n_plus_1), min=0)`); without draft logits (prompt lookup) it samples from the target (`torch.multinomial`) and keeps candidates up to the first mismatch; both are distribution-preserving. TGI validates speculative ids by equality with the ids chosen by its normal sampler (`validate_speculative = _next_ids[:-1] == _speculated_ids`). vLLM exposes `draft_sample_method`: "greedy uses target-only acceptance for random requests; probabilistic retains the full draft distribution for probability-ratio acceptance." EAGLE-2-style deterministic tree drafting makes the acceptance probability equal to the target probability of the drafted token (LANTERN++: "effectively setting $p(\widehat{x}|s)=1$"). — [Source HF code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/transformers/main/src/transformers/generation/utils.py); [Source TGI code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/text-generation-inference/main/server/text_generation_server/utils/tokens.py); [Source vLLM](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/speculative_decoding/); [Source LANTERN++](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06352)
- **[V] Vocabulary pruning in drafters is a quality-neutral but speed-relevant approximation.** SPEED-Bench: "EAGLE3 applies vocabulary pruning (usually to 32k tokens)"; "specific sub-domains, particularly Multilingual data, are disproportionately sensitive to this reduction" (it lowers acceptance; verification remains exact). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09557)

### Inferences
- "Lossless" is best glossed as distribution-preserving in exact arithmetic. In FP16/BF16 serving, with batching and KV-cache reuse, token-for-token equality with plain greedy decoding is not guaranteed, and plain decoding is not even reproducible across batch sizes or precisions. The practical guarantee is that no systematic bias toward the draft model is introduced.
- A corollary for reading lossy-method papers: when a lossless baseline already differs from the target by 0.3 FID, 1–4 points of success rate or 2–3 points of WER in the same table, differences of that size between a lossy method and the target cannot be interpreted as "no degradation" or as "degradation" without confidence intervals.
- The sources never report a case where a correct lossless implementation shifted task accuracy in a consistent direction; the documented discrepancies are trajectory divergences after near-tie token flips.

### Gaps
- No specific Hugging Face GitHub issue documenting assisted-generation output mismatches was located (searches returned only documentation pages); the HF docs contain no numeric-precision caveat.
- No study was found that quantifies, at scale, how often speculative decoding (as opposed to batching/precision alone) flips a greedy token in a production engine; Liu et al. report generation-length statistics only.
- KV-cache-specific sources of divergence (e.g. cache dtype differences between draft and target) are mentioned in vLLM's DFly page as configuration constraints, but no measurement of their effect was found.

## 4. Deployment reality: which lossy options exist in widely used software as of late 2026, and what do inference providers say they run?

### Takeaway
Open-source serving stacks ship lossless speculative decoding by default and, as of November 2026, exactly two documented opt-in lossy verifiers were found: vLLM's FLy verifier (`rejection_sample_method: "fly"`, first present in the v0.33.0 tag; vLLM's older typical-acceptance sampler is documented at least from v0.5.4 through v0.9.2 and is gone in v0.10.0) and Hugging Face Transformers' static ensemble verification (`assistant_ensemble_weight`, first documented for v5.14.0); llama.cpp and TGI expose only draft-shaping parameters and exact verification. No official statement was found from Google, Groq, Cerebras, Together, Fireworks, AWS, SambaNova, OpenAI or Anthropic saying that a production service uses lossy or "relaxed" acceptance; every official description found frames speculative decoding as quality-preserving.

#### 4.2 Hugging Face Transformers
- **[V] Lossless options.** `assistant_model` (speculative decoding; "supports greedy search and sampling but not batched inputs"), `prompt_lookup_num_tokens` (prompt lookup), `assistant_early_exit` (self-speculative), `use_mtp=True` (MTP layers of DeepSeek-V3, GLM-4.5) and MTP drafters ("google/gemma-4-E4B-it-assistant"), universal assisted decoding for different tokenizers. Draft-shaping parameters that do not change the output distribution: `num_assistant_tokens`, `num_assistant_tokens_schedule` ("heuristic": "When all speculative tokens are correct, increase num_assistant_tokens by 2 else reduce by 1"), `assistant_confidence_threshold` ("If the assistant model's confidence in its prediction for the current token is lower than this threshold, the assistant model stops the current token generation iteration"). — [Source docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/main/en/assisted_decoding); [Source GenerationConfig](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/main/en/main_classes/text_generation)
- **[V] The lossy option: static ensemble verification.** "Standard speculative decoding is lossless — it guarantees the output distribution matches the target model exactly. Static ensemble verification relaxes this by verifying against a mixture of the target and draft distributions, increasing the acceptance rate (faster inference) at the cost of making the output distribution a mixture rather than the exact target distribution. A recommended starting value is 0.7." Parameter: "`assistant_ensemble_weight` (float, optional) — Enables static ensemble verification in speculative decoding. If set to a value in (0.0, 1.0), the verifier accepts tokens against the mixture w * p_target + (1 - w) * q_draft instead of p_target, trading a controlled distributional bias for a higher acceptance rate. Defaults to None, which keeps decoding lossless." In code the acceptance ratio becomes `probability_ratio = 1.0 - assistant_ensemble_weight + assistant_ensemble_weight * (p_i / q_i)`, i.e. accept with probability $\min\{1,\ 1-w+w\,p(x)/q(x)\}$; in greedy mode the candidate is compared with $\arg\max(w\,p+(1-w)\,q)$. The section is present in the v5.14.0 documentation and absent from the v5.13.0 page. — [Source docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/main/en/assisted_decoding); [Source GenerationConfig](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/main/en/main_classes/text_generation); [Source code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/transformers/main/src/transformers/generation/utils.py); [Source v5.14.0](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/v5.14.0/en/assisted_decoding); [Source v5.13.0](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/v5.13.0/en/assisted_decoding)
- **[V] Origin of the option.** The docs link arXiv 2604.07622: "DIVERSED: Relaxed Speculative Decoding via Dynamic Ensemble Verification" (Wang, Kasa, et al.; Purdue University, Amazon, UIUC, University of Texas at El Paso; "accepted at AISTATS 2026"), which "learns an ensemble-based verifier that blends the draft and target model distributions with a task-dependent and context-dependent weight"; Transformers implements only the static-weight variant. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07622)

### Inferences
- As of November 2026 lossy acceptance in production is mostly a capability of the open-source stack rather than an advertised provider practice: vLLM and Transformers each expose one opt-in relaxed verifier with an explicit warning, while providers' public language is uniformly quality-preserving (Google: "maintaining the same quality of responses"; Cerebras: "Model output accuracy is likewise unchanged"; Together: "matches the distribution of non-speculative decoding"). Absence of a statement is not evidence of absence; what a closed provider runs cannot be established from public sources.
- The two lossy options in mainstream software were both adopted within months of the corresponding papers (FLy: ICLR 2026, in vLLM by v0.33.0; DIVERSED: AISTATS 2026, in Transformers by v5.14.0), and vLLM's FLy validation was reported on AMD and NVIDIA accelerators with FLy's authors at AMD; adoption appears vendor-driven.
- The historical vLLM typical-acceptance sampler shows that a lossy option can also disappear: it was dropped when the engine was rewritten, and a later paper had to patch vLLM to study relaxed rules.
- Parameters often mistaken for lossy knobs are not: llama.cpp's `--draft-p-min`, Transformers' `assistant_confidence_threshold` and `num_assistant_tokens`, and Fireworks' `--draft-token-count` only change how much is drafted; the verifier is unchanged. The output-changing knobs are vLLM `rejection_sample_method="fly"` (and formerly `typical_acceptance_sampler`), Transformers `assistant_ensemble_weight`, Medusa's posterior thresholds at non-zero temperature, and the benchmark-only synthetic modes.
- The trend toward bundled MTP/EAGLE-style drafters cuts against lossy acceptance in practice, because (per Xia et al. 2026) most training-free relaxations assume the drafter is a competent language model.

## 5. Risks and side effects: watermarking, privacy side channels, safety and behaviour shift, adversarial inputs

### Takeaway
Three risks are documented by primary sources and one is newly quantified in 2026. Watermarking: under Hu and Huang's framework, maximum watermark strength and maximum speculative acceptance cannot be had together (NeurIPS 2024), the same either-or appears in Google's SynthID-Text paper (Nature 2024), and a 2026 ICLR paper shows the trade-off can be improved by making acceptance pseudorandom. Privacy: acceptance patterns are input-dependent and leak through packet sizes and timing (prompt fingerprinting at 77.6–100% accuracy among 50 prompts at temperature 0.3), for lossy and lossless variants alike. Safety: the first systematic measurement (IEEE S&P 2027) finds that lossy verification degrades jailbreak and prompt-injection resistance faster and earlier than utility, and attributes it to draft tokens accepted at the start of the response. No study of hallucination, calibration or bias under lossy acceptance was found.

### Cited Findings

#### 5.1 Watermarking
- **[V] Hu and Huang (result in one paragraph).** Zhengmian Hu, Heng Huang (University of Maryland, College Park), "Inevitable Trade-off between Watermark Strength and Speculative Sampling Efficiency for Language Models", NeurIPS 2024 Main Conference Track (Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 37), arXiv 2410.20418. Setting: unbiased watermarks implemented as reweighting $R_E(P)$ of the target distribution with a watermark code $E$; a "two reweight framework" also reweights the draft distribution, $R'_E(Q)$, to keep overlap high, because "naively applying speculative sampling to a watermarked target distribution $R_{E}(P)$ may significantly reduce the overlap probability". Theorem 1 (No-go; notation lightly normalised, the source writes $\absolutevalue{\Sigma}$ and spells out the signatures of $R$ and $R'$): "When the vocabulary size $|\Sigma|>2$, there do not exist non-trivial reweighting functions $R$ ... and $R'$ ..., and a speculative process $A(j|i)$ such that for all $P,Q\in\Delta_{\Sigma}$: 1. The watermark strength is maintained: $\widehat{R}_{E}(P)=R_{E}(P)$. 2. The sampling efficiency is maintained: $\alpha(P,Q)=\mathbb{E}_{E\sim P_{E}}[\sum_{i\in\Sigma}A(i|i)R'_{E}(Q)(i)]$." They give two algorithms that keep one property each (both use $R'_E=R_E$ but different speculative processes), and both keep the output unbiased. Experiments: Llama-7b target, Llama-68m draft, CNN/DailyMail summarisation; efficiency as "Average Accepted Tokens Per Step (AATPS)", strength as "Average Negative Log P-value Per Token (ANLPPT)". Caveat in the paper: maintaining $\widehat{R}_E(P)=R_E(P)$ is a sufficient condition; "for a fixed scoring function and a specific $R_{E}$, it is possible that $\widehat{R}_{E}(P)\neq R_{E}(P)$ while $w=w'$". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.20418); [Source venue](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6415b5dc9aa4bf4e6404cb221a109ec7-Abstract-Conference.html)
- **[V] Follow-up that softens the result.** Weiqing He, Xiang Li, Li Shen, Weijie Su, Qi Long, "Improving the Trade-off Between Watermark Strength and Speculative Sampling Efficiency for Language Models", arXiv 2602.01428, "Accepted at ICLR 2026": "We revisit this trade-off and show it is not absolute"; criticism of the earlier definition: "watermark strength is defined in a binary manner"; new measure "defined as the expected KL divergence between the watermarked and original token distributions", "maximized when tokens are deterministic functions of pseudorandom numbers"; they "derive explicit Pareto curves for two existing watermarking schemes" and propose "a principled mechanism that injects pseudorandomness into draft-token acceptance, ensuring maximal watermark strength while maintaining speculative sampling efficiency." — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01428)
- **[V] The same trade-off appears in a production watermarking paper.** Dathathri et al. (Google DeepMind), "Scalable watermarking for identifying large language model outputs", Nature, published 23 October 2024 (SynthID-Text): "productionized systems often use speculative sampling"; "we propose high-detectability watermarked speculative sampling, which preserves the detectability of the watermark, but may decrease the efficiency (and thus increase the overall latency) of speculative sampling ... Alternatively, we propose fast watermarked speculative sampling, which (provided the watermark is single-token non-distortionary) preserves the efficiency of speculative sampling, but may decrease the detectability of the watermark"; evaluated "using Gemma 7B-IT as the target model and Gemma 2B-IT as the smaller draft model that proposes three 'lookahead' tokens at a time". He et al. note that "both OpenAI's (Aaronson, 2023) and Google's (Dathathri et al., 2024) watermarking schemes achieve this maximal strength" under their KL-based measure. — [Source Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08025-4); [Source He et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01428)
- **[V] Scope.** All three papers concern lossless (unbiased) speculative sampling combined with unbiased or non-distortionary watermarks: Hu and Huang require that "the generation distribution is unbiased", He et al. define strength "for unbiased watermarks", and SynthID-Text's fast variant assumes "the watermark is single-token non-distortionary". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.20418); [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01428); [Source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08025-4)

#### 5.2 Privacy and side channels
- **[V] Wei et al. (title corrected).** Jiankun Wei, Abdulrahman Abdulrazzag, Tianchen Zhang, Adel Muursepp, Gururaj Saileshwar (University of Toronto); arXiv 2411.01076; v1 title "Privacy Risks of Speculative Decoding in Large Language Models", current title (v3 26 Sep 2025, v4 11 Feb 2026) "When Speculation Spills Secrets: Side Channels via Speculative Decoding In LLMs"; no venue in the arXiv comments. What leaks and how: "(1) the pattern of correct and incorrect speculations of output tokens is dependent on the input, and (2) input-dependent speculation patterns can be inferred from the variations in packet sizes: upon correct speculation, more tokens generated per iteration leads to a larger packet". Threat model: "a malicious ISP or a compromised router that can observe network packet sizes". Results: "an adversary monitoring these patterns can fingerprint user queries (from a set of 50 prompts) with over 75% accuracy across four speculative-decoding schemes at temperature 0.3: REST (100%), LADE (91.6%), BiLD (95.2%), and EAGLE (77.6%). Hiding token lengths alone is insufficient: "speculation itself introduces packet size variations due to varying token counts". BiLD, one of the four targets, is a lossy method. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.01076); [Source v1 title](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01076v1)
- **[V] Carlini and Nasr.** Nicholas Carlini, Milad Nasr (Google DeepMind), "Remote Timing Attacks on Efficient Language Model Inference", arXiv 2410.17175 (22 Oct 2024; no venue in arXiv comments). Efficient-inference techniques "introduce data-dependent timing characteristics"; "By monitoring the (encrypted) network traffic between a victim user and a remote language model, we can learn information about the content of messages by noting when responses are faster or slower. With complete black-box access, on open source systems we show how it is possible to learn the topic of a user's conversation (e.g., medical advice vs. coding assistance) with 90%+ precision, and on production systems like OpenAI's ChatGPT and Anthropic's Claude we can distinguish between specific messages or infer the user's language. The active attack is evaluated with "a 7 billion parameter model and a 2 billion parameter draft model" and a 70B/7B pair; one probe question reaches 97.2% success in their Table II. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.17175)

#### 5.3 Safety and behaviour shift under lossy acceptance
- **[V] First systematic measurement.** Yichi Zhang, Zhiqi Wang, Neil Zhenqiang Gong, Yuchen Yang (Pennsylvania State University, Duke University), "Secure Speculative Decoding for Large Language Models", arXiv 2610.08678 (v2 13 Oct 2026), "18 pages, accepted by IEEE S&P 2027", code github.com/YichiCS/Secure-Speculative-Decoding. #### 5.4 Adversarial attacks on speculative decoding itself (2026)
- **[V] Acceptance-collapse attacks (availability and cost).** Mistletoe (Sun et al.; Harbin Institute of Technology Shenzhen, Tsinghua SIGS, Huawei and others; arXiv 2605.14005): perturbations that "preserve the target model's visible behavior while substantially reducing draft-token acceptability", using "a null-space projection mechanism"; it "substantially reduces average accepted length $\tau$, collapses speedup, and lowers averaged token throughput, while preserving output quality and perplexity." ADSD (Wang et al.; Clemson University and others; arXiv 2607.21804): a "prompt-suffix attack that collapses verifier acceptance"; "On the GSM8K dataset, our attack increases the mean sample time by 62.3% while preserving the task quality." Speedbumps (Jones, Yuan, Maffeis; Imperial College London; arXiv 2610.10929): "Speedbump-P reduces speed-up by 12.2–36.8% under greedy decoding"; "the target model needs 3.2 times as many forward passes per generated token (EAGLE-3 on summarisation)"; "Under sampling, some attacks make SD slower than autoregressive decoding". All three are preprints with no venue listed. — [Source Mistletoe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14005); [Source ADSD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21804); [Source Speedbumps](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10929)
- **[V] Lenient acceptance blunts rejection attacks.** Speedbumps: "Under typical acceptance (Medusa and Hydra), both attacks weaken: Speedbump-P falls from 22.4% to 18.2% and Speedbump-D from 17.7% to 12.6%. Typical acceptance is more lenient than greedy verification". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10929)

### Inferences
- The safety result has a simple mechanism that a guide can state plainly: a lossy verifier outputs a mixture that leans toward the draft model wherever the rule is lenient; small draft models refuse far less reliably than their targets (attack failure rates of 0.1–0.2 versus about 0.9 in the S&P study); and refusal is decided in the first few tokens. Hence the same relaxation that costs a few points of accuracy can cost a large fraction of refusal behaviour, and position-dependent strictness (strict early, lenient later) is the natural mitigation. COOL-SD's annealing result for images points the other way (more lenient early, stricter later, to minimise a TV bound), so the choice of which positions to relax is objective-dependent; the two papers do not discuss each other.
- Lossy acceptance trades one attack surface for another: it reduces the leverage of acceptance-collapse (cost-inflation) attacks, since fewer drafts are rejected, but increases exposure to attacks that exploit the weaker draft model's behaviour.
- Side channels do not depend on losslessness: any scheme whose number of tokens per verification round depends on the input leaks, and BiLD (lossy) was fingerprinted at 95.2%. Relaxation changes the pattern, not the existence of the channel. Batching tokens before sending, or padding, are the documented mitigations, at a latency or bandwidth cost.
- For watermarking, the practical message as of 2026 is that the combination is possible but must be co-designed: naive composition loses either detectability or acceptance; pseudorandom acceptance (ICLR 2026) recovers both under a KL-based notion of strength. Nothing is known for lossy verifiers, whose output is no longer an unbiased sample.

### Gaps
- No paper analysing watermark detectability under lossy (relaxed) acceptance was found.
- No study was found measuring hallucination rates, factuality, calibration (e.g. expected calibration error) or demographic bias under lossy speculative decoding for text; searches returned only efficiency/accuracy trade-off papers.
- The S&P 2027 measurement uses greedy decoding with a separate small draft model and reports Table I for two of its four model pairs; results for tree drafters, MTP heads, sampling temperatures above 0, or frontier-scale targets were not found, and its per-setting curves (their Figure 1) were not extracted here. Table I averages over hyper-parameter sweeps, so it does not say how a specific default configuration (for example vLLM's FLy defaults) behaves.
- SafeSpec (ICML 2026) was read at abstract level only; authors, arXiv ID and experimental details not extracted.
- No independent replication of Mistletoe, ADSD or Speedbumps was found; numbers are from the authors' abstracts or text.
- Publication venue of Wei et al. (arXiv 2411.01076) and of Carlini and Nasr (arXiv 2410.17175): not found.
- No work was found on side channels specific to relaxed acceptance (for example whether the acceptance pattern leaks the relaxation setting or the drafter identity).

## 6. Open problems as stated in surveys, position-style studies and benchmarks (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
Earlier surveys (2024–2025) list efficiency problems: the accuracy/latency trade-off of the drafter, batched and high-throughput serving, integration with other techniques and modalities. The 2026 literature adds problems specific to lossy verification: acceptance rules with explicit quality guarantees per output space, evaluation that reports quality, completion length and end-to-end time on hard tasks, relaxation that works with lightweight bundled drafters, and safety/security under relaxed verification.

- **[V] Xia, Ribar, Balanca 2026 (practitioner study of relaxed SD), concluding remarks.** "Draft length and relative drafter cost can affect speed-up as much, if not more than, relaxation; weak or dedicated MTP drafters are not reliable language models for relaxed rejection; stronger drafters can potentially give approximately lossless relaxed speed-up; and relaxation parameters do not reliably transfer across tasks." They also name "a conflict of narratives": relaxed methods assume "the drafter is a decent language model which can afford relaxation, whilst the community shift towards dedicated MTP drafters leans into the guarantees of strict spec-dec". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08690)
- **[V] Wang et al. 2026 ("Revisiting Lossy Verification"), limitations.** "our evaluation excludes open-ended generation and dialogue"; "our unification centers on verification mechanisms rather than the complete speculative decoding pipeline; therefore, it does not fully capture interactions with draft-model design, candidate construction, tree expansion, or system-level scheduling"; "wall-clock speedups depend on the underlying hardware, implementation, and serving stack." — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26627)
- **[V] Liu et al. 2026 (system study), stated direction.** "a promising direction for future work: developing an accurate yet lightweight predictor capable of adapting to varying levels of acceptance behavior across workloads, requests and token positions"; and the gap "between observed and theoretical upper bounds" of speed-up. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11580)
- **[V] Security as an open problem.** Zhang et al.: "the safety and security implications of efficiency-oriented speculative decoding under adversarial inputs have not been systematically studied"; attack papers call for "more robust designs of LLM acceleration systems" (Mistletoe) and suggest extending attacks "to other verification rules, such as the typical acceptance used by Medusa and Hydra when sampling" (Speedbumps). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08678); [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14005); [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10929)
- **[V] Open theoretical point for lossy rules.** COOL-SD: "any relaxation of the acceptance criterion inevitably introduces bias that cannot be perfectly corrected by adjusting the resampling distributions", and earlier image relaxations "lack any mathematical account of their divergence from the target distribution"; LANTERN++ asks for "adaptive acceptance mechanisms that dynamically adjust relaxation based on token uncertainty". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09212); [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06352)

### Inferences
- A consolidated list for a writer, each item traceable to a quote above: (1) output-space-specific acceptance rules with explicit, sequence-level quality guarantees (only per-step or group-level guarantees exist); (2) a shared benchmark for lossy SD reporting quality, completion length, verifier rounds and end-to-end time across hardware and batch sizes; (3) relaxation that is compatible with lightweight bundled drafters (MTP, EAGLE-style, block-diffusion); (4) task-robust calibration of the relaxation parameter; (5) safety, security, watermarking and side-channel behaviour under relaxed verification; (6) interaction of relaxed verification with trees, block verification and scheduling under load; (7) evaluation on open-ended generation and dialogue, which the 2026 comparison studies exclude; (8) closed-loop validity of accepted action chunks in robotics.
- The surveys of 2024–2025 do not treat lossy verification as a first-class open problem; that framing appears only in 2026 work, which suggests the area is recent enough that a guide should present its conclusions as provisional.
