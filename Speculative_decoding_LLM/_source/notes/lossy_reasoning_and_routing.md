## Reward-Guided Speculative Decoding (RSD): step-level accept rule, mixture distribution, theory, FLOPs and accuracy

### Takeaway
RSD (Liao et al., ICML 2025) lets a small draft model write a whole reasoning step (text up to `\n\n`), scores it with a process reward model (PRM) and keeps it if the reward is at least a threshold (δ = 0.7 by default); otherwise the large target model rewrites that step. The output distribution is a reward-dependent mixture of draft and target, deliberately biased; the only guarantee is that the expected PRM reward is at least that of the draft model, and the headline numbers are in FLOPs (e.g. 88.0 vs 85.6 on MATH500 with "nearly 4.4× fewer FLOPs" for a 7B draft / 72B target / 7B PRM), not wall-clock latency.

#### (b) Problem, in the authors' framing
- [V] "A key limitation is the strict unbiasedness requirement, which ensures the final token distribution matches the large model’s but restricts flexibility in exploring diverse completions ... High-quality tokens (e.g., those favored by a process reward) may still be rejected if their probabilities under the large model are too low, leading to wasted computation and negating potential speedups." — [arXiv HTML v3, §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19324v3)
- [V] "Allowing controlled bias, where the final distribution deviates slightly from the large model, can improve performance. If a draft token is correct but does not match the large model’s distribution exactly, strict rejection is counterproductive. Reward-guided acceptance retains valuable partial solutions, reduces unnecessary queries, and can even surpass the large model’s performance." — [arXiv HTML v3, §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19324v3)

#### (c) Mechanism
- [V] Unit of speculation = one reasoning step, delimited by a blank line: "For process Best-of-N, beam search and RSD, we define a generation ended with \n\n as a reasoning step, and then apply a PRM to rate this step." — [arXiv HTML v3, §3 Default Setting](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19324v3)
- [V] Control flow (Algorithm 1, "RSD: Reward-Guided Speculative Decoding"): for each step $i$: "Generate draft step $\hat{y}_{i}\leftarrow m(x,y_{1:i-1})$"; "Compute reward $r_{i}\leftarrow r(y_{i}|x,y_{1:i-1})$"; "if $\mathcal{A}_{\omega}(r_{i})$ then Accept the draft step $y_{i}\leftarrow\hat{y}_{i}$ else Generate a target step ${y}_{i}\leftarrow M(x,y_{1:i-1})$"; stop at EOS or max length. So: draft model generates every step first; the PRM checks every draft step; the target model writes a step only when the draft step is rejected (it regenerates that step from the same prefix; it does not verify) — [arXiv HTML v3, Alg. 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19324v3)
- [V] Mixture distribution: $\mathbf{P}_{\text{RSD}}(y_{i}|z_{i})=w(y_{i}|z_{i})\mathbf{P}_{m}(y_{i}|z_{i})+v(y_{i}|z_{i})\mathbf{P}_{M}(y_{i}|z_{i})$ with $z_i=[x,y_{1:i-1}]$, $v(y_i|z_i)=\nu$ constant and $w(y_{i}|z_{i})=\omega(r(y_{i}|z_{i}))$. Proposition 2.1: $\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{RSD}}(y|z)=\omega_{r}(y|z)\mathbf{P}_{m}(y|z)+\nu\mathbf{P}_{M}(y|z)$, "where ... $\nu$ is the normalizing constant $\nu=1-\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{m}}\omega_{r}$" — [arXiv HTML v3, §2 and Prop. 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19324v3)
- [V] Acceptance criterion (Algorithm 2): compute $\omega(r)$; "if $\omega(r)=0$ or $1$ then $\mathcal{A}_{\omega}(r)=\omega(r)$ else Sample $u\sim\mathcal{U}(0,1)$; $\mathcal{A}_{\omega}(r)=\mathbf{1}(\omega(r)\geq u)$" — [arXiv HTML v3, Alg. 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19324v3)
- [V] Candidate weighting functions (Table 1): constant $\omega(r)=p$; binary step $\omega(r)=\mathbf{1}(r\geq\delta)$; clipping $\omega(r)=\min(1,\max(0,r))$; "sigmoidal" $\omega(r)=\max(0,\frac{r}{1+r})$; logistic $\omega(r)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-\alpha(r-\delta)}}$ — [arXiv HTML v3, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19324v3)
- [V] Rule actually used: "We employ the binary step function (the second option in Table 1) as the weighting function and set $\delta=0.7$." i.e. accept the draft step iff $r(\hat y_i\mid x,y_{1:i-1})\ge 0.7$, with PRM scores in $[0,1]$ ("The reward score ranges from 0 to 1, the higher the better.") — [arXiv HTML v3, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19324v3)
- [V] Theory 1 (Proposition 2.2): if "$\omega(r)$ is non-decreasing in $r$" and "$\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{M}}[r(y|z)]\geq\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{m}}[r(y|z)]$", then "$\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{\text{RSD}}}[r(y|z)]\geq\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}_{m}}[r(y|z)]$" (expected reward at least that of the draft model) — [arXiv HTML v3, Prop. 2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19324v3)
- [V] Theory 2, the statement about the threshold (Proposition 2.3): "Given a constrained sampling budget $\nu=1-\mathbf{E}_{y\sim p_{m}}\omega_{r}(y|z)\leq\gamma$, $\gamma\in(0,1)$, the optimal sampling strategy that maximize the reward is $\omega_{r}(y|z)=1$ if $r(y|z)\geq\delta_{\gamma}(z)$, $0$ if $r(y|z)<\delta_{\gamma}(z)$, where $\delta_{\gamma}(z)$ is the largest possible threshold that makes the function satisfy the constraint." and "In real implementation, $\delta_{\gamma}$ can be a hyper-parameter $\delta$." The abstract's wording: "We theoretically demonstrate that a threshold-based mixture strategy achieves an optimal balance between resource utilization and performance." — [arXiv HTML v3, Prop. 2.3 and abstract](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19324v3)
- [V] The authors note the reward can exceed the target model's: "Since $\mathbf{P}_{\text{RSD}}$ is a mixture of $\omega_{r}\mathbf{P}_{m}$ and $\mathbf{P}_{M}$, its reward is a weighted sum of their expected rewards. Thus, $\mathbf{P}_{\text{RSD}}$ can exceed $\mathbf{P}_{M}$ in expected reward when we use an aggressive $w$ that only assigns 1 to high-reward regions. ... We also notice this phenomenon empirically." — [arXiv HTML v3, §2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19324v3)
- [V] Likelihood-ratio alternative mentioned but not used: $w(y\mid z)=\min\left(1,\alpha\,\frac{\mathbf{P}_{M}(y\mid z)}{\mathbf{P}_{m}(y\mid z)}\right)$ and hybrid $w(y|z)=\min(1,\beta\,r(y|z)\,\frac{\mathbf{P}_{M}(y|z)}{\mathbf{P}_{m}(y|z)})$ — [arXiv HTML v3, §2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19324v3)
- [V] What is trained: nothing in RSD itself; off-the-shelf PRM: "Our system utilizes Skywork-o1-Open-PRM (o1 Team, 2024) as the process reward model (PRM), as it was the most advanced open-source PRM available during our experiments" — [arXiv HTML v3, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19324v3)

#### (d) In what sense lossy; what is guaranteed; is the large model skipped?
- [V] Lossy by design: "incorporating a controlled bias to prioritize high-reward outputs, in contrast to existing speculative decoding methods that enforce strict unbiasedness" — [arXiv HTML v3, abstract](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19324v3)
- [V] Guarantee = expected-reward lower bound relative to the draft model only (Prop. 2.2 above). A later paper states the weakness explicitly: RSD "only provides a guarantee on the expected reward ... which in the worst case does not yield any improvement over the small model $\pi_{S}$, and also does not guarantee anything about the policy $\pi_{\text{RSD}}$ itself" — [GSI, arXiv HTML v3, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04118v3)
- [V] The target model is skipped (not run in verification mode) on accepted steps; whole questions can be answered without it: "even at $\delta=0.7$, the draft model alone can still solve 48% of questions, as the rewards for its reasoning steps on these questions are sufficiently high. This eliminates the need for the target model to engage with these 48% of questions, distinguishing RSD from SD, which always involves both the draft and target models for every question." (MATH500, RSD 1.5B/7B/7B, Fig. 5) — [arXiv HTML v3, §3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19324v3)
- [V] The PRM runs on every step: "In MATH500, the average number of reasoning steps per question is 18, suggesting that the PRM is invoked 18 times per question, akin to generating 18 tokens." — [arXiv HTML v3, §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19324v3)

#### (g) Costs, caveats, limitations, criticisms
- [V] General-domain result is a loss versus the target: AlpacaEval win rate (vs gpt4_turbo outputs) "Draft Model only (Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct) 7.09; Target Model only (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) 24.47; RSD 18.85", using an outcome reward model (Skywork-Reward-Llama-3.1-8B-v0.2) with δ=0 because "To the best of our knowledge, there is not yet a PRM for general-domain generation." — [arXiv HTML v3, Table 6 and §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19324v3)
- [V] Speculative decoding (lossless in theory) scores below the target model in their runs: "While SD is theoretically unbiased, guaranteeing accuracy equal to the target model, it often underperforms in practice. This discrepancy, as also noted by Chen et al. (2023a), arises due to floating-point errors." — [arXiv HTML v3, §3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19324v3)
- [V] Criticism in GSI (ICLR 2026): with their re-implementation (threshold 0.7), "RSD generally tends to accept almost all samples, which explains why its performance is comparable to S-BoN with the small model"; acceptance of RSD 94.9–98.2% in their Table 1; "as $n$ increases, RSD collapses to soft best-of-$n$ with $\pi_{S}$, at least without more careful hyperparameter tuning"; and RSD "lacks theoretical guarantees on distributional fidelity" — [GSI arXiv HTML v3, §1, §5.1, App. C.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04118v3)
- [V] Criticism in SPECS (NeurIPS 2026): a multi-beam re-implementation "RSD++" with the recommended threshold 0.7 is well below beam search with the target on AMC23 (e.g. n=4: 31.2 ± 17.8 vs 59.2 ± 1.2, Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B-Instruct) and OlympiadBench (33.0 vs 39.3), though above it on MATH500 (77.8 vs 69.3); SPECS argues "other works building on speculative drafting all start with a draft model and switch to target model when the reward signal is low, resulting in lower accuracy or wasted latency for generating speculative drafts" — [SPECS arXiv HTML v2, §1 and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15733v2)
- [V] Criticism in SpecGuard (2026): "Prior approaches mitigate this using external reward models, but incur additional latency, computational overhead, and limit generalizability." — [arXiv abs 2604.15244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15244)
- [V] R2R's appendix describes RSD as working "well on tasks with clear and definable reward signals" (and, inaccurately, classifies RSD as a token-level method) — [R2R arXiv HTML v2, App. D.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21600v2)

### Inferences
- The abstract's "+3.5" is consistent with the Qwen2.5-Instruct block of Table 2: RSD with δ* and the 1.5B PRM averages 62.6 versus 59.1 for SD (62.6 − 59.1 = 3.5). This is my subtraction; the paper does not say which row the +3.5 comes from.
- Versus the *target model alone*, the average gains in Table 2 are small (+0.1 to +1.9 at δ = 0.7, one setting at −0.5; +0.8 to +2.7 with per-task δ*); several are within what one would expect from run-to-run noise on 200–500-question benchmarks (the paper reports no confidence intervals in Table 2). The large single-benchmark jumps (GPQA 32.8 → 38.4) coexist with losses on the same benchmark for another PRM (GPQA 28.8 → 20.2).
- RSD is closer to a step-level cascade with an external judge than to speculative decoding: the large model never verifies, it only regenerates; the acceptance test does not involve the large model's probabilities at all (the likelihood-ratio weighting is discussed but not used).
- Because the saving is reported in FLOPs with the 2N-per-token approximation, the 4.4× figure should not be quoted as a wall-clock speedup.

### Gaps
- Wall-clock latency or tokens/s for RSD: not found in the paper (efficiency is reported as FLOPs in Fig. 4; I did not find a latency table).
- Whether the FLOPs count includes the target model's prefill over draft-accepted steps (needed when the target is later invoked): not found in the text.
- Exact numeric FLOPs values behind Fig. 4 and the exact proportion of steps (not questions) handled by the draft on each benchmark: only the figure is given (not read numerically); the 48% is a share of questions.
- ICML 2025 reviews of RSD: OpenReview search returned no review notes for this forum during this session (not found).

## SpecReason: speculative reasoning steps scored by the large model

### Takeaway
SpecReason (Pan et al., NeurIPS 2025) has a small reasoning model write each thinking step and asks the large base model, in one prefill-only pass, for a single-token 0–9 utility score; the step is kept if the score reaches a static threshold (e.g. ≥ 7), otherwise the base model regenerates it (optionally with token-level speculative decoding, "SpecReason+Decode"). It reports 1.4–3.0× latency reduction with +0.4–9.0% accuracy under an 8192-token budget; the accuracy gain is largely explained by the small model's shorter chains under that budget, and a later paper (Lookahead Reasoning) measured accuracy drops for the same scoring rule without the tight budget.

#### (b) Problem
- [V] "LRM inference, and the reasoning that it embeds, is highly tolerant of approximations: complex tasks are typically broken down into simpler steps, each of which brings utility based on the semantic insight it provides for downstream steps rather than the exact tokens it generates." — [arXiv HTML v2, abstract](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07891v2)
- [V] Three stated properties: "Intermediate steps are easier than end-to-end reasoning"; "Reasoning progress depends on insights, not exact tokens"; "Occasional mistakes can be corrected via self-reflection" ("unlike LLM inference where all output tokens contribute to the final answer, in LRM inference, only the tokens generated after the thinking tokens determine the final outcome") — [arXiv HTML v2, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07891v2)

#### (c) Mechanism
- [V] Unit = reasoning step: "SpecReason offloads easier or less critical reasoning steps—defined as semantically self-contained units such as complete sentences or logical steps—to a smaller, faster speculator model. Each step is decoded in two stages: (1) the lightweight speculator proposes the next reasoning step based on the current context, and (2) the base model evaluates the proposed step for semantic utility. If the step is accepted, SpecReason proceeds to the next step; otherwise, SpecReason falls back to the base model to regenerate the step." — [arXiv HTML v2, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07891v2)
- [V] Score and threshold: "after each speculated reasoning step, the base model is prompted to generate a single-token utility score (e.g., an integer from 0 to 9) indicating the quality of the step. If the utility score is above a static acceptance threshold (e.g., score $\geq 7$), the speculated reasoning step is accepted; otherwise, it is discarded and regenerated by the base model." Thresholds studied: "3, 5, 7, and 9 out of 9" (Fig. 5) — [arXiv HTML v2, §4.1, Fig. 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07891v2)
- [V] Second knob: "forcing the first $n$ reasoning steps to be decoded by the base model" ($n$ from 0 to 40: "accuracy increases from 33.2% to 37.3% while the latency increases from 270.4s to 292.6s") — [arXiv HTML v2, §4.1 and §5.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07891v2)
- [V] Cost of verification: "SpecReason evaluates each speculated step in a single prefill-only pass of the base model. The verification prompt is templated to reuse most of the CoT prefix, so each verification requires prefilling only $\sim$70 new tokens. Since short-prefill forward passes are memory-bound, the overhead is comparable to decoding just 1–2 tokens" — [arXiv HTML v2, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07891v2)
- [V] Execution: models colocated on the same GPU, sequential turns ("the small and base models take turns"); "They do not share any internal model states–only the token IDs of the generated reasoning steps are managed and shared by SpecReason. If a speculative step is rejected, the corresponding KV cache entries are discarded." — [arXiv HTML v2, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07891v2)
- [V] Combination with token-level SD ("SpecReason+Decode"): "first applies step-level speculative reasoning to draft and verify reasoning steps. If a step is rejected and regenerated by the base model, standard token-level speculative decoding can be applied during the base model regeneration to further accelerate decoding." — [arXiv HTML v2, §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07891v2)
- [V] Nothing is trained (prompted base model as critic). The base model's 0–9 scores correlate with a PRM (Math-Shepherd) on AIME steps: "a strong correlation between the base model’s and the PRM’s assessments, particularly for lower-quality reasoning steps" (Fig. 7) — [arXiv HTML v2, §5.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07891v2)

#### (d) Lossy in what sense; guarantees; large model on every token?
- [V] "speculative decoding is an exact optimization: it relies on token-level equivalence between the small and base models ... In contrast, SpecReason explicitly leverages the approximation tolerance inherent in reasoning: it targets thinking tokens ... where semantic alignment, rather than token-level equivalence, is sufficient." - [V] ConfSpec (2026) frames the remaining problem of step-level methods as "a long-standing trade-off among accuracy, inference speed, and resource efficiency" and removes the need for the large model (or an external judge) to verify every step — [arXiv abs 2602.18447](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18447)

#### (h) Relations
- [V] Related-work positioning by Lookahead Reasoning: "Speculative Thinking [35] uses LRMs to guide a smaller draft model, while SpecReason [9] accelerates reasoning by employing the large model to score the output of a small model, thereby deciding whether to accept its generated step. ### Inferences
- SpecReason is the "large model as judge" counterpart of RSD's "external PRM as judge": same control flow (draft step → score → threshold → regenerate), different scorer. - The positive accuracy deltas depend on the 8192-token budget; with a 32K budget and the same scoring rule, Lookahead Reasoning's table shows drops of roughly 2 to 12.5 points. A writer should present "+0.4–9.0%" as budget-conditional.

## Lookahead Reasoning: step-level parallel speculation with a semantic verifier

### Takeaway
Lookahead Reasoning (Fu et al., NeurIPS 2025) keeps the large model generating every step but removes the sequential dependency between steps: a draft model writes several future steps, the target generates its own version of each step in one batched pass conditioned on the draft prefixes, and a verifier (a 7B LLM judge by default) accepts the draft step when it is semantically equivalent to the target's step. It is a latency method that spends more FLOPs; it argues that token-level SD has an algorithmic speedup ceiling of $1/(1-\alpha)$ and reports that combining it with n-gram SD lifts the peak speedup from 1.4× to 2.1× (GSM8K) with accuracy within about +1.0/−2.1 points of the target.

### Cited Findings

#### (a) Identity
- [V] Title "Scaling Speculative Decoding with Lookahead Reasoning"; authors Yichao Fu (UCSD), Rui Ge (Shanghai Jiao Tong University), Zelei Shao (UIUC), Zhijie Deng (Shanghai Jiao Tong University), Hao Zhang (UCSD); arXiv:2506.19830, "Submitted on 24 Jun 2025" — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19830), [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19830)

#### (b) Problem and the argument about the limit of token-level SD
- [V] "Token-level speculative decoding (SD) helps, but its benefit is capped, because the chance that an entire $\gamma$-token guess is correct falls exponentially as $\gamma$ grows. This means allocating more compute for longer token drafts faces an algorithmic ceiling – making the speedup modest and hardware-agnostic." — [arXiv HTML, abstract](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19830)
- [V] Formal version: SD speedup $g(\gamma)=\frac{1-\alpha^{\gamma+1}}{(1-\alpha)\,(1+c\,\gamma)}$ with $\alpha$ the per-token acceptance rate and $c=T_q/T_p$; "for all $c\geq 0$ and $\gamma\geq 0$, the speedup $g(\gamma)$ is upper-bounded by $1/(1-\alpha)$, a limit approached only in the idealized case where $c=0$ ... this algorithmic ceiling implies that the acceleration gains from token-level speculative decoding do not scale with improvements in hardware" — [arXiv HTML, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19830)
- [V] Empirical anchor: "in a real profiling, we observe SD’s speedup caps at 1.4x (Figure 2)" — [arXiv HTML, §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19830)

#### (c) Mechanism
- [V] One cycle (sync version, Algorithm 1): (1) the draft model $q$ autoregressively generates $\gamma$ candidate steps $\hat{s}_0,\dots,\hat{s}_{\gamma-1}$, "we simply use ‘\n\n’ as the step break"; (2) "once all $\gamma$ draft steps are available, target model $p$ generates following steps $s_{0},\dots,s_{\gamma}$ accordingly in parallel", each $s_j$ conditioned on the prefix plus the draft steps $\hat s_0..\hat s_{j-1}$; (3) a verifier $V(s_j,\hat s_j)\to\{\text{True},\text{False}\}$ "assesses whether if $\hat{s}_j$ is an acceptable substitute for $s_j$, i.e. whether they are semantic similar"; $j^{*}=\min(\{j\mid V(s_{j},\hat{s}_{j})=\text{False}\}\cup\{\gamma\})$; output $=(\bigoplus_{k=0}^{j^{*}-1}\hat{s}_{k})\oplus s_{j^{*}}$ ("Append verified drafts + decisive target") — [arXiv HTML, §3.1, Alg. 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19830)
- [V] Async version: the target starts generating $s_j$ "as soon as its required prefix ... becomes available from the draft model"; multi-branch drafting with width $W$ (candidate sequences grow as $W^{\gamma}$) — [arXiv HTML, §3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19830)
- [V] Verifier choices compared: LLM-as-a-Judge (Qwen2.5 7B/32B), embedding similarity (all-mpnet-base-v2, thresholds 0.85/0.95), target-model 0–9 scoring as in SpecReason (thresholds 7/9), random acceptance. Default: "Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct [11] is employed as the judgement model. A deliberately designed judge prompt template allows our model to assess the semantic alignment between two sentences in just one prefill pass" — [arXiv HTML, §4, §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19830)
- [V] Speedup analysis: sync $f_{sync}(k_{1})=\frac{1-\alpha_{1}^{k_{1}}}{(1-\alpha_{1})\,(1-c_{1}+c_{1}\,k_{1})}$; async, slow draft ($k_1\ge\lceil 1/c_1\rceil$): $S_{1}=\frac{1}{c_{1}+(1-c_{1})(1-\alpha_{1})}$; combined with token-level SD: "their speedups multiply, yielding a combined speedup $h(k_{1},k_{2})=f(k_{1})\times g(k_{2})$" — [arXiv HTML, §3.2–3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19830)
- [V] Theorem (hybrid optimality): "Under the conditions of acceptance rates ($0.52<\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}<0.8$), reasonably efficient draft models ($c_{1}<\frac{1}{3},c_{2}<\frac{1}{5}$), and sufficient parallelism budget $M\geq 16$, the maximum speedup $h(k_{1},k_{2})$ is achieved if and only if a hybrid strategy is employed, meaning both $k_{1}\geq 2$ and $k_{2}\geq 2$." — [arXiv HTML, §3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19830)
- [V] Nothing is trained.

#### (d) Lossy in what sense; guarantees; large model on every token?
- [V] Lossy through the verifier: "While an ideal semantic verifier ensures no accuracy loss, practical ones balance compute cost against judgment accuracy ... a looser verification may boost draft acceptance (and speedup) but risks accuracy drop from erroneous steps." No distributional guarantee — [arXiv HTML, §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19830)
- [V] The target model generates every step itself (in parallel); compute is added, not saved: "Speculative decoding (SD) mitigates this token-level dependency by spending additional FLOPs to shorten the critical path of generation"; "Additional FLOPs can draft more (or deeper) steps instead of lengthening token guesses"; Figure 1 caption: accepting two draft steps costs "a lowered latency (e.g., $2t+T$) compared to the sequential target calls in autoregressive decoding (e.g., $3T$)" — [arXiv HTML, §1, Fig. 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19830)

### Inferences
- Lookahead Reasoning is the member of the family that is closest to classical speculative decoding in control flow (draft, parallel target pass, accept the longest verified prefix, target's own output as the correction), with "equal token" replaced by "semantically equivalent step according to a judge". Unlike RSD/SpecReason, acceptance is defined relative to what the target itself would have written, not relative to an absolute quality score; the paper's ablation shows this matters (scoring verifier loses 6–33 points, equivalence judge 0–2).
- It saves no large-model compute: every output step has been generated by the target in some batch slot, and rejected lookahead slots are wasted work. The claim "scales better with additional GPU throughput" is the flip side of that.

## Speculative Thinking, Speculative Chain-of-Thought (SCoT), SpecSearch, Guided Speculative Inference (GSI), SPECS and related speculative test-time-scaling methods

### Takeaway
These five methods occupy different points: Speculative Thinking (COLM 2025) is a training-free heuristic where the *small* model is the main writer and the large model takes over 20–125 tokens after reflection cues; SCoT (ACL 2025 per Semantic Scholar) speculates whole chains of thought (5 parallel drafts from a LoRA-aligned small model, one forward pass of a fine-tuned target picks one or declares all wrong); SpecSearch (ICML 2025) speculates thoughts inside PRM-guided tree search with an adaptive threshold estimated from the large model's past thoughts; GSI (ICLR 2026) and SPECS (NeurIPS 2026) add the large model's log-likelihood ratio to the PRM reward so that selection among small-model candidates provably approximates the reward-tilted large-model distribution as the number of candidates grows. Reported gains are 1.3–2.9× latency for chain-level drafting, up to 2.12× over token-level SD inside tree search, and 18–28% latency for the test-time-scaling variants.

#### Speculative Thinking (Yang et al.)

#### Speculative Chain-of-Thought (SCoT, Wang et al.)
- [V] (a) Title "Efficient Reasoning for LLMs through Speculative Chain-of-Thought"; authors Jikai Wang, Juntao Li, Jianye Hou, Bowen Yan, Lijun Wu, Min Zhang; affiliations Soochow University (Juntao Li, Min Zhang; first author's affiliation line not captured, corresponding author Juntao Li at Soochow University), CUHK (Jianye Hou), Meta Stone (Bowen Yan), Shanghai AI Laboratory (Lijun Wu); arXiv:2504.19095, "Submitted on 27 Apr 2025 (v1), last revised 25 May 2025 (this version, v2)"; code https://github.com/Jikai0Wang/Speculative_CoT — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19095), [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19095v2)
- [V-agg] Venue: Semantic Scholar lists "Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics" (2025). Not confirmed on the ACL Anthology in this session — [Semantic Scholar API record for arXiv:2504.19095](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/arXiv:2504.19095?fields=title,venue,year)
- [V] (b) Problem: "Fundamentally, employing large reasoning models to handle simple tasks is a waste ... why not prioritize this lightweight alternative and only invoke the large model for deeper thinking when the problem exceeds the capability threshold of the small one?" — [arXiv HTML v2, §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19095v2)
- [V] (c) Unit = the whole chain of thought. Control flow: the draft model produces $n$ CoT drafts in parallel, $[T_{d}^{1},\dots,T_{d}^{n}]=M_{d}([q,\dots,q])$, with temperature sampling; a special option $T_{n+1}$ means "all CoT drafts are wrong"; the target picks with one forward pass: $index=\arg\max_{i\in\mathcal{V}}P_{M}(i|S(q,T_{\leq n+1})),\ \mathcal{V}=\{1,2,...,n+1\}$; if $index<n+1$ "we directly let the target model generate the final answer based on $q$ and $T_{index}$"; if $index=n+1$ "we instruct the target model to rethink the question and generate the final answer" — [arXiv HTML v2, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19095v2)
- [V] (c) Training: (i) "thinking behavior alignment" of the draft model with LoRA on "1500 samples from the GSM8K training set" of target-generated CoTs, loss $\mathcal{L}_{Draft}=-\frac{1}{l}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\log p_{M_{d}}(y_{i}|x_{\leq m},y_{<i})$ (motivation: "the average CoT length of the 1.5B model on the GSM8K dataset is more than 4 times that of the 32B model"); (ii) LoRA fine-tuning of the target as selector on "500 samples from the GSM8K training set", loss $\mathcal{L}_{Target}=\min\{-\log p_{M}(y|S(q,T_{\leq n+1}))\,|\,y\in\mathcal{Y}\}$ where $\mathcal{Y}$ is the set of correct drafts; "The fine-tuned target model is only used for CoT selection." — [arXiv HTML v2, §3.3–3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19095v2)
- [V] (c) Hyper-parameters: "The number of CoT drafts $n$ is set to 5"; "temperature for sampling to 0.6 in CoT drafting"; LoRA rank 8 (16 for D-Llama-70B); learning rate 5e-5 (draft) and 1e-4 (target); "maximum draft length is set to 5000"; max CoT length 20480 — [arXiv HTML v2, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19095v2)
- [V] (d) Lossy: the final answer is conditioned on a small-model chain whenever one is selected; no guarantee. Large model: one selection forward pass over all drafts on every query, plus its own full reasoning only when it selects "all wrong"; "the CoT tokens generated by the target model account for 2.8% $\sim$ 45.9% of the total CoT tokens" — [arXiv HTML v2, §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19095v2)
- [V] (e) Setup: targets Deepseek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B and Deepseek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B; drafts Deepseek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B and Deepseek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B; "a single A100-PCIE-40GB GPU for the 1.5B model and 4 A100-PCIE-40GB GPUs for the 32B model"; 8B on one H800-80GB, 70B on 4 H800-80GB; datasets GSM8K, MATH, GaoKao-En-2023, CollegeMath, Olympiad; baselines vanilla target, TokenSkip (compression ratio 0.5), SD with max draft length 5 — [arXiv HTML v2, §4.1, §4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19095v2)
- [V] (f) Results: abstract "SCoT reduces reasoning latency by 48% $\sim$ 66% and 21% $\sim$ 49% for Deepseek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B and Deepseek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B while achieving near-target-model-level performance." Table 1 speed-up ratio of CoT latency (GSM8K / MATH / GaoKao / CollegeMath / Olympiad): SCoT(32B+1.5B) 2.26 / 2.92 / 2.31 / 2.18 / 1.90; SCoT(70B+8B) 1.96 / 1.83 / 1.70 / 1.26 / 1.36. Versus token-level SD with the same (unaligned) draft models (Table 3, latency speed-up): Qwen 1.87 / 1.41 / 1.32 / 1.34 / 0.59; Llama 1.63 / 1.49 / 1.35 / 1.61 / 0.58 (SD is slower than autoregressive decoding on Olympiad) — [arXiv HTML v2, abstract, Tables 1 and 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19095v2)
- [V] (f) Accuracy is given only as a bar chart (Fig. 4): "SCoT maintains near-target-model performance levels on all datasets ... For CollegeMath, SCoT even achieves a better accuracy compared to the target model." Exact accuracy numbers: not found in text — [arXiv HTML v2, §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19095v2)
- [V] (g) Error detection is the weak point: "without fine-tuning, $M$ never selects the “All reasoning paths are wrong.” option"; after fine-tuning "the model can correctly predict the labels for about half of the Class 2 samples" (50 of 500 GSM8K test samples where no draft is correct). Limitations: needs a pre-trained draft model of the same family; "the speedup ratio of SCoT on the Llama group is lower ... because the 8B draft model is still a bit large for the 70B target model". Motivating observation is best-of-5 of the small model: "if we allow the 1.5B model to provide 5 answers ... and select the best one ..., its overall accuracy on GSM8K is close to that of the 32B model" — [arXiv HTML v2, §1, §3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19095v2)

#### SpecSearch (Wang et al., ICML 2025)
- [V] (a) Title "Accelerating Large Language Model Reasoning via Speculative Search"; authors Zhihai Wang, Jie Wang, Jilai Pan, Xilin Xia, Huiling Zhen, Mingxuan Yuan, Jianye Hao, Feng Wu; first author affiliation "MoE Key Laboratory of Brain-inspired Intelligent Perception and Cognition, University of Science and Technology of China" ("This work was done when Zhihai Wang was an intern at Huawei"; co-authors at Noah’s Ark Lab, Huawei Technologies and Tianjin University); arXiv:2505.02865 (v1 3 May 2025, v2 24 May 2025); arXiv comment "Accepted by ICML2025" — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02865), [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02865v2). PMLR entry (from web search, not opened): [proceedings.mlr.press/v267/wang25di.html](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/wang25di.html) [V-agg]. Code URL: not found.
- [V] (b) Problem: tree-search reasoning (Tree-of-Thoughts, beam search, MCTS with a PRM) is slow; "Thought generation dominates computation time in tree-search-based reasoning, consuming over 91% of total runtime"; token-level SD "can be poorly aligned with optimizing search-based reasoning approaches" — [arXiv HTML v2, §2, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02865v2)
- [V] (c) Unit = a thought (tree node, "more than fifty tokens"). "draft-evaluate-reject-correct paradigm": a small model drafts multiple thoughts; the search's own thought evaluator (a PRM $V:\mathbb{Z}\to[0,1]$) scores them; thoughts below a step-wise threshold are rejected; "the rejected thoughts are corrected using a lossless token-level speculative decoding method" with the large model (so the correction has the large model's distribution). Implementation: "We utilize the small model to generate 2*N thoughts in parallel"; "If the reward surpasses the threshold, the thought is retained; otherwise, it is discarded. If more than N thoughts are retained, we select the top N" — [arXiv HTML v2, §4.1–4.2, App. G.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02865v2)
- [V] (c) Threshold: exponential moving average of an estimate of the large model's thought quality from its previous (corrected) thoughts: $\hat{\beta}^{(k+1)}=\theta\hat{\beta}^{(k)}+(1-\theta)\Theta\left(\mathcal{V}_{p}^{(k)}\right)$, "$\Theta$ represents a nonparametric estimation method, such as the sample mean, the confidence upper bound ... or the maximum value"; "we set the EMA weight $\theta$ in SpecSearch to 0.9". Nothing is trained — [arXiv HTML v2, §4.3, §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02865v2)
- [V] (d) Guarantee (in PRM-score terms only, under assumptions): "Undegraded Quality Condition" $\mathbb{E}_{z\sim G_{s}}[V(z)]\geq\mu_{p}$; Theorem 4.3: quality is preserved "if ... $\beta^{(k)}\geq\mu_{p}^{(k)},\;\forall k$"; Theorems 4.5–4.6 give probability lower bounds under a normality assumption for thought qualities and the assumption that large-model quality decays across steps ($\mu_{p}^{(k)}\leq\gamma\mu_{p}^{(k-1)}$). Large model: skipped for accepted thoughts, used (with token-level SD) for rejected ones — [arXiv HTML v2, §4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02865v2)
- [V] (e) Setup: "quantized Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct ... as large and small models ..., along with quantized Llama3-70B-Instruct and Llama3-8B-Instruct"; OpenR settings "tree width of 6, tree depth of 50, MATH-psa as the process reward model (PRM) ... beam search as the main search algorithm" (beam size 2); 100 random samples each from GSM8K and MATH; baselines AR (large model) and SpS (lossless speculative sampling). Hardware and temperature: not found in the text read — [arXiv HTML v2, §5, App. G.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02865v2)
- [V] (f) Results (Table 1; accuracy %, latency s, speedup vs AR, speedup vs SpS): Qwen, MATH-100: AR 87.00 / 275.78; SpS 88.00 / 141.55 (1.95×); SpecSearch 87.00 / 82.35 (3.35× vs AR, 1.72× vs SpS). Qwen, GSM8K-100: AR 97.00 / 138.24; SpS 97.00 / 69.43; SpecSearch 96.00 / 48.18 (2.87×, 1.44×). Llama, MATH-100: AR 62.00 / 170.84; SpS 61.00 / 134.34; SpecSearch 64.00 / 129.65 (1.32×, 1.04×). Llama, GSM8K-100: AR 87.00 / 90.04; SpS 86.00 / 64.29; SpecSearch 88.00 / 45.34 (1.99×, 1.42×) — [arXiv HTML v2, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02865v2)
- [V] (f) The headline "up to 2.12×" is the speedup over lossless SpS (not over autoregressive decoding) in the Math-Shepherd-PRM setting of Table 2 on GSM8K-100: SpecSearch 94.00% / 30.32 s (4.11× vs AR, 2.12× vs SpS) against AR 96.00% / 124.76 s and SpS 95.00% / 64.17 s — a 2-point accuracy drop in that row — [arXiv HTML v2, Table 2 and Experiment 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02865v2)
- [V] (g) Failure mode named by the authors: "the degradation primarily arises from certain misleading thoughts that deceive the PRM." Evaluation on 100-question subsets (1 question = 1 point) — [arXiv HTML v2, §4.1, §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02865v2)
- [V] (h) Authors' claim: "To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to well generalize speculative execution to TSB reasoning"; contrast with TreeBoN, which "treats fixed-length token sequences as speculative units" and "rejects a fixed proportion of token sequences using a preset threshold" — [arXiv HTML v2, §2, App. F](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02865v2)

#### Guided Speculative Inference (GSI, Geuter et al., ICLR 2026)
- [V] (a) Title "Guided Speculative Inference for Efficient Test-Time Alignment of LLMs"; authors Jonathan Geuter (Harvard SEAS; Kempner Institute), Youssef Mroueh (IBM Research), David Alvarez-Melis (Harvard SEAS; Kempner Institute); arXiv:2506.04118 (v1 4 Jun 2025, v3 27 Apr 2026); arXiv comment "41 pages, 11 figures. Published at ICLR 2026"; OpenReview record "ICLR 2026 Poster" (earlier "ES-FoMo III Spotlight", ICML 2025 workshop); code https://github.com/j-geuter/GSI — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04118), [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04118v3), [OpenReview forum](https://openreview.net/forum?id=miNzDqDENd)
- [V] (b) Problem: RSD "lacks theoretical guarantees on distributional fidelity"; goal: approximately sample from the reward-tilted target policy $\pi_{\beta,B}(y\mid x)=\frac{\pi_{B}(y\mid x)\exp(\beta\,r(x,y))}{Z_{\beta,B}(x)}$ using samples from the small model — [arXiv HTML v3, §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04118v3)
- [V] (c) Unit = reasoning step ("drafts are generated until ... a double line break \n\n"). Algorithm 1 per step: sample $n$ steps $y_i\sim\pi_S$; compute tilted rewards $\tilde{r}_{i}=r(x,(y,y_{i}^{t}))+\frac{1}{\beta}\left(\log\pi_{B}(y_{i}^{t}\mid x,y)-\log\pi_{S}(y_{i}^{t}\mid x,y)\right)$; sample $i^{*}\sim\mathrm{softmax}(\beta\tilde{r}_{1},...,\beta\tilde{r}_{n})$; if $\tilde{r}_{i^{*}}\geq u$ append the small model's step; else sample $n$ steps from $\pi_B$, score with the PRM and pick by soft best-of-$n$, $j^{*}\sim\mathrm{softmax}(\beta r_{1},...,\beta r_{n})$ — [arXiv HTML v3, Alg. 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04118v3)
- [V] (c) Values: "$\beta=20$ ..., $u=0.5$ ..., temperature = 0.7, and top_p = 1.0. We set the threshold in RSD to $0.7$"; PRM "Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B", rewards in $[0,1]$; nothing trained — [arXiv HTML v3, §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04118v3)
- [V] (d) Guarantee (Theorem 1): under the coverage assumption $C_{\infty}(x):=\sup_{y:\pi_{B}(y\mid x)>0}\frac{\pi_{B}(y\mid x)}{\pi_{S}(y\mid x)}<\infty$, if $n\geq\frac{\left(\chi^{2}(\pi_{B}\,\|\,\pi_{S})+1\right)e^{2\beta\|r\|_{\infty}}-1}{e^{\epsilon}-1}$ then $\mathrm{KL}\bigl(\pi_{\beta,B}(\cdot\mid x)\,\|\,\tilde{\pi}_{\textnormal{GSI}}(\cdot\mid x)\bigr)\leq\epsilon$; Theorem 2 (informal): the expected golden-reward gap to $\pi_{\beta,B}$ goes to 0 "at rate $\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{n})$". The rejection threshold $u$ "is not required for the distributional guarantee ... it improves performance empirically". The target is the *reward-tilted* large model, not the large model itself, so GSI is still lossy with respect to $\pi_B$ — [arXiv HTML v3, §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04118v3)
- [V] (d) The large model scores every candidate step: "$\log\pi_{B}(y_{i}\mid x)$ can be efficiently computed in parallel by summing over logprobabilities obtained from a single forward pass, as opposed to autoregressive generation" — its forward pass is paid on all $n$ drafts of every step (latency saved, large-model compute not saved) — [arXiv HTML v3, §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04118v3)
- [V] (e) Setup: Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B-Instruct → Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct; Qwen3-1.7B → Qwen3-14B (thinking disabled); vLLM; "Each model is hosted on its own GPU; we evaluated on NVIDIA A100, H100, and H200 GPUs"; MATH500, OlympiadBench (OE_TO_maths_en_COMP), Minerva Math, GSM8K, MMLU-STEM, 500 samples per dataset (Minerva 272), 3 seeds, 95% CIs; baselines RSD (re-implemented, threshold 0.7), S-BoN with $\pi_S$, S-BoN with $\pi_B$ — [arXiv HTML v3, §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04118v3)
- [V] (f) Abstract: "our method achieves higher accuracy than standard soft best-of-$n$ with $\pi_{S}$ and reward-guided speculative decoding (Liao et al., 2025), and in certain settings even outperforms soft best-of-$n$ with $\pi_{B}$, while reducing end-to-end latency by up to $28\%$." — [arXiv HTML v3, abstract](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04118v3)
- [V] (f) Average accuracy over the five benchmarks, Qwen2.5-Math (Table 2), n=4: GSI 59.5 ± 0.7; RSD 57.8 ± 0.9; S-BoN(small) 55.5 ± 1.2; S-BoN(base) 61.3 ± 0.8. n=16: 60.0 / 58.7 / 57.8 / 62.1. Qwen3 (Table 3), n=4: GSI 65.2 ± 0.4; RSD 60.8 ± 1.5; S-BoN(small) 56.6 ± 1.3; S-BoN(base) 68.9 ± 0.9. n=16: 66.9 / 60.9 / 58.5 / 69.4 — [arXiv HTML v3, Tables 2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04118v3)
- [V] (f) Latency (Table 1): Qwen2.5-Math on H100, n=4: GSI 0.43 ± 0.03 s/step, 76.7% accepted, 2.33 steps/s; RSD 0.34, 94.9%, 2.94; S-BoN(small) 0.32, 3.12; S-BoN(base) 0.57, 1.75. Qwen3 on A100, n=16: GSI 1.21 s/step, 91.5% accepted, 0.83 steps/s; RSD 0.96, 98.2%, 1.04; S-BoN(small) 1.01, 0.99; S-BoN(base) 1.82, 0.55. Text: "on Qwen3 with $n=16$, GSI achieves a $51\%$ increased throughput in terms of steps per second, with only $3\%$ in relative performance degradation ... up to $28\%$ reduced end-to-end latency compared to the target model" — [arXiv HTML v3, Table 1, §5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04118v3)
- [V] (g) Caveats: the comparison baseline is soft best-of-$n$ with the *base* model (test-time scaling), not a single greedy run of the base model; GSI is below S-BoN(base) on average (e.g. 65.2 vs 68.9); "our PRM consumes a significant amount of end-to-end runtime"; "inference times rely on many factors and can be unreliable"; tilted rewards "can also sometimes be misleading" ("GSI rejects steps that are correct if the base model tends to word them very differently from the draft model", Fig. 3) — [arXiv HTML v3, §5.1–5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04118v3)
- [V] (h) Relations: "RSD, SPECS, and GSI all have in common that they operate on reasoning steps of reasoning models". Follow-up: "Multiple Importance Sampling for Guided Speculative Inference" (Rowan and Khisti, arXiv:2610.33368, "Presented at the IEEE ISIT 2026 Workshop on Information Theory for Large Language Models (IT4LLM)") — [arXiv HTML v3, §2, §5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04118v3), [arXiv abs 2610.33368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33368)

#### SPECS (Cemri et al., NeurIPS 2026)
- [V] (a) arXiv title "$\texttt{SPECS}$: Faster Test-Time Scaling through Speculative Drafts"; the v2 full text and the NeurIPS record carry the longer title "SPECS: Faster Test-Time Scaling through Speculative Drafts and Dynamic Switching"; authors Mert Cemri, Nived Rajaraman, Rishabh Tiwari, Xiaoxuan Liu, Kurt Keutzer, Ion Stoica, Kannan Ramchandran (UC Berkeley), Ahmad Beirami (Google DeepMind), Ziteng Sun (Google Research); arXiv:2506.15733 (v1 15 Jun 2025, v2 18 Feb 2026); code https://github.com/mert-cemri/SPECS — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15733), [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15733v2)
- [V] Venue history from OpenReview records: "ES-FoMo III" (ICML 2025 workshop); "Submitted to ICLR 2026" (rejected, forum 5XixaecZ8W); "NeurIPS 2026 poster" (forum 24r1YmB7zX) — [OpenReview NeurIPS 2026 forum](https://openreview.net/forum?id=24r1YmB7zX), [OpenReview ICLR 2026 forum](https://openreview.net/forum?id=5XixaecZ8W)
- [V] (b) Problem: "Current test-time scaling methods primarily optimize for accuracy based on total compute resources (FLOPs), often overlooking latency constraints." Research question: "Can we design test-time scaling methods which optimize for inference latency while maintaining accuracy?" — [arXiv HTML v2, abstract and §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15733v2)
- [V] (c) Built on PRM-guided beam search over blocks (reasoning steps). Algorithm 1: generator $\pi_{\mathrm{gen}}$ initialised to the *target* model ("Generation begins with target model"); each iteration draws $n$ candidate blocks from $\pi_{\mathrm{gen}}$, selects one with SubSample, then "if $\max_{i\in[n]}r(Y_{i}|\textsf{tr})>\tau$: $\pi_{\mathrm{gen}}\leftarrow\pi_{\mathrm{draft}}$" ("Switch to generating from draft model if reward signal $r$ is large"). SubSample score: $S_{i}=\log\left(\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{target}}(Y_{i}|\textsf{tr})}{\pi_{\mathrm{gen}}(Y_{i}|\textsf{tr})}\right)+\beta r(Y_{i}|\textsf{tr})$, then "Sample $i^{\star}\in[n]$ w.p. $P(i^{\star}=i)\propto e^{S_{i}}$" — [arXiv HTML v2, Alg. 1–2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15733v2)
- [V] (c) Why latency drops although the target scores every draft: "evaluating a reasoning block with a target model (i.e., computing logits of each token) is much faster than generating a reasoning block from the same model, despite taking the same number of FLOPs" — [arXiv HTML v2, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15733v2)
- [V] (c) Observation behind the switching direction: "Generating blocks from the draft model does not hurt accuracy when the PRM score of a partial trace is high" (Observation 2; first 8 steps from the target, traces bucketed at PRM score 0.5) — [arXiv HTML v2, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15733v2)

### Inferences
- Two families of acceptance signals coexist at the step level: (i) an absolute quality score (PRM in RSD/SpecSearch, large-model self-score in SpecReason, selector in SCoT) and (ii) a relative signal that involves the large model's likelihood of the draft (GSI, SPECS) or its own continuation (Lookahead Reasoning). Family (ii) keeps the large model in the loop on every step (prefill/scoring) and comes with distributional statements; family (i) can skip the large model entirely but only has reward-level statements.
- GSI and SPECS use the same importance-reweighting identity ($\pi_B e^{\beta r}\propto \pi_S e^{\beta r+\log(\pi_B/\pi_S)}$) and differ mainly in control flow: GSI starts with the draft and falls back to the target below a threshold; SPECS starts with the target and switches to the draft once rewards are high.
- "Speculative Thinking" is not speculation in the draft-then-verify sense: nothing is verified, and the trigger is a lexical cue. It belongs with heuristic step-level routing, with the small model as the default writer.
- SCoT's saving is bounded by the fact that the large model must read all five drafts (up to 5000 tokens each) and still writes the final answer.

## Token-level routing and collaborative decoding between a small and a large model (R2R, CITER, CoSD, Co-LLM, edge-cloud variants)

### Takeaway
Token-level routers let the small model write most tokens and hand individual tokens to the large model: R2R (NeurIPS 2025) trains a 56M-parameter router on SLM logits, hidden state and token embedding to detect "path-divergent" tokens and uses the 32B model for 11–15% of tokens (2.8× wall-clock speedup over R1-32B at 92% of its average accuracy, but slightly *more* total FLOPs than the large model alone because of KV-cache catch-up); CITER (COLM 2025) trains an MLP router on the SLM hidden state with preference labels that include long-horizon effects; Co-LLM (ACL 2024) learns a linear deferral head by marginal likelihood; CoSD (ICML 2025) is a different animal: the assistant model verifies every draft token in parallel and replaces it by a confidence rule, with the aim of fusing knowledge of similarly sized models rather than saving compute.

#### R2R (Fu et al., NeurIPS 2025)

- [V] (b) Problem: "only a small fraction of tokens genuinely diverge reasoning paths between LLMs and SLMs. Most generated tokens are either identical or exhibit neutral differences, such as minor variations in abbreviations or expressions." Measurement: "Across 2,094 queries totaling 7.6M tokens generated by the 32B LLM, the 1.5B SLM differs on only 11% of tokens"; divergent rate 4.9% overall (math 2.8%, code 4.7%, QA 9.7%; Table 1). - [V] (c) Who decides, on what signal: a neural router reading only SLM-side features, "a lightweight, six-layer feed-forward network (FFN) with 56M parameters. - [V] (c) Routing rule at inference: "the neural router estimates divergence probability at each generation step using SLM outputs. When this probability exceeds a predefined threshold $p_{\text{th}}$, the LLM is invoked to correct the current output token." Workflow: "the SLM decoding a single token ... The router generates a divergence probability ... If the probability surpasses the predefined threshold, the LLM is activated to extend the sequence by one token ... to replace the SLM’s predicted token." Data: "7.6 million routing labels in approximately 2.3 days on 8 A800 GPUs ... queries from the Bespoke-Stratos [16] dataset" (448 GPU hours, Table 9). - [V] (d) Lossy: the output follows neither model's distribution; the "quality guarantee" (App. E) holds only for the oracle full path-following labelling with a perfect quality verifier ("the mixed-generated token sequence is guaranteed to achieve the same quality as its LLM-only counterpart, as it always remains on a path that could achieve LLM-only quality"), not for the learned router — [arXiv HTML v2, §3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21600v2)

- [V] (a) Title "Speculate, then Collaborate: Fusing Knowledge of Language Models during Decoding"; authors Ziyao Wang, Muneeza Azmat, Ang Li, Raya Horesh, Mikhail Yurochkin; first author affiliation "Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, University of Maryland, College Park" ("Work started and partially done during Ziyao’s internship at IBM Research"); arXiv:2502.08020 (v1 11 Feb 2025, v2 19 Mar 2025); OpenReview record "ICML 2025 poster" (earlier rejected at ICLR 2025). - [V] (b) Problem: test-time *knowledge fusion* of LLMs with complementary strengths: "no single LLM excels uniformly across all domains"; efficiency is secondary — [arXiv HTML v2, §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08020v2)
- [V] (c) Control flow: the draft model $\mathcal{M}_p$ generates $K$ tokens autoregressively (greedy); the assistant model $\mathcal{M}_q$ "verifies the draft tokens by producing an assistant sequence" in parallel (one forward pass over the draft); tokens are scanned in order; a draft token is replaced by the assistant token when the verification rule fires; "When a replacement occurs, all subsequent draft tokens are discarded, and a new draft sequence is generated starting from the replaced token." Works across tokenizers by decode/re-encode — [arXiv HTML v2, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08020v2)
- [V] (c) Rule-based verification: replace iff all three hold: $\widetilde{x}_{t+i}\neq\hat{x}_{t+i}$; $\mathcal{M}_{p}(\widetilde{x}_{t+i})<\alpha$; $\mathcal{M}_{q}(\hat{x}_{t+i})>\beta\cdot\mathcal{M}_{p}(\widetilde{x}_{t+i})$; "we set $\alpha=0.5$ and $\beta=0.5$". Tree-based verification: a decision tree $\mathcal{T}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\{0,1\}$ on the two probabilities, trained on ground-truth continuations (label 0 if the draft token is right, 1 if only the assistant is right, sample dropped if neither); "we randomly select three samples from the AlpacaEval dataset to train the decision tree" — [arXiv HTML v2, §3.2, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08020v2)
- [V] (d) The second model's forward pass is paid for every token (parallel verification as in SD); the rule is a confidence comparison with no guarantee: "it cannot guarantee the replaced assistant token is always better than the discarded draft one" — [arXiv HTML v2, App. A](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08020v2)
- [V] (e) Setup: six pairs, mostly equal-size (Mistral 7B variants; Llama-3 8B variants; Mistral 7B + Mistral Math 7B; TinyLlama + Llama 2 Chat "Capacity Imbalance"; Llama 2 Chat + WizardMath / DeepSeek Coder with different tokenizers); benchmarks MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval, Hellaswag, TruthfulQA (tinyBenchmarks except HumanEval); baselines speculative decoding, average decoding, Co-LLM. - [V] (f) Results (Table 2, average over five benchmarks): pair 1: draft 50.79, assistant 50.58, SD 46.23, Co-LLM 50.90, CoSD-Rule 52.41, CoSD-Tree 51.24; pair 3 (catastrophic forgetting): draft 46.37, assistant 42.83, Co-LLM 48.29, CoSD-Rule 52.16; pair 4 (TinyLlama + Llama 2 Chat): draft 27.05, assistant 37.10, SD 35.65, CoSD-Rule 35.71, CoSD-Tree 35.53. ### Inferences
- "Who decides" splits the token-level family three ways: (i) an external router on small-model features (R2R, CITER); (ii) the small model itself via a head or control token (Co-LLM in 2024; RelayLLM and PyroDash in 2026, see below); (iii) a rule on both models' probabilities after the large model has already run on the token (CoSD; this is structurally lossy speculative decoding, close to the excluded BiLD-style rules, and is included here only because the brief lists it).
- For a small-large pair on long reasoning traces, the practical saving of token routing is memory traffic/latency. R2R's own table shows more total FLOPs than the large model alone, because every routed token forces the large model to prefill the small model's tokens since its last call. Reported "LLM usage 11–15% of tokens" therefore overstates the compute saving.
- Co-LLM's optimum deferral frequency of 0.0 on GSM8k and BioASQ means the gain there comes from fine-tuning the base model with the collaborative objective, not from calling the large model.

## The boundary: speculative decoding versus cascades versus query-level routing, and whether the large model's forward pass is still paid

### Takeaway
The cleanest dividing line, stated explicitly by Narasimhan et al. (ICLR 2025 oral), is the mechanism: cascades and routers use a *deferral rule* and invoke the large model only on "hard" inputs (sequentially, compute is saved, no parallel verification), whereas speculative decoding runs the large model in *parallel scoring mode* on everything the small model drafts (latency is saved, compute is not). Step-level and token-level methods of 2025–2026 fall on both sides: RSD, SpecSearch, Speculative Thinking, SCoT (when a draft is selected), R2R, CITER and Co-LLM skip the large model's *generation* (cascade/routing side, at step or token granularity), while SpecReason, Lookahead Reasoning, GSI, SPECS and CoSD still pay a large-model forward pass over every drafted token (speculative side).

### Cited Findings
- [V] Definition of the two mechanisms: "Both approaches involve interleaving models of different sizes, but via fundamentally distinct mechanisms: cascades employ a deferral rule that invokes the larger model only for “hard” inputs, while speculative decoding uses speculative execution to primarily invoke the larger model in parallel verification mode. These mechanisms offer different benefits: empirically, cascades offer better cost-quality trade-offs, often even outperforming the large model, while theoretically, speculative decoding offers a guarantee of quality-neutrality." — [Narasimhan et al., "Faster Cascades via Speculative Decoding", arXiv HTML v2, abstract](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19261v2); identity: Harikrishna Narasimhan, Wittawat Jitkrittum, Ankit Singh Rawat, Seungyeon Kim, Neha Gupta, Aditya Krishna Menon, Sanjiv Kumar; arXiv:2405.19261 (v1 29 May 2024, v2 21 Oct 2024); OpenReview record "ICLR 2025 Oral" — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19261), [OpenReview forum](https://openreview.net/forum?id=vo9t20wsmd). Affiliation of the authors (Google): [U], not captured in the text read.
- [V] What each one targets: "Speculative decoding seeks to mimic the large model’s output distribution, and is usually used when one wants to match the quality of the large model. On the other hand, token-level cascades seek to output distributions that closely approximate the ground-truth label distribution and potentially offer good cost-quality trade-offs, sometimes yielding better quality than even the large model. Cascades are useful when the draft model fares better than the verifier on some inputs, and one may want to retain the drafter’s predictions even when it disagrees with the verifier." — [arXiv HTML v2, §3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19261v2)
- [V] Why sequential token-level cascades are slow even though they save compute: "token-level cascades require a significantly larger number of deferrals to the large model to achieve the same quality. This is because token-level cascades are executed sequentially: whenever $q$ defers, we execute $p$ once to generate one next token for the prefix accumulated so far, and the control transfers back to $q$. In contrast, speculative decoding runs $p$ in scoring mode to verify $\gamma$ draft tokens from $q$ in parallel." — [arXiv HTML v2, §3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19261v2)
- [V] The compute/latency asymmetry in one sentence each: "unlike speculative approaches, which always call the large model after every $\gamma$ steps, a sequential cascade only invokes the large model when the small model defers"; and, as a stated limitation of speculative cascades, "while it offers a higher throughput, it also incurs a higher total compute cost compared to sequential cascades." — [arXiv HTML v2, §6–7](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19261v2)
- [V] Token-level deferral rules written as target distributions (their Table 1): cascade target $\pi(x)=(1-\delta)\cdot q(x)+\delta\cdot p(x)$ with $\delta$ = Chow: $\mathbf{1}(\max_{v}q(v)<1-\alpha)$ (sequential execution); Diff (oracle, needs the large model at every step): $\mathbf{1}(\max_{v}q(v)<\max_{v}p(v)-\alpha)$; OPT: $\mathbf{1}(\max_{v}q(v)<\max_{v}p(v)-\alpha\cdot D_{\textrm{TV}}(p,q))$; speculative decoding target $p(x)$; lossy SD (Tran-Thien) target $\max\{\min\{q(x),\frac{p(x)}{1-\alpha}\},\frac{p(x)}{\beta}\}$; BiLD* $\delta=\mathbf{1}(D(q,p)>\alpha)$. "Remark 1": the Diff rule "cannot be directly used in a token-level cascade, as it needs the large model to be invoked at every step" — which is exactly what speculative execution makes affordable — [arXiv HTML v2, Table 1, §3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19261v2)
- [V] Ensembling explanation for cascades beating the large model: "Even Chow’s rule, which is sub-optimal for cascading ..., offers better trade-offs, and outperforms speculative decoding in a small region. As noted by Kim et al. (2023), this may be attributed to the ensembling effect in a cascade." — [arXiv HTML v2, §3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19261v2)

### Inferences
- A three-axis description separates all the methods without overlap: (1) unit of the decision (query / chain / step / token); (2) who judges and whether the judgment needs the large model (small-model confidence or router; external reward model or judge LLM; large-model likelihood or self-score; large-model re-generation plus equivalence check); (3) whether the large model processes the accepted material at all (never; prefill only; full parallel generation). "Speculative" in the strict sense means axis (3) is not "never" and the large model's pass is used as the check; "cascade/routing" means the check does not involve the large model and the large model is invoked only on deferral.
- Under that description RSD is a step-level cascade with a learned external deferral rule (a PRM), despite its name; SpecReason and GSI/SPECS are step-level lossy speculative decoding; Lookahead Reasoning is step-level speculative decoding with a relaxed (semantic) equality; R2R/CITER/Co-LLM are token-level routers (sequential token cascades with a learned deferral rule); CoSD is token-level lossy speculative decoding.
- The economics follow the axis-(3) answer: skipping saves FLOPs/cost roughly in proportion to the share of skipped tokens but gives no parallel verification, so quality rests entirely on the judge; verifying in parallel keeps a large-model signal on every token but saves only latency (memory-bound decoding) and typically increases total compute. KV-cache catch-up blurs the line for token routers: with frequent switching the large model ends up prefilling almost all tokens anyway (R2R's FLOPs table).
- "Faster Cascades" predicts the pattern seen in the 2025 step-level papers: cascade-like rules can beat the large model (ensembling, retaining the small model's correct deviations), while equality-based speculation can only match it.

### Gaps
- No paper found that measures all these methods under one harness with both wall-clock and FLOPs/energy; cross-paper comparisons mix metrics (FLOPs in RSD, estimated tokens/s in Speculative Thinking, "average activated parameters" in R2R, "data transformation" in CITER, wall-clock elsewhere).
- Whether the FLOPs/cost reported for RSD and SpecSearch include large-model prefill of accepted small-model steps: not found.
- A formal treatment of step-level cascades in the style of Narasimhan et al. (optimal deferral at step granularity): not found.

## New in 2026: step-level, thought-level and agent-level speculation; token-level routing successors

### Takeaway
(First-pass version; see the items below for what was verified.) 2026 work goes in four directions: cheaper verification for step-level speculation without an external reward model (ConfSpec, SpecGuard), small-model-controlled token handoff trained with reinforcement learning (RelayLLM, PyroDash) and serving systems for token routing (TokenRouter, NeurIPS 2026), theory-backed test-time-scaling speculation reaching main venues (GSI at ICLR 2026, SPECS at NeurIPS 2026), and speculation at the level of agent steps or trajectories (Vanguard, SpecAgent, Speculative Actions and several systems papers).

## Across the family: where accuracy is kept or improved while saving compute, why gains over the large model appear, and known failure modes

### Takeaway
Accuracy is kept or slightly improved mostly on math-style benchmarks (MATH500, GSM8K, AIME, OlympiadBench, GPQA) with same-family pairs of roughly 1.5B–7B drafts and 7B–72B targets, where a strong judge exists (a math PRM or a capable reasoning model); the improvements over the large model alone are small in absolute terms (about +0.4 to +2.7 points on RSD's averages) and have three documented explanations: an ensemble/selection effect of the judge, the small model's shorter chains under a token budget, and comparison against a weaker baseline than it seems (e.g. SD that itself scores below the target). Failure modes are concentrated in the judge (PRM fooled by misleading steps, self-scores that do not discriminate, lexical cues), in step segmentation by `\n\n`, in domains without a reward model, and in pairs where the small model is far weaker.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Benchmarks/pairs where accuracy is at or above the large model with less compute or latency: RSD on Qwen2.5-Math 1.5B→7B and 7B→72B (average +0.4 to +0.9 at δ=0.7; MATH500 88.0 vs 85.6 with "nearly 4.4× fewer FLOPs") — [RSD](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19324v3); SpecReason on QwQ-32B / Skywork-OR1-Preview-32B with 1.5B drafts under an 8192-token budget (+0.4–9.0%, 1.4–3.0× faster) — [SpecReason](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07891v2); Lookahead Reasoning within +1.0 / −2.1 points at 1.04–1.71× (more compute) — [Lookahead](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19830); SpecSearch on Llama3 8B→70B, MATH-100 64 vs 62 and GSM8K-100 88 vs 87 — [SpecSearch](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02865v2); SPECS on Qwen2.5 1.5B→7B, MATH500 n=4 77.0 vs 69.3 for beam search with the target — [SPECS](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15733v2); SCoT "near-target-model" with CollegeMath above the target — [SCoT](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19095v2)
- [V] Explanation 1, selection among small-model candidates acts like an ensemble: SCoT's motivating measurement ("if we allow the 1.5B model to provide 5 answers ... and select the best one ..., its overall accuracy on GSM8K is close to that of the 32B model"); SpecSearch ("over 40% of its reasoning, thoughts outperformed the average reward score of those from the larger Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct model"); RSD ("$\mathbf{P}_{\text{RSD}}$ can exceed $\mathbf{P}_{M}$ in expected reward when we use an aggressive $w$ that only assigns 1 to high-reward regions"); Faster Cascades ("this may be attributed to the ensembling effect in a cascade"); Vanguard 2026 ("a few samples of a small model can contain a correct step about as often as the large model gets the step right, and not always on the same steps") — [SCoT](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19095v2), [SpecSearch](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02865v2), [RSD](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19324v3), [Faster Cascades](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19261v2), [Vanguard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23917)
- [V] Explanation 2, shorter chains under a token budget: SpecReason, "The key reason behind this accuracy improvement is the reduction in token consumption required for reasoning", gap "16.2% at 4096 tokens" vs "4.7% at 8192 tokens" — [SpecReason](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07891v2)
- [V] Explanation 3, an explicit judge adds structured self-checking: SpecReason, "We also attribute these accuracy gains to SpecReason’s explicit judgment and scoring mechanism at each reasoning step, which augments the model’s internal self-reflection with more structured assessment." — [SpecReason](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07891v2)
- [V] Failure mode: judge fooled or not discriminative — SpecSearch: "the degradation primarily arises from certain misleading thoughts that deceive the PRM"; Lookahead Reasoning on self-scoring: "the scoring mechanism may possess limited discriminative power on simpler datasets, even at stricter thresholds" (acceptance 0.93 at threshold 9 on GSM8K with a 5.9-point drop); GSI on RSD: "RSD generally tends to accept almost all samples"; GSI on itself: tilted rewards "can also sometimes be misleading" — [SpecSearch](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02865v2), [Lookahead](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19830), [GSI](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04118v3)
- [V] Failure mode: no reward model outside math — RSD: "there is not yet a PRM for general-domain generation" and AlpacaEval win rate 18.85 vs 24.47 for the target — [RSD](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19324v3)
- [V] Failure mode: weak draft or large capability gap — SpecReason: "the accuracy degrades less gracefully as the threshold is relaxed ... because the small model exhibits a larger performance gap relative to the base model on AIME and GPQA"; SPECS on Llama3 1B→8B: the big model still writes 78–98% of steps and latency is often worse than beam search; Speculative Thinking: the 1.5B+32B system reaches 32.2 on AIME vs 65.6 for the 32B model — [SpecReason](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07891v2), [SPECS](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15733v2), [Speculative Thinking](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12329v2)
- [V] Failure mode: step segmentation and cues — Lookahead Reasoning: "using ‘\n\n‘ to split reasoning steps is simple but may miss optimal breaks"; Speculative Thinking: dependence on "specific structural cues (e.g., \n\n followed by keywords), whose stability across different model sizes, families, and training setups remains uncertain" — [Lookahead](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19830), [Speculative Thinking](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12329v2)
- [V] Failure mode: non-termination and distribution shift for learned token routers — R2R: "the primary failure mode occurs when R2R cannot complete its reasoning within the 32K token limit ... repetitive reasoning patterns that were not encountered during the router’s training phase"; "chiefly tuned and tested for greedy sampling" — [R2R](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21600v2)
- [V] Failure mode: confidence is not correctness — CoSD: "the assistant model provided an incorrect answer with higher confidence, leading to the final output being changed to the wrong answer" — [CoSD](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08020v2)

### Inferences
- Every reported "better than the large model" result is on benchmarks where the small model alone is already within roughly 5–15 points of the large one (Qwen2.5-Math 1.5B vs 7B on MATH500: 73.8 vs 83.2) or where a selection step can exploit several small-model samples; none is on a task where the small model is far behind (AIME with a 1.5B draft: SpecReason-style scoring loses 12 points in Lookahead Reasoning's re-measurement).
- Small evaluation sets make the gains fragile: 100-question subsets (SpecSearch, SPECS), 40-question AMC23, 30–60-question AIME; one question is 1–3 points.
- Hyper-parameters are frequently tuned per dataset on the evaluation distribution (RSD's δ*, SPECS's (β, τ) per dataset and beam width, Co-LLM's η per dataset), which inflates headline numbers relative to a fixed configuration.

### Gaps
- No independent replication study of the whole family under a single protocol was found; the only cross-paper re-measurements found are Lookahead Reasoning's of SpecReason, GSI's and SPECS's of RSD.
- Energy or dollar-cost measurements for the step-level methods: not found.
