## 1. Precursors: blockwise parallel decoding, aggressive decoding, LLMA, SpecDec

### Takeaway
Every draft-then-verify method before late 2022 was deterministic: the target's parallel forward pass checks each drafted token against its own argmax and keeps the longest matching prefix, which reproduces greedy decoding exactly (Stern 2018, Sun 2021, Ge 2022 "GAD", Yang 2023 "LLMA"). The first "lossy" acceptance rules also date from this period: Stern's top-$k$ and distance-based selection, and the top-$\beta$ plus tolerance $\tau$ rule of GAD++/SpecDec ($\beta=3,\tau=1.0$), justified only by BLEU, with no distributional guarantee and no support for sampling.

### Cited Findings

#### 1.1 Stern, Shazeer, Uszkoreit 2018 — "Blockwise Parallel Decoding for Deep Autoregressive Models"
- **(a) Identity [V].** Title exactly as above. Mitchell Stern (University of California, Berkeley; "Work performed while the author was an intern at Google Brain"), Noam Shazeer, Jakob Uszkoreit (Google Brain). NeurIPS 2018 (arXiv comment: "NIPS 2018"). arXiv 1811.03115, single version of 7 Nov 2018. Code: "Our code is publicly available in the open-source Tensor2Tensor library" (no URL in the paper). — [arXiv:1811.03115](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.03115)
- **(b) Problem [V].** "outputs are still generated one token at a time during inference"; the idea: "we make predictions for multiple time steps in parallel then back off to the longest prefix validated by a scoring model." — [arXiv:1811.03115](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.03115)
- **(c) Mechanism [V].** Base model $p_1=p$ plus auxiliary models $p_2,\dots,p_k$, where $p_i(y_{j+i}\mid y_{\le j},x)$ predicts the $(j+i)$-th token from the first $j$. Three sub-steps per iteration:
  - Predict: $\hat y_{j+i}=\arg\max_{y_{j+i}} p_i(y_{j+i}\mid \hat y_{\le j},x)$ for $i=1,\dots,k$.
  - Verify: "Find the largest $\hat k$ such that $\hat y_{j+i}=\arg\max_{y_{j+i}} p_1(y_{j+i}\mid \hat y_{\le j+i-1},x)$ for all $1\le i\le \hat k$. Note that $\hat k\ge 1$ by the definition of $\hat y_{j+1}$."
  - Accept: extend $\hat y$ with $\hat y_{j+1},\dots,\hat y_{j+\hat k}$, set $j\gets j+\hat k$.
  - Combined scoring-and-proposal model: verification $n$ is merged with prediction $n+1$, which reduces model invocations "from $2m/k$ to $m/k+1$". Implementation: one feed-forward layer with hidden size $k\times d_{\rm hidden}$ and output size $k\times d_{\rm model}$ inserted before the output projection; one of the $k$ losses sampled per minibatch. — [arXiv:1811.03115](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.03115)
- **(c') Approximate acceptance variants (Section 5 "Approximate Inference") [V].** "By relaxing the criterion used during verification, we can allow for additional speedups at the cost of potentially deviating from the greedy output."
  - Top-$k$ selection: accept if $\hat y_{j+i}\in \text{top-}k_{\,y_{j+i}}\ p_1(y_{j+i}\mid \hat y_{\le j+i-1},x)$. Values tested: top-2 and top-3.
  - Distance-based selection: accept if $d\big(\hat y_{j+i},\ \arg\max_{y_{j+i}} p_1(y_{j+i}\mid \hat y_{\le j+i-1},x)\big)\le\epsilon$, with $d(u,v)=|u-v|$ on pixel intensities; $\epsilon=2$ in the experiments.
  - Minimum block size: force at least $1<\ell\le k$ tokens per step ($\ell=2$ or $3$ tested); "exhibit much larger drops in BLEU score with only minor improvements in mean accepted block size". — [arXiv:1811.03115](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.03115)
- **(d) Guarantee [V].** Exact variant: "guaranteed to produce the same prediction $\hat y$ that would be found under greedy decoding but uses as few as $m/k$ steps". Approximate variants: no guarantee; quality measured by BLEU or human preference. — [arXiv:1811.03115](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.03115)
- **(e) Setup [V].** WMT 2014 English-German, `transformer_base` trained 1,000,000 steps on 8 P100 GPUs (greedy BLEU 25.56 on newstest2013), then 1,000,000 more steps per block size $k\in\{2,4,6,8,10\}$, with or without distilled data and with or without fine-tuning of the base parameters. CelebA super-resolution $8\times8\to32\times32$ with an Image Transformer (`img2img_transformer_b3`, +250,000 steps). Greedy decoding only (no sampling temperature). — [arXiv:1811.03115](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.03115)
- **(f) Results [V].** Cells are BLEU / mean accepted block size on newstest2013 (Table 1): frozen base, gold data: $k{=}6$: 26.08 / 1.76; distillation: $k{=}8$: 26.25 / 1.91; fine-tuning: $k{=}8$: 24.27 / 3.01; both: $k{=}10$: 25.60 / 4.95. Top-2 approximate decoding on the "both" models: "$k=2$: 26.49 / 1.92, $k=4$: 26.22 / 3.47, $k=6$: 25.90 / 4.59, $k=8$: 25.71 / 5.34, $k=10$: 25.04 / 5.67"; top-3: "$k=10$: 24.68 / 5.91". Wall-clock: "For translation, the wall-clock speedup peaks at 3.3x, corresponding to the setting with $k = 8$ and mean accepted block size 4.7. For super-resolution, the wall-clock speedup reaches 4.0x, corresponding to the setting with $k = 6$ and mean accepted block size 5.3." Test set newstest2014 (Table 4): greedy $k{=}1$ 29.11 BLEU (1.00x); $k{=}2$ 28.95 (1.72x); $k{=}4$ 28.54 (2.69x); $k{=}6$ 28.11 (3.10x); $k{=}8$ 27.88 (3.31x); $k{=}10$ 27.40 (3.04x). Super-resolution mean accepted block size (Table 2), $k{=}10$: exact 1.10, approximate ($\epsilon{=}2$) 1.40, fine-tuned exact 2.04, fine-tuned + approximate 6.79. Abstract: "iteration reductions of up to 2x over a baseline greedy decoder with no loss in quality, or up to 7x in exchange for a slight decrease in performance... real-time speedups of up to 4x". — [arXiv:1811.03115](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.03115)
- **(g) Caveats [V].** Needs extra trained heads; with a frozen base model the accepted block stays below 2 (1.76–1.91); larger blocks require fine-tuning the base model, which lowers BLEU; "larger block sizes $k$ continue to improve in terms of iteration count, but start to decline in terms of wall-clock improvement due to their higher computational cost". — [arXiv:1811.03115](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.03115)
- **(h) Relation [V].** Leviathan et al. describe it as: "it only supports greedy decoding (temperature=0) and not the general stochastic setting, it requires additional training of a custom model, and focuses on preserving down-stream task quality, instead of guaranteeing identical outputs." The Google blog calls it "a precursor to our work". — [arXiv:2211.17192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192); [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/looking-back-at-speculative-decoding/)

#### 1.2 Sun, Ge, Wei, Wang 2021 — "Instantaneous Grammatical Error Correction with Shallow Aggressive Decoding" (SAD)
- **(a) [V].** Xin Sun (MOE Key Lab of Computational Linguistics, School of EECS, Peking University; intern at MSR Asia), Tao Ge, Furu Wei (Microsoft Research Asia), Houfeng Wang. ACL 2021 main conference. arXiv 2106.04970. Code: https://github.com/AutoTemp/Shallow-Aggressive-Decoding. — [arXiv:2106.04970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04970)
- **(c) Mechanism [V].** The draft is the input sentence itself (grammatical error correction outputs are close to inputs). All positions are decoded in parallel conditioned on the input as pseudo-history: $o^*_{j+1}=\arg\max_{o_{j+1}}\log P(o_{j+1}\mid x_{\le j},x;\Phi)$. The first bifurcation $k$ with $o_{1..k-1}=x_{1..k-1}$, $o_k\ne x_k$ is found; $o_{1..k}$ is kept; decoding then continues token by token until the generated suffix matches a unique substring of the input, at which point parallel "aggressive" decoding resumes. It is combined with a shallow decoder: "Since the 9+3 model achieves the best result with an around $2\times$ speedup in the validation set with almost the same parameterization budget, we choose it as the model architecture to combine with aggressive decoding for final evaluation" (9 encoder + 3 decoder layers). — [arXiv:2106.04970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04970)
- **(d) Guarantee [V].** "Since we discard all the computations and predictions after the bifurcation for re-decoding, aggressive decoding guarantees that generation results are exactly the same as greedy decoding (i.e., beam=1)." — [arXiv:2106.04970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04970)
- **(e,f) Setup and results [V].** Transformer-big (6+6 layers), batch size 1, fairseq / PyTorch 1.5.1, one Nvidia Tesla V100 16 GB. CoNLL-13 (1,381 sentences): total latency 440 s (beam=5), 328 s (greedy), 54 s (aggressive) = 8.1× over beam search with identical P/R/F$_{0.5}$ to greedy (52.75 / 18.34 / 38.36); with synthetic data 7.3×. "aggressive decoding achieves a $7\times\sim8\times$ speedup over the original autoregressive beam search (beam=5), and generates exactly the same predictions as greedy decoding". Abstract: "66.4 $F_{0.5}$ in the CoNLL-14 and 72.9 $F_{0.5}$ in the BEA-19 test set with an almost $10\times$ online inference speedup over the Transformer-big model". The CoNLL-14 efficiency table lists "Our approach (9+3)" at 10.5× (no synthetic data) and 10.3× (with synthetic data) over Transformer-big with beam 5. — [arXiv:2106.04970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04970)
- **(g) Caveat [V].** Speedup depends on the edit ratio: examples range from 16.7× (edit ratio 0) to 1.4× (edit ratio 0.41) over greedy. Leviathan: "SAD only supports copying the input to the output, and not general approximation models". — [arXiv:2106.04970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04970); [arXiv:2211.17192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192)

#### 1.3 Ge, Xia, Sun, Chen, Wei 2022 — "Lossless Acceleration for Seq2seq Generation with Aggressive Decoding"
- **(a) [V].** Tao Ge (Microsoft), Heming Xia, Xin Sun, Si-Qing Chen, Furu Wei. arXiv 2205.10350 (v1, 20 May 2022); arXiv comment: "24-page Microsoft Research Technical Report. Content overlap with arXiv:2106.04970 and arXiv:2203.16487". No conference venue found. Code: https://github.com/microsoft/unilm/tree/master/decoding. — [arXiv:2205.10350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10350)
- **(c) Mechanism [V].** Two paradigms: Input-guided Aggressive Decoding (IAD: copy from the input, as in SAD) and Generalized Aggressive Decoding (GAD: a non-autoregressive drafter proposes $k$ tokens, the autoregressive verifier checks them in parallel). Bifurcation position $c=\arg\max_i \frac{\mathbb{1}(\tilde o_{j+i}\ne\hat o_{j+i})}{i},\ 1\le i\le k$, with $\hat o_{j+i}=\arg\max \log P(\cdot\mid o_{\le j},\tilde o_{j+1\cdots j+i-1},x;\Phi_{\rm AR})$. GAD++ relaxes the test: accept the drafted token if $\log P(\tilde o_{j+i}\mid\cdot)\ge\log P(\hat o^{(\beta)}_{j+i}\mid\cdot)$ (in the top-$\beta$) and $\log P(\hat o^{(1)}_{j+i}\mid\cdot)-\log P(\tilde o_{j+i}\mid\cdot)\le\tau$. Values: "we conservatively select $\beta=3, \tau=1.0$ for the high-quality GAD++, and use $\beta=5, \tau=3.0$ as the high-efficiency GAD++"; block size $k=25$ (base), $k=30$ (big). — [arXiv:2205.10350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10350)
- **(d) Guarantee [V].** GAD: "which ensures GAD to yield the same results as greedy decoding of AR". GAD++ is not identical to greedy; the paper says it "enables GAD++ to generate beyond greedy decoding". — [arXiv:2205.10350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10350)
- **(f) Results [V].** Abstract: IAD "a $7\times\sim9\times$ speedup ... with the identical results as greedy decoding"; GAD "a $3\times\sim5\times$ speedup with the identical or even better quality". WMT14 EN→DE (Table of main results): GAD ($k{=}25$) 4.9 iterations, 3.0×, BLEU 28.73 (= greedy 28.73); GAD++ high-quality 3.6×, 28.89; GAD++ high-efficiency 4.5×, 28.73; beam 5 baseline 1.0×, 28.89. Hardware: one V100, batch size 1. — [arXiv:2205.10350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10350)

#### 1.4 Yang et al. 2023 — "Inference with Reference: Lossless Acceleration of Large Language Models" (LLMA)
- **(a) [V].** Nan Yang, Tao Ge, Liang Wang, Binxing Jiao, Daxin Jiang, Linjun Yang, Rangan Majumder, Furu Wei (Microsoft). arXiv 2304.04487 (v1, 10 Apr 2023, "9 pages"). Venue: not found (arXiv only). Code URL given in the author block: https://github.com/microsoft/unilm. — [arXiv:2304.04487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04487)
- **(c) Mechanism [V].** Two hyper-parameters, "the match length $n$ and the copy length $k$". At each step, if the last $n$ generated tokens match a span of a reference document, the next $k$ reference tokens are copied into the decoder input and scored in one step; "copied tokens $\{d_{pos},...d_{pos+j-1}\}$ are identical to previous model output" is required for the $j$-th output to be kept. Grid-searched values (table "Match length n and copy length k"): retrieval: 7B $n{=}1,k{=}18$; 13B $n{=}1,k{=}14$; 30B $n{=}1,k{=}18$; cache: $n{=}1$, $k{=}15/15/18$. — [arXiv:2304.04487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04487)
- **(d) Guarantee [V].** "The verification guarantees our decoding algorithm produces identical generation output as the stepwise decoding method for greedy decoding." — [arXiv:2304.04487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04487)
- **(e,f) Setup and results [V].** LLaMA 7B/13B/30B, half precision, greedy, batch size 1; 7B and 13B on one NVidia 32G V100, 30B on four. Retrieval-augmented generation: 2.48x / 2.22x / 2.49x; cache-assisted generation: 2.22x / 2.19x / 3.06x (tokens/s 23.9→59.2 for 7B RAG). "gains plateaued when copy length $k$ grows past 15". — [arXiv:2304.04487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04487)
- **(g) Caveat [V].** The timing is simulated: reference outputs come from davinci-003 and "We can force LLaMA model to follow the exact decoding steps regardless of its own output, which is sufficient for measuring the execution time of our method." — [arXiv:2304.04487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04487)

- **(d) Lossless or lossy [V].** The vanilla rule (top-1 match) "guarantees that the decoding results are identical to AR greedy decoding". Greedy only; no sampling. — [arXiv:2203.16487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16487)
- **(f) Results [V].** Table 1, SpecDec $k{=}25$: EN→DE 5.1×, BLEU 28.93 (beam 28.89, greedy 28.73); DE→EN 5.5×, 32.55; EN→RO 4.6×, 35.45; RO→EN 4.8×, 35.03. Blockwise Decoding re-implemented ($k{=}10$): 1.9× / 2.0× / 1.4× / 1.4×. Against a 12+2 target: 2.5×–3.3×. Verification ablation on the dev set ($k{=}25$): vanilla 6.41 accepted tokens / 26.62 BLEU; $\beta{=}3,\tau{=}1$: 7.89 / 26.97 (5.0×); $\beta{=}5,\tau{=}5$: 11.01 / 26.58 (6.8×). Drafter ablation: head-based drafter (Stern) 2.32 tokens, 1.7×; Spec-Drafter 8.23 tokens, 5.1×. CNN-DM, BART-base: 5.1× with ROUGE-1/2/L 43.11 / 20.43 / 40.19 versus 43.08 / 20.41 / 40.15 for beam 5. — [arXiv:2203.16487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16487)
- **(g) Caveats [V].** A dedicated drafter must be trained for each target; results are for small seq2seq models; the paper itself notes that with autoregressive drafters "for seq2seq generation, the acceleration effect of AR drafting is severely limited, resulting in an end-to-end speedup at $1.6\times\sim2.8\times$". — [arXiv:2203.16487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16487)

### Inferences
- [mine] With temperature 0 the "standardized" distributions of Leviathan et al. are one-hot, so $\min(1,p(x)/q(x))$ is 1 if the draft argmax equals the target argmax and 0 otherwise, and the residual $\mathrm{norm}(\max(0,p-q))$ is the target's argmax. Exact-match verification (Stern, SAD, GAD, LLMA) is therefore the temperature-0 special case of speculative sampling.
- [mine] The pre-2023 relaxations are deterministic lossy rules of two kinds: rank-based (Stern's top-$k$; SpecDec's top-$\beta$) and margin-based (SpecDec's log-likelihood gap $\tau$; Stern's metric distance $\epsilon$). They are the ancestors of later lossy criteria (typical acceptance, thresholds), and they were validated only on BLEU/ROUGE or human preference.
- [mine] "Lossless" has two meanings in this literature: identical output distribution (Leviathan, Chen) versus no loss of task metric (Ge 2022, SpecDec). A writer should state which one is meant.

## 2. Leviathan, Kalman, Matias 2023 — "Fast Inference from Transformers via Speculative Decoding"

### Takeaway
One step drafts $\gamma$ tokens from $M_q$, scores the $\gamma+1$ prefixes with one parallel run of $M_p$, accepts draft token $x_i$ with probability $\min(1,p_i(x_i)/q_i(x_i))$ and, at the first rejection, resamples from $\mathrm{norm}(\max(0,p-q))$; the output distribution is exactly that of $M_p$ for any $M_q$. With per-token acceptance $\alpha=E[\sum_x\min(p,q)]$ assumed i.i.d., a step yields $(1-\alpha^{\gamma+1})/(1-\alpha)$ tokens and the walltime gain is $(1-\alpha^{\gamma+1})/((1-\alpha)(\gamma c+1))$; measured speedups on T5-XXL are 2.3X–3.4X with T5-small and $\gamma\in\{5,7\}$. The "lenience" parameter $l$ (Appendix A.5) is the paper's lossy variant, with the single guarantee that no token is sampled with more than $p(x)/l$.

- **(a) Identity [V].** Yaniv Leviathan*, Matan Kalman* (equal contribution), Yossi Matias; affiliation "Google Research, Mountain View, CA, USA". ICML 2023 (arXiv comment "ICML 2023 Oral"); Proceedings of the 40th International Conference on Machine Learning, PMLR 202:19274–19286, 2023. arXiv 2211.17192, v1 30 Nov 2022, v2 18 May 2023. Code: no repository linked in the paper or the Google blog (not found). — [arXiv:2211.17192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192); [PMLR v202](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/leviathan23a.html)
- **(b) Problem [V].** "Inference from large autoregressive models like Transformers is slow - decoding $K$ tokens takes $K$ serial runs of the model." Two observations: "(1) hard language-modeling tasks often include easier subtasks that can be approximated well by more efficient models, and (2) using speculative execution and a novel sampling method, we can make exact decoding from the large models faster". Also: "inference from large models is often not bottlenecked on arithmetic operations, but rather on memory bandwidth and communication". — [arXiv:2211.17192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192)
- **(c1) Standardized sampling and temperature 0 (Section 2.2) [V].** "while there are many methods and parameters of sampling, like argmax, top-k, nucleus, and setting a temperature ... they can all easily be cast into standard sampling from an adjusted probability distribution. For example, argmax sampling is equivalent to zeroing out non-max elements of the distribution and normalizing." From then on $p(x)$ and $q(x)$ are "the distributions from $M_p$ and $M_q$ respectively, adjusted for the sampling method". — [arXiv:2211.17192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192)
- **(c2) Speculative sampling (Section 2.3) [V].** "To sample $x\sim p(x)$, we instead sample $x\sim q(x)$, keeping it if $q(x)\le p(x)$, and in case $q(x)>p(x)$ we reject the sample with probability $1-\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}$ and sample $x$ again from an adjusted distribution $p'(x)=norm(max(0,p(x)-q(x)))$ instead." — [arXiv:2211.17192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192)
- **(c3) Algorithm 1 "SpeculativeDecodingStep", transcribed line by line [V].** — [arXiv:2211.17192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192)
  ```
  Inputs: M_p, M_q, prefix.
  ▷ Sample γ guesses x_{1,…,γ} from M_q autoregressively.
  for i = 1 to γ do
      q_i(x) ← M_q(prefix + [x_1, …, x_{i−1}])
      x_i ∼ q_i(x)
  end for
  ▷ Run M_p in parallel.
  p_1(x), …, p_{γ+1}(x) ← M_p(prefix), …, M_p(prefix + [x_1, …, x_γ])
  ▷ Determine the number of accepted guesses n.
  r_1 ∼ U(0,1), …, r_γ ∼ U(0,1)
  n ← min({ i − 1 | 1 ≤ i ≤ γ, r_i > p_i(x)/q_i(x) } ∪ {γ})
  ▷ Adjust the distribution from M_p if needed.
  p′(x) ← p_{n+1}(x)
  if n < γ then
      p′(x) ← norm(max(0, p_{n+1}(x) − q_{n+1}(x)))
  end if
  ▷ Return one token from M_p, and n tokens from M_q.
  t ∼ p′(x)
  return prefix + [x_1, …, x_n, t]
  ```
  In LaTeX: $n\gets\min\big(\{i-1\mid 1\le i\le\gamma,\ r_i>\tfrac{p_i(x)}{q_i(x)}\}\cup\{\gamma\}\big)$, where the ratio is evaluated at $x=x_i$. Each step emits between 1 and $\gamma+1$ tokens: "the number of serial runs of the target model can never, even in the worst case, be larger than the simple autoregressive method".
- **(c4) Definitions and results of Section 3, with the paper's numbering [V].** — [arXiv:2211.17192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192)
  - Definition 3.1: "The acceptance rate $\beta_{x_{<t}}$, given a prefix $x_{<t}$, is the probability of accepting $x_t\sim q(x_t|x_{<t})$ by speculative sampling".
  - $\alpha$ and Equation (1): "If we make the simplifying assumption that the $\beta$s are i.i.d., and denote $\alpha=E(\beta)$, then the number of tokens produced by a single run of Algorithm 1 is a capped geometric variable, with success probability $1-\alpha$ and cap $\gamma+1$", and
    $$E(\#\ \text{generated tokens})=\frac{1-\alpha^{\gamma+1}}{1-\alpha}.$$
  - Definition 3.2: $D_{LK}(p,q)=\sum_x|p(x)-M(x)|=\sum_x|q(x)-M(x)|$ where $M(x)=\frac{p(x)+q(x)}{2}$.
  - Lemma 3.3: $D_{LK}(p,q)=1-\sum_x\min(p(x),q(x))$. Proof as printed: $D_{LK}(p,q)=\sum_x|p(x)-M(x)|=\sum_x\frac{|p-q|}{2}=1-\sum_x\frac{p+q-|p-q|}{2}=1-\sum_x\min(p(x),q(x))$.
  - Corollary 3.4: $D_{LK}$ "is a symmetric divergence in $[0,1]$"; $D_{LK}=0\iff p=q$; $D_{LK}=1\iff$ "p and q have disjoint support".
  - Theorem 3.5: $\beta=1-D_{LK}(p,q)$. Proof: $\beta=E_{x\sim q(x)}\min(1,\frac{p(x)}{q(x)})=\sum_x\min(p(x),q(x))$.
  - Corollary 3.6: $\alpha=1-E(D_{LK}(p,q))=E(\min(p,q))$.
  - Definition 3.7: "Let $c$, the cost coefficient, be the ratio between the time for a single run of $M_q$ and the time for a single run of $M_p$." It "depends on the hardware configuration and software implementation details"; "$c$ was always less than $0.05$ and often negligibly close to 0" when $M_q$ is "a couple of orders of magnitude smaller".
  - Theorem 3.8: "The expected improvement factor in total walltime by Algorithm 1 is $\frac{1-\alpha^{\gamma+1}}{(1-\alpha)(\gamma c+1)}$." Proof: each run "costs $Tc\gamma+T$ (for running the approximation model $M_q$ $\gamma$ times and running $M_p$ once)" and yields Eq. (1) tokens, so the cost per token is $\frac{(c\gamma+1)(1-\alpha)}{1-\alpha^{\gamma+1}}T$. Assumptions stated: the i.i.d. assumption; "we'll assume that we can run $\gamma+1$ concurrent evaluations of $M_p$ in parallel without increasing the walltime"; "assumes long enough generations".
  - Corollary 3.9: "If $\alpha>c$, there exists $\gamma$ for which we'll get an improvement, and the improvement factor will be at least $\frac{1+\alpha}{1+c}$."
  - Definition 3.10 and Theorem 3.11 (arithmetic operations): with $\hat c$ "the ratio of arithmetic operations per token of the approximation model $M_q$ to that of the target model $M_p$", "The expected factor of increase in the number of total operations of Algorithm 1 is $\frac{(1-\alpha)(\gamma\hat c+\gamma+1)}{1-\alpha^{\gamma+1}}$." Proof: one iteration costs $\hat T\hat c\gamma+\hat T(\gamma+1)$ operations. Also: "the total number of arithmetic operations by Algorithm 1 (not counting runs of $M_q$) can be bounded from above by a single run of the same-size Transformer encoder", and "the target model's weights and KV cache can be read once per execution of Algorithm 1, so the number of memory accesses for reading them shrinks by a factor of $\frac{1-\alpha^{\gamma+1}}{1-\alpha}$".
  - Choosing $\gamma$ (Section 3.5): maximise Theorem 3.8 numerically over integer $\gamma$ (Figure 3). Table 1, with $c=\hat c=0$ ($\alpha$, $\gamma$, operations, speed): (0.6, 2, 1.53X, 1.96X); (0.7, 3, 1.58X, 2.53X); (0.8, 2, 1.23X, 2.44X); (0.8, 5, 1.63X, 3.69X); (0.9, 2, 1.11X, 2.71X); (0.9, 10, 1.60X, 6.86X).
  - Oracle draft length: with an oracle for $\gamma$, $E(\#\text{ generated tokens})=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}$; "the enhanced walltime improvement factor can be up to $\sim$60% higher than the improvement factor with a fixed $\gamma$. We leave exploring this for future work".
- **(c5) Drafters that are not neural models (Section 3.6) [V].** "negligible-cost models" with $c\approx0$ give $\frac{1-\alpha^{\gamma+1}}{1-\alpha}\le\frac{1}{1-\alpha}$; n-gram tables ("with $M_p$ being T5-XXL 11B and $M_q$ being a trivial bigram model, we get $\alpha\approx0.2$ which leads to an inference speed improvement factor of $1.25$X with $\gamma=3$"); copying from the context; non-autoregressive drafters "like those from" Stern et al.; a random-token drafter "guarantees some improvement (although very small)". - [mine] The draft lengths used in Table 2 are close to the maximisers of Theorem 3.8 for the tabulated $(\alpha,c)$: the formula's integer optimum is within 2 of the $\gamma$ used in every row (e.g. EnDe, T5-small, T=0: used 7, optimum 9, predicted 3.16 versus 3.20), and the predicted speedup is flat around the optimum (differences of at most 0.06).
- [mine] The lenient-sampling factors "2.5X, 3.1X, 3.6X, and 5X" are not reproduced exactly by Theorem 3.8 with the stated inputs: with $c=0.015$ and $\alpha=0.62,0.71,0.76,0.84$ the best integer $\gamma$ gives 2.33, 2.94, 3.45, 4.79. The temperature-0 figures (3.3, 3.3, 3.9, 4.9 at $\gamma=8$) are reproduced exactly. Quote the paper's numbers as the paper's, not as consequences of the formula.
- [mine] The paper does not say which residual distribution is used with lenience. With either $\mathrm{norm}(\max(0,p-lq))$ or the standard $\mathrm{norm}(\max(0,p-q))$, the output probability of $x$ is at most $p(x)/l$ (accepted mass is $\min(q,p/l)$; the rejection mass $1-\alpha_l$ is at most the residual normaliser in both cases), so the stated guarantee holds for both. See Section 7 for which residual is optimal in total variation.
- [mine] $D_{LK}$ is the total variation distance: $D_{LK}(p,q)=\frac12\sum_x|p(x)-q(x)|$ (the middle expression of the paper's proof of Lemma 3.3). SpecTr and Yin et al. write the same quantity as $d_{TV}$.
- [mine] The i.i.d. assumption is used only for Eq. (1) and the speed formulas; the correctness proof does not need it. Yin et al. (Section 7) remove it for the count of rejections.
- [mine] $c=0.11$ for T5-large explains why the largest drafter has the highest $\alpha$ and the lowest speedup (1.4X–2.2X): the formula penalises $\gamma c$.

## 3. Chen et al. 2023 — "Accelerating Large Language Model Decoding with Speculative Sampling"

### Takeaway
DeepMind's concurrent and independent work gives the same accept/resample rule ("modified rejection sampling"), with a proof for one token, and shows it at scale: Chinchilla 70B with a purpose-trained 4B, 8-layer drafter on 16 TPU v4, lookahead $K=4$, speedups 1.92×–2.46× with unchanged XSum and HumanEval scores.

### Cited Findings
- **(a) [V].** Charlie Chen, Sebastian Borgeaud, Geoffrey Irving, Jean-Baptiste Lespiau, Laurent Sifre, John Jumper; "All authors from DeepMind". arXiv 2302.01318, single version of 2 Feb 2023; no conference venue found; no code. — [arXiv:2302.01318](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01318)
- **Independence [V].** "the work in this manuscript was undertaken concurrently and independently of the work on speculative decoding from" Leviathan et al.; "We focus more heavily the distributed serving setting for large models and offer some incremental optimisations, but otherwise the core underlying idea is the same." — [arXiv:2302.01318](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01318)
- **(c) Mechanism, Chen notation ($q$ = target, $p$ = draft, $K$ = lookahead) [V].** Draft $\tilde x_1,\dots,\tilde x_K$ autoregressively from $p$; compute $K+1$ sets of target logits in parallel; accept $\tilde x_{n+1}$ with probability
  $$\min\Big(1,\frac{q(\tilde x_{n+1}\mid x_1,\dots,x_n)}{p(\tilde x_{n+1}\mid x_1,\dots,x_n)}\Big);$$
  on rejection resample $x_{n+1}\sim(q(x\mid x_1,\dots,x_n)-p(x\mid x_1,\dots,x_n))_+$ with $(f(x))_+=\frac{\max(0,f(x))}{\sum_x\max(0,f(x))}$ and stop; if all are accepted, sample one extra token from the last target distribution: "This gives us a maximum of $K+1$ tokens per loop". Translation to Leviathan: swap $p\leftrightarrow q$, $K=\gamma$. — [arXiv:2302.01318](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01318)
- **Sampling variants [V].** "With standard sampling methods such as nucleus, top-k sampling and adjusting temperature, we can modify the probabilities accordingly before applying this rejection sampling scheme. We have observed that the overall acceptance rate is robust to the exact parameters used." — [arXiv:2302.01318](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01318)
- **(d) Guarantee [V].** "a novel modified rejection sampling scheme which preserves the distribution of the target model within hardware numerics"; Theorem 1 is titled "Modified Rejection Sampling recovers the target distribution". Caveat: "Even with greedy sampling, a single token deviating due to numerics could result in two sequences diverging wildly ... we cannot not expect identical outputs. However, we expect the samples to come from the same distribution within numerics". — [arXiv:2302.01318](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01318)
- **(e) Setup [V].** Target Chinchilla 70B ($d_{\rm model}$ 8192, 64 heads, 80 layers). Draft: "a 4 billion parameter draft model optimised for sampling latency on 16 TPU v4s ... trained with the same tokeniser and dataset as Chinchilla, with a slightly smaller width and with only 8 layers" ($d_{\rm model}$ 6144, 48 heads, 8 layers). "sampling speed of 1.8ms/token compared to 14.1ms/token for Chinchilla". Why not a standard small model: a Chinchilla-optimal 7B reaches 5 ms/token on 4 TPU v4, and "serving a 7B on 16 TPUs actually increases the latency". Batch size 1, $K=4$. - **Why scoring $K$ tokens costs about one token [V].** Three components dominate sampling time for large sharded models: linear layers ("For small $K$, this will continue to be memory bound and therefore take a similar amount of time"), attention ("since the KV-cache size does not change as we increase $K$, there is little to no delta in this component"), and all-reduces ("typically latency bound instead of throughput bound for both sampling and scoring (for small $K$)"). — [arXiv:2302.01318](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01318)

## 4. Correctness proof of the accept/resample rule and the maximal-coupling view

### Takeaway
The proof is a two-term decomposition: the probability of emitting $x'$ is $\min(p,q)(x')$ from acceptance plus $p(x')-\min(p,q)(x')$ from resampling, because the rejection probability $1-\sum\min(p,q)$ equals the normaliser of the residual. The rule is a maximal coupling of draft and target: $\Pr(Y=X)=\sum_x\min(p(x),q(x))=1-d_{TV}(p,q)$, the largest value any coupling can reach, which is why it is optimal for a single draft token.

### Cited Findings
- **Leviathan et al., Appendix A.1 "Correctness of Speculative Sampling" (Leviathan notation) [V].** "Let $\beta$ be the acceptance probability. Note that as $p'(x)=norm(max(0,p(x)-q(x)))=\frac{p(x)-min(q(x),p(x))}{\sum_{x'}(p(x')-min(q(x'),p(x')))}=\frac{p(x)-min(q(x),p(x))}{1-\beta}$, the normalizing constant for the adjusted distribution $p'(x)$ is $1-\beta$". Then
  $$P(x=x')=P(\text{guess accepted},x=x')+P(\text{guess rejected},x=x'),$$
  $$P(\text{guess accepted},x=x')=q(x')\min\Big(1,\frac{p(x')}{q(x')}\Big)=\min(q(x'),p(x')),$$
  $$P(\text{guess rejected},x=x')=(1-\beta)p'(x')=p(x')-\min(q(x'),p(x')),$$
  $$P(x=x')=\min(p(x'),q(x'))+p(x')-\min(p(x'),q(x'))=p(x').$$
  — [arXiv:2211.17192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192)
- **Chen et al., Theorem 1 (Chen notation: $q$ target, $p$ draft) [V].** "Given discrete distributions $q$, $p$ and a single draft sample $\tilde x\sim p$, let $X$ be the final resulting sample." $\mathbb{P}(X=x)=\mathbb{P}(\tilde x=x)\mathbb{P}(\tilde x\text{ accepted}\mid\tilde x=x)+\mathbb{P}(\tilde x\text{ rejected})\mathbb{P}(X=x\mid\tilde x\text{ rejected})$. First term: $p(x)\min(1,\frac{q(x)}{p(x)})=\min(p(x),q(x))$. Rejection probability: $1-\sum_{x'}\min(p(x'),q(x'))=\sum_{x'}\max(0,q(x')-p(x'))$, "equal to the denominator of $(q(x)-p(x))_+$", so the second term is $\max(0,q(x)-p(x))$ and $\mathbb{P}(X=x)=\min(p(x),q(x))+\max(0,q(x)-p(x))=q(x)$. — [arXiv:2302.01318](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01318)
- **Scope of the two proofs [V].** Both are single-token statements. Yin et al. note: "\[Chen et al.\] proves the distribution match for a single token, we complement the proof to show the distribution match holds for a sequence of tokens $x_{1:T}$"; their Theorem 1(2): $\mathbb{P}^{\rm SD}(x_{1:T})=q(x_{1:T})$. SpecTr's Theorem 2 gives the sequence-level statement for one or several drafts: for any $\tau_0\in[1,L+1]$ and sequence $o^{\tau_0}$, $\Pr(Y^{\tau_0}=o^{\tau_0}\mid\tau=\tau_0)=\prod_{i=1}^{\tau_0}\mathcal{M}_b(o(i)\mid x^t,o^{i-1})$. Block Verification's appendix characterises validity: a verifier is valid iff $(X^\tau,Y,Z^{\gamma-\tau})\sim\mathcal{M}_b^{\gamma+1}(\cdot\mid c)$, where $Z$ is the continuation sampled from the target. — [arXiv:2411.00841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00841); [arXiv:2310.15141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15141); [arXiv:2403.10444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10444)
- **Maximal coupling [V].** SpecTr presents the rule as "Algorithm 1 Token-level maximal coupling" (SpecTr notation: draft $p$, target $q$): compute $p^{\rm res}(x)=\frac{q(x)-\min\{p(x),q(x)\}}{1-\sum_{x'}\min\{p(x'),q(x')\}}$, sample $\eta\sim U(0,1)$, return $Y=X$ if $\eta\le\min(1,\frac{q(X)}{p(X)})$, otherwise $Y\sim p^{\rm res}$. "In speculative decoding, this is achieved via recursively applying a token-level maximal coupling algorithm"; and
  $$\Pr(Y=X)=\sum_{x\in\Omega}\min(p(x),q(x))=1-d_{\rm TV}(p,q).$$
  With the indicator cost $c(x,y)=\mathbb{1}\{y\ne x\}$ the optimal-transport problem over couplings $\Pi(p,q)$ is solved by this coupling: "which is achieved by the maximal coupling between $p$ and $q$ ... And hence speculative sampling achieves the optimal cost with one draft token." — [arXiv:2310.15141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15141)
- **Same fact from the communication viewpoint [V].** Daliri, Musco, Suresh: "by sampling from an optimal coupling between the distributions, Alice and Bob can achieve $\Pr[a=b]=1-D_{\rm TV}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})$"; in speculative decoding "Alice actually communicates the entire distribution she used to sample $\tilde t_{i+j}$, which allows Bob to sample in an optimally coupled way \[Leviathan et al., Algorithm 1\]." — [arXiv:2408.07978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07978)
- **Why not plain rejection sampling [V].** Leviathan Appendix A.2: the one-shot rejection-sampling variant has accept probability $\sum_x p(x)\min_{x'}\frac{q(x')}{p(x')}\le\alpha$. — [arXiv:2211.17192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192)
- **Why naive repetition over several drafts fails [V].** SpecTr: with $p=\mathrm{Ber}(1)$ (draft) and $q=\mathrm{Ber}(1/2)$ (target), applying the acceptance test to each of $k$ drafts gives "the probability of getting a $1$ will be at least $1-1/2^k$ and hence the output distribution would not be $\text{Ber}(1/2)$ for $k>1$." — [arXiv:2310.15141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15141)

### Inferences
- [mine] Sequence-level correctness follows by induction on positions: conditional on an accepted prefix, the next emitted token (accepted draft or resampled) has the target conditional law by the single-token result; after a rejection the step stops and the next step restarts from a prefix that is already distributed as the target. Rejected draft tokens beyond the first rejection are discarded, so they cannot bias later tokens.
- [mine] Upper bound for any coupling: $\Pr(Y=X)=\sum_x\Pr(X=x,Y=x)\le\sum_x\min(q(x),p(x))$, because the joint mass on $(x,x)$ cannot exceed either marginal. This one line is the optimality proof for one draft token.
- [mine] SpecTr's displayed Eq. (2) reads "$\min_{\pi}\Pr(Y\ne X)=\sum_{x}\min(p(x),q(x))$" in the arXiv v2 source; the right-hand side is the maximal agreement probability, and the minimal cost is $1-\sum_x\min(p(x),q(x))=d_{TV}(p,q)$, as the paper's own statement of $\Pr(Y=X)$ shows. Treat the display as a typo.

### Gaps
- None of the two founding papers states the coupling inequality as an optimality theorem; the optimality statement is in SpecTr (optimal transport) and Yin et al. (lower bound on rejections).

## 5. SpecTr (Sun, Suresh, Ro, Beirami, Jain, Yu) — "SpecTr: Fast Speculative Decoding via Optimal Transport"

### Takeaway
Token-level draft selection is an optimal-transport problem between the law of the draft tokens and the target law with "membership cost" $\mathbb{1}\{y\notin S(x)\}$; with one draft it is the maximal coupling, with $k$ i.i.d. drafts the optimum is a linear program with $|\Omega|^{k+1}$ variables. The practical $k$-sequential selection rule accepts draft $i$ with probability $\min(1,q(X_i)/(\rho\,p(X_i)))$, is exactly lossless for $\rho\ge\rho^*$, and achieves at least $(1-1/e)$ of the optimal acceptance; with 8 drafts on PALM-2 the wall-clock speedup is 2.13× against 1.56× for one draft at $L=8$.

### Cited Findings
- **(a) [V].** Ziteng Sun*, Ananda Theertha Suresh* (equal contribution), Jae Hun Ro, Ahmad Beirami, Himanshu Jain, Felix Yu; all "Google Research, New York". NeurIPS 2023. arXiv 2310.15141, v1 23 Oct 2023, v2 18 Jan 2024. No code link in the paper. — [arXiv:2310.15141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15141)
- **(b) Questions posed [V].** "What is the relationship between speculative decoding and the broader literature of sampling in statistics? Is speculative decoding optimal in an information-theoretic sense? ... would it be possible to use parallelization along batch (number of drafts) to further improve decoding speed?" — [arXiv:2310.15141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15141)
- **Computational model [V].** One call of the large model computes all next-token distributions for all positions and for up to $K$ contexts "with $O(Kt^2)$ computation and $O(1)$ time". "While this approach incurs the same computation cost as vanilla decoding (under the simplified computational model assumed in this paper), it can significantly improve decoding latency due to parallelization." — [arXiv:2310.15141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15141)
- **(c1) Optimal transport with membership cost (OTM), SpecTr notation ($p$ draft, $q$ target) [V].** $\mathcal{X}=\Omega^k$, $\mathcal{Y}=\Omega$, cost $c(x,y)=\mathbb{1}\{y\notin S(x)\}$ where $S(x)$ is the set of distinct elements of $x$; "When $k=1$, it recovers the indicator cost". Transport cost $C(\pi)=\Pr_{X,Y\sim\pi}(Y\notin S(X))$, "the rejection probability"; acceptance $\alpha(\pi)=1-C(\pi)$. Problem: $\min C(\pi)$ s.t. $\pi\in\Pi(p^{\otimes k},q)$; optimum $\alpha_k(p,q)$. Validity: for any coupling $\pi\in\Pi(P,Q)$, "If $X\sim P$, then $f_\pi(X)\sim Q$". — [arXiv:2310.15141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15141)
- **(c2) Properties and cost of the exact solution [V].** Lemma 1: monotonicity $\alpha_k(p,q)\le\alpha_{k+1}(p,q)$; consistency: if $q(x)/p(x)$ is bounded then $\lim_{k\to\infty}\alpha_k(p,q)=1$, otherwise the limit is $\sum_x\mathbb{1}\{p(x)>0\}q(x)$. The linear program "has $|\Omega|^{k+1}$ variables and $|\Omega|^k+|\Omega|$ equality constraints"; Lemma 2: solvable "in time $O(|\Omega|^{O(k)})$". Appendix bound: $(1-(1-1/k)^k)\,\bar\alpha_k(p,q)\le\alpha_k(p,q)\le\bar\alpha_k(p,q)$ with an explicit $\bar\alpha_k$. — [arXiv:2310.15141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15141)
- **(c3) $k$-sequential selection (K-SEQ), Algorithm 2 [V].** Inputs: $p,q$, samples $X_1,\dots,X_k\sim_{\rm i.i.d.}p$, division factor $\rho\in[1,k]$ (the source macro prints it as $\rho$; later papers sometimes write $\gamma$). Let $\beta_{p,q}(\rho)=\sum_{x\in\Omega}\min(p(x),q(x)/\rho)$ and $p_{\rm acc}=1-(1-\beta_{p,q}(\rho))^k$. For $i=1..k$: sample $\eta_i\sim U(0,1)$ and return $Y=X_i$ if $\eta_i\le\min\big(1,\frac{q(X_i)}{\rho\cdot p(X_i)}\big)$. If none is accepted, return $Y\sim p^{\rm res}$ with
  $$p^{\rm res}(x)=\frac{q(x)-\min\{p(x),\frac{q(x)}{\rho}\}\frac{p_{\rm acc}}{\beta_{p,q}(\rho)}}{1-p_{\rm acc}}.$$
  Theorem 1: let $\rho^*$ solve $1-(1-\beta_{p,q}(\rho))^k=\rho\,\beta_{p,q}(\rho)$. "When $\rho\ge\rho^*$, the coupling ... is a valid transport plan from $p^{\otimes k}$ to $q$. When $\rho=\rho^*$, we have $\alpha(\pi^{\textsc{k-Seq}}_{\rho^*})\ge(1-e^{-1})\alpha_k(p,q)$. Moreover, $\rho^*$ can be computed up to accuracy $\delta$ in time $O(|\Omega|\log((k-1)/\delta))$." — [arXiv:2310.15141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15141)
- **(c4) Sequence-level algorithm [V].** $K$ draft sequences of length $L$ sampled i.i.d. from $\mathcal{M}_s$; all conditionals of $\mathcal{M}_b$ computed in one parallel call; a token-level transport plan selects the first token; only the drafts that agree with it are kept; recurse with $L-1$; when a selected token is not among the drafts the step stops; if the last token is accepted an extra token is sampled, so a step yields between 1 and $L+1$ tokens. A prefix-tree draft construction "did not perform better in our experiments". — [arXiv:2310.15141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15141)
- **(d) Guarantee [V].** Theorem 2 (exact target distribution of the emitted tokens, quoted in Section 4). "all three methods effectively generate samples from the same baseline large model, and hence the quality of the two speculative decoding methods is provably neutral to that of the large model. Thus, we will only focus on measuring the speedup". — [arXiv:2310.15141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15141)
- **(e,f) Experiments [V].** Metric: "Block efficiency $=\frac{\text{Total number of decoded tokens}}{\text{Number of serial calls to }\mathcal{M}_b}$". Table 1, LM1B prompts, PALM-2-Gecko as small model and PALM-2-Bison as large model, "averaged over 1000 test prompts and 3 random seeds": speculative decoding $K{=}1,L{=}4$: block efficiency 2.4, wall-clock 1.67; SpecTr $K{=}8,L{=}4$: 3.1, 2.08; speculative $K{=}1,L{=}8$: 2.9, 1.56; SpecTr $K{=}8,L{=}8$: 4.0, 2.13. "when $L=8$ and $K=8$, our proposed SpecTr algorithm has a speedup of 2.13x, a further 1.37x increase compared to speculative decoding ($K=1$)." Sampling temperature and hardware of the PALM-2 runs: not found. Appendix (97M target, 2M/6M/20M drafters, LM1B, temperature 1.0): e.g. 2M drafter, $L{=}4$: block efficiency 1.86 ($K{=}1$) → 2.56 (SpecTr $K{=}8$). — [arXiv:2310.15141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15141)
- **Does parallel scoring really cost one call? [V]** Appendix table, 97M transformer on GPU, latency relative to length 4 and batch 1: length 4: 1.00, 1.01, 1.06, 1.10 for batch 1, 2, 4, 8; length 8: 1.01, 1.09, 1.10, 1.42 ± 0.4. — [arXiv:2310.15141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15141)
- **(g) Caveats [V].** Wall-clock gain is below block efficiency because "(1) The decoding time for $\mathcal{M}_s$ might not be negligible; (2) Parallelization along the batch and time axis might increase the time for a single call to $\mathcal{M}_b$; (3) Overhead due to the implementation". Block Verification's authors add that multi-draft methods "also increase the required computation from the large model to verify the drafts, which is undesirable when query batching is performed". — [arXiv:2310.15141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15141); [arXiv:2403.10444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10444)

### Inferences
- [mine] In Leviathan notation the K-SEQ acceptance probability is $\min(1,p(x)/(\rho\,q(x)))$ with $\rho\ge1$: each draft is accepted less often than under the single-draft rule, and losslessness is restored by the residual. Lenience does the opposite (divides the draft probability's weight by $1/l\ge1$, i.e. accepts more often) and gives up exactness. The two formulas are easy to confuse.
- [mine] With $k=1$, $\rho^*=1$ and K-SEQ is the standard rule.

## 6. Block verification (Sun, Mendlovic, Leviathan, Aharoni, Ro, Beirami, Suresh) — "Block Verification Accelerates Speculative Decoding"

### Takeaway
Verifying the draft block jointly, keeping the longest accepted sub-block instead of stopping at the first rejected token, is still exactly lossless and is proved optimal in expected decoded tokens among all valid verification rules for the standard single-draft loop; token-by-token verification is strictly suboptimal for $\gamma\ge2$ (toy example: 10/9 versus 11/9 accepted tokens). Measured gains are modest: 7%–10% block efficiency and 5%–8% wall-clock at $\gamma=8$, and none at temperature 0.

### Cited Findings
- **(a) Identity and venue [V].** Ziteng Sun, Uri Mendlovic, Yaniv Leviathan, Asaf Aharoni, Jae Hun Ro, Ahmad Beirami, Ananda Theertha Suresh (Google Research). arXiv 2403.10444: v1 15 Mar 2024, v2 25 Jul 2024, v3 10 Apr 2025. Published at ICLR 2025 (poster). The Google Research publications page carries the title "Block level verification accelerates speculative decoding" (page title read on 23 Nov 2026), so both wordings refer to the same work. Code: sketch Python implementations in the appendix; no repository. — [arXiv:2403.10444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10444); [ICLR 2025 poster page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/28852); [Google Research publication page](https://research.google/pubs/block-level-verification-accelerates-speculative-decoding/)
- **(b) Claim [V].** "In prior works, draft verification is performed independently token-by-token. Surprisingly, we show that this approach is not optimal." — [arXiv:2403.10444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10444)
- **Motivating example [V].** Two tokens, context-independent models: $\mathcal{M}_b(\textsc{a})=1/3,\ \mathcal{M}_b(\textsc{b})=2/3,\ \mathcal{M}_s(\textsc{a})=2/3,\ \mathcal{M}_s(\textsc{b})=1/3$, $\gamma=2$, $d_{TV}=1/3$. Token verification: expected accepted tokens $1-1/3+(1-1/3)^2=10/9$. Ideal algorithm with full joint distributions: $12/9$. Block verification: $11/9$. Lemma 1: "The standard token verification algorithm of speculative decoding is not optimal." — [arXiv:2403.10444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10444)
- **(c) Algorithm 2 "Block Verification" (notation: draft block $X^\gamma$, context $c$) [V].** Sample $\eta_1,\dots,\eta_\gamma\sim U(0,1)$; set $\tau=0$, $p_0=1$. For $i=1..\gamma$:
  $$p_i=\min\Big\{p_{i-1}\frac{\mathcal{M}_b(X_i\mid c,X^{i-1})}{\mathcal{M}_s(X_i\mid c,X^{i-1})},\,1\Big\},$$
  $$h^{\rm block}_\gamma=p_\gamma,\qquad h^{\rm block}_i=\frac{\sum_{x'}\max\{p_i\mathcal{M}_b(x'\mid c,X^i)-\mathcal{M}_s(x'\mid c,X^i),0\}}{\sum_{x'}\max\{p_i\mathcal{M}_b(x'\mid c,X^i)-\mathcal{M}_s(x'\mid c,X^i),0\}+1-p_i}\quad(i<\gamma),$$
  and if $\eta_i\le h^{\rm block}_i$ set $\tau=i$, otherwise **continue** (do not break). If $\tau=\gamma$ sample $Y\sim\mathcal{M}_b(\cdot\mid c,X^\gamma)$, else sample $Y$ from
  $$p^{\rm block}_{\rm res}(x\mid c,X^\tau)=\frac{\max\{p_\tau\mathcal{M}_b(x\mid c,X^\tau)-\mathcal{M}_s(x\mid c,X^\tau),0\}}{\sum_{x'}\max\{p_\tau\mathcal{M}_b(x'\mid c,X^\tau)-\mathcal{M}_s(x'\mid c,X^\tau),0\}}.$$
  Return $X^\tau,Y$. "token verification stops as soon as a token is rejected ..., while block verification always operates on the full block. Equivalently, in token verification, $\tau=\arg\min\{\eta_i\le h^{\rm token}_i\}$ while in block verification, $\tau=\arg\max\{\eta_i\le h^{\rm block}_i\}$." The outer loop (draft, parallel scoring, verify, append) is unchanged. — [arXiv:2403.10444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10444)
- **(d) Guarantees [V].** Definition 1: a verification algorithm is valid if speculative decoding with it has output distribution $\mathcal{M}_b^*(\cdot\mid c)$ for all contexts, models and $\gamma$. Theorem 1: "Block verification (Algorithm 2) is a valid draft verification algorithm." Theorem 2: with $N(i)$ the number of decoded tokens after $i$ iterations, "For any valid draft verification algorithm Verify ... we have $\forall c,\mathcal{M}_s,\mathcal{M}_b,\gamma$, and $i$, $\mathbb{E}_{\rm BlockVerify}[N(i)]\ge\mathbb{E}_{\rm Verify}[N(i)]$", in particular $\ge\mathbb{E}_{\rm TokenVerify}[N(i)]$. Proof idea: $p_i(X^i)$ "is the maximum probability that a valid verification algorithm can accept $X^i$". Scope caveat: "there exist verification procedures that force the decoding logic to depend on the previous accept/reject decisions that produce more accepted tokens in average in one iteration. However, this will affect the decoding speed in subsequent iterations"; such a "greedy block verification" is described in an appendix and "doesn't improve over block verification". — [arXiv:2403.10444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10444)
- **(e) Setup [V].** PALM-2-S target with PALM-2-XXS or PALM-2-XXXS drafters; eight datasets (LM1B, GPT Prompt, WebQA, PIQA, ShareGPT, XSum, GSM8K, WMT DeEn); first 1000 prompts, prompt length up to 512, up to 128 output tokens; batch size 1; "temperature of 1.0 for the experiments on PALM-2 models". Spec-Bench with Vicuna-7B-v1.3 target and Vicuna-68M drafter, "a single NVIDIA H100 GPU with a batch size of 1 and a max generation length of 1024", $\gamma=8$, temperatures $\{0.2,0.6,1.0\}$. — [arXiv:2403.10444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10444)
- **(f) Results [V].** Table 1 ($\gamma=8$, PALM-2-XXS drafter, 3 seeds): average block efficiency 3.41 (token) → 3.70 (block), +8.30%; average wall-clock speedup over the baseline 2.30 → 2.45, +6.49%; per-dataset block-efficiency gains from 7.00% (WMT-DeEn) to 10.06% (GPT Prompt); wall-clock gains from 5.36% to 8.14%. - **Temperature [V].** "for temperature of 0, which corresponds to greedy decoding, our algorithm degenerates to token verification and doesn't provide additional speedups. In non-zero temperature settings, the advantage is consistent and the additional improvement is higher for larger temperatures." — [arXiv:2403.10444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10444)
- **Batching versus multi-draft [V].** With batch size $B=8$ and PALM-2-XXS, "while SpecTr and SpecInfer can achieve higher block efficiencies, due to the increased computation to evaluate more candidates, our method achieves better speedup than SpecTr and SpecInfer". — [arXiv:2403.10444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10444)
- **Independent confirmation of optimality [V].** Weng et al. - [mine] I implemented Algorithm 2 as printed and enumerated the toy example exactly (rational arithmetic): token verification gives 10/9 accepted tokens, block verification 11/9, and in both cases the joint law of the first three output tokens equals the target's. - [mine] The printed formula for the token-level residual in the paper's Figure 2 has a missing "$\max\{$" in its denominator; the intended expression is the usual $\mathrm{norm}(\max(\mathcal{M}_b-\mathcal{M}_s,0))$.

## 7. Yin, Chen, Huang, Wang — "A Theoretical Perspective for Speculative Decoding Algorithm" (NeurIPS 2024)

### Takeaway
Without any i.i.d. assumption, the expected number of rejections of speculative decoding over a horizon $T$ equals the sum of expected total-variation distances between draft and target along the target's trajectory, and no unbiased token-level accept/resample rule can do better. If bias is allowed, the minimal total-variation bias for a given acceptance function $b$ has a closed form, and for every $b$ between $\min\{1,q/p\}$ and 1 (Yin notation) rejection probability plus optimal bias equals $d_{TV}(p,q)$: the Pareto front is a straight line.

### Cited Findings
- **(a) [V].** Ming Yin (Princeton University), Minshuo Chen (Northwestern University), Kaixuan Huang, Mengdi Wang (Princeton University). NeurIPS 2024. arXiv 2411.00841 (v1, 30 Oct 2024). No code link in the source. — [arXiv:2411.00841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00841)
- **(b,c) Model [V].** Decoding as a Markov chain on histories; Yin notation: $p$ = draft, $q$ = target, $[q-p]_+$ = normalised $\max\{0,q-p\}$. General "rejection-based decoding" (Algorithm 2): draft until the end (lookahead $K=T$), compute all target logits in one "oracle call", then for each position "Accept $x_n=\tilde x_t$ with prob. $b_t$", else sample from $\mathcal{P}_t$ and break. Speculative decoding is $b_t=\min\{1,\frac{q_t}{p_t}\}$, $\mathcal{P}_t=[q_t-p_t]_+$. Assumption 1: "(1) compared to the large model, the computational cost of the draft/small model is negligible. (2) each oracle call has runtime $O(1)$." Metric: "inference time = # oracle calls = # rejections", hence "inference acceleration $=T/$# rejections". — [arXiv:2411.00841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00841)
- **Theorem 1 (expected rejections; unbiasedness) [V].** With $R_n\in\{0,1\}$ indicating rejection of the $n$-th token and $N_{\rm rej}=\sum_{n=1}^TR_n$:
  $$\mathbb{E}[N_{\rm rej}]=\sum_{n=1}^T\mathbb{E}_{x_{1:n-1}\sim q}\big[d_{TV}\big(p_n(\cdot\mid x_{1:n-1}),q_n(\cdot\mid x_{1:n-1})\big)\big],$$
  and $\mathbb{P}^{\rm SD}(x_{1:T})=q(x_{1:T})$. Remark: Leviathan's $1/(1-\alpha)$ "equals $T/\sum_n^T\mathbb{E}[d_{TV}(p_n,q_n)(\cdot|x_{1:n-1})]$ when \[the expected TV\] is identical for all $n$ ... In contrast, our guarantee holds for the case that allows the sequential dependence between different decoding steps." Simulation: $T=50$ non-stationary chains, theoretical value 16.41 rejections, "acceleration rate is $50/16.41=3.05$". — [arXiv:2411.00841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00841)
- **Theorem 2 (instance-dependent rejection lower bound) [V].** For $\mathcal{F}:=\{\mathcal{A}$: a specification of Algorithm 2 with $\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{A}}_t=q_t\ \forall t\}$,
  $$\inf_{\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{F}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{P}}[N_{\rm rej}]\ \ge\ \sum_{n=1}^T\mathbb{E}_{x_{1:n-1}\sim q}\big[d_{TV}(p_n,q_n)(\cdot\mid x_{1:n-1})\big].$$
  Takeaway as worded: "No rejection improvement can be made in Algorithm 2 by changing the acceptance probability $b_t$ and the distribution $\mathcal{P}_t$ if we want to keep the distribution unbiasedness." and "there is no need to tweak acceptance probability, as it will not make performance better." Relation to SpecTr: "their optimality result can be cast as a special case of ours". — [arXiv:2411.00841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00841)
- **Theorem 3 (batch speculative decoding) [V].** $M$ draft sequences in parallel, verified depth-first: the second draft's first token is tested against the residual $[q-p]_+$, and so on. Define $q^{m+1}=[q^m-p]_+$, $q^1=q$. Then $\mathbb{P}^{\rm Batch}(x_{1:T})=q(x_{1:T})$ and
  $$\mathbb{E}[N_{\rm rej}]=\sum_{n=1}^T\mathbb{E}_{x_{1:n-1}\sim q}\big[d_{TV}[q,p](\cdot\mid x_{1:n-1})\big]-\sum_{n=1}^T\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{x_{1:n-1}\sim f}\Big[d_{TV}(q,p)(x_{1:n-1})-\prod_{m=1}^Md_{TV}(q^m,p)(x_{1:n-1})\Big],$$
  the second sum being the "Batch Improvement", with $f(x_{1:n}):=\mathbb{P}(x_{1:n}\cap\{n\text{-th draft token rejected}\})$. Examples: uniform distributions with $r=V/V'$: $\mathrm{BI}=(1-\frac1r)-(1-\frac1r)^M$; Bernoulli $p\sim\mathrm{Ber}(u)$, $q\sim\mathrm{Ber}(v)$, $u\ge v$: $\mathrm{BI}=|u-v|\,(1-u^{M-1})$. Heuristic: "batch design can significantly reduce the number of rejections when the draft distribution $p$ and target distribution $q$ are different from each other and won't make too much improvement when $p$ and $q$ are close." Proposition (appendix): "$M\to\infty$, $\mathbb{E}[N_{\rm rej}]\nrightarrow0$. This indicates increasing batch size to $\infty$ doesn't help." — [arXiv:2411.00841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00841)
- **Lossy part: the optimisation problem [V].** Motivation: one "may deploy the small model Gemini Nano as the draft model $p$ and Gemini Ultra as the target model $q$ and tune the acceptance probability $b$ ... higher". Bias is measured in total variation. For an algorithm $\mathcal{A}=(b,\mathcal{P})$ with $b$ fixed:
  $$\text{Loss}^*_{TV}(b):=\min_{\mathcal{P}}d_{TV}[\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{A}},q].$$
  — [arXiv:2411.00841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00841)
- **Theorem 4 (optimal solution) [V].** The objective is equivalent to
  $$\text{Loss}^*_{TV}(b)=\frac12\min_{\mathcal{P}}\sum_x\Big|q(x)-b(x)p(x)-\mathcal{P}(x)\sum_{\tilde x}[1-b(\tilde x)]p(\tilde x)\Big|\quad\text{s.t. }\sum_x\mathcal{P}(x)=1,\ \mathcal{P}(x)\ge0.$$
  Suppose $\sum_x[1-b(x)]p(x)>0$ and define $A(x):=\frac{q(x)-b(x)p(x)}{\sum_{\tilde x}[1-b(\tilde x)]p(\tilde x)}$, $A_+=\{x:A(x)\ge0\}$, $A_-=\{x:A(x)<0\}$. "Then the set of optimal distributions ... is characterized as $\{\mathcal{P}^*:\mathcal{P}^*|_{A_-}(\cdot)=0;\ 0\le\mathcal{P}^*|_{A_+}(\cdot)\le A(\cdot)\}$, and the optimal value is
  $$\text{Loss}^*_{TV}(b)=\frac12\sum_x|q(x)-b(x)p(x)|-\frac12\sum_x(1-b(x))p(x)\ \ge0."$$
  Reading: "If $b$ is less than Speculative Decoding threshold, i.e. $b\le\min\{1,q/p\}$ then $A$ becomes a probability distribution and the optimal distribution $\mathcal{P}^*$ equals $A$ which is also unbiased ... If $b$ exceeds the Speculative Decoding threshold, then $A$ is no longer a distribution and there are multiple optimal distributions". — [arXiv:2411.00841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00841)
- **Theorem 5 (Pareto front) [V].** "For any Algorithm $\mathcal{A}$ in the class \[Algorithm 2\] that satisfies $\min\{1,\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}\}\le b(x)\le1,\ \forall x\in\mathcal{V}$. Then
  $$\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{A}}(\text{reject})+\text{Loss}^*_{TV}(b)=d_{TV}[p,q]."$$
  Here $\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{A}}(\text{reject})=1-\sum_xb(x)p(x)$. Text: "the pareto front is a straight line connecting $(0,d_{TV}[p,q])$ and $(d_{TV}[p,q],0)$, which represents a linear relationship between the rejection probability and the optimal $d_{TV}$ deviation"; "$(0,0)$ is the 'perfect algorithm' (no rejection, no bias) which does not exists, and, in particular, $(0,d_{TV}[p,q])$ stands for Speculative Decoding" (the pair is written as (bias, rejection probability)). The paper restricts this to a single token: "we focus on the pareto front between rejection vs. the minimal $d_{TV}$ deviation for a single token." — [arXiv:2411.00841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00841)
- **Experiment [V].** Draft pythia-70m, target pythia-2.8b; acceptance $b(x)=\min\{1,\frac{q(x)+\epsilon}{p(x)}\}$; "Decoding-OPT" (residual $\mathcal{P}^*$) against "Decoding-UNO" (residual $:=q$); 200 prompts from Alpaca-Farm-Eval, 500 comparisons per prompt. Win rate of OPT with the RM-Mistral-7B scorer: 53%, 53.5%, 57.5%, 52.5% for $\epsilon=0.1,0.2,0.4,0.8$; with GPT-4: 54.5%, 53%, 53.5%, 55%. — [arXiv:2411.00841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00841)
- **Follow-ups that restate and use the front [V].** DIVERSED (AISTATS 2026; Leviathan-style notation, $p$ target, $q$ draft): verifying against the static mixture $\nu_t=wp_t+(1-w)q_t$ with accept rule $\min\{1,\nu_t(x)/q_t(x)\}$ and residual $\propto[\nu_t-q_t]_+$ gives $\Pr(\text{accept})=1-w\,TV(q,p)$ and "achieves the Pareto front": $\mathbb{P}_{\nu_t}(\text{reject})+TV(\nu_t,p)=TV(q,p)$. "Speculative Diffusion is a Tale of Two Tradeoffs" (arXiv, Oct 2026), Theorem "Optimality of Linear Acceptance": for the problem $\max_{\tilde p}1-D_{TV}(\tilde p,q)$ s.t. $D_{TV}(\tilde p,p)\le\epsilon$, an optimal target is $\tilde p^\star=(1-\lambda)p+\lambda q$ with $\lambda=\epsilon/D_{TV}(p,q)$, with maximum acceptance $1-D_{TV}(p,q)+\epsilon$. — [arXiv:2604.07622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07622); [arXiv:2610.31819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31819)

### Inferences
- [mine] Check of Theorem 5 from Theorem 4: for $b\ge\min\{1,q/p\}$, tokens with $q\ge p$ have $b=1$ and contribute $q-p$; tokens with $q<p$ contribute $bp-q$. Then $\frac12[\sum_{q\ge p}(q-p)+\sum_{q<p}(bp-q)]-\frac12\sum_{q<p}(1-b)p+\sum_{q<p}(1-b)p=\frac12\sum_{q\ge p}(q-p)+\frac12\sum_{q<p}(p-q)=d_{TV}(p,q)$. The identity holds.
- [mine] Leviathan's lenience is a member of the class of Theorem 5. In Leviathan notation it is $b(x)=\min(1,p(x)/(l\,q(x)))\ge\min(1,p(x)/q(x))$. Hence, with an optimal residual, lenience $l$ sits on the Pareto front: rejection probability $1-\sum_x\min(q(x),p(x)/l)$ and total-variation bias $\sum_x\min(q(x),p(x)/l)-\sum_x\min(p(x),q(x))$ per token.
- [mine] Which residual is optimal for lenience: Theorem 4 requires zero residual mass on tokens where the acceptance already over-shoots the target ($A(x)<0$, i.e. $q(x)>p(x)$ in Leviathan notation) and at most $(p(x)-q(x))/\Pr(\text{reject})$ elsewhere. The standard residual $\mathrm{norm}(\max(0,p-q))$ satisfies both conditions (because $\Pr(\text{reject})\le d_{TV}$), so it is total-variation optimal for lenient acceptance. The residual $\mathrm{norm}(\max(0,p-lq))$, which a literal reading of "multiply $q(x)$ by $l$" might suggest, puts mass on tokens with $lq<p<q$ and is not in the optimal set in general. Neither paper states this.
- [mine] Theorem 5 is a statement about one token under total variation; the per-sequence bias accumulates over positions, and other divergences (KL, as in Cactus, ICLR 2026) give different fronts. Total variation also ignores which tokens receive the excess mass, which is what quality-oriented lossy rules try to control.
- [mine] Scope of Theorem 2. Its class $\mathcal{F}$ contains only token-by-token rules of Algorithm 2 (accept position $t$ with $b_t$, else resample from $\mathcal{P}_t$ and stop). Block verification is outside this class. I checked by exact enumeration on the toy model of Section 6 with full lookahead: token-level speculative decoding has $\mathbb{E}[N_{\rm rej}]=T\cdot d_{TV}=T/3$ exactly, as Theorem 1 predicts, while block verification has $5/9<2/3$ for $T=2$, $7/9<1$ for $T=3$ and $79/81<4/3$ for $T=4$. So "speculative decoding is optimal" in Yin et al. means optimal among token-level rejection rules, not among all lossless verifiers.

### Gaps
- No sequence-level version of the bias/rejection trade-off is proved (a commented-out bound exists in the source but is not part of the paper).
- The experiment measures win rate with judge models, not total variation or task metrics, and the margins are small (52.5%–57.5%).

## 8. Multi-draft theory: Khisti et al. and Hu et al. (both ICLR 2025)

### Takeaway
For $K$ drafts the optimal lossless token-level rule decomposes into importance-weighted selection of one draft token followed by ordinary single-draft speculative sampling, with a closed-form optimum for $K=2$ (Khisti et al.). Via the dual, the optimal acceptance rate is a subset-selection problem solvable in near-linear time for sampling with replacement and for conditional Poisson sampling; without-replacement drafting beats with-replacement drafting, and RRS and K-SEQ sit 1–3 points below the optimum (Hu et al.).

### Cited Findings
- **Khisti et al. — "Multi-Draft Speculative Sampling: Canonical Decomposition and Theoretical Limits" [V].** Ashish Khisti (Qualcomm AI Research and University of Toronto), M. Reza Ebrahimi, Hassan Dbouk, Arash Behboodi, Roland Memisevic, Christos Louizos (Qualcomm AI Research). arXiv 2410.18234 (v1 23 Oct 2024, v2 8 May 2025); "Published as a (spotlight) conference paper at ICLR 2025". Khisti notation: $p$ draft, $q$ target, $K$ drafts. Theorem 1: $P^\star(\mathrm{acc})=\max_{\{\beta_y(x_{1:K})\}}\sum_{y\in\Omega}\min\big(q(y),\sum_{x_1,\dots,x_K}\beta_y(x_{1:K})\prod_{i=1}^Kp(x_i)\big)$ over weights with $\sum_y\beta_y(x_{1:K})=1$ and $\beta_y(x_{1:K})=0$ for $y\notin\{x_1,\dots,x_K\}$; the optimum "can be attained by a two step canonical decomposition: in the first step ... we apply Importance Weighted Sampling ... to output an intermediate token $y\in\{x_1,\ldots,x_K\}$; in the second step we apply a single-draft speculative sampling scheme". "The impact of temperature is non-monotonic"; "the greedy method performs worse when the temperature is $1$". — [arXiv:2502.18779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18779); [ICLR 2025 poster page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/30699)

### Inferences
- [mine] With $n=1$ the subset formula gives $1+\min_H(p(H)-q(H))=1-d_{TV}(p,q)$, the single-draft optimum, so the three papers (SpecTr, Khisti, Hu) are consistent at $K=1$.
- [mine] Khisti's Theorem 3 in the second form and Hu's formula with $Q(H)=q(H)^2$ are the same expression after renaming target and draft, for two i.i.d. drafts; Hu's result extends it to any $n$ with replacement, which is the form conjectured by Khisti et al. (Remark after Theorem 3).

### Gaps
- Both papers analyse a single token position; optimal multi-step (tree) verification is treated only later (Traversal Verification 2025, UniVer 2026; see Section 11).
- Hu et al. report acceptance rates, not wall-clock speedups, in their main table.

### Takeaway
In more than 350 experiments with LLaMA-65B and OPT-66B targets, speculative-decoding throughput is governed by the drafter's latency, which scales with depth rather than parameter count, while the drafter's task accuracy correlates little with its token acceptance; a wide, shallow pruned drafter (5 layers, 796M) doubles throughput over a deeper 1.3B drafter.

## 10. Why verification is cheap in practice, where the cost goes, and the effect of batch size and load

### Takeaway
A transformer scores all draft positions in one forward pass, and at small batch that pass is limited by reading weights and KV cache from memory, not by arithmetic, so scoring $\gamma+1$ positions costs about the same time as producing one token. The price is extra arithmetic for rejected tokens, serial drafter time, a second KV cache, and the bookkeeping needed to discard rejected cache entries and to re-align batched sequences that accepted different numbers of tokens. As batch size or request rate grows the target becomes compute-bound and speculation can increase latency (SmartSpec), unless contexts are long enough for KV-cache loading to dominate again (MagicDec).

### Cited Findings
- **One forward pass scores all positions [V].** Stern et al.: "While the total number of operations performed during decoding is quadratic in the number of predictions, the number of necessarily sequential operations is constant regardless of output length. This allows us to execute the verify substep for a number of positions in parallel without spending additional wall-clock time." — [arXiv:1811.03115](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.03115)
- **Memory-bound, not compute-bound [V].** Google blog: accelerators are "usually capable of hundreds of trillions of operations per second, while their memory bandwidth is usually around just trillions of bytes per second — a couple of orders of magnitude lower"; the Transformer "usually performs only a few operations for every byte read during inference, meaning that there are ample spare computational resources available". Chen et al.: "the latency of parallel scoring of short continuations ... is comparable to that of sampling a single token from the larger target model", with the three-component argument quoted in Section 3. — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/looking-back-at-speculative-decoding/); [arXiv:2302.01318](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01318)

TurboSpec abstract (v3): "speculative decoding may degrade LLM serving performance if added naively without tuning to the incoming requests and the speculation method." — [arXiv:2406.14066v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14066v1); [arXiv:2406.14066 (v3)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14066)
- **Sadhukhan et al., MagicDec (ICLR 2025) [V].** "MagicDec: Breaking the Latency-Throughput Tradeoff for Long Context Generation with Speculative Decoding"; Ranajoy Sadhukhan*, Jian Chen*, Zhuoming Chen, Vashisth Tiwari, Ruihang Lai (Carnegie Mellon University), Jinyuan Shi, Ian En-Hsu Yen (Moffett AI), Avner May (Together AI), Tianqi Chen, Beidi Chen (CMU). arXiv 2408.11049 (v1 20 Aug 2024, v5 2 Apr 2025); ICLR 2025 poster. Cost decomposition: $\frac{T^{SD}_{Avg}}{T_T}=\frac{1}{\Omega(\gamma,\alpha)}\Big(\frac{\gamma\cdot T_D}{T_T}+\frac{T_V(\gamma)}{T_T}\Big)$ with $\Omega(\gamma,\alpha)=\frac{1-\alpha^{\gamma+1}}{1-\alpha}$. Findings: "For small batches, the main performance bottleneck is the parameter loading cost, which can be amortized by the verification process ... At this point, SD becomes effective again"; "on 8xA100, when the sequence length exceeds 4000, speculative decoding achieves speedup, which increases with batch size". Headline (v5): "up to 2.51x speedup for Llama3.1-8B when serving batch sizes ranging from 32 to 256 on various types of hardware and tasks" (table row: SnapKV drafting, 8×H100, prefill 100000, $\gamma=11$, 2.51). Version difference: v1 states "up to 2x speedup for \[LLaMA-2-7B-32K\] and 1.84x for \[LLaMA-3.1-8B\] on 8 A100 GPUs". Experiments use greedy decoding (96 tokens per sequence). Code: the project page links https://github.com/Infini-AI-Lab/MagicDec (link read on the project page; the GitHub page itself was not reachable from this session). — [arXiv:2408.11049](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11049); [v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11049v1); [ICLR 2025 poster page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/30513); [project page](https://infini-ai-lab.github.io/MagicDec)
- **KV cache after a rejection, single sequence [V].** A CPU systems preprint (Yuan, "Accelerating CPU Speculative Decoding via Unified Graph Execution and Transactional KV Isolation", arXiv 2610.28671, 17 Oct 2026, single author, venue not found) names the cost: "When proposed tokens are rejected during target verification, resetting the KV cache requires allocating temporary memory buffers, remapping user-space page tables, or copying cache blocks, introducing substantial memory traffic." Its remedy is to write speculative entries to a scratch region after the verified cache and to move a pointer: "If $m\le K$ tokens are accepted, write pointers commit in-place ($\text{KV}_{\text{ptr}}\leftarrow t+m$). If rejected, a transactional rollback resets $\text{KV}_{\text{ptr}}\leftarrow t$, bypassing memory copies." Reported: llama.cpp speculative "rollback latency" rising "from 12.59 ms to 24.69 ms" for context lengths 128 to 8192 on Llama-3.2 3B (FP16) with a 1B drafter, against 3.74 ms for the pointer reset. — [arXiv:2610.28671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28671)
- **KV cache after a rejection, batched sequences [V].** Zhang, Dey, Mishra, Wu, Li, Zhang (Pennsylvania State University; eBay), "Correctness Forensics for Batch Speculative Decoding: Diagnosing the Ragged Tensor Problem", arXiv 2510.22876 (v4, 31 Aug 2026), "Accepted to Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: EMNLP 2026": "several widely-used implementations silently produce corrupted outputs (repetitive tokens, <unk> symbols) while reporting competitive speed"; "We trace the root cause to the ragged tensor problem: variable token acceptance desynchronizes position IDs, attention masks, and KV-cache across a batch"; realigning the batch after each verification round "grows superlinearly, reaching 40% at batch size 8"; their corrected methods reach "$3\times$ throughput at batch size 8" and "90.8--97.3% exact match against batch-size-1 non-speculative decoding. The residual mismatches are consistent with batch-dependent floating-point effects." — [arXiv:2510.22876](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22876)
- **Verification must really be parallel [V].** Chordiya (UC San Diego), "Lossless but Not Free: An Empirical Anatomy of Speculative Decoding on Consumer Hardware", arXiv 2607.17283 (19 Jul 2026, single author, venue not found): on an Apple-silicon laptop "The best configuration reaches a measured $1.61\times$ wall-clock speedup at $K{=}6$, on an acceptance profile declining from $69.7\%$ at $K{=}1$ to $37.8\%$ at the optimum, while three of five configurations decelerate --- because the draft fails to out-speed a small target, or because the quantized Metal backend executes 'parallel' verification serially"; "speculative decoding pays off only when verification is genuinely batch-parallel and the draft/target latency gap is real." The same preprint checks exactness empirically with a two-sample test over about 9,200 tokens per method ($\chi^2=162.5$, dof $=200$, $p=0.976$). — [arXiv:2607.17283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17283)
- **Batching hurts multi-draft methods first [V].** Block Verification appendix: with query batching "the inference will be less memory bound", and at $B=8$ one-draft block verification beats SpecTr and SpecInfer in speedup despite lower block efficiency. — [arXiv:2403.10444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10444)
- **Temperature and practical speedup [V].** Xia et al. survey (Spec-Bench): "the acceleration effect of all methods decreases with an increase in sampling temperature." Ouyang et al. (Findings of EMNLP 2024): "a consistent trend of diminishing speedup as the decoding temperature increases from $0$ to $1$". — [arXiv:2401.07851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07851); [arXiv:2410.10141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10141)

### Inferences
- [mine] Leviathan's walltime theorem assumes that verifying $\gamma+1$ positions costs the same as one decoding step ($T_V(\gamma)/T_T=1$ in MagicDec's notation). SmartSpec and MagicDec are, in effect, studies of when that ratio departs from 1: it rises with batch size for short contexts (compute-bound) and returns towards 1 for long contexts (KV-bound).
- [mine] The "goodput" of SmartSpec is Eq. (1) of Leviathan divided by a measured step time that depends on batch size and proposed length; it replaces the constant $c$ by a profiled latency model.

### Gaps
- The founding papers (Leviathan, Chen) do not describe how the target's KV-cache entries of rejected draft tokens are discarded; the only explicit descriptions I found are the 2026 systems preprints cited above, one of them single-author and unreviewed.
- SmartSpec has no peer-reviewed venue that I could find (two searches); its v3 changes title and authors. A search result mentions a Berkeley technical report EECS-2025-224 by Xiaoxuan Liu describing TurboSpec, which I did not open [U].
- I found no primary source for the statement, repeated in the commented-out part of the 2510.22876 source, that vLLM's speculative decoding was deprecated in its v1 engine; treat it as unverified.

## 11. Theory results from 2025–2026 that change or refine the picture

### Takeaway
The single-draft token-level story is closed (maximal coupling; block verification optimal for a chain), and recent theory moved to trees, hierarchies, limits of parallelism, and principled lossy rules. Three results matter for a guide: a logarithmic upper bound on tokens per step as a function of verifier capacity (EACL 2026), the linear rejection/bias front being achieved by simply verifying against a mixture of target and draft (AISTATS 2026 and an Oct 2026 preprint), and an unresolved conflict between the ICLR 2026 "Hierarchical Speculative Decoding" claims and the optimality of block verification.

### Cited Findings
- **Speed limit of parallel speculation [V].** Pankratov and Alistarh (ISTA; ISTA and Red Hat AI), "Speculative Decoding Speed-of-Light: Optimal Lower Bounds via Branching Random Walks", arXiv 2512.11718 (12 Dec 2025); EACL 2026 long paper, pages 6404–6418. For a verifier that can process $P$ tokens in parallel and a deterministic drafting algorithm, under an i.i.d. assumption on acceptance probabilities across prefixes, negligible drafting cost and $P\ge1+\mu_{(2)}/\mu^2$: $\mathbb{E}[X]\le\frac{\mu+\mu_{(2)}}{\mu^2}\log(P)+O(1)$, where $X$ is the number of tokens successfully predicted per iteration, "$\mu$ is the expected entropy of the verifier's output distribution, and $\mu_{(2)}$ is the expected second log-moment". The optimal deterministic draft tree contains the $P$ most probable nodes. The authors themselves call the i.i.d. assumption "unreasonable" but simplifying. — [arXiv:2512.11718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11718); [ACL Anthology 2026.eacl-long.301](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.301/)
- **Hierarchies of drafters [V].** Mohri, Kaplan, Schuster, Mansour, Globerson (Harvard, Google Research, Tel Aviv University, Google DeepMind), "Fast Inference via Hierarchical Speculative Decoding", arXiv 2510.19705 (Oct 2025); listed at ICML 2026. Each model verifies the tokens of the next smaller one; Theorem "Correctness of HSD": the output follows the target model for any set of models and parameters. Expected latency per token (under i.i.d. acceptances): $\sum_{i=0}^Kc_i\prod_{j=i}^KR(\alpha_{j-1,j},j)$ with $R(\alpha,K)=\frac{1-\alpha}{1-\alpha^{T_{K-1}+1}}$; the latency-optimal hierarchy is found by reduction to a generalized shortest path problem in time $O(\overline{T}^4K^4\log(\overline{T}K))$. Empirically "up to 1.2× speed-up over the best single-draft baseline" (abstract) and "as much as 1.17× faster" (experiments section), batch size 1, H100 GPUs, early-exit drafters. This realises the "hierarchical version" suggested in Leviathan's discussion. — [arXiv:2510.19705](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19705); [ICML 2026 page](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/75183)
- **Sequence-level verification on trees [V].** Weng et al. (Lenovo; AMSS, CAS), "Traversal Verification for Speculative Tree Decoding", NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2505.12398: leaf-to-root verification, proved lossless; equals block verification on a chain (theorem quoted in Section 6); "an average improvement in acceptance length of 2.2% to 5.7%" over token-level verification. Weng, Hu, Yairi (The University of Tokyo; Lenovo; AMSS, CAS), "UniVer: A Unified Perspective for Multi-step and Multi-draft Speculative Decoding", arXiv 2605.04543 (6 May 2026), listed as a NeurIPS 2026 poster: tree verification as conditional optimal transport, proved lossless and conditionally optimal, "improves acceptance length by 4.2% to 8.5% over standard recursive rejection sampling without replacement". — [arXiv:2505.12398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12398); [arXiv:2605.04543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04543); [NeurIPS 2026 poster page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/155949)
- **Optimal multi-draft sampling made computable [V].** Thomas and Pal, "Global Resolution: Optimal Multi-Draft Speculative Sampling via Convex Minimization", arXiv 2511.15898 (19 Nov 2025); listed at ICLR 2026 (oral and poster pages) under the slightly different title "Global Resolution: Optimal Multi-Draft Speculative Sampling via Convex Optimization": reduces the optimal-transport linear program for $n$ i.i.d. drafts "to a convex optimization problem in at most $V$ variables"; abstract claims "the first multi-draft algorithm with 90% acceptance and under 100 ms of overhead per generated token with negligible deviation from the target model distribution". — [arXiv:2511.15898](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15898); [ICLR 2026 oral page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/oral/10008116)
- **Zhou et al., "Overcoming Joint Intractability with Lossless Hierarchical Speculative Decoding" (ICLR 2026, listed as poster and oral) [V for the claims].** arXiv 2601.05724 (v2, 2 Mar 2026); Yuxuan Zhou, Fei Huang, Heng Li, Fengyi Wu, Tianyu Wang, Jianwei Zhang, Junyang Lin, Zhi-Qi Cheng (independent researchers; Qwen Team, Alibaba; University of Washington); notation as Leviathan. Claims: "a provably lossless verification method"; Theorem: $\mathbb{E}[\tau]_{\rm branch}\ge\mathbb{E}[\tau]_{\rm block}\ge\mathbb{E}[\tau]_{\rm token}$ "where equality holds in both inequalities if and only if $\gamma=1$"; experiments with Qwen2.5-0.5B drafter and 14B/32B/72B targets (GPTQ 8-bit, temperature 1): e.g. GSM8K block efficiency 5.99 (tokenwise), 6.13 (blockwise), 6.30 (HSD) at 14B; "approximately 6.2% in BE and 6.7% in DS" on average; "integrating HSD into EAGLE-3 yields over a 12% performance gain". Code: https://github.com/ZhouYuxuanYX/Hierarchical-Speculative-Decoding. — [arXiv:2601.05724](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05724); [ICLR 2026 oral page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/oral/10010024)
- **Adaptive draft length as optimal stopping [V].** Huang, Guo, Wang, "SpecDec++: Boosting Speculative Decoding via Adaptive Candidate Lengths" (arXiv 2405.19715; "Accepted to COLM 2025"): the choice of candidate length is a Markov decision process whose optimal policy is a threshold rule: stopping is better when $\Pr(\exists\,1\le i\le k,\ Y_i\text{ is rejected}\mid x_{\rm prefix})\ge\frac{c_2+\Delta}{c_1+c_2+\Delta}$, with $c_1=t_{\rm draft}$, $c_2=t_{\rm target}-t_{\rm draft}$. Oracle study (greedy, llama-2-chat 7B/70B, Alpaca): oracle stopping gives 2.92x against 1.90x for the best fixed $K$. Reported: "2.04x speedup on the Alpaca dataset (7.2% improvement over the baseline speculative decoding)", 2.26x on GSM8K, 2.23x on HumanEval. Code: https://github.com/Kaffaljidhmah2/SpecDec_pp. — [arXiv:2405.19715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19715)
- **Closed-form optimal draft length [V].** Zheng and Yang (Pengcheng Laboratory), "Communication-Efficient Collaborative LLM Inference via Distributed Speculative Decoding", arXiv 2509.04576 (v2 7 Nov 2025; "Accepted in the Seventeenth International Conference on Wireless Communications and Signal Processing Oct. 23-25, 2025"). With per-draft-token relative cost $L=b+c$ ("the relative cost of vocabulary transmission and SLM inference with respect to one LLM run"), the speedup is $S_{\rm inf}(\gamma)=\frac{1-\alpha^{\gamma+1}}{[1+\gamma L](1-\alpha)}$ and "The optimal draft length---$\gamma^{*}$ ... takes $\lceil\gamma_0\rceil$ or $\lfloor\gamma_0\rfloor$ if $\gamma_0>1$, and otherwise, $\gamma^{*}=1$", with
  $$\gamma_0=\frac{1}{\ln\alpha}\Big[W_{-1}\Big(-\frac1e\alpha^{\frac1L-1}\Big)+1\Big]-\frac1L,$$
  "where $W_{-1}(\cdot)$ denotes the Lambert $W$ function ($-1$ branch)". Setting $b=0$ ($L=c$) gives the maximiser of Leviathan's Theorem 3.8. — [arXiv:2509.04576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04576)
- **Entropy as a proxy for acceptance [V].** Zhang et al., SVIP (EMNLP 2025, arXiv 2411.18462): from $\beta=1-\mathrm{TVD}(p,q)$ and Pinsker's inequality, $\beta\geqslant1-\sqrt{\frac12\mathbb{KL}(q\|p)}=1-\sqrt{\frac12H_{q,p}-\frac12H_q}$, where $H_{q,p}$ is the cross entropy between draft and target and $H_q$ the draft entropy (the "oracle bound"); "Utilizing this bound for acceptance prediction is infeasible since it requires instantaneous access to the target model", so the method uses the draft entropy alone as a training-free stopping rule; "up to 17% speedup on MT-Bench at 8K context compared with fixed draft lengths". — [arXiv:2411.18462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18462)
- **Drafter-invariant speculative decoding [V].** Daliri, Musco, Suresh, "Coupling without Communication and Drafter-Invariant Speculative Decoding" (arXiv 2408.07978, v4 Aug 2025; venue not found): with shared randomness and no communication (Weighted MinHash or Gumbel sampling) one gets $\Pr[a=b]\ge\frac{1-D_{TV}}{1+D_{TV}}$, optimal in the worst case; the resulting scheme has output "fixed given a fixed random seed, regardless of what drafter is used"; the price is acceptance $\frac{1-D_{TV}}{1+D_{TV}}$ "instead of" $1-D_{TV}$, which "could lead to a roughly $2\times$ reduction" in tokens drafted correctly. Code: https://github.com/majid-daliri/DISD. — [arXiv:2408.07978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07978)
- **Theory for deterministic and relaxed acceptance [V].** Sharma, "When Is a Draft Accepted? A Theory of Acceptance in Speculative Decoding" (arXiv 2606.30265, Jun 2026; independent researcher; venue not found): certificates stating that a small enough divergence between target $p$ and draft $q$ forces acceptance, for greedy, additive, multiplicative, top-$m$ and entropy-thresholded criteria. Theorem "Exact certificate": for a rejection region $\mathcal{R}$ there is a computable $R_p(\mathcal{R})$ such that "$\mathrm{KL}(p\,\Vert\,q)<R_p(\mathcal{R})$ guarantees acceptance under the criterion whose rejection region is $\mathcal{R}$", with $R_p(\mathcal{R})\le\log2$. E.g. "Suppose that $\mathrm{KL}(p\Vert q)\le\epsilon$. If $\gamma_p>\sqrt{2\epsilon}$, then the draft token is always accepted under greedy decoding" ($\gamma_p$ is the target's top-1 margin), asymptotically tight. — [arXiv:2606.30265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30265)
- **Principled lossy rules [V].** Hao and Mou, "Cactus" (ICLR 2026, arXiv 2604.04987; notation $q$ = verifier, $p$ = draft): speculative sampling as constrained optimisation, maximising acceptance subject to $D_f(h\Vert q)\le\delta$; with KL the sampled token's target probability is raised to $\gamma^*=\min\{q(n)+\sqrt{2\delta q(n)(1-q(n))},1\}$; shows typical acceptance implicitly solves a cross-entropy variant; code https://github.com/MANGA-UOFA/Cactus. Wang et al., "DIVERSED" (AISTATS 2026, arXiv 2604.07622): static and learned mixtures on the Yin front (Section 7); code https://github.com/comeusr/diversed. Wang et al., "Revisiting Lossy Verification in Speculative Decoding: Mechanisms, Trade-offs, and Failure Modes" (arXiv 2607.26627, v2 Sep 2026; venue not found): unifies lossy methods into "truncation-based verification and collaborative verification" and argues that "overshoot suppression and supervision quality, matter far more than the linear interpolation between draft and target". — [arXiv:2604.04987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04987); [arXiv:2604.07622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07622); [arXiv:2607.26627](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26627)
- **Temperature [V].** Ouyang et al., "Temperature-Centric Investigation of Speculative Decoding with Knowledge Distillation" (Findings of EMNLP 2024, arXiv 2410.10141): speedup decreases from temperature 0 to 1, "a relative speedup difference of $31\%$ and $29\%$ for offline distillation and online distillation" at distillation temperature 0; distilling the drafter at the decoding temperature helps (differences up to 24%). Block verification's gain grows with temperature and vanishes at 0 (Section 6). The tree verifiers behave the same way: Traversal Verification, "as the temperature decreases ..., the performance gap between token-level verification and Traversal Verification narrows. Conversely, at higher temperatures, Traversal Verification demonstrates more pronounced advantages"; UniVer, "At temperature 0.3, Traversal, Greedy, and UniVer achieve nearly identical acceptance lengths ..., converging toward the same outcome as observed at temperature 0 ..., where the deterministic nature renders these verification methods equivalent." — [arXiv:2410.10141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10141); [arXiv:2403.10444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10444); [arXiv:2505.12398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12398); [arXiv:2605.04543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04543)

### Inferences
- [mine] **Unresolved conflict to flag, not to settle.** Zhou et al. claim a lossless single-chain verifier with strictly more accepted tokens than block verification for $\gamma>1$. Sun et al. (Theorem 2) and Weng et al. (Theorem 2) prove that block verification maximises the expected number of decoded tokens among all valid single-chain verifiers. The HSD paper does not discuss that optimality theorem (the word "optimal" does not occur in its uncommented source). I compared the printed rules: with $r^*$ as defined in HSD (capped prefix ratio), $\min(1,r^*(X_{1:t}))$ equals block verification's $p_t$, HSD's acceptance $h_t=D^*_{\rm Branch}(p,q\mid X_{1:t})/D^*_{\rm Branch}(q,p\mid X_{1:t})$ equals block verification's $h_t^{\rm block}$, and the capped resampling distribution equals $p^{\rm block}_{\rm res}$. An exact rational-arithmetic comparison on 300 random instances (vocabulary 2–3, $\gamma\in\{2,3,4\}$, context-dependent models; 51,398 comparisons) found zero mismatches. The control flow is also the same: HSD's Algorithm 2 scans $t=\gamma,\dots,1$ with independent uniforms, stops at the first $t$ with $h_t\ge\eta_t$, then samples the bonus token if $\tau=\gamma$ and otherwise one token from the capped residual, which is block verification's "largest accepted index" rule. Under my reading, the single-draft HSD rule coincides with block verification, which fits the optimality theorems and does not explain the reported empirical gap (e.g. 6.30 versus 6.13). I may have misread a definition, and I did not run the authors' code. A guide should present block verification's optimality as proved (two independent papers) and HSD's superiority as a claim under question; HSD's extension to multi-draft settings is a separate contribution that this check does not address.
- [mine] The logarithmic bound of Pankratov and Alistarh concerns deterministic (top-probability) tree drafting with verifier capacity $P$; it explains why very wide trees or many drafts give diminishing returns, in line with Yin et al.'s result that batch improvement saturates and with SmartSpec's "prefer batching over speculating".
- [mine] The optimal-draft-length question now has three layers: Leviathan's static $\gamma$ maximising Theorem 3.8 (numerically in the 2023 paper, in closed form with the Lambert $W$ function in Zheng and Yang 2025); the oracle bound $1/(1-\alpha)$ and "up to ~60%" headroom; and SpecDec++'s threshold policy, which is the dynamic-programming answer under a cost model with $c_1,c_2$.
- [mine] I checked the Lambert-$W$ formula numerically against a brute-force integer search on Leviathan's Theorem 3.8 for $\alpha\in\{0.5,\dots,0.9\}$ and $c\in\{0.01,0.02,0.05,0.1\}$: in all 20 cases the integer optimum is $\lfloor\gamma_0\rfloor$ or $\lceil\gamma_0\rceil$. Examples ($\alpha$, $c$, $\gamma_0$, integer optimum, speedup): (0.6, 0.02, 5.62, 6, 2.17); (0.7, 0.02, 7.61, 8, 2.76); (0.8, 0.02, 11.02, 11, 3.82); (0.8, 0.05, 7.86, 8, 3.09); (0.9, 0.05, 13.29, 13, 4.67). The formula inherits the assumptions of Theorem 3.8 (i.i.d. acceptance, and verifying $\gamma+1$ positions at the cost of one target step); the second stops being true as $\gamma$ or batch size grows (Section 10), so measured optima are usually shorter (Chen et al.: XSum optimal at $K=3$; Block Verification: wall-clock peak at $\gamma=4$ or $6$).
- [mine] Temperature, put together. At temperature 0 every lossless scheme collapses to exact-match verification, so the per-token acceptance is the probability that the two argmaxes agree and the improved single-draft verifiers (block, traversal, UniVer) cannot add anything; multi-draft sampling with replacement does not help either, since, as Hu et al. put it, "When $T=0$, these methods essentially have only one effective draft token", whereas drafting without duplicates still helps because such methods "always select the top $n$ tokens on the draft model" ([arXiv:2502.18779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18779)). Raising the temperature lowers the per-token acceptance in the reported measurements (Leviathan Table 3: 0.75 → 0.62 for T5-small on EnDe; Spec-Bench; Ouyang et al.), and it is exactly there that sequence-level and multi-draft verification recover part of the loss (block verification +3.7% at $T=0.2$ versus +8.7% at $T=1.0$). No paper I read gives a theorem for acceptance as a function of temperature; Hu et al. only observe that "The impact of temperature is non-monotonic" for multi-draft acceptance.
- [mine] For lossy decoding, Yin's front, DIVERSED's Proposition and the Oct 2026 "Optimality of Linear Acceptance" theorem say the same thing: under a per-token total-variation budget $\epsilon$, the best possible acceptance is $1-d_{TV}+\epsilon$, reached by running exact speculative sampling against a mixture of target and draft. Cactus and "Revisiting Lossy Verification" argue that total variation is the wrong yardstick for quality, which is why non-linear rules can do better on task metrics.

### Gaps
- Venue not found (searched on 23 Nov 2026) for: Sharma (2606.30265), Revisiting Lossy Verification (2607.26627), Speculative Diffusion (2610.31819), Daliri et al. (2408.07978; the Google Research publication page lists it with a 2025 date and no venue, per a search summary [U]).
- The closed-form optimal $\gamma$ comes from a wireless-communications conference paper (WCSP 2025) whose proof I did not check line by line; I only verified the formula numerically.
- No impossibility result for stochastic (non-deterministic) drafting comparable to the EACL 2026 bound was found.
- The conflict between HSD (ICLR 2026) and the optimality of block verification is unresolved by any published source I found; no erratum, comment or follow-up addressing it turned up in my searches.

## 12. Overviews to cite: two surveys and the Google Research retrospective

### Takeaway
The ACL Findings 2024 survey supplies the standard Draft / Verify / Correct formalisation and the Spec-Bench benchmark; the 2025 survey widens the frame to "generation-refinement" across modalities and systems; the December 2024 Google blog is the authors' own short account, confirming production use in AI Overviews and listing the follow-ups they consider important.

### Cited Findings
- **Xia et al. survey [V].** "Unlocking Efficiency in Large Language Model Inference: A Comprehensive Survey of Speculative Decoding"; Heming Xia (The Hong Kong Polytechnic University), Zhe Yang, Qingxiu Dong, Peiyi Wang (Peking University), Yongqi Li, Tao Ge (Microsoft Research Asia), Tianyu Liu (Alibaba Group), Wenjie Li, Zhifang Sui. "ACL 2024 Findings (Long Paper), camera-ready version"; arXiv 2401.07851 (v3, 4 Jun 2024). Paper list: https://github.com/hemingkx/SpeculativeDecodingPapers. Definition: "Speculative Decoding is a Draft-then-Verify decoding paradigm in which, at each decoding step, it first efficiently drafts multiple future tokens and then verifies all these tokens in parallel using the target LLM to speed up inference." Formal framework (survey notation: $\mathcal{M}_q$ target, $\mathcal{M}_p$ drafter, $K$ drafted tokens): $p_1,\dots,p_K=\textsc{Draft}(x_{\le t},\mathcal{M}_p)$, $\tilde x_i\sim p_i$; $q_i=\mathcal{M}_q(x\mid x_{\le t},\tilde x_{<i})$, $i=1,\dots,K+1$; each token is tested by $\textsc{Verify}(\tilde x_i,p_i,q_i)$; the first failing token is replaced by $\textsc{Correct}(p_c,q_c)$ and later drafts discarded; if all pass, an extra token is sampled from $q_{K+1}$. Verification taxonomy: greedy ($\tilde x_i=\arg\max q_i$), speculative sampling ($r<\min(1,q_i(\tilde x_i)/p_i(\tilde x_i))$, correction $\mathrm{norm}(\max(0,q_c-p_c))$), token-tree verification; approximate criteria are listed for both (SpecDec's top-k, BiLD's rollback, Leviathan's lenience). Spec-Bench: 80 instances from each of MT-bench, WMT14 DE-EN, CNN/Daily Mail, Natural Questions, GSM8K, DPR; Vicuna-7B at FP16 on one RTX 3090, batch size 1; under greedy settings EAGLE "achieves the highest speedup ratio (1.8×∼2.4×)"; "the acceleration effect of all methods decreases with an increase in sampling temperature." — [arXiv:2401.07851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07851)

### Inferences
- [mine] The survey's framework is convenient for a guide because lossy methods differ only in the choice of $\textsc{Verify}$ and $\textsc{Correct}$; exact speculative sampling is the pair $(\min(1,q/p)\text{ test},\ \mathrm{norm}(\max(0,q-p)))$ in the survey's notation.
- [mine] The blog's "guaranteeing optimality" is the single-draft, token-level optimality (maximal coupling); the same authors co-wrote the block-verification paper showing that token-by-token verification of a block is not optimal.
- [mine] Remember the notation flip when quoting: the survey, Chen, SpecTr, Yin and Khisti write $q$ for the target; Leviathan, Hu (2025), HSD and DIVERSED write $p$ for the target.

### Gaps
- Neither survey is used here as a source of numbers for other papers; all figures above come from the original papers.
- The Hu et al. survey has no venue I could find (a search on 23 Nov 2026 returned only the arXiv record), and I read only its abstract, author block and section outline.
- The Xia et al. survey explains the drop of speedup with temperature by "the increased computational complexity of the speculative sampling criterion at higher temperatures"; the primary papers read here point to lower acceptance instead (Section 11). I did not find a measurement that separates the two explanations.
