11. Exercises

Exercise 1 — A rule that loses on both counts ★

$p=(0.5,\,0.3,\,0.2)$, $q=(0.2,\,0.2,\,0.6)$. A rule accepts the drafted token if and only if $p(x)\ge0.25$, and writes a fresh sample of $p$ otherwise. Compute its acceptance, its output and its bias. Compare with the exact rule.

Hint

$\text{out}(v)=q(v)b(v)+(1-A)\,p(v)$ with $b=(1,1,0)$.

Solution

$A=0.2+0.2=0.4$. Output: $(0.2+0.6\times0.5,\ 0.2+0.6\times0.3,\ 0.6\times0.2)=(0.5,\,0.38,\,0.12)$. Bias: $\frac12(0+0.08+0.08)=0.08$.

The exact rule has acceptance $1-\TV(p,q)=1-0.4=0.6$ and bias $0$. The threshold rule accepts less and is biased: it rejects every draft of the third token, which the exact rule keeps one time in three, and its fresh samples of $p$ refill the second token beyond its target ($0.38$ for $0.3$). A relaxed-looking rule is not automatically a relaxation.

Exercise 2 — The mixture is on the front ★★

Let $\pi=w\,p+(1-w)\,q$ with $w\in[0,1]$, sampled by the exact test toward $\pi$. Show that the rejection probability is $w\,\TV(p,q)$, that the bias is $(1-w)\,\TV(p,q)$, and that the residual is the same as for the exact rule toward $p$.

Hint

$\pi-q=w\,(p-q)$ and $\pi-p=(1-w)\,(q-p)$.

Solution

The exact test toward $\pi$ rejects with probability $\TV(q,\pi)=\frac12\sum_v\abs{\pi(v)-q(v)}=\frac12\sum_vw\,\abs{p(v)-q(v)}=w\,\TV(p,q)$. Its output is $\pi$, at distance $\TV(\pi,p)=\frac12\sum_v(1-w)\abs{q(v)-p(v)}=(1-w)\TV(p,q)$ from the target. The sum is $\TV(p,q)$: on the front, for every $w$. The residual is $\pos{\pi-q}=w\,\pos{p-q}$ renormalised, i.e. $\pos{p-q}$ renormalised.

Exercise 3 — Lenience, token by token ★★

$p=(0.4,\,0.3,\,0.2,\,0.1)$, $q=(0.1,\,0.2,\,0.3,\,0.4)$, lenience $\lambda=0.5$. Classify the four tokens (under-proposed, always accepted, capped), compute the acceptance, $\Delta$ and the output, and check the cap.

Hint

Caps $p/\lambda=(0.8,\,0.6,\,0.4,\,0.2)$; use the three cases of §3.

Solution

Tokens 1 and 2 are under-proposed ($q\le p$). Token 3: $p=0.20.2$: capped at $0.2$. Acceptance $A=0.1+0.2+0.3+0.2=0.8$; $\TV(p,q)=0.4$, so $\Delta=\frac{0.2}{0.4}=\frac12$. Output: $(0.1+\frac12\times0.3,\ 0.2+\frac12\times0.1,\ 0.3,\ 0.2)=(0.25,\,0.25,\,0.3,\,0.2)$. Ratios $\text{out}/p=(0.625,\,0.83,\,1.5,\,2)$: none above $1/\lambda=2$, and token 4 is exactly at the cap. Bias $=\frac12(0.15+0.05+0.1+0.1)=0.2=A-(1-\TV(p,q))$. (This is the first line of the table of §7.)

Exercise 5 — How much longer may the answers get? ★★

An exact method accepts $\bar\ell=3$ drafts per round; a relaxed rule accepts $\bar\ell^{*}=4.4$ with the same $\ga$ and $c$. By what factor may the completions grow before the relaxed rule becomes slower than the exact one?

Hint

Use the formula of §10 for both methods; the denominators $1+\ga c$ cancel.

Solution

Exact: $S=\frac{\bar\ell+1}{1+\ga c}$ (same length as the target). Relaxed: $S^{*}=\frac{1}{\rho}\cdot\frac{\bar\ell^{*}+1}{1+\ga c}$ where $\rho$ is the length ratio. $S^{*}>S$ if and only if $\rho<\frac{\bar\ell^{*}+1}{\bar\ell+1}=\frac{5.4}{4}=1.35$. Beyond 35% of extra length the relaxed rule is slower. The 360-cell study of §10 measured an average of 54% at the loosest settings.

Exercise 6 — At temperature 0 every rule is on the front ★★★

The target decodes greedily: its distribution is the point mass $\delta_a$ on $a=\argmax p$. A rule accepts the drafted token $x\sim q$ if and only if $x\in S$, with $a\in S$, and writes $a$ otherwise. Compute the acceptance and the bias $\TV(\text{out},\delta_a)$, and show that rejection + bias $=\TV(\delta_a,q)$ whatever $S$. What does this say about the theorem of §2 in the greedy regime?

Hint

$\TV(\delta_a,q)=1-q(a)$. The output is $x$ if $x\in S$ and $a$ otherwise.

Solution

Acceptance: $A=q(S)$. Output: $\text{out}(v)=q(v)$ for $v\in S\setminus\{a\}$, $\text{out}(a)=q(a)+1-q(S)$, and $0$ elsewhere. Bias: the output differs from $a$ with probability $q(S\setminus\{a\})$, so $\TV(\text{out},\delta_a)=q(S)-q(a)$. Then rejection + bias $=1-q(S)+q(S)-q(a)=1-q(a)=\TV(\delta_a,q)$.

Every accept set gives a rule on the front. In the greedy regime the theorem is true and useless: it cannot separate top-2 from a margin rule or from typical acceptance, since all pay exactly one point of disagreement per point of acceptance. What separates them is the quality of the tokens in $S$, which is not a property of the two distributions. This is why §4–§6 argue about sets, and why page 4 replaces the set by a learned judgment.
