12. What to remember

Summary
  • A token-level lossy rule is a pair (acceptance probability, replacement distribution), or equivalently a surrogate target $\pi$ sampled with the exact test. Its acceptance and its bias follow.
  • Exchange rate: rejection probability + $\TV(\text{out},p)\ge\TV(p,q)$. The rules that reach equality sample a $\pi$ that lies between $q$ and $p$ with the exact test. Total variation cannot rank them: they differ in which tokens receive the extra mass.
  • Lenience multiplies $q$ by $\lambda\le1$ in the test: on the front, with a cap $p/\lambda$ on every token; proposed without any quality measurement.
  • With greedy decoding a rule is an accept set: top-$k$, a margin below the best, a probability floor (BiLD), an entropy-adaptive floor (typical acceptance). All are “on the front”; what matters is the quality of the tokens let in.
  • Mentored decoding and CACTUS maximise acceptance under a Kullback–Leibler budget; the first keeps the output in a band $[p/\beta,\,p/\alpha]$.
  • Fuzzy speculative decoding and speculative cascades decide per position whether to trust the drafter (distributions close; drafter at least as confident). Mixtures blend the two models.
  • Independent evaluations (2026): rules that cap the deviation from $p$ keep accuracy and gain little; rules that lean on the drafter lose accuracy with weak drafters and hard tasks; relaxed outputs get longer, so accepted tokens per round overstate the speed.

Reflexes

  • A relaxed rule → write its surrogate target $\pi$ (or its accept set). Acceptance is $1-\TV(q,\pi)$ at best; bias is the distance from $\pi$ to $p$.
  • “We accept $x$% more tokens” → at least $x$ points of total variation per position were paid. Ask where they went.
  • A rule that resamples from $p$ (or writes its argmax) after a rejection, with sampling → it is below the front.
  • A guarantee “per token” or “per position” → multiply by the length before being reassured.
  • A cap relative to $p$ (lenience, mentored, top sets) → rare tokens stay rare. An interpolation or a switch to $q$ → they do not.
  • Results on GSM8K, CNN/DailyMail or XSum only → not evidence for a hard task.
  • Tokens per round under a relaxed rule → ask for the completion length and the end-to-end time.
  • A weak or uncalibrated drafter → no relaxed rule is safe; the exact rule is.

Glossary

surrogate target
The distribution $\pi$ a lossy method is willing to sample instead of $p$.
bias
Distance between the output distribution and the target's, at one position.
Pareto front
Here: the pairs (bias, rejection probability) with rejection + bias $=\TV(p,q)$.
lenience
Dividing the drafter's probability by a constant in the acceptance test.
accept set
For a deterministic rule: the tokens accepted when drafted. Also “allowed set”.
overshoot
A token the drafter proposes more often than the target wants, $q(x)>p(x)$.
deferral rule
A test that decides to hand the position over to the large model.
cascade
A system that answers with a small model and defers to a large one when a rule says so.
truncation sampling
Sampling restricted to an allowed set of tokens (top-$k$, nucleus, min-$p$, $\eta$-sampling).
length inflation
Ratio of the average completion length under a relaxed rule to that of the target.
