6. The bill: what a judge can cost

A judge is wrong sometimes, and an answer contains many mismatches. One harmful token let through is enough to change the answer, so the judge's false-accept rate is multiplied by the number of opportunities. Judges also carry their training task with them, and what they let through first is not accuracy but safety.

Errors compound: a toy model

To see why the false-accept rate matters so much, take a deliberately simple model (ours, for intuition; the numbers are not measurements).

Toy model of judged verification
  • A drafted token is a mismatch with probability $\mu$ (so the exact rule accepts with rate $\al=1-\mu$).
  • A share $h$ of the mismatches is harmful: accepting one spoils the answer. The rest are harmless.
  • The judge accepts a harmless mismatch with probability $a$ (its usefulness) and a harmful one with probability $f$ (its false-accept rate).
  1. A drafted token is accepted with probability $\ \al'=1-\mu\,\big(1-a(1-h)-f\,h\big)$.Rejected only if it is a mismatch that the judge refuses; the judge accepts a mismatch with probability $a(1-h)+fh$.
  2. Tokens per target call: $\ \dfrac{1-\al'^{\,\ga+1}}{1-\al'}$.The formula of page 1, §4 with the new acceptance rate.
  3. An answer of $n$ tokens contains at least one accepted harmful token with probability $\ 1-(1-\mu\,h\,f)^{n}$.Each token is, independently, an accepted harmful mismatch with probability $\mu hf$.
Worked example (toy numbers)

$\mu=0.15$, $h=0.15$, drafts of $\ga=25$ tokens, answers of $n=200$ tokens. The exact rule gives $\frac{1-0.85^{26}}{0.15}\approx6.6$ tokens per target call.

An accuracy loss “under 1%” means, in this model, a false-accept rate of about two per thousand.

A judge is a classifier, and “harmless” is not a property of a token: it depends on what the answer is for. In a proof, a digit matters and a connective does not; in a poem it may be the reverse. The training-free rule of §5 avoids the problem of training data, not the underlying one: its two signals (entropy, agreement of the following tokens) are also proxies.

Safety degrades before accuracy

The authors of Judge Decoding had flagged the risk (§2): a judge might accept, from the drafter, tokens that the target “would otherwise never produce”. A 2026 study measured it for six lossy rules, including FLy and four rules of page 3.

In the paper Zhang, Wang, Gong, Yang, “Secure Speculative Decoding for Large Language Models”, accepted at IEEE S&P 2027, arXiv 2610.08678

Small drafters refuse harmful requests far less often than their targets: on the study's jailbreak set, attacks fail 19.5% of the time against Qwen3-0.6B and 89.5% against Qwen3-8B. With lossy verification, “For Qwen3-0.6B/8B, the average PRR is 0.744 on the two security benchmarks, Jailbreak and Prompt Injection, compared with 0.905 on the two utility benchmarks, HumanEval and GSM8K.” And: “Security PRR drops much earlier […] utility PRR on GSM8K and HumanEval remains close to the baseline until the verifier becomes highly aggressive, e.g., at ARs around 0.7. In contrast, security PRR often starts to decline at much lower ARs, e.g., around 0.3.”

PRR
Performance retention ratio: where the lossy system stands between the drafter alone and the target alone, on a given benchmark (1 = the target's level). On security benchmarks, “performance” is the rate at which attacks fail.
AR
The acceptance rate of drafted tokens, raised by loosening the rule.
the explanation given
“draft tokens accepted at early decoding positions have a disproportionate effect because they set the initial direction of the response.” A refusal is decided in the first few tokens; if the drafter's “Sure, here is” is accepted, the target continues a compliant answer.

The numbers are averages over each rule's range of settings, not results at recommended settings, and the study proposes a fix (apply the exact rule to the first tokens of the response, the lossy rule afterwards). The lesson for this page is about evaluation: a judge validated on accuracy benchmarks has not been validated. This is the general price of leaving exactness, stated on page 3: every property of the target must be measured again.
