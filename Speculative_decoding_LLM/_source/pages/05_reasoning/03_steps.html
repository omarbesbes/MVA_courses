3. SpecReason: the large model as a critic of steps

The small model writes each thinking step; the large model reads it and answers with a single token, a mark from 0 to 9; steps below the pass mark are regenerated by the large model. No reward model is needed, and the large model's slow token-by-token generation is avoided on the accepted steps.

SpecReason

Pan, Dai, Zhang, Oliaro, Jia, Netravali · NeurIPS 2025 · arXiv 2504.07891

lossytraining-freestep level · large model reads, does not write2025

What it tries
Use the fact that intermediate reasoning tolerates approximation: a thinking step needs to be useful, not identical to the large model's.
How it decides
After each drafted step “the base model is prompted to generate a single-token utility score (e.g., an integer from 0 to 9)”; the step is accepted if the score reaches a fixed threshold (for example 7), otherwise “discarded and regenerated by the base model”.
What it achieves
Reasoning models of 32B with 1.5B drafters, on AIME, MATH500 and GPQA, with a budget of 8192 tokens: “$1.4-3.0\times$ speedup over vanilla LRM inference while improving accuracy by $0.4-9.0\%$”; 36% to 80% of the steps come from the small model. Combined with token-level speculative decoding on the regenerated steps: a further 8.8% to 58% latency reduction.
What it costs
No guarantee. The accuracy gain depends on the token budget (see below). An independent re-measurement without the tight budget finds accuracy losses of 2 to 12 points for the same scoring rule.

Where does a gain in accuracy come from, if steps of a small model are substituted for those of a large one? The authors give the reason themselves:

In the paper Pan et al. (SpecReason), §5.2

“The key reason behind this accuracy improvement is the reduction in token consumption required for reasoning […] the small model is generally less verbose than the base model, and because SpecReason adopts many speculated steps from the small model, its token consumption is also reduced by 1.2×–2.0×”. “The effect of token reduction on accuracy is the most significant for tighter output token budgets (16.2% at 4096 tokens) but shrinks as the base model is allowed to generate more thinking tokens (4.7% at 8192 tokens)”.

The large reasoning model, cut off at 8192 tokens, sometimes does not finish; the mixture, being terser, does. Remove the budget and the picture changes. The authors of Lookahead Reasoning (§4) re-ran the scoring rule with a 32K-token limit:

Reflex

When a lossy method reports an accuracy above the large model's → look for a token budget, a time limit, or a noisy baseline. A filter over a weaker model's output does not usually beat the stronger model on equal terms.

4. Lookahead Reasoning: keep the large model's steps, parallelise them

The small model drafts several future steps. The large model writes its own version of each of those steps at the same time, each conditioned on the drafts before it. A verifier keeps a drafted step only if it says the same thing as the large model's version. Latency falls; compute rises; the answer stays the large model's, up to rewording.

Lookahead Reasoning

Fu, Ge, Shao, Deng, Zhang (UCSD, Shanghai Jiao Tong, UIUC) · NeurIPS 2025 · arXiv 2506.19830

lossy (semantic equivalence)training-freestep level · large model writes every step2025

What it tries
Go past the $\frac1{1-\al}$ ceiling of token-level speculation by speculating on steps, without giving up the large model's content: aim at “step-level equivalence” with the target.
How it decides
Draft $\ga$ steps with the small model. In one batched pass the large model generates its own step $s_j$ for every position $j$, assuming the drafts before $j$. A verifier (by default a 7B model used as a judge) says whether the draft $\hat s_j$ is semantically equivalent to $s_j$. Output: the drafts up to the first “no”, then the large model's step.
What it achieves
DeepSeek-R1-distilled 32B with a 1.5B draft: alone, 1.14× to 1.71× depending on the benchmark; token-level speculative decoding (an n-gram drafter) alone, 1.25× to 1.53×; both together, 1.51× to 2.11×. Accuracy within about −2.1 / +1.0 points of the target (for example GSM8K 92.8 against 91.8, AIME24 69.2 against 70.8).
What it costs
More compute, not less: the large model writes every step, including those thrown away after a rejected draft; needs spare parallel capacity. The verifier is one more model, and its mistakes are the method's loss.

Read the mechanism against page 1. It is the same idea one level up: there, the large model computes its distribution at every drafted position in parallel and compares token by token; here it writes its own step at every drafted position in parallel, and a verifier compares step by step. Equality of tokens becomes equivalence of meaning. This is why the method is on the speculative side of §1 (the large model produces everything that matters) and why its accuracy tracks the target's — at the price of the extra work.

The two step-level methods of §3 and §4 answer the same question — is this drafted step acceptable? — with two different references. SpecReason asks whether the step is good (a mark out of 9): cheap, but a mark is a weak signal, as the re-measurement above shows. Lookahead Reasoning asks whether the step is the same as what the large model would write: reliable, but the large model has to write it.

Relatives, briefly

  • Speculative Thinking (2025): the small model is the main writer, and the large one takes over for a short span after cues of reflection or doubt in the small model's text. Training-free.
  • Speculative chain-of-thought (SCoT, 2025): the small model drafts several complete chains of thought in parallel; one pass of the large model picks one, or rejects them all and reasons itself.
  • SpecSearch (ICML 2025): speculation inside tree search over thoughts; the small model proposes thoughts, and a threshold estimated from the large model's past thoughts decides which are kept.
  • Guided speculative inference (GSI, ICLR 2026) and SPECS (NeurIPS 2026): among several steps proposed by the small model, select using the reward and the large model's likelihood of the step, so that the choice provably approaches the distribution one would get from the large model weighted by the reward. These are the first step-level methods with a distributional statement; they still pay a pass of the large model over each candidate.
  • Agents: since 2026 the same idea is applied to whole actions of an agent (guess the result of a tool call, or the next action, and verify later). The unit keeps growing; the two questions of §1 still classify the methods.
