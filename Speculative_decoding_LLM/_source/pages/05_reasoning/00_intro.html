What you will be able to do after this page
  1. Explain why token-level speculation has a ceiling that faster hardware does not lift, and what changes when the unit becomes a reasoning step.
  2. For any method, answer the two questions that classify it: what is the unit that is accepted or rejected, and does the large model still process the accepted parts?
  3. Describe reward-guided speculative decoding (RSD): its accept rule, the distribution it samples, its guarantee, and why its headline numbers are in FLOPs.
  4. Describe SpecReason and Lookahead Reasoning, and say why one may lose accuracy where the other does not.
  5. Describe token routing (R2R): which tokens go to the large model, who decides, and what is and is not saved.
  6. Explain the three reasons why some of these methods report an accuracy above the large model's, and their known failure modes.

0. Before you start

How to read this page

You need page 1 (rounds, acceptance rate, the speed-up law). Page 4 is the natural predecessor — a judge decides whether a different token is acceptable; here something decides whether a different step is — but it is not required.

A warning about vocabulary. Many of these papers have “speculative” in their title, and several are not speculative decoding in the sense of page 1: the large model does not verify the small model's tokens in parallel; it is simply not called when the small model's work is judged good enough. That is a cascade. The distinction matters for what you can expect — less latency, or less compute — and §1 sets it up before any method is described.

The story of the page in six steps

Bigger units and a judge of steps make it possible to skip the large model on most of a reasoning trace; what is gained is compute as well as time, what is lost is any link with the large model's distribution.

  1. Two questions (§1). What is the unit — token or step? And is the large model's pass still paid on what is accepted? The answers sort every method into four boxes.
  2. A reward model as verifier (§2). RSD: the small model writes a step, a process reward model scores it, the large model rewrites the step only if the score is low.
  3. The large model as critic (§3). SpecReason: the large model gives each drafted step a score from 0 to 9 in one short pass.
  4. Staying close to the large model (§4). Lookahead Reasoning: the large model writes every step, several steps in parallel; a drafted step is kept only if it means the same as the large model's.
  5. Token routing (§5). R2R: the small model writes; a learned router sends to the large model the few tokens at which the two would take different paths.
  6. The border (§6). Which of these save compute, which save only latency, why accuracy can go up, and how they fail.

stepA unit of a reasoning trace: in most of these papers, the text up to the next blank line.

PRMProcess reward model: a model trained to score a partial solution step by step; here its score $r\in[0,1]$ for a drafted step.
