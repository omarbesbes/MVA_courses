8. What to remember

Summary
  • Token-level speculation is capped at $\frac1{1-\al}$. Reasoning traces are made of steps; two wordings of a step share few tokens but are interchangeable, so the step is a better unit of acceptance.
  • Two questions classify every method: what is the unit, and does the large model still process the accepted units? If yes, latency is saved and compute is not (speculative side). If no, compute is saved and the accepted text is the small model's (cascade side).
  • RSD: a process reward model accepts the small model's steps above a threshold; the large model rewrites the others. A cascade at step level; reported in FLOPs (up to 4.4× fewer on a mathematics benchmark, with about +1 point on average); guarantee relative to the small model only.
  • SpecReason: the large model marks each drafted step out of 9. Faster under a token budget; loses accuracy when re-measured without one.
  • Lookahead Reasoning: the large model writes all the steps in parallel and a verifier keeps the drafts that mean the same. 1.5× to 2.1× together with token-level speculation, accuracy within about two points, more compute.
  • Token routing (R2R): the small model writes; a router sends about one token in eight to the large model. 2.8× the large model's speed at 92% of its average accuracy in the paper; operations are not saved.
  • Scores above the large model's come from selection, token budgets, or noise in the baseline. Failures come from the judge.

Reflexes

  • “Speculative” in a title → ask whether the large model verifies the draft, or is skipped. The second is a cascade.
  • A gain in FLOPs → ask for wall-clock time. A gain in time → ask for the total compute.
  • A step-level method → ask who judges a step, what that judge was trained on, and how steps are delimited.
  • Accuracy above the target's → look for a token budget, and for the exact baseline's own score.
  • A router or judge trained on one kind of traffic → test it on another.
  • A mixture of two models → check the length of the answers and the share that never finish.

Glossary

reasoning step
A unit of a thinking trace, in practice the text between two blank lines.
process reward model (PRM)
A model trained to score partial solutions step by step.
cascade
A system that uses a small model and defers to a large one when a rule says so.
deferral rule
The test that decides to call the large model.
query-level routing
Choosing one model per request. Token-level routing chooses per token.
path-divergent token
A token at which the small and the large model would send the reasoning in different directions (R2R).
prefill / catch-up
The parallel pass by which a model reads tokens it did not write, to update its cache.
semantic verifier
A model that judges whether two steps mean the same thing.
test-time scaling
Spending more computation at inference (longer reasoning, search over candidates) to improve answers.
