1. Beyond text: where relaxing is the natural thing to do

When tokens are entries of a learned codebook (image patches, sound frames) or bins of a continuous quantity (robot actions), many tokens mean almost the same thing and the target spreads its probability over them. The exact test, as it is used for text, then accepts very little. Most methods proposed for these outputs replace “is it the same token?” by “is it in the same class?”.

Why the exact test fails

In text, the target is often nearly certain of the next token. A model that generates an image as a sequence of codebook entries is in a different situation: thousands of codes stand for visually similar patches, and the probability is spread over them. The authors of LANTERN call this token selection ambiguity, and measure it: with a state-of-the-art drafter and the exact test, the first drafted token of an image model is accepted 4% of the time (“only 4% of drafts are accepted when applied to visual AR models”). For images the picture is more mixed: exact methods designed for images reach about 2× (fixed draft trees, or the model's own previous guesses used as the draft, in the table below), and the relaxed rules add 10% to 80% to the exact method of their own tables.

The common idea: a class of tokens instead of a token

If two codes produce nearly the same pixels, rejecting one because the target “preferred” the other is waste. So test the draft against the probability the target gives to the class of the drafted token. The methods differ by where the class comes from:

  • A fixed distance between tokens: nearest neighbours in the codebook's latent space (LANTERN), distance between bin numbers (Spec-VLA), similarity of acoustic embeddings (PCG).
  • The target's own distribution at this step: clusters rebuilt at every position from the target's most likely tokens (GSD).
  • No class at all, only a boost of the acceptance probability: add a constant (Speech SD), or multiply by a factor that decreases along the draft (COOL-SD). These are the lenience of page 3, applied to another modality.

The distance between two bin numbers costs nothing and works for robot actions. Why can the same rule not be applied to the tokens of a text?

Answer

Bin numbers are ordered: bin 120 and bin 123 are nearly the same action. Token numbers of a text vocabulary are arbitrary: neighbouring numbers are unrelated words. For text, “close” would have to mean “close in meaning, in this context”, which no fixed table gives. Learning that notion is the programme of the judges of page 4.

LANTERN — accept on the mass of the latent neighbours

Jang, Park, Yang, Jung, Yun, Kundu, Kim, Yang (KAIST, Intel Labs, AITRICS) · ICLR 2025 · arXiv 2410.03355 · code released

lossyneeds a trained drafterimages2024

What it tries
Make speculation work for autoregressive image generation, where the exact test, with the best drafter design for text, accepts 4% of the first drafted tokens.
How it decides
Around the drafted code $\tilde x$, take its nearest codes in latent space, closest first, for as long as their total target probability stays below $\delta$ (and at most $k$ of them): the set $A_{k,\delta}(\tilde x)$. Accept $\tilde x$ with probability $\min\!\Big(1,\dfrac{\sum_{x\in A_{k,\delta}(\tilde x)}p(x)}{q(\tilde x)}\Big)$.
What it achieves
On a text-to-image model (LlamaGen-XL), one consumer GPU. Greedy decoding: 2.26× where exact speculation gives 1.29×, with FID 30.78 against 28.63. Sampling: 1.69× where exact speculation gives 0.93×, with FID 18.76 against 15.22.
What it costs
Diversity: at the 1.69× setting the recall of the generated images falls from 0.56 to 0.45. The bound $\delta$ holds at each position; nothing is stated for the whole image. Two parameters to tune per model.
In the paper Jang et al., ICLR 2025 — in the guide's notation ($p$ target, $q$ drafter; the paper uses the opposite letters)

The relaxation is equivalent to testing against a distorted target that moves the neighbours' probability onto the drafted code:

$$p_{k,\delta}(x\mid \tilde x)=\begin{cases}\sum_{x'\in A_{k,\delta}(\tilde x)}p(x')&\text{if }x=\tilde x,\\[2pt]0&\text{if }x\in A_{k,\delta}(\tilde x),\ x\ne\tilde x,\\[2pt]p(x)&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}$$

and $A_{k,\delta}(\tilde x)$ is the largest set of nearest neighbours for which $\TV\big(p_{k,\delta}(\cdot\mid\tilde x),\,p\big)<\delta$. The paper states that the relaxation “inevitably distorts the target distribution”.

$A_{k,\delta}(\tilde x)$
The drafted code and some of its nearest codes. The total variation between the distorted target and $p$ is exactly the target probability of the neighbours that were added, so the rule “stay below $\delta$” is a budget on that probability.
first line
The drafted code is treated as if it had the probability of its whole neighbourhood.
second line
The price: when $\tilde x$ is the draft, its neighbours can no longer be produced. Over many positions this removes variety, which is what the fall in recall measures.
surrogate target
This is the frame of page 3, §1: an exact test against another distribution. Here the surrogate depends on the drafted token.

What to look at. Twenty-four codes of a toy codebook, placed so that neighbours on the axis are neighbours in latent space, with the target's probability for each (blue). The drafter proposes one code with certainty, as a draft tree does, so the exact test accepts it with the target's probability of that single code: a few per cent. Raise $\delta$: the neighbours enter the set one by one (amber) and the acceptance probability becomes the sum of their bars. The picture is then the distorted target: the mass of the set is stacked on the drafted code, and its neighbours are emptied (hollow bars). Then move the draft to a code in the small group on the right: the same $\delta$ buys much less, because there is little mass around it. The rule helps where the target hesitates between similar codes, which is where it should.

The other modalities, and the successors

Spec-VLA — accept a robot action if it is close enough

Wang, Yu, Yuan, Yu, Gao, Wang, Wong · EMNLP 2025 · arXiv 2507.22424

lossygreedy decodingrobot actions2025

What it tries
Speed up a vision-language-action model, which writes each action as a few tokens, each one of 256 bins of a continuous quantity.
How it decides
Accept the drafted bin if it is within $r$ bins of the target's choice: $\lvert a-\hat a\rvert\le r$; otherwise use the target's choice. The distance costs nothing: it is the difference of two bin numbers.
What it achieves
In simulation (LIBERO, four task suites): 1.22× to 1.42× where the exact test gives 1.08× to 1.15×. Success rates stay within a few points of the baseline in both directions (for example 85.8% against 85.0%, and 85.0% against 89.0%).
What it costs
The tolerance depends on the task: $r=9$ on three suites, but on the long-horizon suite “the success rate drops significantly when the relaxation threshold exceeds 5”. No guarantee other than the measured success rate.

Four remarks.

  • Models that read images but write text were handled exactly until 2026. Their output is ordinary text, so the methods of page 2 apply; the first loosened rule (LVSpec) is careful to stay strict on the tokens tied to what is seen, because, in its authors' words, treating those loosely “risks factual hallucinations”.
  • Exact methods have not said their last word. Speculative Jacobi Decoding is exact and needs no drafter; a 2026 preprint (PathRelax) describes an exact tree of such paths that, according to its authors, is faster than the relaxed rules of the table.
  • Guarantees are rare. LANTERN bounds each step; COOL-SD bounds the sequence; PCG is exact for the group and not for the token inside it. Everything else rests on task metrics. And COOL-SD proves what one would guess: “any relaxation of the acceptance criterion inevitably introduces bias that cannot be perfectly corrected by adjusting the resampling distributions.”
  • “Quality preserved” usually means one metric. Prompt-alignment scores barely move in these tables; diversity (recall), FID and word error rate move visibly. Read the column that moves.
