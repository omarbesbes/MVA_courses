3. What ships: lossy options in real software

Serving engines are exact by default. In November 2026 a handful of lossy verifiers exist as documented options that must be switched on, each with a warning. No provider says publicly that it runs one.

The Hugging Face rule in full: with the option set, a drafted token is accepted with probability $\min\{1,\ 1-w+w\,p(x)/q(x)\}$ instead of $\min\{1,\ p(x)/q(x)\}$; with greedy decoding the draft is compared with the most likely token of the mixture. Near $w=1$ it is the exact rule; near $w=0$ everything is accepted.

In practice — the numbers published with the vLLM option

The documentation of the FLy verifier reports tests on four target/drafter pairs: “FLy was faster than Standard SD in all 40 primary configurations”, by 1.07× to 1.94× at temperature 0 with a separate drafter and by 1.04× to 1.31× with newer built-in drafters; and “the 29 configurations with ratio-based quality measurements had a minimum quality retention of 97.9%”. Read the sample sizes: the accuracy tests use 100, 100, 30 and 198 questions, and the lowest retention, about 96%, “corresponded to one fewer correct answer” out of 30. This is vendor documentation, not a peer-reviewed evaluation, and it measures accuracy only (see §4 for what else moves).

Pitfall — parameters that look lossy and are not

Several settings change how much is drafted, not how a token is accepted. They are exact: the minimum draft probability of llama.cpp (--spec-draft-p-min), the confidence threshold and the number of assistant tokens of Transformers (assistant_confidence_threshold, num_assistant_tokens), the draft length of any engine. These are the stopping rules of page 2, §5. A second trap in the other direction: engines have a “synthetic” acceptance mode that forces a given acceptance length for benchmarking. Its output “is not valid model output”.

What providers say

Every official description found frames speculative decoding as quality-preserving: Google (“the method guarantees an identical output distribution”), Cerebras (“Model output accuracy is likewise unchanged”), Together (“matches the distribution of non-speculative decoding”), Fireworks (“The target model still verifies every accepted token”), AWS (“without compromising quality”). None of the providers surveyed states that a production service uses relaxed acceptance. That is a statement about what is published: what a closed service runs cannot be established from outside.

Two trends that pull in opposite directions

  • Lossy options arrive fast. Both options of the table above entered mainstream libraries within months of their papers (FLy: ICLR 2026; the ensemble rule: DIVERSED, AISTATS 2026, of which the library implements the fixed-weight version).
  • Drafters are becoming less able to support them. Models now ship with their own drafting heads, and engines use those by default. A practitioners' study notes that such drafters are “unsuited to relaxed spec-dec, precisely due to the fact that they are weak general language models”: a relaxed rule lets the drafter's text through, and a head trained to guess a few tokens is not a model one wants to read. The study calls it “a conflict of narratives”.
