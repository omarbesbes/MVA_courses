8. What to remember

Summary
  • Outside text, tokens are codes or bins, many of them nearly equivalent, and the exact test of page 1 keeps little (about 1× for speech and actions). Relaxed rules test the drafted token against a class: its neighbours in latent space (LANTERN), nearby bins (Spec-VLA), a group of similar sounds (PCG). The cost shows in diversity, FID or word error rate rather than in prompt-alignment scores.
  • A speed-up belongs to a setting: hardware, numerical precision, batch size, temperature, draft length, engine, prompts.
  • A lossy method owes more: the length of its answers, quality against the target and the drafter, the right baselines, a hard task, a curve, and every other property the target was tested for.
  • “Lossless” means no systematic pull toward the drafter, not identical strings: in 16-bit arithmetic and in batches, outputs differ, as they do for the target alone. Exact baselines sit a point or two away from their targets in published tables; that is the noise floor.
  • Software is exact by default. Lossy verifiers exist as options with warnings (FLy in vLLM, a fixed mixture in Transformers). Draft-length and confidence settings are not lossy.
  • Risks. Exact or not: timing and packet sizes leak, and crafted inputs can destroy the gain. Lossy only: refusals degrade before accuracy; keep the first tokens exact. Watermarks need co-design, and nothing is known for lossy rules.
  • Deciding. Take the exact gains first. Relax only with a capable drafter and a quality measurement of your own, with a capped rule, and report the time per answer.

Reflexes

  • A speed-up → which batch size, precision, temperature, engine; tokens per second or time per answer?
  • “Quality preserved” → on which metric, against which baseline, on how many examples?
  • A lossy result → what does the exact method score in the same table? That gap is the noise.
  • A setting in a configuration file → does it change what is drafted, or what is accepted?
  • A lossy option switched on → were refusals tested again? Are the first tokens exact?
  • Streaming with speculation → what do packet sizes reveal?

Glossary

codebook
The finite list of vectors that an image or audio tokenizer uses; a token is the index of one entry.
token selection ambiguity
Many tokens are nearly equivalent, so the target gives each a small probability.
FID, recall
Distance between the distributions of generated and real images (lower is better); share of the variety of real images that the generator covers.
word error rate (WER)
Share of words wrongly recognised when the generated speech is transcribed.
performance retention rate (PRR)
Position of a method between the drafter alone (0) and the target alone (1) on a benchmark.
goodput
Tokens per second that end up in the output.
FP16, BF16, FP32
Number formats of 16 or 32 bits; the smaller ones round more, which can flip a choice between two nearly tied tokens.
watermark
A key-dependent bias in the choice of tokens that a detector can test for.
side channel
Information that leaks through how a computation behaves (its timing, the size of its messages), not through its result.
position-aware verification
The exact rule on the first tokens of an answer, a lossy rule afterwards.
