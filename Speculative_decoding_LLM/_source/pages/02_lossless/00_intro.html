What you will be able to do after this page
  1. Sort any exact method by the lever it pulls in the speed-up law: a higher acceptance rate, a cheaper drafter, a better use of each call of the target.
  2. Describe the main families of drafters — a separate small model, heads attached to the target, lookups, parallel block drafters — and what each reports.
  3. Explain why several candidates per position (trees) help, why accepting them naively would break exactness, and what block verification changes.
  4. Say what happens to batch-1 speed-ups on a loaded server, and what serving software ships.
  5. State the three ceilings of exact methods: statistical, economic, systemic.

0. Before you start

This page is a map, not a course: the guide's subject is lossy speculation (pages 3 to 5), and this page is here so that you know what the lossy methods are competing with. You need page 1. Each family gets a short card with what it tries and what it reports. All the methods here are lossless: with sampling they keep the target's distribution, with greedy decoding they keep its text (up to the numerical caveat of page 1, §5).

Pitfall — speed-ups are per setting

Almost every number on this page is “speed-up over the target decoding alone, batch size 1, greedy decoding, one GPU”, measured by the method's authors on their own choice of model, task and hardware. They are not comparable across papers, and §6 shows what becomes of them on a loaded server.
