15. Summary of the lecture

The lecture on one screen
  1. Schrödinger problem (§1): $L^\eps_C(a,b)=\min_{P\in\U(a,b)}\ps{C}{P}+\eps\KL(P|a\otimes b)$, the penalty being the mutual information of the coupling; same minimizer as $\ps{C}{P}-\eps\mathrm H(P)$ (four conventions, four values). Unique, strictly convex, entrywise positive solution.
  2. Scaling form (§2): $P_\eps=\operatorname{diag}(u)K\operatorname{diag}(v)$, $K=e^{-C/\eps}$, with $u\odot Kv=a$, $v\odot K\T u=b$; potentials $u=ae^{f/\eps}$, $v=be^{g/\eps}$; value $\ps{f}{a}+\ps{g}{b}$; on couplings the objective is $L^\eps_C+\eps\KL(\cdot|P_\eps)$.
  3. Limits (§3): $\eps\to0$: the maximal-entropy optimal plan, with a value bias between $\eps\KL(P_\eps)$ and $\eps\KL(P^\star)$; $\eps\to\infty$: $a\otimes b$.
  4. Sinkhorn (§4–§5): $u\leftarrow a/(Kv)$, $v\leftarrow b/(K\T u)$, $O(nm)$ per cycle; each half-step is the KL projection onto one marginal constraint, and the iterates converge to the KL projection of $(a\otimes b)\odot e^{-C/\eps}$ onto $\U(a,b)$ (Pythagoras).
  5. Dual (§6): $\max\ps{f}{a}+\ps{g}{b}-\eps\sum a_ib_je^{(f_i+g_j-C_{ij})/\eps}+\eps$, unconstrained; block maximizers are soft $c$-transforms $f=\min^\eps_b(C-g)$; implement with log-sum-exp. Softmax attention is one row step.
  6. Convergence (§7): Birkhoff contraction $\lambda(K)=\tanh(\frac14\log\eta(K))$ per half-step in Hilbert’s metric (global, pessimistic); local factor $\sigma_\eps^2$ per cycle, the squared maximal correlation of $P_\eps$; $\approx1/\eps$ cycles; robust $O(\norm{C}^2_\infty/(\eps\ell))$ dual gap and $O(n^2\log n/\delta^2)$ operations to approximate $L_C$.
  7. Measures (§8): density $e^{(f\oplus g-c)/\eps}$ with respect to $\al\otimes\be$; quadratic cost: map plus Gaussian noise of variance $\approx\frac\eps2T'$; Gaussians in closed form; bias $-\frac{d\eps}2\log(\pi\eps)-\frac\eps2(\mathrm H(\al)+\mathrm H(\be))+O(\eps^2)$.
  8. Sinkhorn divergence (§9): $S_\eps=\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\be)-\frac12\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\al)-\frac12\mathcal L^\eps(\be,\be)\ge0$, $=0$ iff $\al=\be$; $\to\mathcal L_c$ as $\eps\to0$ (error $O(\eps^2)$ for smooth densities), $\to-\frac12\iint c\,\dd(\al-\be)^{\otimes2}$ as $\eps\to\infty$; the raw cost shrinks fits ($s^\star=\sqrt{1-\eps/2}$ for Gaussians).
  9. Statistics (§10): $n^{-1/d}$ for $W_p$, $n^{-1/2}$ for MMD and for $S_\eps$ at fixed $\eps$ (constant exploding as $\eps\to0$).
  10. Learning (§11–§12): $\nabla_C=P_\eps$, $\nabla_a=f$, $\nabla_{x_i}=a_i(x_i-T_\eps(x_i))$; envelope formulas or autodiff. Unbalanced transport: $\tau\KL$ marginal penalties, damped update $u\leftarrow(a/Kv)^{\tau/(\tau+\eps)}$.

The reflexes to acquire

  • An entropic value → first ask which convention (§0); minimizers agree, values do not.
  • Entropic OT → $P=\operatorname{diag}(u)K\operatorname{diag}(v)$: $n+m$ unknowns, two scaling equations.
  • Sinkhorn by hand → $u=a/(Kv)$, then $v=b/(K\T u)$; check the row sums, not the column sums.
  • Small $\eps$ or single precision → log domain; subtract the maximum before exponentiating.
  • Half-step of Sinkhorn → KL projection; dual block maximization; soft $c$-transform; softmax.
  • Speed → $\sigma_\eps^2$ per cycle, about $1/\eps$ cycles; Birkhoff’s $\lambda^2$ is a (very) pessimistic guarantee.
  • Quadratic cost → “map plus noise” of variance $\approx\frac\eps2T'$; Gaussians: solve $2k^2+\eps k-2\sigma_\al^2\sigma_\be^2=0$.
  • Loss between distributions → debias: $S_\eps$, never the raw $\mathcal L^\eps$; $S_\eps\ge0$ by kernel Cauchy–Schwarz.
  • Samples → $S_\eps$ and MMD at $n^{-1/2}$, $W_p$ at $n^{-1/d}$; $\eps$ trades geometry for statistics.
  • Gradient of an optimal value → envelope theorem: plan, potential, barycentric projection.
  • Mass mismatch, outliers, growth → unbalanced: exponent $\tau/(\tau+\eps)$.

Notes on the sources (things that could confuse you)
  • Four conventions for the penalty. OT4ML (8.1) uses $-\eps\mathrm H(P)$, OT4ML (8.8) and the 2019 notes use $+\eps\KL(P|a\otimes b)$, CompOT and the sheet use $\pm\eps\sum P(\log P-1)$. Same minimizers, values shifted by $\eps(\mathrm H(a)+\mathrm H(b))$ or $\eps(1+\mathrm H(a)+\mathrm H(b))$: $-0.3213$, $0.3307$, $-0.8213$, $-0.8213$ on the running example (§0). Joint convexity of the value in $(a,b)$ holds in the sheet’s convention but not in the KL one (§13, Exercise 6), as OT4ML p. 260 says.
  • Two signs for $\mathrm H$ in OT4ML. Def. 8.1: $\mathrm H=-\sum P\log P$; Prop. 8.17: $\mathrm H(\al)=\int\rho\log\rho$. The expansion is right with the second meaning (with the other sign it would be off by $0.35$ at $\eps=0.1$; §8).
  • OT4ML Prop. 8.5 is stated as an equivalence but the printed proof shows only that the solution has the scaling form; the converse is the identity $E(Q)=E(P)+\eps\KL(Q|P)$ (§2).
  • Indexing of half-steps. OT4ML (9.2), CompOT (4.16) and the 2019 notes (37) write $P^{(\ell+1)}=\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal C^1}(P^{(\ell)})$, $P^{(\ell+2)}=\mathrm{Proj}_{\mathcal C^2}(P^{(\ell+1)})$: one index per half-step, read for even $\ell$; Algorithm 9.1 uses half-integer indices for the same thing.
  • 2019 notes, p. 18. The reference-change identity “$\KL(P|a\otimes b)=\KL(P|a'\otimes b')+\KL(a'\otimes b'|a\otimes b)$ [ToDo: check this]” is wrong; the correct one is OT4ML Prop. 8.9, $\KL(P|a'\otimes b')-\KL(a|a')-\KL(b|b')$. On a random $3\times4$ coupling the left side is $0.025447$, the printed right side $1.635244$, OT4ML’s $0.025447$.
  • 2019 notes, (29), p. 18. The two KL terms are swapped, and so is the conclusion; the book’s (8.12) is right (check: $0.024253$ against a printed bound of $-0.030694$; §3).
  • 2019 notes, typos. p. 19: “$P\in\U(a,\beta)$” for $\U(a,b)$; p. 21: $d_{\mathcal H}(u,u')=\norm{\log(u)-\log(v)}_V$ for $\log(u')$.
  • 2019 notes, pp. 36–37. The definition of $\bar{\mathcal L}^\eps_c$ subtracts $\frac12\mathcal L^\eps_c(\al,\be)$ instead of $\frac12\mathcal L^\eps_c(\be,\be)$; the proof of Prop. 21 has $-\iint c$ for $+\iint c$; the proof of Prop. 22 uses the wrong potentials in the exponent, an extra “$-1$”, and $e^{f_{\al,\al}}$ for $e^{f_{\al,\al}/\eps}$ (§9).
  • CompOT Prop. 4.7, p. 85. In CompOT’s own conventions, the dual value at the optimum is $D^\eps_C=\ps{f^\star}{a}+\ps{g^\star}{b}=\mathrm L^\eps_C+\eps$, so “$D^\eps_C\le\mathrm L^\eps_C$” fails, and (4.47) should read $P^\eps_C-D^\eps_C=\eps(\mathbf H(P^\star)-1)$, i.e. $\eps$ times the Shannon entropy. The chain that does hold is $D^\eps_C\le L_C\le P^\eps_C$. Check on the running example at $\eps=0.5$: $D=-0.321267$, $\mathrm L^\eps=-0.821267$, $P=0.224253$, $L_C=0.2$; $P-D=0.545520=\eps(\mathbf H-1)$, while $\eps(\mathbf H+1)=1.545520$.
  • CompOT Prop. 4.8, p. 86. For the same reason, $D^{(L)}_C\le\mathrm L^\eps_C$ can fail: after one cycle at $\eps=0.5$, $D^{(1)}=-0.3445>\mathrm L^\eps=-0.8213$. It holds with $\mathrm L^\eps_C+\eps$ (or $L_C$) on the right.
  • CompOT Remark 4.6, pp. 69–70. The temperature in Altschuler et al.’s bound is $\eps=\tau/(4\log n)$, not $4\log(n)/\tau$ (a smaller target error needs a smaller $\eps$); in the rounding scheme, $\Delta_b=b-v'\odot(K\T u')$ uses the updated $u'$.
  • CompOT Remark 4.4, p. 66. “For fully dependent variables, $\eps=+\infty$” should be $\eps=0$.
  • StatOT Exercise 4.6-3, p. 137. The identities $\nabla\varphi_\eps=\E[Y|X=x]$, $\nabla^2\varphi_\eps=\eps^{-1}\mathrm{cov}(Y|X)$ with $\varphi_\eps=\frac12\norm{\cdot}^2-f_\eps$ hold for the cost $\frac12\norm{x-y}^2$; with the chapter’s cost $\norm{x-y}^2$, take $\varphi_\eps=\frac12(\norm{\cdot}^2-f_\eps)$ and $\nabla^2\varphi_\eps=\frac2\eps\mathrm{cov}(Y|X)$. Check ($\N(0,1)$ to itself, $\eps=0.5$): the slope of $\E[Y|X]$ is $0.8828$, the printed $\varphi''_\eps$ gives $0.7656$, the corrected one $0.8828$.
  • StatOT (4.12), p. 123. The left side should read $f_t(x)$, not $f_t(y)$.
  • The sheet. Exercise 1(4): the small parameter is $e^{-2/\eps}$, not $e^{-\eps/2}$; Exercise 6(1): “that that”; Exercise 8: the printed dual flips the sign of the potentials in the constraint but not in the objective (its supremum $0.46$ exceeds the primal value $0.146264$ in a test) — all corrected in §13.
