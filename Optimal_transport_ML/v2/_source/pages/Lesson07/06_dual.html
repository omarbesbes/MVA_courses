6. The dual: soft $c$-transforms and the log domain OT4ML §8.5, pp. 150–152

The dual of entropic transport is an unconstrained, smooth, concave problem in the potentials $(f,g)$; maximizing it in $f$ for fixed $g$ is a soft $c$-transform; Sinkhorn is exactly this block ascent, and written with log-sum-exp it never overflows or underflows.

Why go to the potentials

The scalings $u,v$ of §4 are exponentials of the potentials: $u=a\odot e^{f/\eps}$, $v=b\odot e^{g/\eps}$. For a small $\eps$ these numbers are astronomically large or small, and a computer stores them as $0$ or $\infty$. The potentials themselves stay of the size of the costs. They are also what you need later: the gradient of the entropic cost (§11) and the extension to continuous measures (§8). Finally, the dual shows how entropic transport relaxes the Kantorovich dual of Lesson 4 (refresher).

The dual problem

In the course OT4ML Prop. 8.18, (8.20)–(8.21), p. 150 · compact Prop. 8.16, p. 38

Proposition 8.18 (Dual of entropic OT). The optimal value of (8.8) is

$$\begin{aligned}\min_{P\in\U(a,b)}\ps{P}{C}+\eps\KL(P|a\otimes b)=\max_{f\in\R^n,\,g\in\R^m}\ &\ps{f}{a}+\ps{g}{b}\\&-\eps\sum_{i,j}\exp\Big(\frac{f_i+g_j-C_{i,j}}{\eps}\Big)a_ib_j+\eps.\end{aligned}\qquad(8.20)$$

The optimal $(f,g)$ are linked to scalings $(u,v)$ appearing in (8.2) through $\ u_i=a_ie^{f_i/\eps}$ and $v_j=b_je^{g_j/\eps}$. (8.21)

$f_i$, $g_j$
The dual potentials, Lagrange multipliers of the row and column constraints; as in Lesson 4, prices paid at departure from $x_i$ and at arrival in $y_j$.
$-\eps\sum_{ij}e^{(f_i+g_j-C_{ij})/\eps}a_ib_j$
A soft version of the constraint $f_i+g_j\le C_{ij}$ of the Kantorovich dual: prices that exceed the cost are not forbidden, they are penalized exponentially, with a penalty that becomes a wall as $\eps\to0$ (the exponential of a positive number divided by a tiny $\eps$).
$+\eps$
A constant that makes the two values equal for the KL normalization; it is $\eps\sum_{ij}a_ib_j$.
“max”, unconstrained
No constraint on $(f,g)$: the dual is a smooth concave function on $\R^n\times\R^m$, which is why simple coordinate ascent works. Its maximizers are unique up to $(f+c,g-c)$.
Idea of the proof

Dualize the two marginal constraints with multipliers $f,g$. For fixed $(f,g)$, the Lagrangian separates into one scalar problem per entry $P_{ij}\ge0$, whose minimum is a convex conjugate: $\min_{r\ge0}\{\varphi(r)-sr\}=-\varphi^*(s)$ with $\varphi(r)=r\log r-r+1$ and $\varphi^*(s)=e^s-1$. This gives the dual function and weak duality. Equality: at the potentials of §2 the dual value equals the primal value.

Full proof

Write $E(P)=\ps{P}{C}+\eps\KL(P|a\otimes b)$ and, for $P\ge0$, $f\in\R^n$, $g\in\R^m$, the Lagrangian $\mathcal L(P,f,g)=E(P)+\ps{f}{a-P\ind_m}+\ps{g}{b-P\T\ind_n}$.

  1. $$\mathcal L(P,f,g)=\ps{f}{a}+\ps{g}{b}+\sum_{ij}a_ib_j\Big[\eps\,\varphi\Big(\frac{P_{ij}}{a_ib_j}\Big)-\frac{f_i+g_j-C_{ij}}{\eps}\cdot\eps\,\frac{P_{ij}}{a_ib_j}\Big].$$$\KL(P|a\otimes b)=\sum_{ij}a_ib_j\,\varphi\big(P_{ij}/(a_ib_j)\big)$ with $\varphi(r)=r\log r-r+1$; and $\ps{f}{P\ind}+\ps{g}{P\T\ind}-\ps{P}{C}=\sum_{ij}(f_i+g_j-C_{ij})P_{ij}$.
  2. $$\min_{r\ge0}\big\{\varphi(r)-sr\big\}=-\varphi^*(s)=-(e^s-1),\ \text{attained at }r=e^s .$$$\frac{\dd}{\dd r}(r\log r-r+1-sr)=\log r-s=0$ gives $r=e^s$, and the value is $se^s-e^s+1-se^s=1-e^s$ (refresher).
  3. $$D(f,g):=\min_{P\ge0}\mathcal L(P,f,g)=\ps{f}{a}+\ps{g}{b}-\eps\sum_{ij}a_ib_j\Big(e^{(f_i+g_j-C_{ij})/\eps}-1\Big).$$Minimize each entry separately with step 2, $r=P_{ij}/(a_ib_j)$, $s=(f_i+g_j-C_{ij})/\eps$. This is the right side of (8.20), since $\sum_{ij}a_ib_j=1$.
  4. $D(f,g)\le E(P)$ for every $P\in\U(a,b)$ and all $(f,g)$ (weak duality).For $P\in\U(a,b)$ the constraint terms vanish: $E(P)=\mathcal L(P,f,g)\ge\min_{Q\ge0}\mathcal L(Q,f,g)=D(f,g)$.
  5. With the potentials of §2, $P_{\eps,ij}=a_ib_je^{(f_i+g_j-C_{ij})/\eps}$, so $D(f,g)=\ps{f}{a}+\ps{g}{b}-\eps\sum_{ij}P_{\eps,ij}+\eps=\ps{f}{a}+\ps{g}{b}=E(P_\eps)$.$P_\eps$ has mass $1$; the last equality is step 4 of the “if” part in §2.

Steps 4–5: $\max D\ge D(f,g)=E(P_\eps)=\min E\ge\max D$, so the two values are equal and these potentials are optimal; (8.21) is the definition of $f,g$ in §2. Any maximizer satisfies the first-order conditions below, which force $a_ib_je^{(f_i+g_j-C_{ij})/\eps}$ to be a coupling of the form (8.2), hence $P_\eps$: the maximizers are unique up to $(f+c,g-c)$. $\square$

Worked example — the dual of the running example

At $\eps=0.5$, with $f=(-0.344364,\ 0.079317)$, $g=(0.670307,\ 0.086744)$ from §2: $D(f,g)=0.330739$, the primal value. With $f=g=0$: $D(0,0)=\eps\big(1-\sum_{ij}a_ib_je^{-C_{ij}/\eps}\big)=0.5\times(1-0.567668)=0.216166$, below the optimum as weak duality requires. With $(f+1,g-1)$: again $0.330739$ — the gauge.

Soft $c$-transforms

Fix $g$ and maximize $D$ in $f$. The derivative in $f_i$ is $a_i-a_ie^{f_i/\eps}\sum_jb_je^{(g_j-C_{ij})/\eps}$; setting it to zero gives an explicit formula.

In the course OT4ML Def. 8.19, (8.22), p. 151 · compact Def. 8.17, p. 38

Definition 8.19 (Soft-min and discrete soft $c$-transform). For $h\in\R^m$ and weights $b\in\Sigma_m$, the weighted soft-min at temperature $\eps>0$ is

$$\min\nolimits^\eps_b(h):=-\eps\log\sum_je^{-h_j/\eps}b_j .$$

It converges to $\min_{j:\,b_j>0}h_j$ as $\eps\to0$; for positive weights this is $\min_jh_j$. Given a cost matrix C, the discrete soft $c$-transforms are

$$f_i=\min\nolimits^\eps_b(C_{i,\cdot}-g),\qquad g_j=\min\nolimits^\eps_a(C_{\cdot,j}-f).\qquad(8.22)$$

Exponentiating these iterations recovers exactly the Sinkhorn algorithm.

$\min^\eps_b(h)$
A smooth minimum: $-\eps\log$ of a weighted average of $e^{-h_j/\eps}$. The smallest $h_j$ dominates the average when $\eps$ is small. It lies between $\min_jh_j$ and $\min_jh_j+\eps\log(1/b_{j^\star})$, $j^\star$ an argmin (Exercise 5 of §14).
$f_i=\min^\eps_b(C_{i,\cdot}-g)$
The best price at $x_i$ given the prices $g$: in Lesson 4 it was $\min_j(C_{ij}-g_j)$, the largest $f_i$ with $f_i+g_j\le C_{ij}$ for all $j$. Now it is the soft minimum: the soft constraint lets $f_i$ exceed the hard value slightly.
“exponentiating”
$e^{-f_i/\eps}=\sum_jb_je^{(g_j-C_{ij})/\eps}$, i.e. $\frac{a_i}{u_i}=\sum_jK_{ij}v_j$: this is $u=a/(Kv)$.
Worked example — a soft minimum

$h=(0,1,2)$, $b=(\frac13,\frac13,\frac13)$, $\min h=0$. Then $\min^\eps_b(h)=0.691006$ at $\eps=1$, $0.477840$ at $\eps=0.5$, $0.109857$ at $\eps=0.1$ and $0.010986$ at $\eps=0.01$: it approaches $0$ from above, and for small $\eps$ the gap is $\eps\log3$ ($0.109861$ at $\eps=0.1$), the price of the weight $\frac13$ of the minimizing entry. On the running example, $\min^{0.5}_b(C_{1,\cdot}-g)=-0.5\log\big(0.5e^{(0.670307-0)/0.5}+0.5e^{(0.086744-1)/0.5}\big)=-0.344364=f_1$: the optimal potentials are soft $c$-transforms of each other.

Hard versus soft alternation

In Lesson 4, alternating hard $c$-transforms stalls after one cycle: $f=g^{\bar c}$, then $g=f^c$, then $f=g^{\bar c}$ again gives back the same $f$ (the double transform is idempotent). The soft transforms are exact block maximizers of a smooth, strictly concave (up to the gauge) function, so each one strictly increases $D$ until the optimum is reached. Entropy turns a degenerate coordinate ascent into a convergent one.

The log domain

In floating point, $e^{-C_{ij}/\eps}$ is rounded to $0$ as soon as $C_{ij}/\eps$ exceeds about $745$ in double precision (the smallest positive double is $4.9\times10^{-324}\approx e^{-744.4}$) and about $104$ in single precision, the default on GPUs. With costs of order $1$, that is $\eps$ below about $1.3\times10^{-3}$ in double and $10^{-2}$ in single precision. A row of zeros in $K$ makes $Kv$ vanish, and $u=a/(Kv)$ becomes infinite. The cure is to never form $K$, $u$, $v$.

In the course OT4ML p. 151 and Algorithm 8.2, p. 152

These iterations, however, become unstable for small $\eps$. To apply the algorithm in this regime, one needs to stabilize it using the celebrated log-sum-exp trick. This follows from noticing that, similarly to the minimum operator, one has $\ \min^\eps_b(h-\text{cst})=\min^\eps_b(h)-\text{cst}\ $ and to replace the computation of $\min^\eps_b(h)$ by its stabilized version (equal when using infinite precision computation) $\ \min^\eps_b(h-\min(h))+\min(h)$.

Algorithm 8.2 (Log-domain Sinkhorn by soft transforms). […] For $i=1,\dots,n$: set $M_i=\max_j\{g^{(\ell-1)}_j-C_{i,j}\}$; set $f^{(\ell)}_i=-M_i-\eps\log\sum_jb_j\exp\big((g^{(\ell-1)}_j-C_{i,j}-M_i)/\eps\big)$. For $j=1,\dots,m$: set $N_j=\max_i\{f^{(\ell)}_i-C_{i,j}\}$; set $g^{(\ell)}_j=-N_j-\eps\log\sum_ia_i\exp\big((f^{(\ell)}_i-C_{i,j}-N_j)/\eps\big)$. Set $P^{(\ell)}_{i,j}=a_ib_j\exp\big((f^{(\ell)}_i+g^{(\ell)}_j-C_{i,j})/\eps\big)$. […]

$M_i=\max_j\{g_j-C_{ij}\}$
The largest exponent of row $i$. After subtracting it, every exponent is $\le0$ and the largest one is exactly $0$: the sum contains a term $b_j\cdot e^0=b_j$, so it can neither overflow nor be rounded to $0$.
$-M_i-\eps\log(\dots)$
The same number as $\min^\eps_b(C_{i,\cdot}-g)$, computed safely. Libraries call this a log-sum-exp reduction.

Pitfall — “no entry underflowed, so the plain iteration is safe”

The scalings can overflow even when $K$ does not underflow: $v_j=b_je^{g_j/\eps}$ with $g_j=1$ and $\eps=10^{-3}$ is $e^{1000}=\infty$. And before anything is exactly $0$ or $\infty$, the relative precision of tiny numbers degrades (subnormal numbers carry fewer digits). For $\eps$ below a few percent of the cost scale, use the log domain; for large $\eps$ the plain iteration is faster (no logarithms), which is why libraries offer both.

In machine learning — softmax attention is half a Sinkhorn step; Sinkformers

In a transformer, the attention weights between queries $Qx_i$ and keys $Kx_j$ are $a_{ij}=\exp(\ps{Qx_i}{Kx_j})/\sum_\ell\exp(\ps{Qx_i}{Kx_\ell})$ (OT4ML (4.14), p. 91; the usual factor $1/\sqrt r$ is absorbed in $Q$ or $K$). Read $-\ps{Qx_i}{Kx_j}$ as a cost $C_{ij}$ and set $\eps=1$: the matrix $(e^{-C_{ij}})$ is a Gibbs kernel, and the softmax normalizes its rows to sum to $1$ — exactly the first row step of Sinkhorn, with all row targets equal to $1$. Nothing constrains the columns: some keys receive much more total attention than others. Sinkformers (Sander, Ablin, Blondel and Peyré, AISTATS 2022; OT4ML p. 417) continue the iteration, alternating row and column normalizations, so that the attention matrix becomes doubly stochastic (approximately a transport plan between queries and keys). The authors report accuracies comparable to or better than standard attention on several benchmark tasks. (The letter $K$ is overloaded here: the key matrix of the transformer, not the Gibbs kernel.)

Reflexes
  • Entropic dual = Kantorovich dual with the hard constraint $f\oplus g\le C$ replaced by the penalty $-\eps\sum e^{(f\oplus g-C)/\eps}a\otimes b$.
  • Block maximizer = soft $c$-transform: $f=\min^\eps_b(C-g)$; Sinkhorn = alternating soft $c$-transforms.
  • Small $\eps$, or single precision → log domain: subtract the max before exponentiating.
  • Softmax over scores = one row step of Sinkhorn on the kernel $e^{\text{scores}/\text{temperature}}$.

Show that the gradient of the dual objective at $(f,g)$ vanishes if and only if the plan $a_ib_je^{(f_i+g_j-C_{ij})/\eps}$ belongs to $\U(a,b)$.

Answer

$\partial D/\partial f_i=a_i-\sum_ja_ib_je^{(f_i+g_j-C_{ij})/\eps}=a_i-(P\ind)_i$ and $\partial D/\partial g_j=b_j-(P\T\ind)_j$ with $P_{ij}=a_ib_je^{(f_i+g_j-C_{ij})/\eps}$. The gradient of the dual is the marginal defect of the associated plan: dual ascent and primal feasibility are the same thing.
