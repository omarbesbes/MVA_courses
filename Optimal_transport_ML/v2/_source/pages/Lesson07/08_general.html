8. Entropic transport between general measures OT4ML §8.4–8.5, pp. 145–153 · §9.6, p. 190

Between arbitrary measures, the entropic plan has a density $e^{(f(x)+g(y)-c(x,y))/\eps}$ with respect to $\al\otimes\be$, the potentials are soft $c$-transforms of each other, and for the quadratic cost the plan is a transport map blurred by Gaussian noise of variance about $\eps/2$; Gaussians have a closed form, and the bias with respect to $W_2^2$ is of order $\eps\log(1/\eps)$.

The problem and its dual

Machine learning works with distributions that are known through samples, or with densities; the matrices of §1–§7 are the special case of discrete measures. Every object has a continuous counterpart, obtained by replacing sums by integrals and entries of matrices by densities with respect to $\al\otimes\be$.

Definition 8.12 (Continuous entropic optimal transport). For $\eps>0$, define

$$\mathcal L^\eps_c(\al,\be):=\min_{\pi\in\U(\al,\be)}\int_{\X\times\Y}c(x,y)\,\dd\pi(x,y)+\eps\KL(\pi|\al\otimes\be).\qquad(8.14)$$

Proposition 8.20 (Continuous entropic duality). Let $\X$ and $\Y$ be compact metric spaces, let $\al\in\Pc(\X)$ and $\be\in\Pc(\Y)$, and let $c\in\mathcal C(\X\times\Y)$. For every $\eps>0$, […] $\ \mathcal L^\eps_c(\al,\be)=\sup_{f\in\mathcal C(\X),\,g\in\mathcal C(\Y)}\mathcal D_\eps(f,g)$, (8.23) where the concave dual objective is

$$\mathcal D_\eps(f,g):=\int_\X f\,\dd\al+\int_\Y g\,\dd\be-\eps\int_{\X\times\Y}\Big(e^{\frac{f(x)+g(y)-c(x,y)}{\eps}}-1\Big)\dd\al(x)\dd\be(y).\qquad(8.24)$$

If $\pi^\star$ and $(f^\star,g^\star)$ are primal and dual optimizers, then $\ \dfrac{\dd\pi^\star}{\dd(\al\otimes\be)}(x,y)=\exp\Big(\dfrac{f^\star(x)+g^\star(y)-c(x,y)}{\eps}\Big)\ $ $(\al\otimes\be)$-a.e. (8.25)

Definition 8.21 (Continuous soft $c$-transforms). For $f\in\mathcal C(\X)$ and $g\in\mathcal C(\Y)$, define

$$f^{c,\eps}(y):=-\eps\log\int_\X e^{\frac{f(x)-c(x,y)}{\eps}}\dd\al(x),\qquad g^{\bar c,\eps}(x):=-\eps\log\int_\Y e^{\frac{g(y)-c(x,y)}{\eps}}\dd\be(y).\qquad(8.26)\text{–}(8.27)$$
$\KL(\pi|\al\otimes\be)$
$\int\log\frac{\dd\pi}{\dd(\al\otimes\be)}\,\dd\pi$ for couplings: the mutual information of $(X,Y)\sim\pi$ (§1). It is $+\infty$ if $\pi$ has a part that $\al\otimes\be$ does not see — for instance any transport map between densities, which lives on a graph of zero area. So the entropic problem never returns a map.
(8.25)
The plan has a density with respect to the product, of the scaling form $e^{f/\eps}\,e^{-c/\eps}\,e^{g/\eps}$: the continuous version of $P=\operatorname{diag}(u)K\operatorname{diag}(v)$.
(8.26)–(8.27)
The soft $c$-transforms: Sinkhorn alternates $f\leftarrow g^{\bar c,\eps}$, $g\leftarrow f^{c,\eps}$. With samples, the integrals become averages over the samples: the discrete algorithm of §6.

OT4ML also proves that optimal potentials exist and are unique up to $(f+\lambda,g-\lambda)$ when the supports are compact and $c$ is continuous (Prop. 8.22, p. 153; compact Prop. 8.20, p. 39), and that the limits of §3 hold for measures: $\mathcal L^\eps_c\to\mathcal L_c$ as $\eps\to0$, every cluster point of $\pi_\eps$ is an optimal plan — the Brenier plan $(\Id,T)_\#\al$ for the quadratic cost when $\al$ has a density — and $\pi_\eps\to\al\otimes\be$ as $\eps\to\infty$ (Prop. 8.15, p. 147; compact Prop. 8.13, p. 37).

Entropic transport is “transport plus noise”

In the course OT4ML Prop. 8.14 and its example, pp. 146–147 · compact Prop. 8.12, p. 36

Proposition 8.14 (Closed-form Gibbs coupling). […] Define the normalized Gibbs transition and its output density by $\ p_\eps(x,y):=\frac{k_\eps(x,y)}{Z_\eps(x)}$, $q_\eps(y):=\int_\X p_\eps(x,y)\,\dd\al(x)$, $\dd\be_\eps=q_\eps\,\dd\be_0$ (8.18), where $k_\eps=e^{-c/\eps}$ and $Z_\eps(x)=\int k_\eps(x,y)\,\dd\be_0(y)$. […] Then the unique solution of (8.14) between $\al$ and $\be_\eps$ is $\ \dd\pi_\eps(x,y)=p_\eps(x,y)\,\dd\al(x)\,\dd\be_0(y).$ (8.19)

For example, let $\X=\Y=\R^d$, let $\be_0$ be Lebesgue measure, and take $c(x,y)=\norm{x-y}^2$. Then $Z_\eps=(\pi\eps)^{d/2}$ and $\ p_\eps(x,y)=(\pi\eps)^{-d/2}e^{-\norm{x-y}^2/\eps}$, $\be_\eps=\al*\N\big(0,\frac\eps2\Id\big)$. Equivalently, (8.19) is the law of $(X,X+\sqrt{\eps/2}\,G)$ for independent $X\sim\al$ and $G\sim\N(0,\Id)$. Time-indexed Gaussian blurrings of this type are the forward noising mechanism behind diffusion models […].

$\be_\eps=\al*\N(0,\frac\eps2\Id)$
The target is the source blurred by Gaussian noise of variance $\frac\eps2$ in each direction. For this particular target, no iteration is needed: the entropic plan is “do not move, then add noise”.
$(X,X+\sqrt{\eps/2}\,G)$
The conditional law of $Y$ given $X=x$ is $\N(x,\frac\eps2\Id)$: the plan is the identity map blurred at the scale $\sqrt{\eps/2}$.
Proof of Proposition 8.14 (the main identity)
  1. $\pi_\eps\in\U(\al,\be_\eps)$.$p_\eps(x,\cdot)\,\dd\be_0$ is a probability measure for every $x$ (normalization by $Z_\eps$), so the first marginal is $\al$; the second is $\int p_\eps(x,y)\,\dd\al(x)\,\dd\be_0(y)=q_\eps\,\dd\be_0=\be_\eps$ (Fubini).
  2. $\dfrac{\dd\pi_\eps}{\dd(\al\otimes\be_\eps)}(x,y)=\dfrac{k_\eps(x,y)}{Z_\eps(x)q_\eps(y)}$, so $\eps\log\dfrac{\dd\pi_\eps}{\dd(\al\otimes\be_\eps)}=-c(x,y)-\eps\log Z_\eps(x)-\eps\log q_\eps(y)$.Divide $p_\eps(x,y)\,\dd\al\,\dd\be_0$ by $\dd\al\,q_\eps\dd\be_0$; $\eps\log k_\eps=-c$.
  3. For every $\gamma\in\U(\al,\be_\eps)$: $$\eps\KL(\gamma|\pi_\eps)=\int c\,\dd\gamma+\eps\KL(\gamma|\al\otimes\be_\eps)+\eps\int\log Z_\eps\,\dd\al+\eps\int\log q_\eps\,\dd\be_\eps .$$$\log\frac{\dd\gamma}{\dd\pi_\eps}=\log\frac{\dd\gamma}{\dd(\al\otimes\be_\eps)}-\log\frac{\dd\pi_\eps}{\dd(\al\otimes\be_\eps)}$; integrate against $\gamma$ and use step 2; the terms in $x$ alone and $y$ alone integrate against the marginals of $\gamma$.

The last two terms do not depend on $\gamma$: minimizing the entropic objective over $\U(\al,\be_\eps)$ is minimizing $\KL(\gamma|\pi_\eps)$, whose unique minimizer is $\gamma=\pi_\eps$. (The same argument as the “if” part of §2.) $\square$

Check by simulation (three atoms for $\al$, $\eps=0.2$): solving the entropic problem between $\al$ and $\al*\N(0,0.1)$ on a fine grid gives conditional laws $Y|X=x$ with mean $x$ and variance $0.100000=\eps/2$ for each atom.

In machine learning — the noising process of diffusion models

Diffusion models generate images by learning to reverse a process that progressively adds Gaussian noise to the data; in its simplest (“variance-exploding”) form, $X_t=X_0+\sigma_tG$. Proposition 8.14 says that each pair $(X_0,X_t)$ of this forward process is exactly the entropic optimal plan between the data distribution and its noisy version, for the quadratic cost and the temperature $\eps=2\sigma_t^2$. (Variance-preserving schedules $X_t=s_tX_0+\sigma_tG$ add a deterministic rescaling, OT4ML p. 147; the density of $(X_0,X_t)$ then has the form $u(x)e^{-\norm{x-y}^2/\eps}v(y)$ with $\eps=2\sigma_t^2/s_t$, so it is again an entropic plan.) The dynamic version of this statement is the Schrödinger bridge (OT4ML §14.3, Def. 14.10): the most likely noisy evolution between two prescribed distributions. Learning such bridges between two arbitrary distributions, rather than between data and pure noise, is a natural generalization of diffusion models (OT4ML §16.1 discusses diffusion models).

Gaussians in closed form

Reminder — Gaussian pairs and matrix square roots

A Gaussian pair $(X,Y)$ with $X\sim\N(m_\al,\sigma_\al^2)$, $Y\sim\N(m_\be,\sigma_\be^2)$ is fixed by $k=\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)$, with $\abs k\le\sigma_\al\sigma_\be$. Then $\E(X-Y)^2=(m_\al-m_\be)^2+\sigma_\al^2+\sigma_\be^2-2k$ and $Y\,|\,X=x\sim\N\big(m_\be+\frac{k}{\sigma_\al^2}(x-m_\al),\ \sigma_\be^2-\frac{k^2}{\sigma_\al^2}\big)$. Example, $\N(0,1)$ and $\N(1,4)$: $k=2$ is the Monge map $Y=1+2X$ ($\E(X-Y)^2=2=W_2^2$), $k=0$ is independence ($\E(X-Y)^2=6$). In dimension $d$, $\B(\Sigma_\al,\Sigma_\be)^2=\tr\Sigma_\al+\tr\Sigma_\be-2\sum_i\sigma_i$, with $\sigma_i$ the singular values of $M=\Sigma_\al^{1/2}\Sigma_\be^{1/2}$, i.e. the eigenvalues of $(M\T M)^{1/2}=\big(\Sigma_\be^{1/2}\Sigma_\al\Sigma_\be^{1/2}\big)^{1/2}$. More on the Reminders page: Gaussian vectors, square roots.

Proposition 9.26 (Balanced entropic OT between Gaussians). Let $\al=\N(m_\al,\Sigma_\al)$ and $\be=\N(m_\be,\Sigma_\be)$ have positive-definite covariances, and let $\Sigma_\al^{1/2}\Sigma_\be^{1/2}=U\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_i)V\T$ be a singular-value decomposition. For the balanced entropic problem (8.14) with $c(x,y)=\norm{x-y}^2$, the optimizer is Gaussian with cross-covariance

$$K_\eps=\Sigma_\al^{1/2}U\operatorname{diag}(s_i)V\T\Sigma_\be^{1/2},\qquad s_i=\frac{\sqrt{\eps^2+16\sigma_i^2}-\eps}{4\sigma_i}.$$

The optimal value is $\ \norm{m_\al-m_\be}^2+\tr(\Sigma_\al)+\tr(\Sigma_\be)+\sum_i\Big(-2\sigma_is_i-\frac\eps2\log(1-s_i^2)\Big).$ As $\eps\downarrow0$, $s_i\to1$ and the full covariance contribution, including the two trace terms, converges to $\B(\Sigma_\al,\Sigma_\be)^2$.

On the line ($d=1$, variances $\sigma_\al^2$, $\sigma_\be^2$), the only singular value is $\sigma_\al\sigma_\be$, and the cross-covariance $k_\eps=\sigma_\al\sigma_\be s$ and the value are

$$k_\eps=\frac{\sqrt{\eps^2+16\sigma_\al^2\sigma_\be^2}-\eps}{4},\qquad\begin{aligned}\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\be)=\ &(m_\al-m_\be)^2+\sigma_\al^2+\sigma_\be^2-2k_\eps\\&-\frac\eps2\log\Big(1-\frac{k_\eps^2}{\sigma_\al^2\sigma_\be^2}\Big).\end{aligned}$$
Idea of the proof (in one dimension)

Replacing a coupling by the Gaussian with the same means and covariances keeps the cost $\E(X-Y)^2$ and does not increase the mutual information (Gaussians maximize entropy at fixed covariance). So only the cross-covariance $k$ is free. For a Gaussian pair with correlation $\rho=k/(\sigma_\al\sigma_\be)$, $\E(X-Y)^2=(m_\al-m_\be)^2+\sigma_\al^2+\sigma_\be^2-2k$ and $\mathcal I(X,Y)=-\frac12\log(1-\rho^2)$. Minimize in $k$: $-2+\frac{\eps k}{\sigma_\al^2\sigma_\be^2-k^2}=0$, i.e. $2k^2+\eps k-2\sigma_\al^2\sigma_\be^2=0$, whose positive root is $k_\eps$.

The bias at low temperature

In the course OT4ML Prop. 8.17, p. 149 · compact Prop. 8.15, p. 37

Proposition 8.17 (Small-temperature quadratic expansion). Let $\al=\rho_\al\dd x$ and $\be=\rho_\be\dd x$ be probability measures on $\R^d$ with bounded compactly supported densities. Let $\al_t=\rho_t\dd x$ be their quadratic displacement interpolation and assume $\ \mathcal I_{geo}(\al,\be):=\int_0^1\int_{\R^d}\norm{\nabla\log\rho_t(x)}^2\rho_t(x)\,\dd x\,\dd t<+\infty.$ For $c(x,y)=\norm{x-y}^2$, define $\ \mathrm H(\al):=\int\rho_\al\log\rho_\al\,\dd x$, $\mathrm H(\be):=\int\rho_\be\log\rho_\be\,\dd x$. Then, as $\eps\downarrow0$,

$$\mathcal L^\eps_{\norm{\cdot}^2}(\al,\be)=\mathcal W_2^2(\al,\be)-\frac{d\eps}2\log(\pi\eps)-\frac\eps2\big(\mathrm H(\al)+\mathrm H(\be)\big)+\frac{\eps^2}{16}\mathcal I_{geo}(\al,\be)+o(\eps^2).$$
$-\frac{d\eps}2\log(\pi\eps)$
The leading bias, positive for $\eps<1/\pi$ and of order $\eps\log(1/\eps)$: the price of blurring a map into a plan of width $\sqrt\eps$ in $d$ directions. It grows with the dimension.
$\mathrm H(\al)=\int\rho_\al\log\rho_\al$
Careful: this is minus the differential entropy (it is large for concentrated densities), while Def. 8.1 used $\mathrm H$ for the entropy itself. See the note below.
$\mathcal I_{geo}$
The Fisher information integrated along the McCann interpolation of Lesson 3: a second-order correction. It will cancel in the Sinkhorn divergence (§9).

Note on the sources — the sign of $\mathrm H$

OT4ML uses the letter $\mathrm H$ with opposite signs: in Def. 8.1, $\mathrm H(P)=-\sum P\log P$ is the entropy; in Prop. 8.17, $\mathrm H(\al)=\int\rho_\al\log\rho_\al$ is minus the (differential) entropy. The expansion is correct with the second meaning: with the differential entropies $+1.418939$ and $+2.112086$ instead, the first-order terms would give $-0.1187$ at $\eps=0.1$ instead of $0.2344$, far from the exact $0.2348$. The exercise sheet also writes $H(P)=\sum P(\log P-1)$, i.e. minus an entropy (conventions box).

Pitfall — “the entropic bias is of order $\eps$”

For discrete problems with a unique optimal plan, yes (§3: $\eps\KL(P^\star|a\otimes b)$). Between densities, no: the optimal plan has infinite mutual information, and the bias is of order $\frac d2\eps\log\frac1\eps$, larger in high dimension. Moreover the bias depends on the measures (through $\mathrm H(\al)+\mathrm H(\be)$), so it does not cancel when you compare two candidate fits: this is why minimizing $\mathcal L^\eps$ over a model shrinks it (§9).

Reflexes
  • Measures → $\frac{\dd\pi_\eps}{\dd(\al\otimes\be)}=e^{(f\oplus g-c)/\eps}$; Sinkhorn = alternating soft $c$-transforms (integrals, or averages over samples).
  • Quadratic cost → “map plus Gaussian noise”: $\mathrm{Var}(Y|X=x)\approx\frac\eps2T'(x)$ in 1D; target $\al*\N(0,\frac\eps2)$ → exact Gibbs plan $(X,X+\sqrt{\eps/2}G)$.
  • Gaussians → solve $2k^2+\eps k-2\sigma_\al^2\sigma_\be^2=0$; bias $\approx-\frac{d\eps}2\log(\pi\eps)-\frac\eps2(\mathrm H(\al)+\mathrm H(\be))$.

Check the Gibbs coupling on Gaussians: if $\al=\N(0,1)$ and $\be=\N(0,1+\frac\eps2)$, what does the closed form give for $k_\eps$?

Answer

Prop. 8.14 predicts $Y=X+\sqrt{\eps/2}\,G$, so $k_\eps=\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)=1$. Closed form: $16\sigma_\al^2\sigma_\be^2=16+8\eps$ and $\sqrt{\eps^2+16+8\eps}=\eps+4$, so $k_\eps=\frac{(\eps+4)-\eps}{4}=1$. ✓
