7. How fast does Sinkhorn converge? OT4ML §9.3–9.5, pp. 178–187

Sinkhorn converges linearly: each cycle multiplies the error by at most $\lambda(K)^2<1$ (Birkhoff’s contraction in Hilbert’s metric), and near the solution by exactly $\sigma_\eps^2$, the squared maximal correlation of the plan. Both tend to $1$ as $\eps\to0$: about $1/\eps$ cycles are needed, and a robust bound gives $O(n^2\log n/\delta^2)$ operations for accuracy $\delta$.

Hilbert’s projective metric

The scalings $u,v$ are only defined up to a positive factor (the gauge), so the right way to measure their convergence ignores that factor. Hilbert’s metric does exactly this, and positive matrices are contractions for it.

In the course OT4ML Def. 9.15, Prop. 9.16, Thm. 9.17, p. 180 · compact Def. 9.13, Thm. 9.15, p. 49

Definition 9.15 (Hilbert metric). On $\R^n_{+,*}$, Hilbert’s projective metric is $\ \forall(u,u')\in(\R^n_{+,*})^2,\ \ d_{\mathcal H}(u,u'):=\norm{\log(u)-\log(u')}_V.\ $ (9.7) Here, $\norm{\cdot}_V$ is the variation seminorm […]: $\norm{z}_V=\max_iz_i-\min_iz_i$.

Proposition 9.16. The function $d_{\mathcal H}$ defines a complete distance on the projective cone $\R^n_{+,*}/\!\sim$, where $u\sim u'$ means that $u=su'$ for some $s>0$.

Theorem 9.17 (Birkhoff contraction theorem). Let $K\in\R^{n\times m}_{+,*}$. Then, for $(v,v')\in(\R^m_{+,*})^2$,

$$d_{\mathcal H}(Kv,Kv')\le\lambda(K)\,d_{\mathcal H}(v,v'),\qquad\lambda(K):=\frac{\sqrt{\eta(K)}-1}{\sqrt{\eta(K)}+1}<1,\qquad\eta(K):=\max_{i,j,k,\ell}\frac{K_{i,k}K_{j,\ell}}{K_{j,k}K_{i,\ell}}.$$
$d_{\mathcal H}(u,u')$
$\log\max_i\frac{u_i}{u'_i}-\log\min_i\frac{u_i}{u'_i}$: how unevenly $u'$ must be stretched, coordinate by coordinate, to become $u$. It is $0$ iff $u=su'$, and does not change if $u$ or $u'$ is multiplied by a positive number: a distance between rays.
$\R^n_{+,*}$
Vectors with positive entries.
$\eta(K)$
The largest cross-ratio of $K$; $\eta\ge1$, and $\log\eta(K)$ is the Hilbert diameter of the image cone $K\R^m_+$.
$\lambda(K)$
The Birkhoff contraction factor, also $\tanh\big(\frac14\log\eta(K)\big)$. For the Gibbs kernel, $\log\eta\le2R/\eps$ with $R=\max C-\min C$, so $\lambda\le\tanh\big(R/(2\eps)\big)$ (OT4ML p. 184).
Idea of the proof of Birkhoff’s theorem

In logarithmic coordinates the map is $F(z)=\log(Ke^z)$, whose derivative is a row-stochastic matrix $P(z)$: each output is a weighted average of the inputs. An average can only shrink the spread $\max-\min$ of $h$, and it shrinks it by the factor $\frac12\max_{i,j}\norm{P_{i\cdot}-P_{j\cdot}}_1$, how different two rows of averaging weights can be. Since the rows of $P(z)$ are rows of $K$ reweighted by the same vector, their ratio is controlled by the cross-ratios of $K$; an elementary inequality turns $\eta(K)$ into $\tanh(\frac14\log\eta)$.

Full proof of Theorem 9.17

Let $F(z)=\log(Ke^z)$ for $z\in\R^m$ (entrywise $\log$ and $\exp$), so that $d_{\mathcal H}(Kv,Kv')=\norm{F(\log v)-F(\log v')}_V$.

  1. $$\frac{\partial F_i}{\partial z_k}(z)=\frac{K_{ik}e^{z_k}}{(Ke^z)_i}=:P(z)_{ik},\qquad\sum_kP(z)_{ik}=1 .$$Chain rule for $\log\sum_kK_{ik}e^{z_k}$. Each row of $P(z)$ is a probability vector.
  2. For probability vectors $p,q$ and any $h$: $\abs{\ps{p-q}{h}}\le\frac12\norm{p-q}_1\norm{h}_V$.$\sum_k(p_k-q_k)=0$, so we may replace $h$ by $h-\min h$, which takes values in $[0,\norm{h}_V]$; then $\ps{p-q}{h}\le\norm{h}_V\sum_k(p_k-q_k)_+=\norm{h}_V\cdot\frac12\norm{p-q}_1$, and the same for $q-p$.
  3. $$\norm{P(z)h}_V\le\delta\big(P(z)\big)\norm{h}_V,\qquad\delta(P):=\tfrac12\max_{i,j}\sum_k\abs{P_{ik}-P_{jk}}.$$$\norm{Ph}_V=\max_{i,j}\big((Ph)_i-(Ph)_j\big)=\max_{i,j}\ps{P_{i\cdot}-P_{j\cdot}}{h}$; apply step 2.
  4. Fix rows $p=P(z)_{i\cdot}$, $q=P(z)_{j\cdot}$ and $r_k=p_k/q_k$. Then $\sum_kq_kr_k=1$ and $\frac{\max_kr_k}{\min_kr_k}=\max_{k,\ell}\frac{K_{ik}K_{j\ell}}{K_{jk}K_{i\ell}}\le\eta(K)$.$r_k=\frac{K_{ik}}{K_{jk}}\cdot\frac{(Ke^z)_j}{(Ke^z)_i}$: the factor $e^{z_k}$ cancels, and the second factor does not depend on $k$.
  5. With $\alpha=\min_kr_k\le1\le\beta=\max_kr_k$: $$\tfrac12\norm{p-q}_1=\sum_kq_k(r_k-1)_+\le\frac{(1-\alpha)(\beta-1)}{\beta-\alpha}.$$$\frac12\norm{p-q}_1=\sum_k(p_k-q_k)_+$. On $[\alpha,\beta]$ the convex function $(r-1)_+$ lies below its chord $(\beta-1)\frac{r-\alpha}{\beta-\alpha}$; and $\sum_kq_k(r_k-\alpha)=1-\alpha$. ($\alpha\le1\le\beta$ because $\sum_kq_kr_k=1$ is an average of the $r_k$.)
  6. $$\frac{(1-\alpha)(\beta-1)}{\beta-\alpha}=\frac{2\sinh(s/2)\sinh(t/2)}{\sinh\big((s+t)/2\big)}\le\tanh\frac{s+t}{4}\le\tanh\frac{\log\eta(K)}4=\lambda(K),$$ where $\alpha=e^{-s}$, $\beta=e^{t}$.Factor $1-e^{-s}=2e^{-s/2}\sinh\frac s2$, $e^t-1=2e^{t/2}\sinh\frac t2$, $e^t-e^{-s}=2e^{(t-s)/2}\sinh\frac{s+t}2$. Then $\sinh x\sinh y=\frac12(\cosh(x+y)-\cosh(x-y))\le\sinh^2\frac{x+y}2$ and $\frac{2\sinh^2(w/2)}{\sinh w}=\tanh\frac w2$. Finally $s+t=\log(\beta/\alpha)\le\log\eta(K)$ (step 4), and $\tanh$ is increasing; $\tanh(\frac14\log\eta)=\frac{\sqrt\eta-1}{\sqrt\eta+1}$.
  7. $$\norm{F(z)-F(z')}_V=\Big\|\int_0^1P(z_t)(z-z')\,\dd t\Big\|_V\le\int_0^1\norm{P(z_t)(z-z')}_V\,\dd t\le\lambda(K)\norm{z-z'}_V,$$ with $z_t=(1-t)z'+tz$.Fundamental theorem of calculus along the segment; $\norm{\cdot}_V$ is a seminorm, so it passes inside the integral as an inequality; steps 3–6 bound $\delta(P(z_t))$ by $\lambda(K)$ for every $t$.

Take $z=\log v$, $z'=\log v'$. $\square$

Global linear convergence

In the course OT4ML Thm. 9.18, (9.9), p. 182 · compact Thm. 9.16, p. 49

Theorem 9.18 (Projective linear convergence of Sinkhorn). Starting from $v^{(0)}>0$, use the scaling iterates of (8.5) […]. Fix any optimal scaling pair $(u^\star,v^\star)$. Writing $\lambda=\lambda(K)$, one has

$$d_{\mathcal H}(v^{(\ell)},v^\star)\le\lambda^{2\ell}d_{\mathcal H}(v^{(0)},v^\star),\qquad d_{\mathcal H}(u^{(\ell+1)},u^\star)\le\lambda^{2\ell+1}d_{\mathcal H}(v^{(0)},v^\star).\qquad(9.9)$$

Thus the scaling rays converge linearly. […] The following result, due to Franklin and Lorenz [229], makes this argument precise.

Proof
  1. For a fixed positive vector $w$: $d_{\mathcal H}(w\odot u,w\odot u')=d_{\mathcal H}(u,u')$ and $d_{\mathcal H}(1/u,1/u')=d_{\mathcal H}(u,u')$.$\log(w\odot u)-\log(w\odot u')=\log u-\log u'$; and $\log(1/u)-\log(1/u')=-(\log u-\log u')$, which has the same $\max-\min$.
  2. The row map $R(v)=a/(Kv)$ and the column map $C(u)=b/(K\T u)$ are $\lambda$-Lipschitz for $d_{\mathcal H}$.Theorem 9.17 for $K$ and for $K\T$ (same cross-ratios, so $\lambda(K\T)=\lambda(K)$), then step 1 for the inversion and the multiplication by $a$ or $b$.
  3. $d_{\mathcal H}(v^{(\ell)},v^\star)=d_{\mathcal H}\big(C(R(v^{(\ell-1)})),C(R(v^\star))\big)\le\lambda^2d_{\mathcal H}(v^{(\ell-1)},v^\star)$.$v^{(\ell)}=C(R(v^{(\ell-1)}))$ and the fixed point $v^\star=C(R(v^\star))$ (the scaling equations (8.4)); step 2 twice.

Iterate step 3; the bound for $u^{(\ell+1)}=R(v^{(\ell)})$ costs one more factor $\lambda$. $\square$

Worked example — the guarantee and the reality

Running example at $\eps=0.5$: $\eta(K)=\frac{1\cdot1}{e^{-2}e^{-2}}=e^4=54.6$, so $\lambda(K)=\frac{e^2-1}{e^2+1}=\tanh1=0.7616$ and $\lambda^2=0.5800$ per cycle. The observed ratio of successive row errors in §4 tends to $0.3621$: convergence is faster than guaranteed. The guarantee degrades fast: $1-\lambda^2=1/\cosh^2\big(\frac14\log\eta(K)\big)\le4/\sqrt{\eta(K)}$, and for a Gibbs kernel $\log\eta(K)=\Delta/\eps$, where $\Delta=\max_{i,j,k,\ell}(C_{jk}+C_{i\ell}-C_{ik}-C_{j\ell})\le2(\max C-\min C)$. So $1-\lambda^2\le4e^{-\Delta/(2\eps)}$: exponentially small in $1/\eps$. For the histograms of the widget below ($\Delta=1.92$), $1-\lambda^2=2.7\times10^{-4}$ at $\eps=0.1$ and $5\times10^{-14}$ at $\eps=0.03$: useless, although Sinkhorn converges in $10$ and $27$ cycles there.

The actual rate: the maximal correlation of the plan

In the course OT4ML Def. 9.19, p. 184 · Props. 9.20–9.21, (9.14), (9.19), pp. 185–186 · compact Prop. 9.18, p. 51

Definition 9.19 (Conditional coupling operator). For a coupling $\pi\in\U(\al,\be)$ […] define $T_\pi:L^2(\be)\to L^2(\al)$ by $(T_\pi k)(x):=\int_{\mathcal Y}k(y)\,\dd\pi_x(y)$. For the optimal entropic coupling $\pi_\eps$, set […] $\sigma_\eps:=\sigma(\pi_\eps)$.

[…] In the discrete setting […] $\sigma_\eps$ is the second singular value of $\operatorname{diag}(a)^{-1/2}P_\eps\operatorname{diag}(b)^{-1/2}$, whose first singular value is one.

Proposition 9.20 (Dual Hessian and local Sinkhorn rate). […] Consequently, the Jacobian of a complete cycle […] is $T^*_\eps T_\eps$. […] $\ \limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\frac{\norm{e^{(\ell+1)}}_{L^2(\be)}}{\norm{e^{(\ell)}}_{L^2(\be)}}\le\sigma_\eps^2$. […] The law of total variance gives $\ 1-\sigma_\eps^2=\inf_{k\in L^2_0(\be),\,\norm{k}_{L^2(\be)}=1}\int\mathrm{Var}_{\pi_{\eps,x}}\big(k(Y)\big)\,\dd\al(x).\ $ (9.19) Thus Sinkhorn slows down when the optimal conditional laws $Y|X=x$ become nearly deterministic. This is exactly what happens as $\eps\to0$ in a smooth Monge regime.

Proposition 9.21. […] $\sigma_\eps\le\lambda(K)<1$, $\ \sigma_\eps^2\le\lambda(K)^2$. (9.20)

$\pi_x$, $T_\pi k(x)$
The conditional law of $Y$ given $X=x$, and the conditional expectation $\E[k(Y)\,|\,X=x]$. For a matrix plan, $T_\eps=\operatorname{diag}(a)^{-1}P_\eps$: row $i$ of the plan divided by $a_i$.
$\sigma(\pi)$
The maximal correlation of $(X,Y)\sim\pi$: the largest correlation between a function of $X$ and a function of $Y$. $0$ for independent variables, $1$ if some non-constant function of $Y$ is a function of $X$ (e.g. $Y=T(X)$).
$e^{(\ell)}$
The error $g^{(\ell)}-g_\eps$ on the potential, centered (gauge fixed). Near the solution it is multiplied by $T^*_\eps T_\eps$ at each cycle, an operator of norm $\sigma_\eps^2$.
(9.19)
$1-\sigma_\eps^2$ is the smallest average conditional variance of a normalized function of $Y$ given $X$: the less randomness the plan leaves, the slower Sinkhorn.
Worked example — the $0.3621$ explained

For a $2\times2$ plan the centered spaces have dimension one, so $\sigma_\eps$ is the absolute correlation of the indicators $X=\ind\{\text{row }2\}$ and $Y=\ind\{\text{column }2\}$ under $P_\eps$:

$$\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)=P_{22}-a_2b_2=0.487873-0.35=0.137873,\qquad\sigma_\eps=\frac{0.137873}{\sqrt{0.7\times0.3}\sqrt{0.5\times0.5}}=0.601729 .$$

So $\sigma_\eps^2=0.362078$: the limit of the ratios $0.459,\ 0.404,\ 0.378,\ 0.368,\ 0.364,\ 0.3629,\ 0.3624$ of successive errors in §4. And $\sigma_\eps^2=0.362\le\lambda^2=0.580$, as (9.20) says.

A robust rate, and the cost of approximating optimal transport

In the course OT4ML Prop. 9.13, p. 178 · Cor. 9.14, pp. 179–180 · compact Prop. 9.11, Cor. 9.12, p. 48

Proposition 9.13 (Robust $O(1/\ell)$ dual rate for discrete Sinkhorn). Let $a\in\Sigma_n$ and $b\in\Sigma_m$ be positive histograms, and let $C\in\R^{n\times m}_+$ be finite. Initialize $g^{(0)}=0$, perform complete Sinkhorn dual cycles […]. Let $\Delta^{(\ell)}:=\mathcal D_\eps(f^\star,g^\star)-\mathcal D_\eps(f^{(\ell)},g^{(\ell)})$ be the dual suboptimality gap […]. Then $\ 0\le\Delta^{(\ell)}\le\frac{2\norm{C}_\infty^2}{\eps\ell}$, $\ell\ge1$.

Corollary 9.14. […] For any $\delta>0$, choose $\eps=\delta/\log(nm)$. Then $\ \ell\ge\frac{2\norm{C}^2_\infty\log(nm)}{\delta^2}\implies L_0-\mathcal D_\eps(f^{(\ell)},g^{(\ell)})\le\delta$.

For dense $n\times n$ problems, one Sinkhorn cycle costs $O(n^2)$ operations. Corollary 9.14 therefore yields the arithmetic complexity $\ O\Big(\frac{n^2\norm{C}^2_\infty\log n}{\delta^2}\Big)\ $ for additive accuracy $\delta$.

$O(1/\ell)$
Slower than linear, but with a constant $2\norm{C}^2_\infty/\eps$ that is only polynomial in $1/\eps$, whereas $1/(1-\lambda^2)$ is exponential in $1/\eps$. It describes the first phase, before the linear regime of the widget sets in.
$\eps=\delta/\log(nm)$
The bias of §3 is at most $\eps\log(nm)$; this choice makes it at most $\delta$. The two errors (bias and optimization) are then both $\le\delta$.
$O(n^2\log n/\delta^2)$
Nearly quadratic in $n$, against $O(n^3)$ for the Hungarian method — but polynomial in $1/\delta$, against exact for combinatorial solvers. In the book’s words, Sinkhorn is “especially attractive for large-scale problems requiring low or moderate precision, rather than for computing highly accurate exact solutions” (p. 180).
Idea of the proof of Proposition 9.13

Soft $c$-transforms have a bounded oscillation: $\norm{f}_V\le\norm{C}_\infty$. Concavity bounds the gap by $\ps{f^\star-f^{(\ell)}}{a-r^{(\ell)}}\le2\norm{C}_\infty\norm{a-r^{(\ell)}}_1$, while each half-step increases the dual by $\eps\KL(a|r^{(\ell)})\ge\frac\eps2\norm{a-r^{(\ell)}}^2_1$ (Pinsker; this increase is computed in Exercise 11 of §14). So the gap decreases at least by a constant times its square at each cycle, $\Delta_{\ell}-\Delta_{\ell+1}\ge\frac{\eps}{8\norm{C}^2_\infty}\Delta_\ell^2$, and such sequences decay like $1/\ell$.

Acceleration — over-relaxation

OT4ML Prop. 9.22 (p. 187) extrapolates each block update, $f\leftarrow(1-\omega)f+\omega\,g^{\bar c,\eps}$ and similarly for $g$, and shows that the best local factor is $r^\star=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-\sigma_\eps^2}}{1+\sqrt{1-\sigma_\eps^2}}$ at $\omega^\star=\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1-\sigma_\eps^2}}$. Since $1-r^\star\approx2\sqrt{1-\sigma_\eps^2}$, the number of cycles drops from about $1/(1-\sigma_\eps^2)$ to $1/\sqrt{1-\sigma_\eps^2}$. On the widget’s histograms: at $\eps=0.01$, $\omega^\star=1.342$ and the cycles to $10^{-9}$ drop from $80$ to $26$; at $\eps=0.001$, $\omega^\star=1.751$ and they drop from $1006$ to $97$. Another classical trick is $\eps$-scaling: start with a large $\eps$ and decrease it along the iterations, warm-starting the potentials (OT4ML p. 175).

Pitfall — “Sinkhorn converges linearly, so it is fast”

Linear with a factor $\sigma_\eps^2$ that tends to $1$: the number of cycles grows like $1/\eps$, and the global bound $\lambda^2$ is so close to $1$ for small $\eps$ that it says nothing. Conversely, do not conclude from the pessimistic bound that Sinkhorn is useless at small $\eps$: measure the marginal error.

Reflexes
  • Convergence of scalings → Hilbert metric (ignores the gauge); positive kernel → Birkhoff factor $\lambda=\tanh(\frac14\log\eta)$; one cycle → $\lambda^2$.
  • Observed rate → $\sigma_\eps^2$, the squared second singular value of $\operatorname{diag}(a)^{-1/2}P_\eps\operatorname{diag}(b)^{-1/2}$; slow when $Y|X$ is nearly deterministic.
  • Approximating $L_C$ to accuracy $\delta$ → $\eps\approx\delta/\log n$, $O(\log n/\delta^2)$ cycles of cost $n^2$.
