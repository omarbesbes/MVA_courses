9. Entropic bias and Sinkhorn divergences OT4ML §8.9, pp. 166–169

The raw entropic cost is not a distance: $\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\al)>0$, and fitting a model by minimizing it shrinks the model (even to a point at high temperature). Subtracting the two self-transport terms gives the Sinkhorn divergence, which is non-negative, vanishes only at $\al=\be$, and interpolates between optimal transport ($\eps\to0$) and a kernel distance of MMD type ($\eps\to\infty$).

The problem: the raw cost prefers a shrunk copy

In the course OT4ML p. 166, Prop. 8.35 and Example 8.36

A basic defect of the raw entropic value is its self-interaction bias. Even when $c(x,x)=0$, one generally has $\mathcal L^\eps_c(\al,\al)>0$ for a non-Dirac measure. […] Consequently, a minimizer $\be_\eps\in\argmin_\be\mathcal L^\eps_c(\al,\be)$ need not equal $\al$ for $\eps>0$.

Proposition 8.35 (Large-temperature entropic bias). Let $\X,\Y$ be compact and assume that $c$ is continuous. Then $\mathcal L^\eps_c(\al,\be)\to\iint c(x,y)\,\dd\al(x)\dd\be(y)$ as $\eps\to+\infty$.

Example 8.36 (Large temperature collapse). […] The limiting functional is linear in the second argument: $\ \be\mapsto\int V_\al(y)\,\dd\be(y)$, $V_\al(y):=\int c(x,y)\,\dd\al(x)$. Thus every cluster point is supported on $\argmin V_\al$. […] For the quadratic cost $c(x,y)=\norm{x-y}^2$ […] the collapse is toward the Dirac mass at the mean of $\al$.

$\mathcal L^\eps_c(\al,\al)>0$
Transporting $\al$ to itself costs something: the identity plan has infinite (densities) or large (discrete) mutual information, so the entropic plan blurs, and blurred mass travels. A loss that is positive at the perfect fit is a biased loss.
$\iint c\,\dd\al\,\dd\be$
The cost of the independent coupling. It is linear in $\be$, so it is minimized by a Dirac at the point $y$ minimizing the average cost $V_\al(y)$ to $\al$: for the quadratic cost, the mean of $\al$.
Worked example — the bias and the shrinkage for Gaussians

For $\al=\N(0,1)$, the closed form of §8 gives the self-cost $\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\al)$:

$\eps$$0.01$$0.1$$0.5$$1$$10$$100$
$\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\al)$$0.0315$$0.2004$$0.6122$$0.9088$$1.8038$$1.9800$

It tends to $\iint\abs{x-y}^2\dd\al\,\dd\al=2$ as $\eps\to\infty$. Now fit $\be=\N(0,s^2)$ to $\al$ by minimizing $s\mapsto\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\N(0,s^2))$: the best width is $s^\star=0.9975$ at $\eps=0.01$, $0.9747$ at $\eps=0.1$, $0.8660$ at $\eps=0.5$, $0.7071$ at $\eps=1$, and for $\eps\ge2$ the loss decreases all the way to $s=0$ — the collapse of Example 8.36. These are the values $s^\star=\sqrt{1-\eps/2}$ derived below.

Derivation of $s^\star=\sqrt{1-\eps/2}$ (added for this page)

Write $L(s)=\min_k\Phi(s,k)$ with $\Phi(s,k)=1+s^2-2k-\frac\eps2\log\big(1-\frac{k^2}{s^2}\big)$ (§8 with $m_\al=m_\be=0$, $\sigma_\al=1$, $\sigma_\be=s$).

  1. The optimal $k$ satisfies $\partial_k\Phi=0$: $\ -2+\dfrac{\eps k}{s^2-k^2}=0$, i.e. $\eps k=2(s^2-k^2)$.$\partial_k\big[-\frac\eps2\log(1-k^2/s^2)\big]=\frac{\eps k/s^2}{1-k^2/s^2}=\frac{\eps k}{s^2-k^2}$.
  2. $$L'(s)=\partial_s\Phi(s,k)=2s-\frac{\eps k^2/s}{s^2-k^2}=2s-\frac{2k}{s}.$$Envelope theorem (the derivative of a minimum is the partial derivative at the minimizer; Exercise 5 of the sheet). $\partial_s\big[-\frac\eps2\log(1-k^2s^{-2})\big]=-\frac\eps2\cdot\frac{2k^2s^{-3}}{1-k^2s^{-2}}$; then step 1.
  3. $L'(s)=0\iff s^2=k$, and then step 1 reads $\eps s^2=2(s^2-s^4)$, i.e. $s^2=1-\frac\eps2$.Substitute $k=s^2$.
  4. For $\eps\ge2$, $L'(s)>0$ for every $s>0$: no interior minimizer, the infimum is approached as $s\to0$.$s^2>k$ is equivalent (with $k=\frac{\sqrt{\eps^2+16s^2}-\eps}4$) to $16s^4+8\eps s^2-16s^2>0$, i.e. $s^2>1-\frac\eps2$, true for all $s$ when $\eps\ge2$.

The Sinkhorn divergence

In the course OT4ML Def. 8.37, (8.52), p. 166 · Lemma 8.39, (8.54), p. 167 · compact Def. 8.31, Lemma 8.32, p. 44

Definition 8.37 (Sinkhorn divergence). Let $c$ be a symmetric cost on a common state space. For $\eps>0$, the debiased Sinkhorn divergence associated with the entropic OT value $\mathcal L^\eps_c$ is

$$\bar{\mathcal L}^\eps_c(\al,\be):=\mathcal L^\eps_c(\al,\be)-\frac12\mathcal L^\eps_c(\al,\al)-\frac12\mathcal L^\eps_c(\be,\be).\qquad(8.52)$$

Although this formula is a debiasing by self-costs, its non-negativity is not automatic from the definition […].

Lemma 8.39 (Entropic dual cost at optimum). Let $(f_{\al,\be},g_{\al,\be})$ be optimal dual potentials, normalized arbitrarily. Then $\ \mathcal L^\eps_c(\al,\be)=\ps{f_{\al,\be}}{\al}+\ps{g_{\al,\be}}{\be}.$ (8.54)

$\bar{\mathcal L}^\eps_c$, written $S_\eps$ on this page
The cross term minus the average of the two self terms. The same construction turns a kernel into a squared distance: $\norm{\phi(x)-\phi(y)}^2=k(x,x)+k(y,y)-2k(x,y)$; here $-\mathcal L^\eps$ plays the role of the kernel.
$S_\eps(\al,\al)=0$
Immediate from the definition: the bias is removed at the perfect fit.
(8.54)
At the optimum the exponential term of the dual (8.24) integrates to $0$, so the value is just $\ps{f}{\al}+\ps{g}{\be}$ (the continuous version of §2). For the self term $\al=\be$, symmetry allows $f_{\al,\al}=g_{\al,\al}$, so $\ps{f_{\al,\al}}{\al}=\frac12\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\al)$.

$$\bar{\mathcal L}^\eps_c(\al,\be)\to\frac12\int-c\ \dd(\al-\be)\otimes\dd(\al-\be)\qquad\text{when }\eps\to+\infty.$$

$\frac12\int-c\,\dd(\al-\be)^{\otimes2}$
$\iint c\,\dd\al\dd\be-\frac12\iint c\,\dd\al\dd\al-\frac12\iint c\,\dd\be\dd\be$: the large-$\eps$ limit of each term (Prop. 8.35) put into (8.52). For $c=\norm{x-y}$ it is the energy distance, an MMD with the kernel $-\norm{x-y}$ (refresher).
$k_\eps$ positive semidefinite
True for $c=\norm{x-y}^2$ (Gaussian kernel) and $c=\norm{x-y}$ (Laplace kernel): $\sum_{ij}c_ic_je^{-c(x_i,x_j)/\eps}\ge0$. Both are also universal.
$\al_n\wto\al$
Weak convergence (Lesson 3): $S_\eps$ metrizes the same convergence as $W_p$ on a compact space.
Idea of the proof of positivity

Plug into the dual of $\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\be)$ the (suboptimal) pair made of the self-potentials $f_{\al,\al}$ and $f_{\be,\be}$. Their dual value is $\frac12\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\al)+\frac12\mathcal L^\eps(\be,\be)$ minus a penalty that is a kernel inner product $\eps\ps{\tilde\al}{\tilde\be}_{k_\eps}$ of two reweighted measures, plus $\eps$. The self Sinkhorn equations say that each reweighted measure has kernel norm $1$; Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\ps{\tilde\al}{\tilde\be}_{k_\eps}\le1$.

Full proof that $S_\eps\ge0$

Let $f_\al=f_{\al,\al}=g_{\al,\al}$ and $f_\be=f_{\be,\be}=g_{\be,\be}$ be symmetric optimal potentials of the self problems, and $k_\eps=e^{-c/\eps}$.

  1. $$\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\be)\ge\ps{f_\al}{\al}+\ps{f_\be}{\be}-\eps\iint\Big(e^{\frac{f_\al(x)+f_\be(y)-c(x,y)}{\eps}}-1\Big)\dd\al(x)\dd\be(y).$$Weak duality (8.23): the dual objective $\mathcal D_\eps$ at any pair is below the primal value; here the pair $(f_\al,f_\be)$.
  2. $\ps{f_\al}{\al}=\frac12\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\al)$ and $\ps{f_\be}{\be}=\frac12\mathcal L^\eps(\be,\be)$.Lemma 8.39 for the self problems with $f=g$.
  3. $$S_\eps(\al,\be)\ge\eps\big(1-\ps{\tilde\al}{\tilde\be}_{k_\eps}\big),\qquad\tilde\al=e^{f_\al/\eps}\al,\ \tilde\be=e^{f_\be/\eps}\be,\ \ \ps{\mu}{\nu}_{k_\eps}=\iint k_\eps\,\dd\mu\,\dd\nu .$$Steps 1–2 and $e^{(f_\al(x)+f_\be(y)-c(x,y))/\eps}=e^{f_\al(x)/\eps}k_\eps(x,y)e^{f_\be(y)/\eps}$; the “$-1$” integrates to $1$ against $\al\otimes\be$.
  4. $\norm{\tilde\al}^2_{k_\eps}=1$ and $\norm{\tilde\be}^2_{k_\eps}=1$.The self Sinkhorn equation $f_\al=(f_\al)^{c,\eps}$ exponentiated reads $e^{f_\al(x)/\eps}\int k_\eps(x,y)\,\dd\tilde\al(y)=1$ for $\al$-a.e. $x$; integrate against $\al$: $\iint k_\eps\,\dd\tilde\al\,\dd\tilde\al=\int1\,\dd\al=1$.
  5. $\ps{\tilde\al}{\tilde\be}_{k_\eps}\le\norm{\tilde\al}_{k_\eps}\norm{\tilde\be}_{k_\eps}=1$, hence $S_\eps(\al,\be)\ge0$.$k_\eps$ positive semidefinite, so $\ps{\cdot}{\cdot}_{k_\eps}$ is a (semi-)inner product and Cauchy–Schwarz holds. $\square$

(The equality case and the metrization need universality of the kernel; see OT4ML p. 169.)

Note on the sources — the 2019 notes, §7

The same material in the 2019 notes (pp. 36–37) has four slips, each easy to check: (i) the definition of $\bar{\mathcal L}^\eps_c$ subtracts $\frac12\mathcal L^\eps_c(\al,\be)$ instead of $\frac12\mathcal L^\eps_c(\be,\be)$; (ii) the proof of Prop. 21 ends with $\mathcal L^\eps_c(\al,\be)=-\iint c\,\dd\al\dd\be+o(1)$, with a wrong sign (for $\N(0,1)$ and $\N(1,4)$ the value at $\eps=1000$ is $5.992$, close to $+\iint c=6$); (iii) in the proof of Prop. 22 the exponent should contain $f_{\al,\al}\oplus g_{\be,\be}$, not $f_{\al,\be}\oplus g_{\al,\be}$, and the bound should read $1-\ps{e^{(\dots)/\eps}}{\al\otimes\be}$ without the extra “$-1$” inside (with it, the case $\al=\be$ would give $0\ge1$); (iv) the reweighted measure is $e^{f_{\al,\al}/\eps}\al$, not $e^{f_{\al,\al}}\al$. OT4ML’s version, followed above, is correct.

Worked example — from $W_2^2$ to the means

For $\al=\N(0,1)$ and $\be=\N(1,4)$ ($W_2^2=2$), the closed forms give:

$\eps$$0.01$$0.1$$1$$10$$100$$1000$
raw $\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\be)$$2.0350$$2.2348$$3.2244$$5.2534$$5.9201$$5.9920$
$S_\eps(\al,\be)$$1.999999$$1.999922$$1.992309$$1.650977$$1.089554$$1.009000$
  • Small $\eps$: the raw bias $\mathcal L^\eps-W_2^2$ is of order $\eps\log\frac1\eps$ (§8), while $S_\eps-W_2^2=-7.812\times10^{-7}$ at $\eps=0.01$ and $-7.811\times10^{-5}$ at $\eps=0.1$: of order $\eps^2$. In the expansion of Prop. 8.17, the $\eps\log\eps$ and entropy terms cancel between the cross and self terms, and what remains is $\frac{\eps^2}{16}\big(\mathcal I_{geo}(\al,\be)-\frac12\mathcal I_{geo}(\al,\al)-\frac12\mathcal I_{geo}(\be,\be)\big)=-\frac{\eps^2}{32}\big(\frac1{\sigma_\al}-\frac1{\sigma_\be}\big)^2=-\frac{\eps^2}{128}$ here, i.e. $-7.8125\times10^{-7}$ at $\eps=0.01$.
  • Large $\eps$: $S_\eps\to\frac12\int-\abs{x-y}^2\dd(\al-\be)^{\otimes2}=(m_\al-m_\be)^2=1$. For the quadratic cost the limit only sees the means: $-\abs{x-y}^2$ is at the edge of the family of Remark 8.41 ($p=2$ is excluded), and the limit is a seminorm, not a norm. With $c=\abs{x-y}$ the limit would be the energy distance, which separates all measures.
  • Translations: $S_\eps\big(\N(0,1),\N(1.5,1)\big)=2.25=1.5^2$ for every $\eps$ ($0.01$, $1$, $100$): the Sinkhorn divergence is exact on translations (Exercise 10 of §14; OT4ML Cor. 9.27, p. 190).

With “the width $s$”, the raw loss (orange-red) has its minimum to the left of $s=1$ and the minimizer moves left as $\eps$ grows — it reaches $s=0$ beyond $\eps=2$ — while the Sinkhorn divergence (green) always vanishes exactly at $s=1$, where it touches $0$. With “the mean $m$”, all three curves are parabolas with the right minimizer $m=0$: the bias does not depend on a translation, so it does not hurt the fit of a location; it hurts the fit of a shape.

In machine learning — Sinkhorn divergences as training losses (GeomLoss)

To train a generative model $g_\theta$ (a network mapping random noise $z$ to images, or 3D points), one compares the cloud of generated samples $\{g_\theta(z_i)\}$ with a mini-batch of data $\{y_j\}$ and descends the gradient of a discrepancy. Genevay, Peyré and Cuturi (Learning generative models with Sinkhorn divergences, AISTATS 2018) proposed $S_\eps$ for this: unlike the KL divergence it is finite between clouds with different supports, unlike exact transport it is differentiable and computed by Sinkhorn on a GPU, and unlike the raw $\mathcal L^\eps$ it does not reward shrunk or collapsed models. Feydy et al. (Interpolating between optimal transport and MMD using Sinkhorn divergences, AISTATS 2019) proved the properties above (positivity, metrization, the OT–MMD interpolation) and released the GeomLoss library, used for shape registration, point-cloud and generative modelling. The temperature is a genuine design choice: small $\eps$ gives the geometry of transport (good matchings, informative gradients), large $\eps$ the statistical robustness of MMD (§10).

Pitfall — using the raw entropic cost as a loss

$\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\be)$ is minimized by a $\be$ that is a shrunk version of $\al$, and by a Dirac at the mean when $\eps$ is large. A model trained with the raw cost will be too concentrated (at high temperature it collapses towards the mean of the data, Example 8.36; OT4ML Fig. 8.15, p. 167, shows the same on a point cloud). Always debias: $S_\eps=\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\be)-\frac12\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\al)-\frac12\mathcal L^\eps(\be,\be)$. The extra cost is one more Sinkhorn per self term, and in a training loop the self term of the data is computed once.

Reflexes
  • Loss between distributions with entropy → debias with the two self terms.
  • $S_\eps\ge0$ ← self-potentials in the cross dual + kernel Cauchy–Schwarz ($e^{-c/\eps}$ positive definite).
  • $\eps\to0$: $S_\eps\to\mathcal L_c$ with an error $O(\eps^2)$ for smooth densities; $\eps\to\infty$: $S_\eps\to-\frac12\iint c\,\dd(\al-\be)^{\otimes2}$ (energy distance for $c=\norm{x-y}$).

For two Diracs, compute $\mathcal L^\eps(\delta_x,\delta_y)$ and $S_\eps(\delta_x,\delta_y)$ for a symmetric cost with $c(x,x)=0$.

Answer

The only coupling is $\delta_{(x,y)}=\delta_x\otimes\delta_y$, whose KL with respect to $\delta_x\otimes\delta_y$ is $0$. So $\mathcal L^\eps(\delta_x,\delta_y)=c(x,y)$, $\mathcal L^\eps(\delta_x,\delta_x)=0$, and $S_\eps(\delta_x,\delta_y)=c(x,y)$ for every $\eps$: no bias for Diracs (Exercise 7 of §14).
