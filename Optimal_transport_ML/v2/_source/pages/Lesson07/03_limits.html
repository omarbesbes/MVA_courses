3. The two limits: $\eps\to0$ and $\eps\to\infty$ OT4ML Prop. 8.10, p. 144

At low temperature the entropic plan converges to an optimal transport plan (the one of largest entropy if there are several) and its value to $L_C$; at high temperature it converges to the independent coupling $a\otimes b$. In between, $\eps$ is a dial from “transport” to “independence”.

The statement

The penalty was added to get a well-behaved problem; the question is now how far its solution is from the one we wanted. The temperature $\eps$ controls the answer.

Proposition 8.10 (Convergence with $\eps$). Assume, after removing zero-mass rows and columns, that a and b are positive and that C is finite. The unique solution $P_\eps$ of (8.1) converges to the optimal solution with maximal entropy within the set of all optimal solutions of the Kantorovich problem, namely

$$P_\eps\xrightarrow{\ \eps\to0\ }\argmin_P\big\{-\mathrm H(P)\ ;\ P\in\U(a,b),\ \ps{P}{C}=L_C(a,b)\big\}\qquad(8.10)$$

so that in particular $\ \mathrm L^\eps_C(a,b)\xrightarrow{\eps\to0}L_C(a,b)$. Moreover, $\ P_\eps\xrightarrow{\eps\to\infty}a\otimes b.\qquad(8.11)$

Idea of the proof

$\eps\to0$: compare the entropic objective at $P_\eps$ and at an optimal plan $P$. Optimality of each gives a sandwich $0\le\ps{C}{P_\eps}-\ps{C}{P}\le\eps\big(\KL(P)-\KL(P_\eps)\big)$: in the limit, every cluster point is optimal for the cost and, after dividing by $\eps$, has a smaller KL than any optimal $P$. Strict convexity of KL gives a unique limit. $\eps\to\infty$: compare with $a\otimes b$, whose KL is $0$: $\KL(P_\eps|a\otimes b)\le\norm{C}_\infty/\eps\to0$.

Full proof

Write $\KL(P)$ for $\KL(P|a\otimes b)$ and use the normalized objective (8.8), which has the same minimizers as (8.1).

Case $\eps\to0$. Let $\eps_\ell\to0$, $\eps_\ell>0$, and let $P_\ell$ be the entropic plan for $\eps_\ell$.

  1. Up to a subsequence, $P_\ell\to P^\star$ for some $P^\star\in\U(a,b)$.$\U(a,b)$ is compact (§1): Bolzano–Weierstrass.
  2. Let $P$ be any optimal plan of the linear program: $\ps{C}{P}=L_C(a,b)$. Then $\ps{C}{P}\le\ps{C}{P_\ell}$.$P_\ell\in\U(a,b)$ and $P$ minimizes $\ps{C}{\cdot}$ over $\U(a,b)$.
  3. $\ps{C}{P_\ell}+\eps_\ell\KL(P_\ell)\le\ps{C}{P}+\eps_\ell\KL(P)$.$P_\ell$ minimizes the entropic objective and $P$ is feasible.
  4. $$0\le\ps{C}{P_\ell}-\ps{C}{P}\le\eps_\ell\big(\KL(P)-\KL(P_\ell)\big).\qquad(8.12)$$Steps 2 and 3.
  5. $\ps{C}{P^\star}=\ps{C}{P}=L_C(a,b)$: $P^\star$ is optimal.Let $\ell\to\infty$ in step 4: the right side tends to $0$, since $\KL$ is bounded on $\U(a,b)$ (by $\log(nm)$, in fact by $\mathrm H(a)+\mathrm H(b)$).
  6. $\KL(P_\ell)\le\KL(P)$ for every $\ell$, hence $\KL(P^\star)\le\KL(P)$.Divide step 4 by $\eps_\ell>0$: $0\le\KL(P)-\KL(P_\ell)$. Then let $\ell\to\infty$, using the continuity of $\KL(\cdot|a\otimes b)$ on $\U(a,b)$ ($t\log t$ is continuous on $[0,1]$).
  7. $P^\star$ minimizes $\KL(\cdot|a\otimes b)$, equivalently maximizes $\mathrm H$, over the optimal face $\{P\in\U(a,b):\ps{C}{P}=L_C\}$.Steps 5 and 6, since $P$ was any optimal plan; on $\U(a,b)$, $\KL=-\mathrm H+\text{const}$ (§1).
  8. This minimizer is unique, so the whole family converges: $P_\eps\to P^\star$ as $\eps\to0$.The optimal face is convex and compact and $\KL$ is strictly convex: unique minimizer. Every subsequence has a further subsequence converging to the same $P^\star$, which forces convergence of the whole family.
  9. $\mathrm L^\eps_C(a,b)\to L_C(a,b)$.$L_C\le\ps{C}{P_\eps}\le\ps{C}{P_\eps}+\eps\KL(P_\eps)\le L_C+\eps\KL(P)$ by steps 2–3; the bounds pinch. For the convention (8.1), subtract the constant $\eps(\mathrm H(a)+\mathrm H(b))\to0$.

Case $\eps\to\infty$.

  1. We may assume $C\ge0$.Subtracting $\min_{ij}C_{ij}$ from every entry changes $\ps{C}{P}$ by the same constant for every $P\in\U(a,b)$ (total mass $1$), so the minimizer does not change.
  2. $\ps{C}{P_\eps}+\eps\KL(P_\eps)\le\ps{C}{a\otimes b}+\eps\cdot0$.$a\otimes b\in\U(a,b)$ and $\KL(a\otimes b|a\otimes b)=0$.
  3. $$\KL(P_\eps|a\otimes b)\le\frac{\ps{C}{a\otimes b}}{\eps}\le\frac{\norm{C}_\infty}{\eps}\xrightarrow{\eps\to\infty}0.$$Drop $\ps{C}{P_\eps}\ge0$ and divide by $\eps$.
  4. $P_\eps\to a\otimes b$.Pinsker’s inequality: $\norm{P_\eps-a\otimes b}_1\le\sqrt{2\KL(P_\eps|a\otimes b)}\to0$ (refresher). $\square$
Corollary (added for this page) — the bias of the value

Steps 2–3 of the proof, with $P=P^\star$ an optimal plan, give a two-sided bound on the bias of the value:

$$0\ \le\ \eps\KL(P_\eps|a\otimes b)\ \le\ L^\eps_C(a,b)-L_C(a,b)\ \le\ \eps\KL(P^\star|a\otimes b)\ \le\ \eps\min\big(\mathrm H(a),\mathrm H(b)\big).$$

(The last inequality: $\KL(P^\star|a\otimes b)=\mathrm H(a)+\mathrm H(b)-\mathrm H(P^\star)$ and $\mathrm H(P)\ge\max(\mathrm H(a),\mathrm H(b))$ on couplings, Exercise 1 of §14.) So the value is biased upwards, by at most $\eps$ times the mutual information of an optimal plan. Running example: at $\eps=0.5$, $0.1065\le0.1307\le0.1372$; at $\eps=0.1$ all three numbers equal $0.0274$ to four digits, because $P_\eps$ is then exponentially close to $P^\star$. For measures with densities the mutual information of the optimal plan is infinite (a map has no spread) and the bias is larger, of order $\eps\log(1/\eps)$ (§8).

Note on the sources — a flipped inequality in the 2019 notes

The 2019 notes prove the same result (Prop. 10, p. 18), but their inequality (29) reads $0\le\ps{C}{P_\ell}-\ps{C}{P}\le\eps_\ell\big(\KL(P_\ell|a\otimes b)-\KL(P|a\otimes b)\big)$, with the two KL terms in the wrong order, and the next line concludes $\KL(P|a\otimes b)\le\KL(P^\star|a\otimes b)$, also reversed. The book’s (8.12), reproduced above, has the right order. Check on the running example at $\eps=0.5$: $\ps{C}{P_\eps}-\ps{C}{P^\star}=0.024253$, while the printed right side is $0.5\times(0.212971-0.274358)=-0.030694<0$, which would contradict the left inequality; the correct right side is $+0.030694$.

Intuition — a temperature

The name comes from statistical physics: $P_{ij}\propto u_iv_je^{-C_{ij}/\eps}$ is a Boltzmann distribution, the cost is an energy and $\eps$ a temperature. At zero temperature the system freezes in its lowest-energy state (an optimal plan); at infinite temperature thermal agitation wins and every configuration compatible with the marginals is equally likely (the independent coupling, the maximum-entropy coupling).

Pitfall — “a smaller $\eps$ is always better”

Smaller $\eps$ means less bias, but the Gibbs kernel $e^{-C/\eps}$ underflows (§6), Sinkhorn needs more iterations (about $1/\eps$, §7), and the statistical error from samples grows (§10). In practice $\eps$ is chosen on the scale of the costs you want to resolve, for instance a few percent of the typical $C_{ij}$; it is a hyperparameter, not a precision.

Worked example — when several plans are optimal

If $C$ is constant, every coupling has the same cost, so the optimal face is all of $\U(a,b)$. Then $P_\eps=a\otimes b$ for every $\eps>0$ (it minimizes the penalty and the cost cannot tell plans apart), and the limit selected by (8.10) is the independent coupling, the maximum-entropy plan. A less obvious case: on the line, $x=(0,1)$, $y=(1,2)$, $a=b=(\frac12,\frac12)$, cost $\abs{x-y}$. Every coupling $\begin{pmatrix}s&\frac12-s\\\frac12-s&s\end{pmatrix}$ costs $s\cdot1+(\tfrac12-s)\cdot2+(\tfrac12-s)\cdot0+s\cdot1=1$: when all the mass moves to the right, every plan is optimal for the distance cost. So $P_\eps=a\otimes b$ ($s=\frac14$) for every $\eps$, while the linear program may return either vertex. The entropic limit picks the “most hedged” optimal plan, not a permutation.

In machine learning — differentiable sorting and ranking

Sorting $n$ numbers $x_1,\dots,x_n$ is a transport problem on the line: send the uniform measure on the $x_i$ to the uniform measure on fixed increasing targets $y_1<\dotssoft ranks and soft sorts, smooth in $x$, which tend to the exact ones as $\eps\to0$ (Cuturi, Teboul and Vert, NeurIPS 2019). Example with $x=(0.3,-1,2)$, targets $(1,2,3)$, cost $(x-y)^2$: the ranks are $(2,1,3)$; the soft ranks are $(1.94,\ 1.20,\ 2.86)$ at $\eps=1$ and $(1.99,\ 1.01,\ 3.00)$ at $\eps=0.3$; the soft sort is $(-0.74,\ 0.28,\ 1.76)$ at $\eps=1$ and $(-0.98,\ 0.29,\ 1.99)$ at $\eps=0.3$, against the exact $(-1,\ 0.3,\ 2)$. Here $\eps$ trades exactness against smoothness of the gradient.

Reflexes
  • $\eps\to0$ → optimal plan of maximal entropy, value $\to L_C$; bias of the value between $\eps\KL(P_\eps)$ and $\eps\KL(P^\star)$.
  • $\eps\to\infty$ → $a\otimes b$; proof: compare with $a\otimes b$ and use $\KL\le\norm{C}_\infty/\eps$ plus Pinsker.
  • Discrete problem with a unique optimal plan → the plan converges exponentially fast ($e^{-\Delta/\eps}$), the value only linearly ($\eps\KL(P^\star)$).

On the running example, the bound $\KL(P_\eps|a\otimes b)\le\ps{C}{a\otimes b}/\eps$ of the proof: compute it at $\eps=10$ and compare with the true value $0.001048$.

Answer

$\ps{C}{a\otimes b}=0.15+0.35=0.5$ (the off-diagonal entries of $a\otimes b$ are $0.3\times0.5$ and $0.7\times0.5$), so the bound is $0.05$. The true value is about $48$ times smaller: the bound is crude, the actual decay is like $1/\eps^2$ ($0.001048$ at $\eps=10$, $0.0000105$ at $\eps=100$; Exercise 8 of §14).
