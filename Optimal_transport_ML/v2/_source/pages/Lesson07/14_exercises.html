14. More exercises

Twelve exercises on the lecture itself, from ★ (direct application) to ★★★ (needs an idea). Try each one before opening the hint, and open the hint before the solution. All numerical answers were checked by a script.

Exercise 1 — How large can the entropy of a coupling be? ★

Let $P\in\U(a,b)$ and $\mathrm H$ the Shannon entropy. Show that (a) $\mathrm H(P)\le\mathrm H(a)+\mathrm H(b)$, with equality iff $P=a\otimes b$; (b) $\mathrm H(P)\ge\mathrm H(a)$, with equality iff every row of $P$ has a single non-zero entry. Check both on the running example’s optimal plan $P^\star=\begin{pmatrix}0.3&0\\0.2&0.5\end{pmatrix}$.

Hint

(a) §1: $\KL(P|a\otimes b)=\mathrm H(a)+\mathrm H(b)-\mathrm H(P)$. (b) Write $P_{ij}=a_i\,q_{ij}$ with $q_{i\cdot}$ the conditional law of $Y$ given $X=x_i$.

Solution

(a) $0\le\KL(P|a\otimes b)=\mathrm H(a)+\mathrm H(b)-\mathrm H(P)$, with equality iff $P=a\otimes b$.

(b) With $P_{ij}=a_iq_{ij}$ ($\sum_jq_{ij}=1$): $\ -\sum_{ij}a_iq_{ij}\log(a_iq_{ij})=-\sum_ia_i\log a_i-\sum_ia_i\sum_jq_{ij}\log q_{ij}=\mathrm H(a)+\sum_ia_i\mathrm H(q_{i\cdot})$. The last sum is $\ge0$, and $0$ iff each $q_{i\cdot}$ is a Dirac. In probabilistic words: $\mathrm H(X,Y)=\mathrm H(X)+\mathrm H(Y|X)$, and $\mathrm H(Y|X)=0$ iff $Y$ is a function of $X$. By symmetry also $\mathrm H(P)\ge\mathrm H(b)$.

Check: $\mathrm H(a)=0.610864$, $\mathrm H(b)=0.693147$, $\mathrm H(P^\star)=1.029653$, $\mathrm H(a)+\mathrm H(b)=1.304011$. Row $2$ of $P^\star$ has two non-zero entries, so $\mathrm H(P^\star)>\max(\mathrm H(a),\mathrm H(b))$. So on $\U(a,b)$ the entropy is largest at $a\otimes b$, and a deterministic plan $Y=T(X)$, when there is one, has the smallest possible entropy $\mathrm H(a)$; the mutual information $\KL(P|a\otimes b)$ is then $\mathrm H(b)$, its largest value.

Exercise 2 — The plan that no longer jumps ★★

Take the example of §1: $a=b=(\frac12,\frac12)$, $C_\theta=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&\theta\end{pmatrix}$, couplings $P_s=\begin{pmatrix}s&\frac12-s\\\frac12-s&s\end{pmatrix}$. (a) Show that the entropic plan (KL convention) has $s(\theta)=\frac12\,\sigma\big(\frac{2-\theta}{2\eps}\big)$, where $\sigma(t)=1/(1+e^{-t})$. (b) Show that $\frac{\dd}{\dd\theta}L^\eps_{C_\theta}=s(\theta)$, and compare with the kink of $L_{C_\theta}=\min(\frac\theta2,1)$. (c) Compute $s$ and $L^\eps$ at $\theta\in\{1.8,2,2.2\}$ for $\eps=0.1$.

Hint

(a) $\KL(P_s|a\otimes b)=2s\log(4s)+2(\frac12-s)\log(4(\frac12-s))$; differentiate in $s$. (b) Envelope theorem: $\theta$ appears only in the entry $C_{22}$.

Solution

(a) The objective is $E(s)=1+s(\theta-2)+\eps\big[2s\log(4s)+2(\frac12-s)\log(4(\frac12-s))\big]$, and $E'(s)=\theta-2+2\eps\log\frac{s}{\frac12-s}$. Setting $E'=0$: $\frac{s}{1/2-s}=e^{(2-\theta)/(2\eps)}$, i.e. $s=\frac12\,\sigma\big(\frac{2-\theta}{2\eps}\big)$ — a logistic sigmoid in $\theta$, smooth, which tends to the step $\frac12\ind_{\theta<2}$ as $\eps\to0$.

(b) $\theta$ enters only through $C_{22}$, whose derivative in $\theta$ is $1$: by the envelope theorem (§11, $\nabla_C L^\eps=P_\eps$), $\frac{\dd}{\dd\theta}L^\eps=P_{22}=s(\theta)$. The derivative of $L_{C_\theta}$ jumps from $\frac12$ to $0$ at $\theta=2$; that of $L^\eps$ goes smoothly from $\frac12$ to $0$ over a window of width a few $\eps$ around $\theta=2$.

(c) $\eps=0.1$: $s=0.365529$, $0.25$, $0.134471$ and $L^\eps=0.937989$, $1.000000$, $1.037989$ at $\theta=1.8$, $2$, $2.2$ (finite differences of $L^\eps$ give the same $s$). At $\theta=2$ the bias is $0$: all couplings cost $1$ there, and $a\otimes b$ ($s=\frac14$) has zero penalty.

Exercise 3 — Raking a contingency table ★

A survey of $100$ people gives the table of counts $N=\begin{pmatrix}30&10\\20&40\end{pmatrix}$ (rows: two age groups; columns: two answers). The census says the row totals should be $(50,50)$ and the column totals $(40,60)$. (a) Perform one cycle of iterative proportional fitting (rows, then columns). (b) Show that the limit has the same cross-ratio as $N$ and compute it in closed form. (c) Which entropic transport problem does this solve?

Hint

(b) Row and column scalings do not change $\frac{M_{11}M_{22}}{M_{12}M_{21}}$ (quiz of §2). Parametrize the tables with the right margins by one number $x=M_{11}$.

Solution

(a) Rows: multiply row $1$ by $50/40$ and row $2$ by $50/60$: $\begin{pmatrix}37.5&12.5\\16.667&33.333\end{pmatrix}$. Columns (sums $54.167$ and $45.833$): multiply by $40/54.167$ and $60/45.833$: $\begin{pmatrix}27.692&16.364\\12.308&43.636\end{pmatrix}$, with row sums $(44.056,\ 55.944)$. Next cycles: $M_{11}=29.630$, then $29.939$.

(b) Scalings keep the cross-ratio $\frac{30\times40}{10\times20}=6$. Tables with the right margins are $\begin{pmatrix}x&50-x\\40-x&10+x\end{pmatrix}$, and $\frac{x(10+x)}{(50-x)(40-x)}=6$ gives $x^2-110x+2400=0$, so $x=30$ (the other root, $80$, is not admissible): the limit is $\begin{pmatrix}30&20\\10&40\end{pmatrix}$.

(c) With $K=N/100$ as a Gibbs kernel ($C=-\eps\log K$) and $a=(0.5,0.5)$, $b=(0.4,0.6)$, the limit divided by $100$ is $P_\eps=\operatorname{diag}(u)K\operatorname{diag}(v)$ with the right marginals: the KL projection of $N/100$ onto $\U(a,b)$ (§5). Raking keeps the “association” of the survey (its cross-ratios) and imposes the census margins.

Exercise 4 — When one cycle suffices ★

Let $C=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ and $a=b=(\frac12,\frac12)$. (a) Show that Sinkhorn started from $v=\ind$ converges in one cycle and give $P_\eps$. (b) Compute $\ps{C}{P_\eps}$, $\KL(P_\eps|a\otimes b)$ and $L^\eps_C$ at $\eps=0.5$. (c) With $a=(0.3,0.7)$ instead (the running example), is one cycle enough?

Hint

Quiz of §4: symmetric kernel with equal row sums and uniform marginals.

Solution

(a) $K\ind=(1+e^{-1/\eps})\ind$, so $u=\frac{1}{2(1+e^{-1/\eps})}\ind$, then $K\T u=\frac12\ind$ and $v=\ind$: nothing changes any more. $P_\eps=\frac{1}{2(1+e^{-1/\eps})}\begin{pmatrix}1&e^{-1/\eps}\\e^{-1/\eps}&1\end{pmatrix}$.

(b) At $\eps=0.5$ the off-diagonal mass is $t=\frac{e^{-2}}{1+e^{-2}}=0.119203$, so $P_{11}=0.440399$, $\ps{C}{P_\eps}=t=0.119203$, $\KL=\log2-h(t)=0.327813$ where $h$ is the binary entropy, and $L^\eps_C=0.119203+0.5\times0.327813=0.283110$.

(c) No: with $a=(0.3,0.7)$ the row error is multiplied by about $0.362$ per cycle (§4, §7) and never vanishes exactly.

Exercise 5 — Properties of the soft minimum ★

For $h\in\R^m$ and $b\in\Sigma_m$ with $b>0$, let $\min^\eps_b(h)=-\eps\log\sum_jb_je^{-h_j/\eps}$. Show that (a) $\min_jh_j\le\min^\eps_b(h)\le\min_jh_j+\eps\log\frac1{b_{j^\star}}$, $j^\star$ an index of the minimum; (b) $\nabla_h\min^\eps_b(h)$ is the probability vector $w_j\propto b_je^{-h_j/\eps}$, so $\min^\eps_b$ is $1$-Lipschitz for $\norm{\cdot}_\infty$; (c) compute it for $h=(0,1,2)$, $b=(0.1,0.45,0.45)$, $\eps\in\{1,0.1,0.01\}$.

Hint

(a) Bound the sum $\sum_jb_je^{-h_j/\eps}$ above by $e^{-\min h/\eps}$ and below by its term $j^\star$.

Solution

(a) Let $m=\min_jh_j$. $\sum_jb_je^{-h_j/\eps}\le\sum_jb_je^{-m/\eps}=e^{-m/\eps}$, and $\ge b_{j^\star}e^{-m/\eps}$. Apply the decreasing map $-\eps\log$. As $\eps\to0$, both bounds tend to $m$.

(b) $\partial_{h_j}\big(-\eps\log\sum_kb_ke^{-h_k/\eps}\big)=\frac{b_je^{-h_j/\eps}}{\sum_kb_ke^{-h_k/\eps}}=w_j$, with $w_j>0$, $\sum_jw_j=1$. Hence $\abs{\min^\eps_b(h)-\min^\eps_b(h')}\le\sup\norm{w}_1\norm{h-h'}_\infty=\norm{h-h'}_\infty$ (mean value theorem). These weights are the softmax of $-h/\eps$: in §6 they are the conditional law of the plan in row $i$.

(c) $\min^\eps_b(h)=1.119489$, $0.230238$, $0.023026$ for $\eps=1$, $0.1$, $0.01$, with upper bound $\eps\log10=2.302585$, $0.230259$, $0.023026$: because the minimizing entry has the small weight $0.1$, the soft minimum sits near its upper bound. The weights $w$ are $(0.31,0.51,0.19)$ at $\eps=1$ and $(0.9998,0.0002,0)$ at $\eps=0.1$.

Exercise 6 — The local rate against Birkhoff’s factor ★★

Keep $C=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ and $\eps=0.5$, $b=(\frac12,\frac12)$. (a) Compute $\lambda(K)$. (b) Compute $\sigma_\eps$ for $a=(0.3,0.7)$ and for $a=(\frac12,\frac12)$, and check Prop. 9.21. (c) For $a=(\frac12,\frac12)$, Sinkhorn converges in one cycle (Exercise 4), although $\sigma_\eps^2=\lambda^2=0.58$. Explain.

Hint

(b) For $2\times2$ plans, $\sigma_\eps$ is the absolute correlation of the indicators of row $2$ and column $2$ (§7). (c) Read the hypothesis of the local rate in Prop. 9.20.

Solution

(a) $\eta=e^{4}$, $\lambda=\tanh1=0.761594$.

(b) $a=(0.3,0.7)$: $\sigma_\eps=0.601729$ (§7). $a=(\frac12,\frac12)$: $P_{22}=\frac{1}{2(1+t)}$ with $t=e^{-1/\eps}$, $\mathrm{Cov}=\frac{1}{2(1+t)}-\frac14=\frac{1-t}{4(1+t)}$, variances $\frac14$, so $\sigma_\eps=\frac{1-t}{1+t}=\tanh\frac1{2\eps}=0.761594=\lambda(K)$: the bound $\sigma_\eps\le\lambda(K)$ is attained.

(c) The local rate describes how a generic small error decays. Prop. 9.20 states it “unless the centered error vanishes in finitely many cycles”; here the starting point $v=\ind$ is already optimal up to the gauge, so after the first row step the error is exactly $0$ and there is nothing to contract. Started from another $v^{(0)}$, the errors do decay like $0.58^\ell$.

Exercise 7 — No bias against a Dirac ★

(a) Show that $\mathcal L^\eps_c(\delta_x,\be)=\int c(x,y)\,\dd\be(y)$ for every $\eps$ and every $\be$. (b) With $c=\abs{x-y}^2$ on $\R$, $x=0.5$ and $\be=\frac12(\delta_0+\delta_1)$, compute $S_\eps(\delta_x,\be)$ for $\eps\in\{0.1,1,10,100\}$ and identify its limits as $\eps\to0$ and $\eps\to\infty$.

Hint

(a) How many couplings does a Dirac have? (b) You need $\mathcal L^\eps(\be,\be)$, a $2\times2$ problem of the type of Exercise 4.

Solution

(a) The only coupling of $\delta_x$ and $\be$ is $\delta_x\otimes\be$ (Lesson 3, sheet Exercise 1), whose KL with respect to $\delta_x\otimes\be$ is $0$. So the value is its cost, $\int c(x,y)\,\dd\be(y)$, for every $\eps$; also $\mathcal L^\eps(\delta_x,\delta_x)=c(x,x)$.

(b) $\mathcal L^\eps(\delta_{0.5},\be)=\frac12(0.25)+\frac12(0.25)=0.25$ and $S_\eps=0.25-\frac12\mathcal L^\eps(\be,\be)$: $\ 0.215345$, $0.060057$, $0.006247$, $0.000625$ for $\eps=0.1$, $1$, $10$, $100$. As $\eps\to0$, $\mathcal L^\eps(\be,\be)\to0$ and $S_\eps\to0.25=W_2^2(\delta_{0.5},\be)$. As $\eps\to\infty$, $\mathcal L^\eps(\be,\be)\to\iint\abs{y-y'}^2\dd\be\dd\be=\frac12$ and $S_\eps\to0=(0.5-0.5)^2$: the large-temperature limit of the quadratic cost only compares the means (§9), and these two measures have the same mean.

Exercise 8 — High temperature, quantitatively ★★

(a) From the proof of Prop. 8.10, show $\KL(P_\eps|a\otimes b)\le(\max C-\min C)/\eps$. (b) OT4ML Prop. 8.16 (p. 148) gives, for large $\eps$, $\frac{\dd\pi_\eps}{\dd(\al\otimes\be)}=1-\frac{c_0}{\eps}+O(\eps^{-2})$ and $\mathcal L^\eps=\bar c-\frac1{2\eps}\int c_0^2\,\dd(\al\otimes\be)+O(\eps^{-2})$, with $\bar c=\int c\,\dd(\al\otimes\be)$ and the centred cost $c_0(x,y)=c(x,y)-\int c(x,\cdot)\dd\be-\int c(\cdot,y)\dd\al+\bar c$. Deduce $\KL(P_\eps|a\otimes b)\approx\frac{1}{2\eps^2}\int c_0^2$, and check both expansions on the running example at $\eps=10$, $100$, $1000$.

Hint

(b) $\int p\log p\,\dd r$ with $p=1-\delta$, $\int\delta\,\dd r=0$: expand $p\log p=-\delta+\frac{\delta^2}2+O(\delta^3)$.

Solution

(a) Steps 1–3 of the case $\eps\to\infty$ in §3, after subtracting $\min C$: $\KL\le\ps{C-\min C}{a\otimes b}/\eps\le(\max C-\min C)/\eps$.

(b) With $\delta=c_0/\eps+O(\eps^{-2})$, $\int\delta\,\dd r=O(\eps^{-2})$ (the density integrates to $1$ and $c_0$ is centred), so $\KL=\int p\log p\,\dd r=\int\big(-\delta+\frac{\delta^2}2\big)\dd r+O(\eps^{-3})=\frac{1}{2\eps^2}\int c_0^2\,\dd r+O(\eps^{-3})$. Running example: $\bar c=0.5$, $c_0=\begin{pmatrix}-0.7&0.7\\0.3&-0.3\end{pmatrix}$, $\int c_0^2=0.21$. So $\KL\approx0.105/\eps^2$ and $L^\eps\approx0.5-0.105/\eps$:

$\eps$$\KL(P_\eps|a\otimes b)$$0.105/\eps^2$$L^\eps_C$$0.5-0.105/\eps$
$10$$1.04806\times10^{-3}$$1.05\times10^{-3}$$0.489506$$0.489500$
$100$$1.04998\times10^{-5}$$1.05\times10^{-5}$$0.498950$$0.498950$
$1000$$1.05000\times10^{-7}$$1.05\times10^{-7}$$0.499895$$0.499895$

The bound of (a), $1/\eps$, is far from the true $1/\eps^2$ decay: the first-order deviation from independence is linear in $c_0/\eps$, and KL is quadratic in deviations.

Exercise 9 — Entropic transport between Gaussians, from scratch ★★★

Let $\al=\N(m_\al,\sigma_\al^2)$, $\be=\N(m_\be,\sigma_\be^2)$ on $\R$, $c=\abs{x-y}^2$. (a) Show that for a Gaussian pair with correlation $\rho$, $\KL(\pi|\al\otimes\be)=-\frac12\log(1-\rho^2)$. (b) Admitting that the optimal plan is Gaussian (sheet Exercise 4, or OT4ML Prop. 9.26), derive $k_\eps$ and $\mathcal L^\eps$ of §8. (c) Show that the barycentric projection is $T_\eps(x)=m_\be+\frac{k_\eps}{\sigma_\al^2}(x-m_\al)$ and that $\mathrm{Var}(Y|X)=\frac\eps2\frac{k_\eps}{\sigma_\al^2}$. (d) Show that $\pf{(T_\eps)}{\al}$ is narrower than $\be$; give numbers for $\N(0,1)\to\N(1,4)$.

Hint

(a) The KL between two Gaussians on $\R^2$ with the same mean: $\frac12\big(\tr(\Sigma_2^{-1}\Sigma_1)-2+\log\frac{\det\Sigma_2}{\det\Sigma_1}\big)$, here with $\Sigma_2$ diagonal. (c) Conditional law of a Gaussian vector.

Solution

(a) $\Sigma_1=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_\al^2&k\\k&\sigma_\be^2\end{pmatrix}$, $\Sigma_2=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_\al^2,\sigma_\be^2)$: $\tr(\Sigma_2^{-1}\Sigma_1)=2$ and $\frac{\det\Sigma_2}{\det\Sigma_1}=\frac{\sigma_\al^2\sigma_\be^2}{\sigma_\al^2\sigma_\be^2-k^2}=\frac1{1-\rho^2}$. So $\KL=-\frac12\log(1-\rho^2)$.

(b) $\E\abs{X-Y}^2=(m_\al-m_\be)^2+\sigma_\al^2+\sigma_\be^2-2k$, so $\mathcal L^\eps=\min_k\Phi(k)$ with $\Phi(k)=(m_\al-m_\be)^2+\sigma_\al^2+\sigma_\be^2-2k-\frac\eps2\log\big(1-\frac{k^2}{\sigma_\al^2\sigma_\be^2}\big)$ on $\abs k<\sigma_\al\sigma_\be$. $\Phi$ is strictly convex and $\Phi'(k)=-2+\frac{\eps k}{\sigma_\al^2\sigma_\be^2-k^2}$; $\Phi'=0$ gives $2k^2+\eps k-2\sigma_\al^2\sigma_\be^2=0$, whose root in $(0,\sigma_\al\sigma_\be)$ is $k_\eps=\frac{\sqrt{\eps^2+16\sigma_\al^2\sigma_\be^2}-\eps}{4}$.

(c) For a Gaussian pair, $\E[Y|X=x]=m_\be+\frac{k}{\sigma_\al^2}(x-m_\al)$ and $\mathrm{Var}(Y|X)=\sigma_\be^2-\frac{k^2}{\sigma_\al^2}$. With $2k_\eps^2=2\sigma_\al^2\sigma_\be^2-\eps k_\eps$: $\mathrm{Var}(Y|X)=\frac{\eps k_\eps}{2\sigma_\al^2}$.

(d) $\pf{(T_\eps)}{\al}=\N\big(m_\be,\ k_\eps^2/\sigma_\al^2\big)$, of standard deviation $k_\eps/\sigma_\al<\sigma_\be$ since $k_\eps<\sigma_\al\sigma_\be$: the barycentric projection contracts, by the law of total variance ($\mathrm{Var}\,Y=\mathrm{Var}\,\E[Y|X]+\E\,\mathrm{Var}(Y|X)$). For $\N(0,1)\to\N(1,4)$: slope (and standard deviation of the image) $1.997502$, $1.975156$, $1.765564$ at $\eps=0.01$, $0.1$, $1$, against $2$; $\mathrm{Var}(Y|X)=0.009988$, $0.098758$, $0.882782$.

Exercise 10 — Sinkhorn divergences are exact on translations ★★

Let $c(x,y)=\norm{x-y}^2$ on $\R^d$, $\al$ with finite second moment, $h\in\R^d$ and $\al_h=\pf{(\Id+h)}{\al}$. Show that $\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\al_h)=\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\al)+\norm h^2$ and deduce $S_\eps(\al,\al_h)=\norm h^2$ for every $\eps$. Check with $\N(0,1)$ and $h=1.5$.

Hint

Transport $\al$ to $\al$ first and then translate the second coordinate; expand $\norm{x-y-h}^2$ and use sheet Exercise 7(1).

Solution

The map $\pi\mapsto\pf{(x,y\mapsto(x,y+h))}{\pi}$ is a bijection from $\U(\al,\al)$ to $\U(\al,\al_h)$ that preserves the KL with respect to the product (both reference products are moved by the same bijection). Its cost: $\int\norm{x-y-h}^2\dd\pi=\int\norm{x-y}^2\dd\pi-2\ps{h}{\int x\,\dd\al-\int y\,\dd\al}+\norm h^2=\int\norm{x-y}^2\dd\pi+\norm h^2$, since both marginals of $\pi$ are $\al$. So every objective is shifted by $\norm h^2$ and $\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\al_h)=\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\al)+\norm h^2$. Moreover $\mathcal L^\eps(\al_h,\al_h)=\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\al)$ (translate both coordinates; the cost is unchanged). Hence $S_\eps(\al,\al_h)=\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\al)+\norm h^2-\frac12\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\al)-\frac12\mathcal L^\eps(\al,\al)=\norm h^2$. Check: the closed forms of §8 give $S_\eps(\N(0,1),\N(1.5,1))=2.25$ at $\eps=0.01$, $1$ and $100$.

Exercise 11 — How much does one Sinkhorn step gain? ★★★

Use the dual $D(f,g)$ of (8.20) (KL convention). Let $P=P(f,g)=(a\otimes b)\odot e^{(f\oplus g-C)/\eps}$, with row sums $r$ and total mass $1$, and let $f'$ be the soft $c$-transform update. (a) Show that $D(f',g)-D(f,g)=\eps\KL(a|r)$. (b) Deduce that along Sinkhorn, $\sum_{\ell\ge1}\KL(a|r^{(\ell)})\le\frac{D^\star-D^{(0)}}{\eps}$, and that the smallest row error among the first $L$ cycles is at most $\sqrt{2(D^\star-D^{(0)})/(\eps L)}$. (c) Check (a) on the running example at $\eps=0.5$.

Hint

(a) The update multiplies row $i$ of $P$ by $a_i/r_i$, i.e. $f'_i=f_i+\eps\log(a_i/r_i)$. (b) Telescoping, then Pinsker.

Solution

(a) The soft transform makes the row sums equal to $a$; since the row sums of $(a\otimes b)\odot e^{(f'\oplus g-C)/\eps}$ are $r_ie^{(f'_i-f_i)/\eps}$, $f'_i=f_i+\eps\log\frac{a_i}{r_i}$. Then

$$\begin{aligned}D(f',g)-D(f,g)&=\ps{f'-f}{a}-\eps\Big(\sum_{ij}P'_{ij}-\sum_{ij}P_{ij}\Big)\\&=\eps\sum_ia_i\log\frac{a_i}{r_i}-\eps(1-1)=\eps\KL(a|r).\end{aligned}$$

(The same holds for the column step with $\KL(b|s)$. Without the mass condition, the identity holds with the generalized KL.)

(b) The dual increases at every half-step and is bounded by $D^\star$; summing the gains of the row steps (the column steps also gain, $\ge0$) gives $\eps\sum_\ell\KL(a|r^{(\ell)})\le D^\star-D^{(0)}$. Among $L$ terms, the smallest is at most the average, $\frac{D^\star-D^{(0)}}{\eps L}$, and Pinsker gives $\norm{a-r}_1\le\sqrt{2\KL(a|r)}$. This is the mechanism behind the robust rate of §7 (Prop. 9.13), whose proof adds a concavity bound of the gap by the marginal error.

(c) Start from $f=0$ and $g$ such that the columns are exact. First cycle: the row step gains $0.012397=\eps\KL(a|r)$, the column step $0.006229=\eps\KL(b|s)$; second cycle: $0.002741$ and $0.001167$; the dual reaches $0.326085$, $0.329993$, $0.330632$, … $\to0.330739=L^\eps_C$.

Exercise 12 — Unbalanced transport between two Diracs ★★

Let $\al=\delta_0$, $\be=\delta_\ell$ on $\R$, $c=\abs{x-y}^2$, and the unbalanced problem $\min_{\pi\ge0}\int c\,\dd\pi+\tau\KL(\pi_1|\al)+\tau\KL(\pi_2|\be)$ (no entropy). (a) Show that the optimal plan is $m^\star\delta_{(0,\ell)}$ with $m^\star=e^{-\ell^2/(2\tau)}$ and value $2\tau\big(1-e^{-\ell^2/(2\tau)}\big)$. (b) Compare with $W_2^2=\ell^2$ for $\tau=1$ and $\ell\in\{0.5,1,2,5\}$. What is the “range” of transport?

Hint

A plan between two Diracs is $m\,\delta_{(0,\ell)}$; its marginals are $m\delta_0$ and $m\delta_\ell$, and $\KL(m\delta_0|\delta_0)=m\log m-m+1$.

Solution

(a) Minimize $\phi(m)=m\ell^2+2\tau(m\log m-m+1)$ over $m\ge0$: $\phi'(m)=\ell^2+2\tau\log m=0$ gives $m^\star=e^{-\ell^2/(2\tau)}$, and $\phi(m^\star)=m^\star\ell^2-m^\star\ell^2-2\tau m^\star+2\tau=2\tau(1-m^\star)$ ($\phi$ is strictly convex).

(b) $\tau=1$: $m^\star=0.8825$, $0.6065$, $0.1353$, $4\times10^{-6}$ and value $0.2350$, $0.7869$, $1.7293$, $1.99999$ against $\ell^2=0.25$, $1$, $4$, $25$. For $\ell\ll\sqrt\tau$ the value is $\approx\ell^2$ (transport); for $\ell\gg\sqrt\tau$ it saturates at $2\tau$: destroying the mass at $0$ and creating it at $\ell$ is cheaper than moving it. Transport acts up to distances of order $\sqrt\tau$ — the length scale hidden in $\tau$ (§12).
