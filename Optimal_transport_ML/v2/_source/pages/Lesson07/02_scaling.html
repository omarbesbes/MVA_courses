2. The Gibbs kernel and the scaling form OT4ML §8.2, pp. 138–139

The entropic plan is the Gibbs kernel $K=e^{-C/\eps}$ with its rows and columns rescaled: $P_\eps=\operatorname{diag}(u)K\operatorname{diag}(v)$. Finding it means finding two positive vectors, $n+m$ numbers instead of $nm$.

The problem: $nm$ unknowns, $n+m$ constraints

A coupling of $n$ source points and $m$ target points has $nm$ entries, but only $n+m-1$ independent marginal constraints act on it. For the linear program this mismatch is what makes the solution set large (a whole face of minimizers can exist). For the entropic problem, the strictly convex penalty removes this freedom, and the first-order optimality conditions turn out to have a remarkable product structure: the optimal plan is determined by one number per row and one number per column.

The scaling form

In the course OT4ML Prop. 8.5, (8.3)–(8.4), pp. 138–139 · compact Prop. 8.4, p. 34

Proposition 8.5 (Scaling form of entropic OT). P is the unique solution to (8.1) if and only if there exists $(u,v)\in\R^n_+\times\R^m_+$ such that

$$\forall(i,j)\in[\![n]\!]\times[\![m]\!],\qquad P_{i,j}=u_iK_{i,j}v_j\qquad\text{where}\qquad K_{i,j}:=e^{-\frac{C_{i,j}}{\eps}},\qquad(8.2)$$

and $P\in\U(a,b)$.

[…] The factorization in (8.2) can be written compactly as $P=\operatorname{diag}(u)K\operatorname{diag}(v)$. $u,v$ must therefore satisfy the nonlinear mass-conservation equations

$$\operatorname{diag}(u)K\operatorname{diag}(v)\ind_m=a,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\operatorname{diag}(v)K\T\operatorname{diag}(u)\ind_n=b,\qquad(8.3)$$

Using entrywise multiplication, these equations reduce to

$$u\odot(Kv)=a\qquad\text{and}\qquad v\odot(K\T u)=b\qquad(8.4)$$

[…] Proposition 8.3 guarantees a unique scaled matrix P, although the two scaling vectors retain one multiplicative gauge degree of freedom.

$K=e^{-C/\eps}$
The Gibbs kernel: the cost matrix turned into a similarity matrix, entry by entry. $K_{ij}=1$ for a free trip, and $K_{ij}$ decays exponentially with the cost, on the scale $\eps$. (Not the matrix exponential: $\exp$ acts on each entry.)
$\operatorname{diag}(u)K\operatorname{diag}(v)$
Multiply row $i$ of $K$ by $u_i$ and column $j$ by $v_j$: $P_{ij}=u_iK_{ij}v_j$. The pattern of $P$ (ratios such as $\frac{P_{ij}P_{kl}}{P_{il}P_{kj}}=\frac{K_{ij}K_{kl}}{K_{il}K_{kj}}$) is the pattern of $K$; only row and column “volumes” change.
(8.3)–(8.4)
The row sums of $P$ are $u_i(Kv)_i$ and must equal $a_i$; the column sums are $v_j(K\T u)_j$ and must equal $b_j$. A system of $n+m$ nonlinear equations in $n+m$ unknowns: the matrix scaling problem.
$(u,v)\in\R^n_+\times\R^m_+$
The scalings are positive: when $a,b>0$, the constraint $P\in\U(a,b)$ forces $u_i>0$ and $v_j>0$ (a zero $u_i$ would empty row $i$).
“gauge degree of freedom”
$(\lambda u,\ v/\lambda)$ gives the same $P$ for every $\lambda>0$: the scalings are unique only up to this rescaling.
Idea of the proof

“Only if”: the minimizer is positive (Prop. 8.3), so the constraints $P\ge0$ are inactive and the Lagrange conditions hold with one multiplier per row ($f_i$) and per column ($g_j$). The derivative of $P_{ij}\log P_{ij}$ is $\log P_{ij}+1$, so the condition reads $\log P_{ij}=(f_i+g_j-C_{ij})/\eps+\text{const}$: exponentiate, and $e^{f_i/\eps}$, $e^{g_j/\eps}$ are the scalings. “If”: for a plan of the form (8.2), the objective of any other coupling $Q$ equals a constant plus $\eps\KL(Q|P)$, which is minimal exactly at $Q=P$.

Full proof (both directions, and uniqueness of the scalings)

We work with the normalized objective $E(P)=\ps{P}{C}+\eps\KL(P|a\otimes b)$ of (8.8), which has the same minimizer as (8.1) (§1), and assume $a,b>0$ (rows or columns with zero mass are removed: their entries are $0$ in every coupling).

Only if.

  1. The minimizer $P_\eps$ has all entries positive, so near $P_\eps$ the only active constraints are the linear equalities $P\ind_m=a$, $P\T\ind_n=b$.Prop. 8.3.
  2. $$\frac{\partial E}{\partial P_{ij}}=C_{ij}+\eps\log\frac{P_{ij}}{a_ib_j}.$$On positive matrices $\KL(P|a\otimes b)=\sum_{ij}\big(P_{ij}\log P_{ij}-P_{ij}\log(a_ib_j)-P_{ij}+a_ib_j\big)$, and $\frac{\dd}{\dd t}(t\log t-t\log c-t)=\log t-\log c$.
  3. $$C_{ij}+\eps\log\frac{P_{ij}}{a_ib_j}=f_i+g_j\quad\text{for all }i,j,\ \text{for some }f\in\R^n,\ g\in\R^m.$$Lagrange: at a minimizer of a smooth convex function under linear equality constraints, the gradient is a combination of the gradients of the constraints (refresher). The constraint “row sum $i$” has gradient $\big(\ind_{k=i}\big)_{kl}$, the constraint “column sum $j$” has gradient $\big(\ind_{l=j}\big)_{kl}$; their multipliers are $f_i$ and $g_j$.
  4. $$P_{ij}=a_ib_j\,e^{(f_i+g_j-C_{ij})/\eps}=\underbrace{a_ie^{f_i/\eps}}_{u_i}\ \underbrace{e^{-C_{ij}/\eps}}_{K_{ij}}\ \underbrace{b_je^{g_j/\eps}}_{v_j}.$$Solve step 3 for $P_{ij}$. This is (8.2), with positive $u,v$.

If (and uniqueness again). Let $P=\operatorname{diag}(u)K\operatorname{diag}(v)\in\U(a,b)$ with $u,v>0$, and set $f_i=\eps\log(u_i/a_i)$, $g_j=\eps\log(v_j/b_j)$, so that step 4 holds, i.e. $C_{ij}=f_i+g_j-\eps\log\frac{P_{ij}}{a_ib_j}$. Take any $Q\in\U(a,b)$.

  1. $$\ps{Q}{C}=\sum_{ij}Q_{ij}(f_i+g_j)-\eps\sum_{ij}Q_{ij}\log\frac{P_{ij}}{a_ib_j}=\ps{f}{a}+\ps{g}{b}-\eps\sum_{ij}Q_{ij}\log\frac{P_{ij}}{a_ib_j}.$$Insert the expression of $C_{ij}$; then $\sum_jQ_{ij}=a_i$ and $\sum_iQ_{ij}=b_j$.
  2. $$\eps\KL(Q|a\otimes b)=\eps\sum_{ij}Q_{ij}\log\frac{Q_{ij}}{a_ib_j}.$$$Q$ and $a\otimes b$ both have mass $1$, so the terms $-Q_{ij}+a_ib_j$ sum to $0$.
  3. $$E(Q)=\ps{f}{a}+\ps{g}{b}+\eps\sum_{ij}Q_{ij}\log\frac{Q_{ij}}{P_{ij}}=\ps{f}{a}+\ps{g}{b}+\eps\KL(Q|P).$$Add steps 1 and 2: $\log\frac{Q_{ij}}{a_ib_j}-\log\frac{P_{ij}}{a_ib_j}=\log\frac{Q_{ij}}{P_{ij}}$; $Q$ and $P$ both have mass $1$.
  4. $E(Q)\ge\ps{f}{a}+\ps{g}{b}=E(P)$, with equality if and only if $Q=P$.$\KL(Q|P)\ge0$ with equality iff $Q=P$ (refresher); take $Q=P$ in step 3 for the value of $E(P)$.

The scalings are unique up to the gauge. If $u_iK_{ij}v_j=u'_iK_{ij}v'_j$ for all $i,j$, then $u'_i/u_i=v_j/v'_j$ for all $i,j$ (divide by $K_{ij}>0$). The left side does not depend on $j$ and the right side does not depend on $i$, so both equal a constant $\lambda>0$: $(u',v')=(\lambda u,v/\lambda)$. In potentials, $(f',g')=(f+\eps\log\lambda,\ g-\eps\log\lambda)$. $\square$

Step 3 of the “if” part is worth keeping in mind: on the couplings, the entropic objective is $\eps$ times the KL divergence to the solution, plus a constant. It is the same mechanism as for least squares, where the objective is the squared distance to the solution plus a constant; it returns in §5 as a projection.

Note on the sources — which direction is proved, and which potentials

OT4ML Prop. 8.5 is stated as an equivalence, but the printed proof (pp. 138–139) only derives the form (8.2) from the Lagrange conditions (“only if”). The converse is the identity $E(Q)=E(P)+\eps\KL(Q|P)$ above. The book works with (8.1), whose Lagrange condition is $C_{ij}+\eps(\log P_{ij}+1)=f_i+g_j$, hence $u_i=e^{f_i/\eps-1}$, $v_j=e^{g_j/\eps}$; with the normalized objective (8.8) one gets instead $u_i=a_ie^{f_i/\eps}$, $v_j=b_je^{g_j/\eps}$, which is the book’s (8.21) and the convention of this page. The plan is the same; only the labels of the potentials change.

The running example in scaling form

Worked example — $u$, $v$, $f$, $g$ at $\eps=0.5$

With $C=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ and $\eps=0.5$: $K=\begin{pmatrix}1&e^{-2}\\e^{-2}&1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0.135335\\0.135335&1\end{pmatrix}$. Sinkhorn’s algorithm (§4), started from $v=\ind$, returns

$$u=(0.150664,\ 0.820337),\qquad v=(1.910696,\ 0.594723).$$
  1. $$\begin{aligned}P_{11}&=u_1K_{11}v_1=0.150664\times1\times1.910696=0.287873,\\P_{12}&=u_1K_{12}v_2=0.150664\times0.135335\times0.594723=0.012127.\end{aligned}$$(8.2). The same computation for the second row gives $P_{21}=0.212127$, $P_{22}=0.487873$: the plan $P_{0.5}$ of §1.
  2. $$f=\eps\log\frac ua=(-0.344364,\ 0.079317),\qquad g=\eps\log\frac vb=(0.670307,\ 0.086744).$$$u=a\odot e^{f/\eps}$, $v=b\odot e^{g/\eps}$; e.g. $f_1=0.5\log(0.150664/0.3)$.
  3. $$f\oplus g-C=\begin{pmatrix}0.325943&-1.257620\\-0.250375&0.166061\end{pmatrix},\qquad a_ib_j\,e^{(f_i+g_j-C_{ij})/\eps}=\begin{pmatrix}0.287873&0.012127\\0.212127&0.487873\end{pmatrix}.$$Step 4 of the proof, read backwards. Note that $f_i+g_j-C_{ij}$ is positive on some entries: the hard constraint $f_i+g_j\le C_{ij}$ of the Kantorovich dual is not required here (§6).
  4. $$\begin{aligned}\ps{f}{a}+\ps{g}{b}&=0.3\times(-0.344364)+0.7\times0.079317+0.5\times(0.670307+0.086744)\\&=0.330739=L^{0.5}_C(a,b).\end{aligned}$$Step 4 of the “if” part: $E(P_\eps)=\ps{f}{a}+\ps{g}{b}$. It matches the value $0.3307$ found in §1.

Replacing $(u,v)$ by $(2u,v/2)$, or $(f,g)$ by $(f+1,g-1)$, changes none of these results (the last line changes by $\ps{\ind}{a}-\ps{\ind}{b}=0$).

Intuition — a similarity matrix, corrected by prices

Without marginal constraints, the best “soft” matching would just be proportional to $K_{ij}=e^{-C_{ij}/\eps}$: much mass on cheap pairs, little on expensive ones, like a softmax over costs. But $K$ has the wrong row and column sums. The scalings correct this: $u_i=a_ie^{f_i/\eps}$ raises or lowers the whole output of source $i$, $v_j=b_je^{g_j/\eps}$ the whole input of target $j$. In economic terms, $f_i$ and $g_j$ are prices (§6), adjusted until supply $a$ and demand $b$ are met.

In machine learning — plans with $10^{10}$ entries, stored as $n+m$ numbers

For two point clouds of $n=m=10^5$ points (a medium-size 3D scan, or a large mini-batch of image features), a transport plan has $10^{10}$ entries: 80 GB in double precision. The scaling form means that the plan never needs to be stored: keep $u$, $v$ (or $f$, $g$) and recompute $K_{ij}=e^{-c(x_i,y_j)/\eps}$ on the fly when a product $Kv$ is needed. This is how GPU libraries such as GeomLoss (Feydy et al., AISTATS 2019, built on the KeOps library) compute Sinkhorn losses between large clouds with a memory cost linear in $n$. Any quantity you need from the plan — a cost $\ps{P}{C}$, a barycentric projection $\sum_jP_{ij}y_j$ (§11) — is a reduction of the same kind.

Pitfall — “$P=\operatorname{diag}(u)K\operatorname{diag}(v)$ for some $u,v$, so $P$ is optimal”

Only if $P$ also has the right marginals. Every positive matrix with the pattern of $K$ (for instance $K$ itself) is of the form (8.2); the marginal equations (8.4) are what select the solution. Conversely, a plan with the right marginals but not of the form (8.2) is not optimal for this $\eps$.

Reflexes
  • Entropic OT → $P=\operatorname{diag}(u)K\operatorname{diag}(v)$, $K=e^{-C/\eps}$: solve $u\odot Kv=a$, $v\odot K\T u=b$.
  • Scalings → potentials: $f=\eps\log(u/a)$, $g=\eps\log(v/b)$; value $L^\eps_C=\ps{f}{a}+\ps{g}{b}$.
  • Two pairs of scalings give the same plan → they differ by $(\lambda u,v/\lambda)$, i.e. $(f+c,g-c)$.

For $n=m=1000$, how many unknowns does the scaling form have, and how many entries does the plan have? What does one evaluation of the row sums of $\operatorname{diag}(u)K\operatorname{diag}(v)$ cost?

Answer

$2000$ unknowns (minus one for the gauge) against $10^6$ entries. The row sums are $u\odot(Kv)$: one matrix–vector product, about $nm=10^6$ multiplications and additions.

Show that the cross-ratios of $P_\eps$ do not depend on the marginals: $\dfrac{P_{ij}P_{kl}}{P_{il}P_{kj}}=e^{-(C_{ij}+C_{kl}-C_{il}-C_{kj})/\eps}$. Check it on the running example.

Answer

The factors $u_iu_kv_jv_l$ appear in the numerator and in the denominator. Running example ($i=j=1$, $k=l=2$): $\frac{0.287873\times0.487873}{0.012127\times0.212127}=54.6=e^{4}=e^{-(0+0-1-1)/0.5}$. The cross-ratio measures how much the plan prefers “$1\to1$ and $2\to2$” to “$1\to2$ and $2\to1$”; it is fixed by the costs, while the marginals only set the volumes.
