12. More exercises

The five exercises of the sheet are solved in §11. Here are more, graded from ★ (direct application) to ★★★ (needs an idea). Exercises 3 and 10 are Exercises 2 (§5.10) and 3 (§8.5) of StatOT. Try before opening the hint; open the hint before the solution. All numbers were computed by script.

Exercise 1 — A translation is an optimal motion ★

Let $\al_0\in\Pc_2(\R^d)$, $a\in\R^d$ and $\al_t=\pf{(\tau_{ta})}{\al_0}$ with $\tau_{ta}(x)=x+ta$. Find a velocity field for this curve, its action, and show that this motion is optimal between $\al_0$ and $\al_1$.

Hint

Each particle moves with constant velocity $a$. Lesson 3, Exercise 2: translating a measure by $a$ moves it by $W_2=\norm a$.

Solution

$\partial_t\tau_{ta}(x)=a$, so $v_t\equiv a$ (Prop. 14.2). Action $\int_0^1\!\int\norm a^2\dd\al_t\,\dd t=\norm a^2$. By Lesson 3 (Exercise 2), $W_2(\al_0,\pf{\tau_a}{\al_0})=\norm a$, so the action equals $W_2^2$: by Benamou–Brenier this motion is optimal. (It is McCann’s interpolation for the optimal map $T=\tau_a=\nabla(\frac12\norm x^2+\ps ax)$.)

Exercise 2 — A rotation that costs a lot and moves nothing ★

(StatOT §5.10, Exercise 1.) Let $X_0\sim\N(0,\Id_2)$ and $X_t=R_tX_0$ with $R_t$ the rotation of angle $\theta(t)$. Compute the velocity $v_t$ with $\dot X_t=v_t(X_t)$, show that $\dv(\mu_0v_t)=0$ (so $X_t\sim\mu_0$ for all $t$), and compute the action on $[0,1]$ for $\theta(t)=2\pi t$.

Hint

$\dot R_t=\theta'(t)JR_t$ with $J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$; $\dv(\rho v)=\ps{\nabla\rho}{v}+\rho\dv v$.

Solution

$\dot X_t=\theta'(t)JR_tX_0=\theta'(t)JX_t$, so $v_t(x)=\theta'(t)Jx$. With $\nabla\rho=-x\rho$: $\dv(\rho v_t)=\theta'(t)\big(-\rho\ps{x}{Jx}+\rho\tr J\big)=0$. So the constant curve $\mu_0$ solves the continuity equation with $v_t$; directly, $R_tX_0\sim\N(0,R_tR_t\T)=\N(0,\Id)$. Kinetic energy $\theta'(t)^2\E\norm{JX}^2=2\theta'(t)^2$; for $\theta=2\pi t$ the action is $2(2\pi)^2=8\pi^2\approx78.96$, while $W_2(\mu_0,\mu_0)=0$ and the least-energy velocity of the constant curve is $0$ (§1).

Exercise 3 — Product measures are geodesically convex ★★

(StatOT §5.10, Exercise 2.) Show that the set of product measures over $\R^d$ is a geodesically convex subset of $\Pc_2(\R^d)$: between two product measures there is a constant-speed geodesic made of product measures. Check $W_2^2(\mu,\nu)=\sum_iW_2^2(\mu_i,\nu_i)$ on $\mu=\mu_1\otimes\mu_2$, $\nu=\nu_1\otimes\nu_2$ with $\mu_1=0.2\delta_0+0.5\delta_1+0.3\delta_3$, $\mu_2=0.6\delta_{-1}+0.4\delta_2$, $\nu_1=\frac12(\delta_{0.5}+\delta_{2.5})$, $\nu_2=0.3\delta_0+0.3\delta_1+0.4\delta_4$.

Hint

$\norm{x-y}^2=\sum_i\abs{x_i-y_i}^2$, and the $i$-th coordinates of any coupling of $\mu$ and $\nu$ form a coupling of $\mu_i$ and $\nu_i$. Try the product of optimal one-dimensional plans.

Solution
  1. For any coupling $(X,Y)$ of $\mu$ and $\nu$: $\E\norm{X-Y}^2=\sum_i\E\abs{X_i-Y_i}^2\ge\sum_iW_2^2(\mu_i,\nu_i)$.$(X_i,Y_i)$ couples $\mu_i$ and $\nu_i$.
  2. The product $\pi^\star=\bigotimes_i\pi_i^\star$ of optimal 1D plans has marginals $\mu$ and $\nu$ and cost $\sum_iW_2^2(\mu_i,\nu_i)$: it is optimal.Under $\pi^\star$ the pairs $(X_i,Y_i)$ are independent across $i$ with the right laws.
  3. The displacement interpolation along $\pi^\star$, $\mathrm{Law}((1-t)X+tY)$, has independent coordinates: it is $\bigotimes_i\mu_{i,t}$, a product of 1D geodesics, and a constant-speed geodesic (Lesson 3 §6).Functions of independent pairs are independent.

Numbers: the exact LP between the two products (6 and 6 atoms) gives $W_2^2=3.75$, and $W_2^2(\mu_1,\nu_1)+W_2^2(\mu_2,\nu_2)=0.65+3.10=3.75$. (When $\mu$ has a density the geodesic is unique; otherwise other optimal plans may give geodesics leaving the set — the statement is about existence.)

Exercise 4 — Flow matching between two Gaussians on the line ★★

$X_0\sim\N(0,1)$ and $X_1\sim\N(3,2^2)$ independent, $X_t=(1-t)X_0+tX_1$. (a) Compute $v_t(x)$. (b) Show that the flow is $x(t)=3t+\sigma_tx(0)$ with $\sigma_t=\sqrt{(1-t)^2+4t^2}$, and that the flow map is optimal. (c) Compare the action of this path with $W_2^2$ and with the cost of the independent coupling. When is $\al_t$ narrowest?

Hint

Gaussian conditioning: $\E[X_1\mid X_t=x]=3+\frac{\mathrm{Cov}(X_1,X_t)}{\sigma_t^2}(x-3t)$. For (c), use the energy $\dot m^2+\dot\sigma^2$ of a Gaussian path (§2).

Solution

(a) $X_t\sim\N(3t,\sigma_t^2)$, $\mathrm{Cov}(X_1,X_t)=4t$, so $\E[X_1\mid X_t=x]=3+\frac{4t}{\sigma_t^2}(x-3t)$ and, by (1), $v_t(x)=3+\frac{4t-(1-t)}{\sigma_t^2}(x-3t)$ (this is (16.11) in 1D, shifted by the means).

(b) $x(t)=3t+\sigma_tx_0$ gives $\dot x=3+\dot\sigma_tx_0=3+\frac{\dot\sigma_t}{\sigma_t}(x-3t)$, and $\frac{\dot\sigma_t}{\sigma_t}=\frac{-(1-t)+4t}{\sigma_t^2}$: it is the flow (checked numerically at $x_0=0.7$, $t=0.4$: $1.9$). The flow map $x_0\mapsto3+2x_0$ is increasing, hence the optimal map (Exercise 3 of the sheet, or Lesson 1).

(c) Action $\int_0^1(9+\dot\sigma_t^2)\,\dd t=10.858$, against $W_2^2=9+(2-1)^2=10$ and $\E\abs{X_1-X_0}^2=9+1+4=14$. The path is not the geodesic: $\sigma_t$ dips to $\min\sigma_t=\frac{2}{\sqrt5}=0.894$ at $t=\frac1{1+4}=0.2$ (minimize $(1-t)^2+4t^2$), while the geodesic has $\sigma_t=1+t$.

Exercise 5 — Tweedie gives the Gaussian shrinkage ★

Let $W\sim\N(m,s^2)$ and $Z=W+\sigma\eps$ in $\R$. Compute the score of $Z$ and deduce $\E[W\mid Z=z]$ from Tweedie’s formula. Compare with the Gaussian conditioning formula.

Solution

$Z\sim\N(m,s^2+\sigma^2)$, so $(\log\rho)'(z)=-\frac{z-m}{s^2+\sigma^2}$ and $\E[W\mid Z=z]=z-\frac{\sigma^2(z-m)}{s^2+\sigma^2}=m+\frac{s^2}{s^2+\sigma^2}(z-m)$. Conditioning: $\mathrm{Cov}(W,Z)/\mathrm{Var}(Z)=\frac{s^2}{s^2+\sigma^2}$, the same. Numbers: $m=1$, $s=2$, $\sigma=1$, $z=3$: $2.6$. The noisier the observation, the more the denoiser shrinks towards the prior mean.

Exercise 6 — When does the flow-matching loss reach zero? ★★

Show that the minimum over $v$ of $\E\norm{v(X_t)-(X_1-X_0)}^2$ at a fixed $t$ is $\E\norm{X_1-X_0}^2-\E\norm{v_t(X_t)}^2$, and that it is $0$ iff $X_1-X_0$ is a function of $X_t$. Give a coupling of $\N(0,1)$ and $\N(0,4)$ for which the loss vanishes at every $t$, and compute the minimum at $t=0$ for the independent coupling.

Hint

Pythagoras for the conditional expectation. A deterministic coupling $X_1=T(X_0)$ with $x\mapsto(1-t)x+tT(x)$ injective.

Solution

With $D=X_1-X_0$: $\min_v\E\norm{v(X_t)-D}^2=\E\norm{D-\E[D\mid X_t]}^2=\E\norm D^2-\E\norm{\E[D\mid X_t]}^2$ (orthogonality), which is $\E[\mathrm{Var}(D\mid X_t)]$: zero iff $D$ is a.s. a function of $X_t$. For $X_1=2X_0$ (the optimal coupling), $X_t=(1+t)X_0$ determines $X_0$, so the loss vanishes for all $t$. For the independent coupling at $t=0$: $X_t=X_0$, $\E[D\mid X_0]=-X_0$, minimum $\E\abs{X_1}^2=4$. (In the two-atom example of §6, the minimum is $1$ at $t=0$ and $1.80$ at $t=\frac12$.)

Exercise 7 — The kinetic energy of a mixture path ★★

On the line, $\rho_t=(1-t)\rho_0+t\rho_1$. (a) Show that the velocity is $v_t=\frac{F_0-F_1}{\rho_t}$ and that the action is $\int\frac{(F_0-F_1)^2}{\mathrm{LM}(\rho_0,\rho_1)}\dd x$ with the logarithmic mean $\mathrm{LM}$. (b) Show that it is infinite between $\mathcal U[0,1]$ and $\mathcal U[1,2]$. (c) Between $\N(0,1)$ and $\N(1,1)$ the action is $1.086$: compare with $W_2^2$.

Solution

(a) §2: $\rho_tv_t=-\partial_tF_t=F_0-F_1$, and $\int_0^1\frac{\dd t}{(1-t)a+tb}=\frac{\log b-\log a}{b-a}=\frac1{\mathrm{LM}(a,b)}$. (b) On $(0,1)$: $\rho_1=0$ and $F_0-F_1=x>0$, and $\int_0^1\frac{x^2}{(1-t)\cdot1}\dd t=\infty$ for each such $x$; mass must appear where there was none. (c) $W_2^2=1$ (translation): the mixture wastes $8.6\%$; for $\N(0,1)\to\N(4,2^2)$ it wastes $171\%$ ($46.07$ against $17$).

Exercise 8 — Hopf–Lax: the dual potential from its initial value ★★★

With the convention $\partial_t\varphi+\frac14\norm{\nabla\varphi}^2=0$, check that $\varphi_t(x)=\min_y\big(\varphi_0(y)+\frac{\norm{x-y}^2}t\big)$ reproduces the two potentials of §3: (a) $\varphi_0(y)=2ay$ gives $\varphi_t(x)=2ax-a^2t$; (b) $\varphi_0(y)=\frac{\sigma_1-\sigma_0}{\sigma_0}y^2$ gives $\varphi_t(x)=\frac{\sigma_1-\sigma_0}{\sigma_t}x^2$ with $\sigma_t=(1-t)\sigma_0+t\sigma_1$. Explain why at $t=1$ this is a $c$-transform.

Hint

Minimize a quadratic in $y$. At $t=1$ the formula is $\varphi_1(x)=\min_y\varphi_0(y)+\norm{x-y}^2$.

Solution

(a) The minimizer of $2ay+\frac{(x-y)^2}t$ is $y=x-at$, value $2a(x-at)+a^2t=2ax-a^2t$ ✓. (b) With $k=\frac{\sigma_1-\sigma_0}{\sigma_0}$, minimize $ky^2+\frac{(x-y)^2}t$: $y=\frac{x}{1+kt}$, value $\frac{kx^2}{1+kt}=\frac{(\sigma_1-\sigma_0)x^2}{\sigma_0(1+kt)}=\frac{(\sigma_1-\sigma_0)x^2}{\sigma_t}$ ✓ (numerically $0.57857$ both ways at $t=0.4$, $x=0.9$, $\sigma_0=1$, $\sigma_1=2$). At $t=1$, $\varphi_1(x)=\min_y\big(\norm{x-y}^2-(-\varphi_0(y))\big)$ is the $c$-transform of $f=-\varphi_0$ for $c=\norm{x-y}^2$ (Lesson 4): the dual Benamou–Brenier problem is Kantorovich’s dual, written along time.

Exercise 9 — No schedule restores optimality for Gaussians ★★★

(OT4ML Remark 16.9.) For independent $X_0\sim\N(0,\Sigma_0)$, $X_1\sim\N(0,\Sigma_1)$ and a schedule $Z_t=a_tX_0+b_tX_1$ with $(a_0,b_0)=(1,0)$, $(a_1,b_1)=(0,1)$ and $a_t^2\Id+b_t^2C\succ0$, show that the flow map at time $1$ is $\Sigma_0^{1/2}C^{1/2}\Sigma_0^{-1/2}$, whatever the schedule.

Hint

Whiten with $\Sigma_0^{-1/2}$. The whitened covariance $\tilde\Sigma_t=a_t^2\Id+b_t^2C$ is a function of $C$; guess $\tilde T_t=\tilde\Sigma_t^{1/2}$.

Solution

Gaussian conditioning: $v_t(z)=\mathrm{Cov}(\dot a_tX_0+\dot b_tX_1,Z_t)\,\mathrm{Cov}(Z_t)^{-1}z=(\dot a_ta_t\Sigma_0+\dot b_tb_t\Sigma_1)\Sigma_t^{-1}z$. Whitened: $\tilde A_t=(\dot a_ta_t\Id+\dot b_tb_tC)\tilde\Sigma_t^{-1}=\frac12\dot{\tilde\Sigma}_t\tilde\Sigma_t^{-1}$. Since all these matrices are functions of $C$, they commute, and $\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\tilde\Sigma_t^{1/2}=\frac12\dot{\tilde\Sigma}_t\tilde\Sigma_t^{-1/2}=\tilde A_t\tilde\Sigma_t^{1/2}$ with $\tilde\Sigma_0^{1/2}=\Id$: the whitened flow is $\tilde\Sigma_t^{1/2}$. At $t=1$, $\tilde\Sigma_1^{1/2}=C^{1/2}$; un-whitening gives $\Sigma_0^{1/2}C^{1/2}\Sigma_0^{-1/2}$, independent of $(a,b)$. By Prop. 16.8 it is optimal iff $\Sigma_0\Sigma_1=\Sigma_1\Sigma_0$: a different speed along the bridge cannot remove the hidden rotation.

Exercise 10 — Barycenters by multimarginal transport ★★

(StatOT §8.5, Exercise 3.) Let $\mu_1,\dots,\mu_n\in\Pc_2(\R^d)$ and $\ga^\star$ optimal for $\min_\ga\int\sum_{i=1}^n\norm{x_i-\frac1n\sum_jx_j}^2\dd\ga$ over the couplings of $\mu_1,\dots,\mu_n$. Prove that the law of $\frac1n\sum_iX_i$, $(X_i)\sim\ga^\star$, is a barycenter (equal weights). Then compute the barycenter of $\frac12(\delta_0+\delta_2)$ and $\frac12(\delta_1+\delta_5)$ and check its value against Lesson 3.

Hint

$\sum_i\norm{x-x_i}^2=\sum_i\norm{x_i-\bar x}^2+n\norm{x-\bar x}^2$ with $\bar x=\frac1n\sum_ix_i$. Glue optimal couplings of a candidate $b$ with each $\mu_i$. For two inputs, Lesson 3: the barycenter value is $\lambda(1-\lambda)W_2^2$.

Solution

It is Prop. 12.21 (§10) with $\lambda_i=\frac1n$; StatOT’s cost is $n\,c_{\mathrm{bar}}$. For any $b$, glue optimal couplings into $(Z,X_1,\dots,X_n)$: $\sum_iW_2^2(b,\mu_i)=\E\sum_i\norm{Z-X_i}^2=\E\big[\sum_i\norm{X_i-\bar X}^2+n\norm{Z-\bar X}^2\big]\ge\mathrm{MM}$; and $b^\star=\mathrm{Law}(\bar X^\star)$ attains $\mathrm{MM}$ by coupling $\bar X^\star$ with each $X_i^\star$. Example: on the line the optimal plan is comonotone, teams $(0,1)$ and $(2,5)$ with mass $\frac12$ each, means $\frac12$ and $\frac72$: the barycenter is $\frac12\delta_{1/2}+\frac12\delta_{7/2}$, with value $\sum_s\frac12W_2^2=\frac12\cdot\frac14+\frac12\cdot\frac94=1.25$, equal to $\frac12\cdot\frac12\cdot W_2^2(\be_1,\be_2)=\frac14\cdot5$ ✓ (it is the midpoint of the geodesic).

Exercise 11 — The Brownian bridge ★

Let $B$ be a standard Brownian motion in $\R$ and $X_t=(1-t)x+ty+\sqrt\eps\,(B_t-tB_1)$. Show that $X_0=x$, $X_1=y$, and $X_t\sim\N\big((1-t)x+ty,\eps t(1-t)\big)$. At which time is the bridge the most uncertain?

Solution

$B_0=0$ and $B_1-B_1=0$ give the endpoints. $B_t-tB_1$ is centred Gaussian with variance $\mathrm{Var}(B_t)-2t\,\mathrm{Cov}(B_t,B_1)+t^2\mathrm{Var}(B_1)=t-2t^2+t^2=t(1-t)$. So the variance is $\eps t(1-t)$, maximal at $t=\frac12$ ($\eps/4$; for $\eps=0.2$: $0.05$, and $0.04995$ in a simulation with $2\times10^5$ bridges).

Exercise 12 — One proximal step from an empty cell ★★

Compute $\mathrm{prox}_{\tau J}$ at $(\tilde a,\tilde m)=(0,1)$ with $\tau=1$, i.e. minimize $\frac12a^2+\frac12(m-1)^2+\frac{m^2}a$. Interpret.

Hint

§4: $m=\frac{a}{a+2\tau}\tilde m$ and $(a-\tilde a)(a+2\tau)^2=\tau\norm{\tilde m}^2$.

Solution

The cubic is $a(a+2)^2=1$, with root $a^\star=0.20557$; then $m^\star=\frac{a^\star}{a^\star+2}=0.09320$ (objective $0.47453$; a direct numerical minimization agrees). Interpretation: the input asks for a momentum $1$ in a cell with no mass, which costs $+\infty$; the prox resolves the conflict by creating a little mass ($0.21$) and keeping only a small momentum ($0.09$), so that $m^2/a=0.042$ stays moderate. The splitting algorithm repeats such local compromises, while the projection step restores mass conservation globally.
