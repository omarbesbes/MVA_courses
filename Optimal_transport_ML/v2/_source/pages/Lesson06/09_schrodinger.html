9. Noisy paths: Schrödinger bridges (going further) OT4ML §14.3, pp. 334–339; CompOT §7.6, pp. 115–117

Replace the straight paths of optimal transport by random paths that stay close (in relative entropy) to Brownian motion while matching the two endpoint laws: the endpoints are then paired by the entropic OT plan of Lesson 7, and each pair is joined by a Brownian bridge.

The problem

The path-space form of Benamou–Brenier (§2) pairs the endpoints optimally and joins each pair by a straight segment. Real trajectories are noisy (cells, particles, diffusion samplers), and the sharp optimum is numerically fragile. Schrödinger (1931) asked: if particles perform a Brownian motion and we observe their distribution at time $0$ and at time $1$, what is the most likely way they went? The answer smooths optimal transport, and it is the dynamic picture of the entropic regularization of Lesson 7.

In the course OT4ML Def. 14.10, p. 336

Entropic path-space problem. Let $\mathrm R^\eps\in\Pc(\Omega)$ be a reference path law, for instance a Brownian or Langevin dynamics at noise level $\eps$. The corresponding entropy projection defines the dynamic Schrödinger problem.

Definition 14.10 (Schrödinger bridge).

$$\mathrm{SB}_\eps(\al,\be):=\inf_{M\in\Pc(\Omega)}\big\{\eps\,\KL(M\mid\mathrm R^\eps)\ ;\ \pf{(e_0)}{M}=\al,\ \pf{(e_1)}{M}=\be\big\}.\qquad(14.18)$$

It asks for the most likely path law, relative to the prior dynamics $\mathrm R^\eps$, among all path laws matching the observed endpoint marginals.

$\Omega$
The space of continuous paths $\omega:[0,1]\to\X$; $e_t(\omega)=\omega_t$ evaluates a path at time $t$.
$M$
A law of random paths (a stochastic process). $\pf{(e_0)}{M}=\al$: the paths start distributed as $\al$; $\pf{(e_1)}{M}=\be$: they end as $\be$.
$\mathrm R^\eps$
The prior: what the particles would do on their own, e.g. $\dd X_t=\sqrt\eps\,\dd B_t$ (OT4ML’s convention on p. 336), a Brownian motion with variance $\eps$ per unit time.
$\KL(M\mid\mathrm R^\eps)$
Relative entropy of path laws: how unlikely $M$ is under the prior (large deviations). Minimizing it = choosing the most likely explanation of the observed endpoints.
Reminder — relative entropy and its chain rule

$\KL(\mu\mid\nu)=\int\log\frac{\dd\mu}{\dd\nu}\dd\mu\ge0$, with equality iff $\mu=\nu$. Chain rule: if $\mu=\int\mu^z\,\dd\mu_Z(z)$ and $\nu=\int\nu^z\,\dd\nu_Z(z)$ are disintegrated along the same variable $Z$, then $\KL(\mu\mid\nu)=\KL(\mu_Z\mid\nu_Z)+\int\KL(\mu^z\mid\nu^z)\,\dd\mu_Z(z)$. More: Reminders — entropy and KL.

In the course OT4ML Prop. 14.11, p. 337

Proposition 14.11 (Endpoint reduction of the Schrödinger problem). Assume that the regular conditional laws above exist and that the relative-entropy chain rule applies, with value $+\infty$ when absolute continuity fails. Then

$$\mathrm{SB}_\eps(\al,\be)=\inf_{\pi\in\U(\al,\be)}\eps\,\KL\big(\pi\mid\mathrm R^\eps_{0,1}\big).\qquad(14.19)$$

For a fixed endpoint coupling $\pi$ with finite $\KL(\pi\mid\mathrm R^\eps_{0,1})$, the minimizing path law is the mixture of reference bridges

$$M^\pi=\int\mathrm R^{\eps,x,y}\,\dd\pi(x,y).\qquad(14.20)$$

Consequently, if $\pi^\star$ solves (14.19), then $M^{\pi^\star}$ solves the path-space problem (14.18).

$\mathrm R^\eps_{0,1}$
The law of the pair (start, end) under the prior; for Brownian motion its density is proportional to $e^{-\norm{x-y}^2/2\eps}$ (times the initial law).
$\mathrm R^{\eps,x,y}$
The prior conditioned to start at $x$ and end at $y$: for Brownian motion, the Brownian bridge.
(14.19)–(14.20)
The path problem splits: first choose the endpoint coupling (a static problem), then fill each pair with the prior’s bridge.
Proof (chain rule of the relative entropy)
  1. Disintegrate a feasible $M$ along its endpoints: $M=\int M^{x,y}\,\dd\pi(x,y)$ with $\pi=\pf{(e_0,e_1)}{M}\in\U(\al,\be)$; likewise $\mathrm R^\eps=\int\mathrm R^{\eps,x,y}\dd\mathrm R^\eps_{0,1}$.Regular conditional laws exist on path space (Polish).
  2. $$\KL(M\mid\mathrm R^\eps)=\KL(\pi\mid\mathrm R^\eps_{0,1})+\int\KL\big(M^{x,y}\mid\mathrm R^{\eps,x,y}\big)\dd\pi(x,y).\qquad(14.21)$$Chain rule (reminder).
  3. The second term is $\ge0$, and $=0$ iff $M^{x,y}=\mathrm R^{\eps,x,y}$ for $\pi$-a.e. $(x,y)$. So for fixed $\pi$ the best $M$ is (14.20), and it remains to minimize the first term over $\pi$: (14.19). $\square$Positivity of KL and its equality case.

Brownian prior: Sinkhorn plus Brownian bridges

In the course OT4ML p. 338

[…] Thus multiplying the endpoint entropy by $\eps$ produces the static cost $\norm{x-y}^2/2$; the convention $e^{-\norm{x-y}^2/\eps}$ used for quadratic Sinkhorn is recovered by replacing the Brownian noise variance by $\eps/2$. […] In words, Sinkhorn computes which endpoints should be paired; the path-space Schrödinger bridge then connects each paired endpoint by a Brownian bridge rather than by a deterministic straight line.

With the prior $\dd X_t=\sqrt\eps\,\dd B_t$, $\eps\KL(\pi\mid\mathrm R^\eps_{0,1})=\frac12\int\norm{x-y}^2\dd\pi+\eps\KL(\pi\mid\al\otimes\be)+\text{const}$ (OT4ML p. 338): the endpoint problem is entropic optimal transport, solved by Sinkhorn’s algorithm in Lesson 7. The Brownian bridge from $x$ to $y$ is $X_t=(1-t)x+ty+\sqrt\eps\,(B_t-tB_1)$:

$$X_t\sim\N\big((1-t)x+ty,\ \eps\,t(1-t)\,\Id\big):$$

McCann’s segment, blurred by a Gaussian whose variance vanishes at both ends. Numbers: $\eps=0.2$, $t=\frac12$: variance $0.05$ per coordinate (a simulation with $2\times10^5$ bridges gives $0.04995$). As $\eps\to0$ the coupling becomes the OT plan and the bridges collapse on the segments: back to Benamou–Brenier (OT4ML p. 337, under large-deviation assumptions).

The fluid picture: Benamou–Brenier plus Fisher information

In the course OT4ML pp. 336–337

[…] Write $\sigma>0$ for the diffusion intensity; thus the uncontrolled noise is $\sqrt\sigma\,\dd B_t$ and its generator is $(\sigma/2)\Delta$. Formally, for smooth positive densities the controlled Fokker–Planck equation is $\partial_t\rho_t+\dv(\rho_tv_t)=\frac\sigma2\Delta\rho_t$, one minimizes, among curves joining the prescribed endpoint densities, the kinetic action $\int_0^1\!\int\frac12\norm{v_t(x)}^2\rho_t(x)\,\dd x\,\dd t$. Equivalently, one can absorb the diffusion into the velocity by writing $u_t=v_t-\frac\sigma2\nabla\log\rho_t$, $\partial_t\rho_t+\dv(\rho_tu_t)=0$. […] the same minimizers are obtained from the modified Benamou–Brenier action

$$\int_0^1\!\!\int\Big(\frac12\norm{u_t(x)}^2+\frac{\sigma^2}8\norm{\nabla\log\rho_t(x)}^2\Big)\rho_t(x)\,\dd x\,\dd t .$$

Thus the Schrödinger bridge is a least-action interpolation with both transport kinetic energy and a Fisher-information penalty.

The derivation is the computation of §7 in reverse: expand $\frac12\norm{u+\frac\sigma2\nabla\log\rho}^2$; the cross term integrates to $\frac\sigma2\big(\int\rho_1\log\rho_1-\int\rho_0\log\rho_0\big)$, a constant fixed by the endpoints. The Fisher information $\int\norm{\nabla\log\rho}^2\rho$ penalizes sharp densities: Schrödinger interpolations are blurred versions of McCann’s.

In machine learning — Schrödinger bridges for generation and for cells

Generative modelling and translation. The Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge (De Bortoli, Thornton, Heng, Doucet, NeurIPS 2021) learns the bridge between noise and data — or between two datasets, e.g. two image domains — by alternately fitting forward and backward diffusions, an iterative proportional fitting on path space, i.e. Sinkhorn’s algorithm (Lesson 7) lifted to processes. Unlike a diffusion model, the forward process does not need to run until the data are completely destroyed.

Single-cell trajectory inference. Population snapshots of cells (§2) are naturally modelled by noisy dynamics. Lavenant, Zhang, Kim and Schiebinger (Towards a mathematical theory of trajectory inference, 2021, cited in OT4ML Example 3.24, pp. 61–62) reconstruct the law of cell trajectories from snapshots by minimizing a relative entropy to a Brownian-type reference — a Schrödinger problem — and Waddington-OT pairs consecutive snapshots with entropic OT plans (OT4ML Example 3.24).

For the Brownian prior with $\eps\to\infty$ (very large noise), what does the endpoint coupling of the Schrödinger bridge become?

Answer

The endpoint problem is $\min\frac12\int\norm{x-y}^2\dd\pi+\eps\KL(\pi\mid\al\otimes\be)$: when $\eps$ dominates, the minimizer tends to the independent coupling $\al\otimes\be$ (KL $=0$). Huge noise forgets where particles started — the opposite limit of optimal transport ($\eps\to0$).
