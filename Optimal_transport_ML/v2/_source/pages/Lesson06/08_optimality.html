8. When is the generated map optimal? OT4ML pp. 412–414

The flow learned by flow matching or a diffusion model always transports noise onto data, but its map is the optimal (Brenier) map only in special cases: always on the line, for Gaussians exactly when the covariances commute, and when the training pairs are optimally coupled — which minibatch OT approximates and rectification does not guarantee.

The question

In the course OT4ML p. 412

When is the induced map optimal? Integrating the learned velocity gives a deterministic map from $\al_0$ to $\al_1$, but this map is not automatically the Brenier optimal map. It is optimal only in special cases where the accumulated flow remains the gradient of a convex potential. The Gaussian product-coupling case already shows the precise obstruction: the interpolated covariances are simple, the velocity is affine, but the terminal map can contain a hidden rotational part. This phenomenon, and its extensions to rectified flows and mixtures, is analyzed in depth in [283].

Why care, if any transport map generates the data? Three reasons. (i) Straightness: the optimal flow moves every particle on a straight line at constant speed (§2), so one Euler step is exact; curved flows need many network evaluations (§6). (ii) Meaning: an optimal map preserves as much as possible of the geometry of the noise (nearby noise vectors give nearby samples, the map is monotone in the sense of convex gradients), which matters when the map itself is used, e.g. to edit or translate data. (iii) Understanding: it tells us what the regression actually computes. By Brenier, “optimal” means “gradient of a convex function” (refresher). Reference [283] is Hertrich, Chambolle and Delon, On the relation between rectified flows and optimal transport (NeurIPS 2025), co-authored by the lecturer.

On the line: always optimal

In dimension one the trajectories of a well-posed ODE cannot cross, so the flow map is increasing; an increasing map that pushes $\al_0$ to $\al_1$ is the monotone rearrangement, which is optimal — whatever the coupling used for training. This is Exercise 3 of the sheet, solved in §11. In the two-atom example the flow map is $\mathrm{sign}(x)$, the optimal map (§6), and in the widget of §7 the landing boundaries are exactly $\Phi^{-1}$ of the cumulative weights. The path is still not the geodesic: its action ($0.982$) exceeds $W_2^2$ ($0.404$).

Gaussians: optimal exactly when the covariances commute

In the course OT4ML Prop. 16.8, pp. 412–413

Proposition 16.8 (Gaussian flow matching and optimality). Let $\Sigma_0,\Sigma_1\succ0$ and let $X_0\sim\N(0,\Sigma_0)$ and $X_1\sim\N(0,\Sigma_1)$ be independent. Consider the linear flow-matching interpolation $Z_t=(1-t)X_0+tX_1$, $\al_t=\mathrm{Law}(Z_t)=\N(0,\Sigma_t)$, where

$$\Sigma_t=(1-t)^2\Sigma_0+t^2\Sigma_1.\qquad(16.10)$$

Then the exact flow-matching velocity is affine, $v_t(z)=A_tz$, with

$$A_t=\big(t\Sigma_1-(1-t)\Sigma_0\big)\Sigma_t^{-1}.\qquad(16.11)$$

The induced flow map $T^{\mathrm{FM}}_t$ from $\al_0$ to $\al_t$ is

$$T^{\mathrm{FM}}_t=\Sigma_0^{1/2}\Big((1-t)^2\Id+t^2\,\Sigma_0^{-1/2}\Sigma_1\Sigma_0^{-1/2}\Big)^{1/2}\Sigma_0^{-1/2}.\qquad(16.12)$$

In particular, $\ T^{\mathrm{FM}}_1=\Sigma_0^{1/2}\big(\Sigma_0^{-1/2}\Sigma_1\Sigma_0^{-1/2}\big)^{1/2}\Sigma_0^{-1/2}$. (16.13) This terminal map coincides with the quadratic optimal transport map

$$T^{\mathrm{OT}}=\Sigma_0^{-1/2}\big(\Sigma_0^{1/2}\Sigma_1\Sigma_0^{1/2}\big)^{1/2}\Sigma_0^{-1/2}\qquad(16.14)$$

if and only if $\Sigma_0\Sigma_1=\Sigma_1\Sigma_0$.

Covariance of $(1-t)X_0+tX_1$ for independent $X_0,X_1$: it shrinks in the middle (at $t=\frac12$ it is $\frac14(\Sigma_0+\Sigma_1)$). With independent pairs the path is not the geodesic.
$A_t$
$\mathrm{Cov}(X_1-X_0,Z_t)\,\mathrm{Cov}(Z_t)^{-1}$: Gaussian conditioning. In general not symmetric.
$C=\Sigma_0^{-1/2}\Sigma_1\Sigma_0^{-1/2}$
The target covariance seen in the whitened coordinates of the source (where $\al_0$ becomes $\N(0,\Id)$).
(16.13)
“Whiten, take the square root of $C$, un-whiten”: similar to the symmetric matrix $C^{1/2}$, but not symmetric itself in general.
(16.14)
Brenier’s map between the two Gaussians: the unique symmetric positive definite matrix sending $\Sigma_0$ to $\Sigma_1$ (Lesson 1).
Idea of the proof

In whitened coordinates both covariances, $\Id$ and $C$, commute, so the flow is the simple square root $\big((1-t)^2\Id+t^2C\big)^{1/2}$. Coming back multiplies by $\Sigma_0^{1/2}$ on the left and $\Sigma_0^{-1/2}$ on the right — a similarity, not a congruence — which destroys symmetry unless $\Sigma_0$ commutes with $C$. A pushing map is optimal iff it is symmetric positive definite.

Full proof

  2. Whiten: $\tilde z=\Sigma_0^{-1/2}z$. Then $\tilde\Sigma_t=(1-t)^2\Id+t^2C$, and the whitened velocity is $\tilde A_t=(tC-(1-t)\Id)\tilde\Sigma_t^{-1}$.Conjugate step 1 by $\Sigma_0^{-1/2}$: $\Sigma_0^{-1/2}(t\Sigma_1-(1-t)\Sigma_0)\Sigma_t^{-1}\Sigma_0^{1/2}=(tC-(1-t)\Id)\tilde\Sigma_t^{-1}$.
  3. $\tilde T_t=\tilde\Sigma_t^{1/2}$ solves $\dot{\tilde T}_t=\tilde A_t\tilde T_t$, $\tilde T_0=\Id$.$\Id$ and $C$ commute, so everything is a function of $C$ and commutes: $\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\tilde\Sigma_t^{1/2}=\frac12\tilde\Sigma_t^{-1/2}\dot{\tilde\Sigma}_t=(tC-(1-t)\Id)\tilde\Sigma_t^{-1/2}=\tilde A_t\tilde\Sigma_t^{1/2}$.
  4. $T^{\mathrm{FM}}_t=\Sigma_0^{1/2}\tilde\Sigma_t^{1/2}\Sigma_0^{-1/2}$, which is (16.12); at $t=1$, (16.13).Undo the whitening.

In the course OT4ML Remark 16.9, p. 414

Remark 16.9 (Changing the bridge speed does not restore optimality). The same terminal map (16.13) is obtained for every continuously differentiable scalar schedule $Z_t=a_tX_0+b_tX_1$ satisfying $(a_0,b_0)=(1,0)$, $(a_1,b_1)=(0,1)$, and $a_t^2\Id+b_t^2C\succ0$ for all $t$. Indeed, after whitening, the covariance path is $a_t^2\Id+b_t^2C$, and the flow map is its positive square root. Thus changing the speed of a nondegenerate scalar Gaussian bridge, for instance by using an OU schedule, cannot repair the non-optimality created by non-commuting covariances.

Worked example — a hidden rotation

$\Sigma_0=\mathrm{diag}(4,1)$, $\Sigma_1=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}$ (they do not commute: $\Sigma_0\Sigma_1-\Sigma_1\Sigma_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&3\\-3&0\end{pmatrix}$). Then

$$T_1^{\mathrm{FM}}=\begin{pmatrix}0.664&0.486\\0.122&1.393\end{pmatrix},\qquad T^{\mathrm{OT}}=\begin{pmatrix}0.697&0.243\\0.243&1.328\end{pmatrix}.$$

Both push $\Sigma_0$ to $\Sigma_1$ (to $10^{-15}$), but $T_1^{\mathrm{FM}}$ is not symmetric. Costs $\E\norm{T(X_0)-X_0}^2=\tr\big((T-\Id)\Sigma_0(T-\Id)\T\big)$: $0.9015$ for the flow map, $W_2^2=0.7712$ for Brenier’s — 16.9% more; the independent coupling costs $\tr\Sigma_0+\tr\Sigma_1=9$. With commuting covariances, $\Sigma_0=\mathrm{diag}(4,1)$ and $\Sigma_1=\mathrm{diag}(1,4)$, both maps are $\mathrm{diag}(\frac12,2)$ and cost $W_2^2=2$ — but the flow-matching path still has action $3.717$: an optimal map along a non-geodesic path.

With an optimal coupling, flow matching is optimal transport

Train on pairs from an optimal coupling, $X_1=T(X_0)$ with $T=\nabla\varphi$ Brenier’s map. Then $X_t=(1-t)X_0+tT(X_0)=S_t(X_0)$ with $S_t$ injective for $t<1$ (§2, proof of Benamou–Brenier, Part 2). Knowing $X_t$ means knowing $X_0$, so the conditional expectation is trivial:

$$v_t(X_t)=\E[T(X_0)-X_0\mid X_t]=T(X_0)-X_0,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad v_t\big((1-t)x+tT(x)\big)=T(x)-x,$$

the Benamou–Brenier velocity (14.10). The interpolant is McCann’s path (OT4ML Example 16.2), the paths do not cross, the flow map is $T$, and one Euler step is exact. The catch: we would need the OT plan between noise and data, which is what we wanted to avoid (§5).

Minibatch OT. A cheap approximation: draw a minibatch of $B$ noise vectors and $B$ data points, solve the $B\times B$ assignment problem (Lesson 2), and use the matched pairs in the regression. With $B=1$ this is the independent coupling; as $B$ grows the empirical coupling approaches an optimal one. This is OT-CFM (Tong, Fatras, Malkin, Huguet, Zhang, Rector-Brooks, Wolf, Bengio, Improving and generalizing flow-based generative models with minibatch optimal transport, TMLR 2024) and multisample flow matching (Pooladian, Ben-Hamu, Domingo-Enrich, Amos, Lipman, Chen, ICML 2023).

Rectified flows: straight is not optimal

Rectified flow (Liu, Gong, Liu, ICLR 2023; OT4ML Example 16.2) proposes to straighten the flow without solving OT: after a first training, generate pairs $(X_0,T_1(X_0))$ with the learned flow (“reflow”) and train again on this deterministic coupling. By the chain of inequalities of §6, $\E\norm{T_1(X_0)-X_0}^2\le\E_\pi\norm{X_1-X_0}^2$: the quadratic cost never increases (Liu et al. prove it for all convex costs), and the paths get straighter. But the fixed points need not be optimal.

Worked example — one reflow on the Gaussian example (derived here)
  1. Reflow coupling: $X_1=TX_0$ with $T=T_1^{\mathrm{FM}}=\begin{pmatrix}0.664&0.486\\0.122&1.393\end{pmatrix}$; its eigenvalues $0.590$ and $1.467$ are positive.$T$ is similar to $C^{1/2}$.
  2. $X_t=M_tX_0$ with $M_t=(1-t)\Id+tT$, invertible for all $t\in[0,1]$ (its eigenvalues $(1-t)+t\lambda>0$; numerically $\min_t\det M_t=0.866$).So the straight segments of the pairs do not cross.
  3. $v_t(x)=\E[(T-\Id)X_0\mid X_t=x]=(T-\Id)M_t^{-1}x$, whose flow is $x(t)=M_tx_0$: the flow reproduces the coupling exactly (checked by integrating the ODE).$X_t$ determines $X_0=M_t^{-1}X_t$, and $\frac{\dd}{\dd t}M_tx_0=(T-\Id)x_0$.
  4. So $T$ is a fixed point of rectification after one step, with straight paths, and its cost is still $0.9015>W_2^2=0.7712$.Rectification preserves the map here; only an optimal coupling would give $T^{\mathrm{OT}}$.

Hertrich, Chambolle and Delon (2025) analyse these questions in depth: invariance properties of rectified flows, explicit velocity fields for Gaussians and Gaussian mixtures — the setting of Exercises 1, 4 and 5 of the sheet — and counterexamples showing that constraining rectified flows to gradient fields does not, in general, produce optimal transport maps.

Noise to atoms in the plane: close to optimal, not optimal

Take Gaussian noise $\N(0,\Id_2)$ and data made of three atoms with weights $\frac13$. Both the flow-matching / probability-flow map (§7: all schedules give the same map) and the optimal map send each noise sample to one atom; the optimal map is semi-discrete, with Laguerre cells (Lesson 4). We computed both on a $801\times801$ grid of $[-6,6]^2$ (flow: the schedule-free ODE of §7 with the exact posterior; OT: Laguerre weights balancing the Gaussian masses):

  • atoms $(-3,0)$, $(3,0)$, $(0,1)$: both maps give each atom mass $\frac13$, but about 5.7% of the noise mass lands on a different atom, and the flow map costs about $0.7\%$ more than $W_2^2\approx3.74$;
  • atoms $4(-1,-1)$, $4(1.3,-0.7)$, $4(0,1.2)$: $0.3\%$ of the mass changes atom, excess cost $0.005\%$ ($W_2^2=20.74$);
  • two atoms $(\pm1,0)$ with weights $0.3,0.7$: identical maps (the posterior depends on the first coordinate only, which follows a 1D monotone flow; the boundary is the line $x=\Phi^{-1}(0.3)=-0.5244$ at every height).

In machine learning — straighter flows, fewer steps

The number of network evaluations per sample is the main cost of flow and diffusion models. Minibatch OT couplings (OT-CFM, multisample flow matching) straighten the learned paths at the price of a $B\times B$ assignment per batch (Hungarian or Sinkhorn, Lesson 7), and report good samples with fewer integration steps on image benchmarks; they also reduce the variance of the regression target, since fewer pairs cross. Reflow straightens the paths of a trained model; InstaFlow (Liu et al., ICLR 2024) applies it to Stable Diffusion before distilling the flow into a one-step generator. Neither makes the map exactly optimal in general (this section), but for sampling speed straightness is what matters.

Pitfall — “straight paths” and “Gaussian start” do not imply optimal transport

A flow whose trajectories are straight lines is optimal only if the coupling it realizes is optimal: the reflow example has straight paths and a non-optimal map. And the map from an isotropic Gaussian to a non-Gaussian target given by flow matching or a diffusion model is in general not the Brenier map (three atoms in the plane, non-commuting Gaussians).

Reflexes
  • Is a generated map optimal? → Is it the gradient of a convex function? For linear maps: is the matrix symmetric positive definite?
  • Dimension one → monotone flow → optimal map, for any coupling.
  • Gaussians with independent coupling → optimal iff $\Sigma_0\Sigma_1=\Sigma_1\Sigma_0$; no schedule changes that.
  • Want straight paths → better couplings (minibatch OT), or reflow; want exact OT → an optimal coupling.

Flow matching from $\N(0,\Id)$ to $\N(0,\Sigma_1)$ with the independent coupling: is the flow map optimal?

Answer

Yes: $\Sigma_0=\Id$ commutes with everything, and $T_1^{\mathrm{FM}}=\Sigma_1^{1/2}=T^{\mathrm{OT}}$. Non-optimality needs a non-isotropic source that does not commute with the target — or a non-Gaussian target (three atoms).
