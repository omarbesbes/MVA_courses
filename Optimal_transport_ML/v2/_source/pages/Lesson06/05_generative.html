5. Generative modelling: transport noise onto data OT4ML §16.1, p. 407

To generate new data, draw noise and push it through a map that transports the noise distribution onto the data distribution; flows build that map by integrating a velocity field, which turns the design of a generative model into the choice of a motion.

The problem

You have samples $y_1,\dots,y_n\in\R^d$ of an unknown distribution $\be$ — images ($d=3\times256\times256\approx2\times10^5$), molecules, audio, cell profiles — and you want new samples of $\be$. The strategy of all the models of this lesson: pick a reference law $\al$ that is trivial to sample (usually $\N(0,\Id)$), find a map $T$ with $\pf{T}{\al}=\be$, then output $T(z)$ for fresh $z\sim\al$. Generative modelling is a transport problem in which only samples of the target are known.

In the course OT4ML §16.1, p. 407

Generative models aim to build a transportation map $T$ between a reference distribution $\al$ (typically an isotropic Gaussian) and the target data distribution $\be$. Since such reference measures are non-atomic, a measurable map with $\pf{T}{\al}=\be$ exists on standard Borel spaces, for instance by identifying both probability spaces with the unit interval and using a quantile-type rearrangement. This abstract existence statement is much weaker than having an explicit and numerically stable construction of $T$. Optimal transport is one approach to achieving this, but it is computationally expensive and raises questions about how to estimate it from samples. A different route is to prescribe an interpolation between noise and data, learn its velocity, and obtain $T$ by integrating a time-dependent vector field $v_t$.

reference $\al$
The noise distribution; in this lesson it is $\al_0$, at time $t=0$.
non-atomic
No single point carries positive mass ($\al$ has a density, for instance). From a non-atomic law one can reach any law by some map; from a Dirac one cannot (a map sends a Dirac to a Dirac).
“quantile-type rearrangement”
On the line, $T=C_\be^{-1}\circ C_\al$ works for any $\be$ (Lesson 1); in general one transfers the problem to $[0,1]$. Brenier’s map is another, canonical choice: the optimal one.
“integrating a time-dependent vector field”
$T=T_1$, the flow map at time $1$ of an ODE $\dot x=v_t(x)$: the Lagrangian picture of §1.

The last two rows are the subject of the next sections. Their common feature: the network never has to output the whole map; it outputs a local object (a velocity, a score) that can be fitted by regression on pairs (noise, data), and the map appears by integration. Benamou–Brenier (§2) tells us which motion would be the cheapest; we will see that the models do not use it by default, and what it costs (§8).

In machine learning — where these models are used

Diffusion models and flow-matching models are the basis of current generators of images, video, audio and molecules. For instance Stable Diffusion 3 (Esser et al., Scaling rectified flow transformers for high-resolution image synthesis, ICML 2024) trains a transformer $v_\theta(t,x,c)$ on latent codes $x$ of images and text prompts $c$ with the flow-matching loss of §6 and the straight interpolant between noise and data (called “rectified flow” in that paper, which puts the noise at $t=1$ and draws the times $t$ from a logit-normal law instead of uniformly, to favour intermediate noise levels); images are generated by solving the ODE from Gaussian noise in a few dozen steps. One-step generators such as Wasserstein GANs (Lesson 4: the critic is a 1-Lipschitz Kantorovich potential) remain useful when sampling must be instantaneous. Pointers: OT4ML p. 407 lists the diffusion literature (score matching, DDPM, score SDEs) and the three names of the regression branch (flow matching, rectified flow, stochastic interpolants).

Pitfall — “generative model” does not mean “optimal transport”

Any map with $\pf{T}{\al}=\be$ generates the data distribution. Optimality (least displacement) is an extra property, useful for straight paths and fast sampling (§8), but not required for correctness. Conversely, computing the exact OT map between noise and $10^6$ images would need a huge assignment problem and is statistically hard in high dimension: that is why the models learn a velocity instead.
