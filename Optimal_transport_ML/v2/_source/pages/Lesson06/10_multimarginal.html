10. Multimarginal transport and barycenters (in brief) OT4ML §12.2, pp. 265–270

Couple $S$ distributions at once with one joint law whose $S$ marginals are prescribed; for the cost “spread around the weighted mean”, the optimal joint law gives the Wasserstein barycenter of Lesson 3 by pushing it through the weighted mean.

The problem

Some questions involve more than two distributions at once: averaging $S$ distributions (barycenters, Lesson 3 §7), matching workers, firms and locations together (“matching for teams”), or placing $N$ electrons with a prescribed density so as to minimize their repulsion (density-functional theory). Instead of $S-1$ separate couplings, multimarginal transport chooses one joint law of $(X_1,\dots,X_S)$.

In the course OT4ML Def. 12.16, p. 265

Definition 12.16 (Multimarginal optimal transport). Let $\al_s\in\Pc(\X_s)$ for $s=1,\dots,S$, and denote by $\U(\al_1,\dots,\al_S)$ the probability measures whose $s$-th marginal is $\al_s$. For a measurable cost $c$ bounded from below, define

$$\mathrm{MMOT}_c(\al_1,\dots,\al_S):=\inf_{\pi\in\U(\al_1,\dots,\al_S)}\int_{\X_1\times\dots\times\X_S}c(x_1,\dots,x_S)\,\dd\pi(x_1,\dots,x_S).\qquad(12.31)$$

This is still a linear program in the discrete setting, but its ambient tensor has size $\prod_sn_s$.

$\U(\al_1,\dots,\al_S)$
Joint laws of $(X_1,\dots,X_S)$ with $X_s\sim\al_s$ for each $s$: the dependence between all the variables is chosen at once. For $S=2$ these are the couplings of Lesson 2.
$c(x_1,\dots,x_S)$
A cost of a whole team $(x_1,\dots,x_S)$, not only of pairs.
$\prod_sn_s$
For discrete marginals with $n_s$ atoms, the plan is a tensor $P_{i_1\dots i_S}\ge0$ with $\prod_sn_s$ entries and $\sum_sn_s$ marginal constraints: exponential in $S$, the computational obstacle.
Reminder — Wasserstein barycenters (Lesson 3)

OT4ML Def. 12.1 (p. 251): given inputs $\be_1,\dots,\be_S$ and weights $\lambda$ in the simplex, a barycenter solves $\min_\al\sum_s\lambda_s\Lc(\al,\be_s)$. For $W_2^2$ on $\R^d$: the barycenter of Diracs is the Dirac at the weighted mean; on the line, the barycenter’s quantile function is the average of the quantile functions (OT4ML Prop. 12.7, p. 254). See Lesson 3 §7 and §8.

Barycenters as a multimarginal problem

In the course OT4ML Prop. 12.21, p. 268

Proposition 12.21 (Multi-marginal formula for quadratic barycenters). Let $\be_1,\dots,\be_S\in\Pc_2(\R^d)$ and $\lambda\in\Sigma_S$. Define

$$B(x_1,\dots,x_S)=\sum_{s=1}^S\lambda_sx_s,\qquad c_{\mathrm{bar}}(x_1,\dots,x_S)=\min_x\sum_s\lambda_s\norm{x-x_s}^2 .$$

If $\pi^\star$ solves the multi-marginal OT problem with marginals $(\be_s)_s$ and cost $c_{\mathrm{bar}}$, then $\al^\star=\pf{B}{\pi^\star}$ is a Wasserstein barycenter. Conversely, every barycenter is obtained this way from an optimal multi-marginal plan.

$B(x_1,\dots,x_S)$
The weighted mean of one point of each input: the barycenter of points (Lesson 3 §7).
$c_{\mathrm{bar}}$
The minimum is attained at $x=B$, so $c_{\mathrm{bar}}=\sum_s\lambda_s\norm{x_s-B}^2$: the weighted spread of the team around its mean.
$\pf{B}{\pi^\star}$
Draw a team from the optimal joint law and record its mean: that distribution is the barycenter.
Idea of the proof

From a candidate barycenter $\al$, glue optimal couplings with each input along $\al$ (the gluing of Lesson 3 §2); replacing the barycenter point by the best point for each team can only decrease the cost, and the teams form a multimarginal plan. Conversely, from an optimal team law, put the barycenter point at the team’s mean.

Full proof
  1. Let $\al\in\Pc_2$ and optimal couplings $\pi_s\in\U(\al,\be_s)$. Glue them along $\al$ into $(X,Y_1,\dots,Y_S)$ with $(X,Y_s)\sim\pi_s$.Gluing lemma with a common marginal (Lesson 3 §2): draw $X\sim\al$, then each $Y_s$ from its conditional law given $X$.
  2. $$\sum_s\lambda_sW_2^2(\al,\be_s)=\E\sum_s\lambda_s\norm{X-Y_s}^2\ \ge\ \E\min_x\sum_s\lambda_s\norm{x-Y_s}^2=\E\,c_{\mathrm{bar}}(Y_1,\dots,Y_S).$$Optimality of each $\pi_s$; then pointwise, the value at $x=X$ is at least the minimum over $x$.
  3. $\E\,c_{\mathrm{bar}}(Y_1,\dots,Y_S)\ge\mathrm{MMOT}_{c_{\mathrm{bar}}}(\be_1,\dots,\be_S)$, so the barycenter value is $\ge$ the multimarginal value.The law of $(Y_1,\dots,Y_S)$ belongs to $\U(\be_1,\dots,\be_S)$.
  4. Conversely, let $(Y_s)\sim\pi^\star$ optimal and $X=B(Y_1,\dots,Y_S)$, so $X\sim\al^\star=\pf{B}{\pi^\star}$. Then $\sum_s\lambda_sW_2^2(\al^\star,\be_s)\le\E\sum_s\lambda_s\norm{X-Y_s}^2=\E\,c_{\mathrm{bar}}(Y)=\mathrm{MMOT}$.Each $(X,Y_s)$ is a coupling of $\al^\star$ and $\be_s$ (an upper bound); and $x=B$ attains the min defining $c_{\mathrm{bar}}$.
  5. By steps 3–4, $\al^\star$ attains the lower bound: it is a barycenter, and the two values are equal. For the converse statement, equality in step 2 for a barycenter forces $X=B(Y_1,\dots,Y_S)$ a.s. (the minimizer in $x$ is unique). $\square$OT4ML’s proof, p. 268.
In the course OT4ML Cor. 12.22, pp. 268–269

Corollary 12.22 (Sparse discrete barycenters). Let $\be_s=\sum_{i_s=1}^{n_s}b_{s,i_s}\delta_{x_{s,i_s}}$, $s=1,\dots,S$, be discrete input measures. There exists a quadratic Wasserstein barycenter $\al^\star$ supported on weighted averages $\sum_s\lambda_sx_{s,i_s}$ of one support point from each input and satisfying $\#\supp(\al^\star)\le\sum_{s=1}^Sn_s-S+1$.

Why $\sum_sn_s-S+1$: the multimarginal LP has $\sum_sn_s$ equality constraints, of which $S-1$ are redundant (all the marginals have total mass $1$); a vertex of the feasible polytope has at most as many positive entries as independent constraints (the argument of Lesson 2 for $S=2$, which gives $n+m-1$); each positive entry gives one atom $B(x_{1,i_1},\dots,x_{S,i_S})$.

Worked example — three measures on the line

$\be_1=\frac12(\delta_0+\delta_3)$, $\be_2=\frac13(\delta_0+\delta_1+\delta_2)$, $\be_3=\frac14(\delta_1+\delta_2+\delta_3+\delta_5)$, $\lambda_s=\frac13$. On the line the optimal joint law is comonotone: $(Y_1,Y_2,Y_3)=(C_{\be_1}^{-1}(U),C_{\be_2}^{-1}(U),C_{\be_3}^{-1}(U))$ with one uniform $U$. With random weights on $3$, $4$ and $5$ atoms (all levels distinct), the LP solution has exactly $10=3+4+5-3+1$ atoms.

Why the comonotone plan is optimal on the line

$c_{\mathrm{bar}}(x)=\sum_s\lambda_sx_s^2-\big(\sum_s\lambda_sx_s\big)^2=\sum_s\lambda_s(1-\lambda_s)x_s^2-\sum_{r\ne s}\lambda_r\lambda_sx_rx_s$. Under fixed marginals the first sum has a fixed expectation, so minimizing $\E\,c_{\mathrm{bar}}$ means maximizing $\sum_{r\ne s}\lambda_r\lambda_s\E[X_rX_s]$. Each $\E[X_rX_s]$ is maximal when $(X_r,X_s)$ is comonotone (this is the optimality of the monotone plan for $\abs{x-y}^2$ on the line, Lesson 1), and the comonotone multi-coupling makes all pairs comonotone at once.

In machine learning — fair scores by barycenters

OT4ML Example 12.3 (p. 252): a score $f(X,S)$ (credit, risk) has a different distribution $\be_s$ in each group $s$ of a protected attribute. Demographic parity asks the score distribution to be the same in every group. Repair: compute the $W_2$ barycenter $\al^\star$ of the $\be_s$ with weights $p_s=\P(S=s)$ — on the line, by averaging quantile functions — and replace each score by its image under the monotone map $T_s$ from $\be_s$ to $\al^\star$. Then every group has score law $\al^\star$, and among repairs that transport all groups to a common law, this one changes the scores the least in mean square. The multimarginal view explains the cost of the repair: it is the value of the comonotone multimarginal problem.
