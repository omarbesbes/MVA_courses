13. Summary of the lesson

The lesson on one screen
  1. Moving mass (§1): particles $\dot x=v_t(x)$, flow $T_t$, $\al_t=\pf{(T_t)}{\al_0}$ (Lagrangian) $\iff$ continuity equation $\partial_t\al_t+\dv(\al_tv_t)=0$, weak form $\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\int\varphi\,\dd\al_t=\int\ps{\nabla\varphi}{v_t}\dd\al_t$ (Eulerian). A curve has many velocities (add fields with $\dv(\al w)=0$, e.g. rotations of a Gaussian); the least-energy one is a gradient (OT4ML Prop. 14.3); in 1D, $v_t=-\partial_tF_t/\rho_t$ is unique.
  2. Benamou–Brenier (§2): $W_2^2(\al_0,\al_1)=\min\int_0^1\!\int\norm{v_t}^2\dd\al_t\,\dd t$ under the continuity equation (OT4ML Thm. 14.5). Minimizer: McCann’s path $\pf{((1-t)\Id+tT)}{\al_0}$ with constant velocities $T(x)-x$. Proof: Jensen in time ($\ge$) and the McCann motion ($\le$).
  3. Convexity and duality (§3): with $m=\rho v$, minimize $\int\!\!\int J(\rho,m)$, $J=\norm m^2/\rho$ (convex, 1-homogeneous, $+\infty$ for momentum without mass), under the linear constraint $\partial_t\rho+\dv m=0$. Dual: $\sup\int\varphi_1\dd\al_1-\int\varphi_0\dd\al_0$ with $\partial_t\varphi+\frac14\norm{\nabla\varphi}^2\le0$; at optimum $v=\nabla\varphi/2$; $(-\varphi_0,\varphi_1)$ is a Kantorovich pair.
  4. Numerics (§4): staggered space-time grid; Douglas–Rachford alternates a pointwise cubic (prox of $J$) and a Poisson solve (projection on the continuity equation).
  5. Generative modelling (§5): sample $z\sim\N(0,\Id)$, output $T(z)$ with $\pf{T}{\al}=\be$; flows build $T$ by integrating a learned velocity.
  6. Flow matching (§6): pairs $(X_0,X_1)\sim\pi$, $X_t=(1-t)X_0+tX_1$; the field $v_t(x)=\E[X_1-X_0\mid X_t=x]=\frac{\E[X_1\mid X_t=x]-x}{1-t}$ moves $\al_t$ (OT4ML Prop. 16.3) and is learned by least squares; the loss has a positive minimum. $W_2^2\le$ cost of the flow map $\le$ action $\le$ cost of $\pi$.
  7. Diffusion (§7): OU noising $=$ Gaussian interpolant; Tweedie $\E[W\mid Z=z]=z+\sigma^2\nabla\log\rho_\sigma(z)$; velocity $=\frac xt+\frac{1-t}t\nabla\log\rho_t$ (noise at $0$), a gradient; probability-flow ODE $\bar v=v-\frac{\sigma^2}2\nabla\log\rho$; schedules change the paths, not the map.
  8. Optimality (§8): the flow map is optimal iff it is a gradient of a convex function: always in 1D (sheet Ex. 3); Gaussians with independent coupling iff $\Sigma_0\Sigma_1=\Sigma_1\Sigma_0$ (OT4ML Prop. 16.8), whatever the schedule; with an optimal coupling, flow matching is McCann’s flow. Minibatch OT straightens paths; reflow gives straight but not necessarily optimal flows; noise to three atoms: about 5.7% of the mass assigned differently.
  9. Schrödinger bridges (§9): $\min\eps\KL(M\mid\text{Brownian})$ with endpoint laws $=$ entropic OT for the endpoints $+$ Brownian bridges in between; fluid form: kinetic energy $+$ Fisher information.
  10. Multimarginal (§10): one joint law with $S$ marginals; barycenter $=\pf{B}{\pi^\star}$ for the cost $c_{\mathrm{bar}}=\sum_s\lambda_s\norm{x_s-B}^2$; at most $\sum n_s-S+1$ atoms; comonotone plans on the line.

The reflexes to acquire

  • A moving distribution → write both views: particles $T_t$ and the continuity equation; check with $\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\E\varphi(X_t)$.
  • “The velocity of a curve” → which one? The least-energy one is a gradient; in 1D it is unique.
  • Lower bound on an action → couple $X_0$ with its endpoint, Jensen in time; upper bound on $W_2^2$ → exhibit a motion.
  • Non-convex in $(\rho,v)$ → change variables to $(\rho,m=\rho v)$: perspective function, linear constraint.
  • Duality with a continuity constraint → multiply by $\varphi$, integrate by parts, Young.
  • Flow matching → conditional expectation $\E[X_1-X_0\mid X_t]$; Bayes and softmax for mixtures and atoms.
  • Score ↔ denoiser ↔ velocity → Tweedie.
  • Is a map optimal? → gradient of a convex function; for matrices, symmetric positive definite; 1D → monotone.
  • Gaussians → whiten the source; commuting covariances make everything scalar.
  • Time conventions and factors $\frac12$ → check them before comparing two formulas.

What comes next

  • Lesson 7 (entropic regularization). The endpoint problem of the Schrödinger bridge (§9) is the entropic OT problem; Sinkhorn’s algorithm solves it, and its multimarginal version computes barycenters on grids. Minibatch OT couplings (§8) are often computed with it.
  • Lesson 8 (gradient flows). A Wasserstein gradient flow is a curve solving the continuity equation with the velocity $v_t=-\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\al}(\al_t)$: the language of §1, with the Riemannian picture given by Benamou–Brenier (§2). The Fokker–Planck equation of §7 is the gradient flow of an entropy plus a potential energy.
  • Back to Lessons 1, 3 and 4. Brenier’s map and McCann’s interpolation (Lesson 1, Lesson 3 §6) are the minimizers of §2; Kantorovich duality and $c$-transforms (Lesson 4) reappear as the Hamilton–Jacobi dual and the Hopf–Lax formula (§3); semi-discrete OT (Lesson 4) gives the Laguerre cells of §8.

Notes on the sources (things that could confuse you), each with its check
  • Sheet, Exercise 4(ii): “jointly diagonalized”. Must mean “in one orthonormal basis” ($\Sigma_0\Sigma_1=\Sigma_1\Sigma_0$). Every pair of positive definite matrices is diagonalizable together by a congruence; for $\Sigma_0=\mathrm{diag}(4,1)$, $\Sigma_1=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}$ the flow map is $16.9\%$ more expensive than $W_2^2$ (§11, §8). Also: $\al,\be$ are not defined in Exercise 4, and the block names $\Sigma_{01}$ (lower-left) $=\mathrm{Cov}(X_1,X_0)$ are reversed with respect to the usual convention (consistent inside the sheet; reading them the other way changes formula (2) by $O(10)$ on a random example).
  • Sheet, Exercise 5. The Dirac question uses $\Sigma_1^k=0$, which is not positive definite as assumed; formula (2) still holds for $t<1$. Minor typography: “with $p_t^j$ is the Gaussian density”, and Exercise 1(iii) writes $v_t^c=\dots$ without the argument $(x)$.
  • OT4ML p. 407, “the least-squares formula (14.8)”. The flow-matching field (16.3) moves the path but is the least-energy field only when it is a gradient (true for a Gaussian endpoint with independent coupling, OT4ML p. 411). Check: for $\N(0,\mathrm{diag}(4,1))\to\N\big(0,\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}\big)$ its action is $2.6394$ against $2.5414$ for the least-energy field of the same path (§6).
  • Proofs of Benamou–Brenier. OT4ML (p. 331) and the compact notes use the superposition principle without stating it; StatOT’s proof (p. 150) simply assumes that the flow of the velocity exists (its restriction to absolutely continuous measures serves the second half of the proof, which uses Brenier’s map). Not errors; gaps to know (§2).
  • Conventions. OT4ML (14.7) has a factor $\frac12$ that (14.9) does not; the Hamilton–Jacobi constant is $\frac14$ without $\frac12$ and $\frac12$ with it (§3). OT4ML Props. 16.6–16.7 and the compact notes put the data at $t=0$, the sheet puts the noise at $t=0$ (§7). The sheet’s $\varphi_t$ is a flow, OT4ML’s $\varphi_t$ a dual potential.
  • Compact notes, cut sentences. p. 100 starts §14.1 with “At the particle level, this advection is governed by”, the advection never having been introduced (the book’s p. 328 has the missing sentences); p. 102 goes from (14.12) to “In particular, $\mathbb J(\al,\omega)<+\infty\iff\dots$”, dropping the book’s sentence (p. 331) that $J$ is lower semicontinuous, convex and 1-homogeneous — the point of the reformulation. p. 126, Prop. 16.2: the statement stops at the conditional expectation; the conclusion “the pair $(\al_t,v_t)$ satisfies the continuity equation” is only in the last line of the proof (it is in OT4ML Prop. 16.3).
  • CompOT §7.2–7.3. p. 109: the second momentum array is printed $J_1$ (it is $J_2$); p. 108: “$\theta:\to\R^+\cup\{+\infty\}$” lacks its domain and “from density to functions” should read “from densities to measures”; p. 111: index $\tilde J_{i,j}$ should be $\tilde J_{k,i}$ (§4).
  • CompOT (7.17), p. 115. $\int\mathcal L(\ga)^2\dd\bar\pi$ with $\mathcal L$ the kinetic energy: on a segment, $\mathcal L^2=\norm{x-y}^4$; drop the square (or use the length). “Respectively” is reversed in the next sentence (§9).
  • CompOT (7.19)–(7.21), pp. 116–117. Bridge variance $t(1-t)\eps^2$ and Fisher weight $\frac\eps4$ correspond to different noise levels ($\sigma=\eps^2$ would give $\frac{\eps^4}4$) (§9).
  • CompOT Remark 10.2, p. 165. $-\sum_{r\le s}\lambda_r\lambda_s\ps{x_r}{x_s}$ counts each cross term once instead of twice: same optimal plan, different value ($-3.1389$ versus the barycenter value $0.7963$ on the example of §10, related by an explicit affine formula).
