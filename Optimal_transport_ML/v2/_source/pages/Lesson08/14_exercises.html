More exercises

Twelve more exercises on the lecture, from ★ (direct application) to ★★★ (needs an idea). Five of them are exercises of StatOT (Chewi, Niles-Weed and Rigollet), quoted with their numbers; the others are ours. Every number was checked by a script.

Exercise 1 — For a linear potential, JKO is exact ★

Let $V(x)=\ps{c}{x}$ on $\R^d$ ($c$ fixed) and $f(\al)=\int V\dd\al$. Show that one JKO step from any $\al\in\Pc_2(\R^d)$ gives $\pf{(\Id-\tau c)}{\al}$, and compare with the gradient flow.

Hint

Write the JKO objective with a coupling $\pi$ of $\al$ and $\be$, and minimise the integrand in $y$ for each $x$.

Solution

For any $\be$ and any coupling $\pi$ of $(\al,\be)$, $\int\big(\frac{\abs{x-y}^2}{2\tau}+\ps cy\big)\dd\pi\ge\int\min_y\big(\frac{\abs{x-y}^2}{2\tau}+\ps cy\big)\dd\al(x)=\int\big(\ps cx-\frac\tau2\abs c^2\big)\dd\al(x)$, the minimum being at $y=x-\tau c$. Taking $\pi$ optimal, the JKO objective $\frac1{2\tau}W_2^2(\al,\be)+f(\be)$ is at least this bound, and $\be=\pf{(\Id-\tau c)}{\al}$ attains it (with the coupling $(x,x-\tau c)$). So one step translates $\al$ by $-\tau c$. The gradient flow has $\nabla_Wf=\nabla V=c$, i.e. $\dot x=-c$ for every particle: after $k$ steps the JKO scheme is exactly the flow at time $k\tau$. As in $\R^d$, implicit Euler, explicit Euler and the exact flow agree when the gradient is constant (§1).

Exercise 2 — From energies to PDEs ★

Write the first variation, the Wasserstein gradient and the PDE for (a) $f(\al)=\int V\dd\al+\sigma^2\int\rho\log\rho$; (b) $f(\al)=\frac12\iint\frac12\abs{x-y}^2\dd\al(x)\dd\al(y)$, and solve the flow; (c) $f(\rho\,\dd x)=\frac1{m-1}\int\rho^m\dd x$, $m>1$.

Hint

Use the table of §3. In (b), $\al$ appears in two slots, which cancels the factor $\frac12$ in front; the velocity depends on $\al$ only through its mean.

Solution

(a) $\delta f=V+\sigma^2(\log\rho+1)$, $\nabla_Wf=\nabla V+\sigma^2\nabla\log\rho$, and $\partial_t\rho=\dv(\rho\nabla V)+\sigma^2\Delta\rho$ (since $\rho\nabla\log\rho=\nabla\rho$): Fokker–Planck with temperature $\sigma^2$ (Example 15.10, §12).

(b) $\delta f(\al)(x)=\int\frac12\abs{x-y}^2\dd\al(y)$, $\nabla_Wf(\al)(x)=\int(x-y)\dd\al(y)=x-m$, with $m$ the mean of $\al$. Each particle moves by $\dot x=-(x-m_t)$; averaging, $\dot m=0$, so $x(t)=m+e^{-t}(x(0)-m)$ and $\al_t=\pf{\big(m+e^{-t}(\Id-m)\big)}{\al_0}$: a homothety of ratio $e^{-t}$ towards the mean, whatever the shape of $\al_0$ (compare the constant-kernel quiz of §11).

(c) $\delta f=\frac m{m-1}\rho^{m-1}$, $\rho\nabla\delta f=m\rho^{m-1}\nabla\rho=\nabla(\rho^m)$, so $\partial_t\rho=\Delta(\rho^m)$: the porous medium equation (OT4ML (15.8), p. 361). As $m\to1$, $\frac1{m-1}\int(\rho^m-\rho)\to\int\rho\log\rho$ and the equation becomes the heat equation.

Exercise 3 — Rotations are invisible (StatOT Ex. 5.10-1) ★
StatOT StatOT Ex. 5.10-1, p. 170

Let $X_0\sim\mu_0$, where $\mu_0$ is the standard Gaussian over $\R^2$. For $t\ge0$, let $X_t=R_tX_0$ where $R_t$ is a rotation by $\theta(t)$ radians. Compute the vector field $v_t$ such that $\dot X_t=v_t(X_t)$ and show that $\dv(\mu_0v_t)=0$ for all $t\ge0$ (and hence that $X_t\sim\mu_0$ for all $t\ge0$).

Hint

$\dot R_t=\dot\theta(t)JR_t$ with $J$ the rotation by $\frac\pi2$. Then $\dv(\mu v)=\ps{\nabla\mu}{v}+\mu\dv v$.

Solution

$\dot X_t=\dot R_tX_0=\dot\theta JR_tX_0$, so $v_t(x)=\dot\theta(t)Jx$. With $\mu_0(x)\propto e^{-\abs x^2/2}$: $\dv(\mu_0v_t)=\dot\theta\big(\ps{-x\mu_0}{Jx}+\mu_0\tr J\big)=0$, since $\ps{x}{Jx}=0$ and $\tr J=0$. So $\partial_t\mu+\dv(\mu v_t)=0$ is solved by the constant curve $\mu_t=\mu_0$, and indeed $R_tX_0\sim\mu_0$ (the standard Gaussian is rotation invariant). Moral: a curve of measures does not determine its velocity field: any $w$ with $\dv(\mu w)=0$ can be added. The Wasserstein velocity is the one of least kinetic energy, the gradient one (§3: tangent vectors are gradients) — here $0$. This is why trajectory inference from snapshots assumes gradient drifts (§12).

Exercise 4 — The Ornstein–Uhlenbeck flow in closed form ★★

On $\R$, solve the Fokker–Planck equation $\partial_t\rho=\partial_x(x\rho)+\partial_x^2\rho$ (the flow of $\KL(\cdot|\N(0,1))$) from $\N(m_0,s_0^2)$. Compute $\KL(\al_t|\N(0,1))$ for $m_0=2$, $s_0^2=0.25$ at $t=0.5,1,2$, and compare with $e^{-2t}\KL(\al_0|\N(0,1))$.

Hint

For a Gaussian, the velocity $-x-\partial_x\log\rho$ is affine in $x$ (§9). $\KL(\N(m,s^2)|\N(0,1))=\frac12(s^2+m^2-1-\log s^2)$.

Solution

For $\rho=\N(m,s^2)$, $v=-x-\partial_x\log\rho=-x+\frac{x-m}{s^2}$, affine, so the law stays Gaussian with $\dot m=\E[v]=-m$ and $\frac{\dot s}{s}=-1+\frac1{s^2}$ (the slope of $v$), i.e. $\frac{\dd}{\dd t}s^2=2-2s^2$:

$$m_t=m_0e^{-t},\qquad s_t^2=1+(s_0^2-1)e^{-2t}.$$

From $\N(2,0.25)$: $\KL_0=2.3181$; at $t=0.5$: $\N(1.2131,0.7241)$, $\KL=0.7592\le e^{-1}\KL_0=0.8528$; at $t=1$: $\N(0.7358,0.8985)$, $\KL=0.2734\le0.3137$; at $t=2$: $\KL=0.0367\le0.0425$. The bound is the log-Sobolev rate of §7 ($\N(0,1)$ satisfies LSI with constant $1$, so $\KL$ decays at least like $e^{-2t}$); the true rate is also $e^{-2t}$, set by the mean: $\KL_t=\frac12m_t^2+\frac12(s_t^2-1-\log s_t^2)$, where $\frac12m_t^2=2e^{-2t}$ while the variance part decays like $e^{-4t}$ (at $t=2$: $2e^{-4}=0.0366$ out of $0.0367$).

Exercise 5 — Convex energies converge at rate $1/t$ (StatOT Ex. 5.10-4) ★★
StatOT StatOT Ex. 5.10-4, p. 170

Let $\mathcal F:\Pc_{2,ac}(\R^d)\to\R\cup\{\infty\}$ be a geodesically convex functional which is minimized at $\pi$. Let $(\mu_t)_{t\ge0}$ denote the Wasserstein gradient flow for $\mathcal F$. By differentiating $t\mapsto2t\mathcal F(\mu_t)+W_2^2(\mu_t,\pi)$, prove $\mathcal F(\mu_t)-\inf\mathcal F\le\frac{W_2^2(\mu_0,\pi)}{2t}$.

Hint

Use $\nabla_W\big[W_2^2(\cdot,\pi)\big](\mu)=2(\Id-T_{\mu\to\pi})$ (sheet, Exercise 6) and the “above the tangent” form of geodesic convexity at $\mu_t$, along the geodesic to $\pi$: $\mathcal F(\pi)\ge\mathcal F(\mu_t)+\ps{\nabla_W\mathcal F(\mu_t)}{T-\Id}_{\mu_t}$.

Solution

Let $T=T_{\mu_t\to\pi}$, $g=\nabla_W\mathcal F(\mu_t)$, so the velocity is $-g$.

  1. $\frac{\dd}{\dd t}W_2^2(\mu_t,\pi)=\ps{2(\Id-T)}{-g}_{\mu_t}=2\ps{g}{T-\Id}_{\mu_t}\le2\big(\mathcal F(\pi)-\mathcal F(\mu_t)\big)$.Chain rule with the gradient of $W_2^2(\cdot,\pi)$; then geodesic convexity (tangent inequality).
  2. $\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\big[2t(\mathcal F(\mu_t)-\inf\mathcal F)\big]=2(\mathcal F(\mu_t)-\inf\mathcal F)-2t\norm{g}^2_{\mu_t}\le2(\mathcal F(\mu_t)-\inf\mathcal F)$.Dissipation identity $\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\mathcal F(\mu_t)=-\norm{g}^2$ (§4).
  3. $\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\big[2t(\mathcal F(\mu_t)-\inf\mathcal F)+W_2^2(\mu_t,\pi)\big]\le0$.Add lines 1–2; $\mathcal F(\pi)=\inf\mathcal F$.
  4. $2t(\mathcal F(\mu_t)-\inf\mathcal F)\le W_2^2(\mu_0,\pi)$.The bracket is non-increasing, equals $W_2^2(\mu_0,\pi)$ at $t=0$, and $W_2^2\ge0$.

Example. $\mathcal F=\int\frac{x^4}4\dd\mu$ on $\R$ (convex, not strongly: $V''(0)=0$), from $\delta_1$: the particle solves $\dot x=-x^3$, $x(t)=(1+2t)^{-1/2}$, $\pi=\delta_0$. At $t=10$: $\mathcal F=5.67\times10^{-4}\le\frac1{20}=0.05$. The bound holds but is far from tight here: the true decay is $\mathcal F\approx\frac1{16t^2}$ ($t^2\mathcal F=0.0619$ at $t=100$), as in §7.

Exercise 6 — The $\chi^2$ flow (StatOT Ex. 5.10-8a) ★★
StatOT StatOT Ex. 5.10-8a, p. 171

Compute the Wasserstein gradient of the chi-squared divergence $\chi^2(\cdot\,\|\,\pi)$ at $\mu$. Recall that $\chi^2(\mu\|\pi):=\int\frac{\dd\mu}{\dd\pi}\dd\mu-1$. Also, write down the equation for the Wasserstein gradient flow of the chi-squared divergence.

Hint

$\chi^2(\mu\|\pi)=\int\big(\frac{\dd\mu}{\dd\pi}\big)^2\dd\pi-1$ is the $\varphi$-divergence of the sheet’s Exercise 1 with $\varphi(s)=s^2-1$ (or $(s-1)^2$).

Solution

With $r=\frac{\dd\mu}{\dd\pi}$: perturbing $\mu$ by $\eps\chi$ changes $\int r^2\dd\pi$ by $2\eps\int r\,\frac{\dd\chi}{\dd\pi}\dd\pi=2\eps\int r\,\dd\chi$, so $\delta\chi^2(\cdot\|\pi)(\mu)=2\frac{\dd\mu}{\dd\pi}$ and

$$\begin{aligned}\nabla_W\chi^2(\cdot\|\pi)(\mu)&=2\nabla\frac{\dd\mu}{\dd\pi},\\ \partial_t\mu_t&=2\,\dv\Big(\mu_t\nabla\frac{\dd\mu_t}{\dd\pi}\Big).\end{aligned}$$

(The sheet’s Exercise 1(2)(a) with $\varphi'(s)=2s$.) With densities $\rho$ for $\mu_t$ and $p$ for $\pi$: $\partial_t\rho=2\dv\big(\rho\nabla(\rho/p)\big)$; for a constant $p$ (uniform reference on a box or torus), $\partial_t\rho=\frac1p\Delta(\rho^2)$, the porous medium equation with $m=2$ (Exercise 2(c)): the $\chi^2$ flow is a nonlinear diffusion, slow where $\rho$ is small. StatOT’s part (b) states that $\chi^2(\cdot\|\pi)$ is geodesically convex when $\pi$ is log-concave.

Exercise 8 — Mean shift on Gaussians ★★

On $\R$, let $\al=\N(m,v)$ (variance $v$). (a) Compute $M_\eps[\al](x)$ of (16.36). (b) Deduce that the continuous mean-shift flow keeps $\al_t$ Gaussian, with $m$ constant and $v$ solving an ODE; solve it. (c) For $\eps=0.1$, $v_0=1$: when does $v$ reach $0.01$?

Hint

$m_\eps[\al](x)$ is the mean of $y$ under the weights $K_\eps(x,y)\dd\al(y)\propto\N(y;x,\eps)\,\N(y;m,v)$: a product of two Gaussian densities in $y$ (or: Tweedie, §11).

Solution

(a) The product $\N(y;x,\eps)\N(y;m,v)$ is proportional to a Gaussian in $y$ with mean $\frac{vx+\eps m}{v+\eps}$. So $m_\eps[\al](x)=\frac{vx+\eps m}{v+\eps}$ and

$$M_\eps[\al](x)=\frac{\eps\,(m-x)}{v+\eps}.$$

(b) The velocity is affine with slope $-\frac\eps{v+\eps}$: Gaussianity is preserved (sheet, Exercise 3), $\dot m=\E[M_\eps]=0$, and $\dot v=-\frac{2\eps v}{v+\eps}$. Separating variables, $\big(1+\frac\eps v\big)\dd v=-2\eps\,\dd t$:

$$v_t+\eps\log v_t=v_0+\eps\log v_0-2\eps t .$$

Two regimes: while $v\gg\eps$ (a wide cloud, narrow kernel), $\dot v\approx-2\eps$: slow, linear shrinking; once $v\ll\eps$, $\dot v\approx-2v$: exponential collapse to the consensus point $m$.

(c) $t=\frac{v_0-v+\eps\log(v_0/v)}{2\eps}=\frac{0.99+0.1\log100}{0.2}=7.25$. Values on the way: $v=0.820$, $0.475$, $0.175$ at $t=1,3,5$.

A warning from the particles. Running the blurring dynamics (16.39) on $n=3000$ particles sampled from $\N(0,1)$ (explicit steps $0.01$), the empirical variance is $0.8228$, $0.4915$, $0.2330$ at $t=1,3,5$: it follows the Gaussian solution at first, then lags behind. The Gaussian solution is unstable: when $\rho_\eps\approx\rho$, $\partial_t\rho=-\dv(\rho\,\eps\nabla\log\rho_\eps)\approx-\eps\Delta\rho$ is a backward heat equation (the smoothing only tames the scales below the bandwidth $\sqrt\eps$), so the sampling fluctuations grow into bumps and isolated groups — the multimodal transient of §11. With more particles the departure comes later: at $t=5$ the variance is $0.277$, $0.233$, $0.198$ for $n=1000$, $3000$, $8000$ (Gaussian solution $0.175$); for $n=3000$ the histogram then has several bumps in the bulk, and 8 particles left behind near $x=-2.5$, too far to feel the rest of the cloud.

Exercise 9 — Two tokens on the circle ★★

Two tokens on the unit circle at angles $\vartheta_1,\vartheta_2$ follow the dynamics of the widget of §11. Show that the gap $\theta=\vartheta_2-\vartheta_1$ solves $\dot\theta=-\frac{2\sin\theta}{1+e^{(1-\cos\theta)/\eps}}$. What happens near $\theta=0$ and at $\theta=\pi$? Compute the speed at $\theta=\frac\pi2$ for $\eps=1$ and $\eps=0.1$.

Hint

Each token gives weight $e^{\cos0/\eps}$ to itself (with $\sin0=0$) and $e^{\cos\theta/\eps}$ to the other.

Solution

For token 1: $w_{12}=\frac{e^{\cos\theta/\eps}}{e^{1/\eps}+e^{\cos\theta/\eps}}=\frac1{1+e^{(1-\cos\theta)/\eps}}$, and $\dot\vartheta_1=w_{12}\sin\theta$; by symmetry $\dot\vartheta_2=-w_{12}\sin\theta$. Subtracting gives the formula. Near $0$: $\dot\theta\approx-\theta$ — once close, two tokens merge at rate $1$ whatever $\eps$. At $\theta=\pi$: $\sin\pi=0$, an equilibrium (unstable: a small perturbation grows). At $\theta=\frac\pi2$: speed $\frac2{1+e^{1/\eps}}=0.538$ for $\eps=1$ and $9.1\times10^{-5}$ for $\eps=0.1$; the time to go from $\frac\pi2$ to $0.01$ is $5.77$ and $1132$ (numerical integration; it is the last line of the widget’s readout).

Exercise 11 — The flow of $\frac12W_2^2$ and its clock (StatOT Ex. 5.10-11) ★★
StatOT StatOT Ex. 5.10-11, p. 172

Use (5.30) to show that if $(\mu_t)_{t\ge0}$ is the Wasserstein gradient flow of $\frac12W_2^2(\cdot,\nu)$, then $t\mapsto\mu_{1-\exp(-t)}$ is the constant-speed Wasserstein geodesic joining $\mu_0$ to $\nu$.

Hint

(5.30) is $\nabla_W\big[W_2^2(\cdot,\nu)\big](\mu)=2(\Id-T_{\mu\to\nu})$. Redo the sheet’s Exercise 6(3) with half the energy.

Solution

The velocity is $-\nabla_W\big[\frac12W_2^2\big]=T_{\mu\to\nu}-\Id$. With $\ga_s$ the McCann geodesic from $\mu_0$ to $\nu$ and the ansatz $\mu_t=\ga_{s(t)}$, the velocity of the curve is $\dot s\frac{T_s-\Id}{1-s}$ (sheet, Exercise 6, line 2), so $\dot s=1-s$, $s(t)=1-e^{-t}$:

$$\mu_t=\ga_{1-e^{-t}},\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad\ga_s=\mu_{-\log(1-s)},\ s\in[0,1).$$

The flow runs along the geodesic and $W_2(\mu_t,\nu)=e^{-t}W_2(\mu_0,\nu)$ ($1.1189$ at $t=1$ for $\N(0,1)\to\N(3,0.5^2)$).

Note on the sources — the time change in StatOT Ex. 5.10-11

The statement composes the time change the wrong way round: the curve that is the constant-speed geodesic is $s\mapsto\mu_{-\log(1-s)}$ (equivalently, $\mu_t=\ga_{1-e^{-t}}$), not $t\mapsto\mu_{1-\exp(-t)}$. Check: by the solution, $\mu_{1-e^{-t}}=\ga_{1-\exp(-(1-e^{-t}))}$; at $t=1$ this is $\ga_{0.4685}$, so this curve is not traversed at constant speed (its parameter is not $t$) and never reaches $\nu$ ($s\to1-e^{-1}=0.632$ as $t\to\infty$).

Exercise 12 — Log-Sobolev implies Talagrand: Otto–Villani (StatOT Ex. 5.10-5a) ★★★
StatOT StatOT Ex. 5.10-5a, p. 170

Let $\mathcal F:\Pc_{2,ac}\to\R\cup\{\infty\}$ be a functional. We saw that $\alpha$-strong convexity of $\mathcal F$ implies the Polyak–Łojasiewicz (PL) inequality $\norm{\nabla_W\mathcal F(\mu)}_\mu^2\ge2\alpha(\mathcal F(\mu)-\inf\mathcal F)$ (5.31). Show that (5.31) implies the quadratic growth inequality $\mathcal F(\mu)-\inf\mathcal F\ge\frac\alpha2W_2^2(\mu,\pi)$ (5.32), where $\pi$ is the minimizer of $\mathcal F$. This is known as the Otto–Villani theorem. Hint: Differentiate $t\mapsto\sqrt{\frac\alpha2}W_2(\mu_t,\mu_0)+\sqrt{\mathcal F(\mu_t)-\inf\mathcal F}$ along the Wasserstein gradient flow of $\mathcal F$. You may assume that the gradient flow converges to $\pi$ […].

Hint

Two facts: the metric speed of a curve is at most the norm of any velocity field that moves it, $\big|\frac{\dd}{\dd t}W_2(\mu_t,\mu_0)\big|\le\norm{v_t}_{\mu_t}$ (Benamou–Brenier); and $\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\mathcal F(\mu_t)=-\norm{\nabla_W\mathcal F}^2$ (§4).

Solution

Write $G=\nabla_W\mathcal F(\mu_t)$, $D_t=\mathcal F(\mu_t)-\inf\mathcal F$ (assume $D_t>0$; otherwise $\mu_t=\pi$ and we stop).

  1. $\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\sqrt{\frac\alpha2}W_2(\mu_t,\mu_0)\le\sqrt{\frac\alpha2}\norm{G}_{\mu_t}$.The velocity of the flow is $-G$; metric speed $\le$ kinetic norm.
  2. $\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\sqrt{D_t}=-\frac{\norm{G}^2}{2\sqrt{D_t}}\le-\frac{\norm{G}\sqrt{2\alpha D_t}}{2\sqrt{D_t}}=-\sqrt{\frac\alpha2}\norm{G}$.Dissipation, then PL in the form $\norm{G}\ge\sqrt{2\alpha D_t}$ applied to one factor $\norm{G}$.
  3. $t\mapsto\sqrt{\frac\alpha2}W_2(\mu_t,\mu_0)+\sqrt{D_t}$ is non-increasing; its value at $t=0$ is $\sqrt{D_0}$.Add lines 1–2.
  4. $\sqrt{\frac\alpha2}W_2(\pi,\mu_0)\le\sqrt{D_0}$, i.e. $\mathcal F(\mu_0)-\inf\mathcal F\ge\frac\alpha2W_2^2(\mu_0,\pi)$.Let $t\to\infty$: $\mu_t\to\pi$ and $D_t\to0$.

For $\mathcal F=\KL(\cdot|\pi)$, $\norm{\nabla_W\KL}^2$ is the relative Fisher information, PL is the log-Sobolev inequality and (5.32) is Talagrand’s inequality (§7): LSI implies Talagrand, with the same constant. Check with $\pi=\N(0,1)$ ($\alpha=1$), $\mu_0=\N(2,0.25)$: $\KL=2.3181\ge\frac12W_2^2=\frac12\big(4+(0.5-1)^2\big)=2.125$.
