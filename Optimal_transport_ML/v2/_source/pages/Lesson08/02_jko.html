2. The JKO scheme: an implicit step in $W_2$ OT4ML Def. 15.1, p. 356

A JKO step replaces the current measure by the best compromise between a lower energy and a small transport distance $W_2$; for a Dirac it is the Euclidean implicit step, for a Gaussian and the entropy it is one quadratic equation, and on the line it is a convex problem in the quantile function.

Why measure the step with $W_2$

There are two natural ways to go from a measure $\al$ to a nearby one.

Mixing (“vertical”)

$(1-\vartheta)\al+\vartheta\be$: mass disappears here and reappears there, without travelling. It is the linear structure of measures.

Moving (“horizontal”)

$\pf{(\Id+\tau v)}{\al}$: every bit of mass slides from $x$ to $x+\tau v(x)$. It is the transport structure, measured by $W_2$: $W_2^2\big(\al,\pf{(\Id+\tau v)}{\al}\big)\le\tau^2\int\norm{v}^2\dd\al$.

The energies of this lesson are decreased by moving mass: spreading a density (entropy), pulling samples towards data (MMD), sliding neurons towards useful features (§10). And $W_2$ heavily penalises mixing: from $\al=\delta_0$ to $(1-\vartheta)\delta_0+\vartheta\delta_1$ the distance is $\sqrt\vartheta$ (mass $\vartheta$ must travel a distance $1$), which is $0.1$ for $\vartheta=0.01$ — far more than the size $\vartheta$ of the change of weights. Measuring steps with $W_2$ therefore forces each step to be a small transport. (This is the phenomenon of Lesson 3, §6: the mixture path is infinitely long for $W_2$.)

The JKO step

In the course OT4ML Def. 15.1, p. 356

Definition 15.1 (JKO minimizing movement). Given $\tau>0$ and $\al^{(0)}$, define recursively

$$\al^{(\ell+1)}\in\argmin_{\al\in\Pc_2(\R^d)}\Big\{\frac1{2\tau}W_2(\al^{(\ell)},\al)^2+f(\al)\Big\}.\qquad(15.1)$$

The piecewise-constant interpolation $\al_\tau(t)=\al^{(\ell)}$ for $t\in[\ell\tau,(\ell+1)\tau)$ is called a minimizing movement.

$f(\al)$
The energy on measures that we want to decrease: an entropy, a KL divergence, an MMD to data… It may take the value $+\infty$ (the entropy of a point cloud).
$\al^{(\ell)}$
The current measure, after $\ell$ steps; it approximates the flow at time $t=\ell\tau$.
$\frac1{2\tau}W_2(\al^{(\ell)},\al)^2$
The price of moving from $\al^{(\ell)}$ to $\al$: the cheapest way to move the mass, in squared distance, divided by $2\tau$.
$\argmin$
Exactly the implicit Euler step of §1, with $\norm{x-y}$ replaced by $W_2$. Existence and uniqueness of the minimiser need assumptions on $f$: for instance convexity along generalised geodesics, a variant of the convexity of §7 under which the whole JKO objective is strictly convex (StatOT Exercise 5.10-9, pp. 171–172). We assume them freely.
$\al_\tau(t)$
The staircase curve “stay at $\al^{(\ell)}$ during $[\ell\tau,(\ell+1)\tau)$”. As $\tau\to0$ it converges (under assumptions) to a curve $\al_t$, the Wasserstein gradient flow of $f$ (§4).
JKO
Jordan, Kinderlehrer and Otto (1998), who introduced the scheme to show that the Fokker–Planck equation is the gradient flow of the free energy for $W_2$ (cited in CompOT p. 154 and StatOT p. 183).
Same step, three normalisations
  • OT4ML (above): $\frac1{2\tau}W_2^2+f$ — the step has length $\tau$ in time.
  • StatOT (6.14), p. 189: $\mathrm{prox}_{hH}(\mu)=\argmin_\nu\{hH(\nu)+\frac12W_2^2(\mu,\nu)\}$, “the proximal operator on the Wasserstein space” — the same step with $\tau=h$ (multiply OT4ML’s objective by $\tau$).
  • CompOT Remark 9.10, (9.27), p. 153: $\argmin_\al W_2(\al,\al^{(\ell)})^2+\tau F(\al)$ with “the time $t=\tau\ell$”. Dividing by $\tau$ gives $\frac1{2(\tau/2)}W_2^2+F$: a JKO step of size $\tau/2$, so the iterates follow the flow at time $\tau\ell/2$. See the note on the sources below.

First example: one Dirac and a potential energy

Take $f(\al)=\int V\,\dd\al$ (a potential energy: each bit of mass pays $V$ where it sits) and start from a single Dirac $\al^{(\ell)}=\delta_x$.

  1. $$W_2^2(\delta_x,\al)=\int\norm{x-y}^2\dd\al(y).$$The only coupling of $\delta_x$ and $\al$ sends all of $\al$’s mass from $x$: it is $\delta_x\otimes\al$.
  2. $$\frac1{2\tau}W_2^2(\delta_x,\al)+f(\al)=\int\Big(\frac{\norm{y-x}^2}{2\tau}+V(y)\Big)\dd\al(y).$$Both terms are integrals against $\al$.
  3. The minimiser is $\delta_{y^\star}$ with $y^\star\in\argmin_y\frac{\norm{y-x}^2}{2\tau}+V(y)$.The average of a function against a probability measure is at least its minimum, with equality when all the mass sits at a minimiser.

So from a Dirac, the JKO step is exactly the Euclidean implicit step of $h=V$ (this is the remark “restrict to $\al=\delta_x$” of OT4ML p. 356). With $V(y)=\frac12y^2$, $x=2$ and $\tau=0.5$: $y^\star=\frac{x}{1+\tau}=\frac43\approx1.333$, while the exact flow $\dot x=-x$ gives $2e^{-0.5}\approx1.213$ and the explicit step gives $1$ — the numbers of the quick check of §1. The mass does not spread: a potential energy moves each particle on its own (§4).

Second example: the heat equation, one Gaussian step by hand

Now take the neg-entropy $f(\al)=\int\rho\log\rho\,\dd x$ (and $+\infty$ if $\al$ has no density). It is small for spread-out densities, so its gradient flow should spread mass: we will see in §4 that it is the heat equation $\partial_t\rho=\Delta\rho$, whose solution from $\N(m,s^2)$ is $\N(m,s^2+2t)$. Let us run one JKO step from a Gaussian, on the line.

Reminder — $W_2$ between Gaussians on the line, and the Gaussian entropy

On $\R$, the optimal map from $\N(m,\sigma^2)$ to $\N(m',\sigma'^2)$ is the increasing affine map $x\mapsto m'+\frac{\sigma'}{\sigma}(x-m)$, so $W_2^2=(m-m')^2+(\sigma-\sigma')^2$: for $W_2$, the 1D Gaussians form a Euclidean half-plane of pairs $(m,\sigma)$ (Lesson 1). The neg-entropy of $\N(m,\sigma^2)$ is $\int\rho\log\rho=-\log\sigma-\frac12\log(2\pi e)$: it does not depend on $m$ and decreases when $\sigma$ grows. More: closed forms, entropy.

Proposition (JKO step of the entropy from a Gaussian; computed here)

On $\R$, the JKO step (15.1) for $f(\al)=\int\rho\log\rho$ from $\al^{(\ell)}=\N(m,\sigma_\ell^2)$ is $\al^{(\ell+1)}=\N(m,\sigma_{\ell+1}^2)$ with

$$\sigma_{\ell+1}=\frac{\sigma_\ell+\sqrt{\sigma_\ell^2+4\tau}}{2}\qquad\text{(the positive root of }\sigma^2-\sigma_\ell\,\sigma-\tau=0).$$
Idea

The minimiser is a Gaussian, and on Gaussians both terms are explicit: $W_2^2$ is the squared Euclidean distance between the pairs $(m,\sigma)$, and the neg-entropy is $-\log\sigma$ plus a constant. One derivative in $\sigma$ finishes the job.

Full derivation
  1. The minimiser is a Gaussian.Replacing a candidate $\al$ by the Gaussian $\mathrm{Gauss}(\al)$ with the same mean and variance does not increase $W_2^2(\al^{(\ell)},\cdot)$ (Gelbrich’s theorem, OT4ML Thm. 16.30, p. 439: equality between Gaussians, inequality in general) and does not increase the neg-entropy (the Gaussian has the largest entropy at given variance). This is the “Gaussian-preservation criterion” of OT4ML Thm. 16.31, p. 439. With a unique minimiser, it is Gaussian.
  2. $$\begin{aligned}&\frac1{2\tau}W_2^2\big(\N(m,\sigma_\ell^2),\N(m',\sigma^2)\big)+f\big(\N(m',\sigma^2)\big)\\&\qquad=\frac{(m'-m)^2+(\sigma-\sigma_\ell)^2}{2\tau}-\log\sigma-\tfrac12\log(2\pi e).\end{aligned}$$The 1D Gaussian formula $W_2^2=(m-m')^2+(\sigma-\sigma')^2$ (refresher) and $\int\rho\log\rho=-\log\sigma-\frac12\log(2\pi e)$ (refresher).
  3. $m'=m$: the mean does not move.Only the first term depends on $m'$.
  4. $$\frac{\sigma-\sigma_\ell}{\tau}-\frac1\sigma=0\iff\sigma^2-\sigma_\ell\,\sigma-\tau=0.$$Derivative in $\sigma$; the function of $\sigma$ is strictly convex on $(0,\infty)$ (a parabola plus $-\log$), so the zero of the derivative is the minimiser.
  5. $$\sigma_{\ell+1}=\tfrac12\big(\sigma_\ell+\sqrt{\sigma_\ell^2+4\tau}\big)=\sigma_\ell+\frac\tau{\sigma_\ell}+O(\tau^2).$$Positive root. Expanding $\sqrt{\sigma^2+4\tau}=\sigma\big(1+\frac{2\tau}{\sigma^2}-\frac{2\tau^2}{\sigma^4}+\dots\big)$.
  6. $\dfrac{\sigma_{\ell+1}-\sigma_\ell}{\tau}\to\dfrac1\sigma$, i.e. $\dfrac{\dd}{\dd t}\sigma^2=2$: the limit is $\sigma(t)^2=\sigma_0^2+2t$, the heat flow.$\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\sigma^2=2\sigma\dot\sigma=2$. Consistency of the scheme with the exact solution.

Halving the step halves the error at $t=1$: $0.0764$, $0.0414$, $0.0217$, $0.0111$ for $\tau=0.25$, $0.125$, $0.0625$, $0.03125$ — a first-order scheme, like the implicit Euler step. Do the same computation for the Fokker–Planck energy $f(\al)=\KL\big(\al\,|\,\N(0,1)\big)=\int\rho\log\rho+\int\frac{x^2}2\dd\al+\mathrm{const}$, from $\N(2,0.5^2)$ with $\tau=0.25$.

Answer

On $\N(m',\sigma^2)$ the potential term is $\frac12(m'^2+\sigma^2)$. Mean: $\frac{m'-m}{\tau}+m'=0$, so $m'=\frac{m}{1+\tau}=1.6$. Standard deviation: $\frac{\sigma-\sigma_\ell}\tau+\sigma-\frac1\sigma=0$, i.e. $(1+\tau)\sigma^2-\sigma_\ell\sigma-\tau=0$, so $\sigma=\frac{\sigma_\ell+\sqrt{\sigma_\ell^2+4\tau(1+\tau)}}{2(1+\tau)}=\frac{0.5+\sqrt{1.5}}{2.5}=0.6899$. The exact flow (§4) is at $\big(2e^{-0.25},\sqrt{1-0.75e^{-0.5}}\big)=(1.5576,\,0.7383)$.

On the line: a convex problem in the quantile function

For non-Gaussian data there is no closed form, but in one dimension the JKO step becomes a convex problem once the measure is described by its quantile function $Q=C^{-1}$ (Lesson 3 refresher).

  1. $$W_2^2(\al,\al^{(\ell)})=\int_0^1\big(Q(s)-Q_\ell(s)\big)^2\dd s.$$The 1D formula: the optimal plan matches quantiles.
  2. $$\rho\big(Q(s)\big)\,Q'(s)=1\qquad(0Differentiate $C(Q(s))=s$: $C'=\rho$. Mass $\dd s$ between the levels $s$ and $s+\dd s$ is spread over a length $Q'(s)\dd s$.
  3. $$\int\rho\log\rho\,\dd x=\int_0^1\log\rho\big(Q(s)\big)\,\dd s=-\int_0^1\log Q'(s)\,\dd s.$$Change of variables $x=Q(s)$, $\dd x=Q'(s)\dd s$, and $\rho(Q)Q'=1$; then line 2 again.
  4. $$\min_Q\ \int_0^1\Big(\frac{(Q-Q_\ell)^2}{2\tau}-\log Q'\Big)\dd s .$$The JKO step written in $Q$. It is strictly convex: $Q\mapsto Q'$ is linear and $-\log$ is convex; the constraint “$Q$ increasing” is enforced by the logarithm.

Particles. Put $N$ particles $x_1<\dots $$\begin{aligned}G(x)=\sum_{i=1}^N\frac{\big(x_i-x_i^{(\ell)}\big)^2}{2\tau}&-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\log\big(x_{i+1}-x_i\big)\\&\Big(+\sum_iV(x_i)\ \text{for Fokker–Planck}\Big).\end{aligned}$$

Its optimality condition, $\frac{x_i-x_i^{(\ell)}}{\tau}=\frac1{x_i-x_{i-1}}-\frac1{x_{i+1}-x_i}$, is the implicit Euler step of the particle system $\dot x_i=\frac1{x_i-x_{i-1}}-\frac1{x_{i+1}-x_i}$: a particle is pushed away from the side where its neighbour is closer, i.e. away from high density — diffusion seen from the particles. The Hessian of $G$ is tridiagonal, so each Newton iteration costs $O(N)$, and the step is solved for any $\tau$.

In machine learning — proximal steps on distributions

Sampling. To sample a posterior $\be\propto e^{-V}$, StatOT (§6.2.2, pp. 188–190) writes $\KL(\cdot|\be)=\int V\dd\al+\int\rho\log\rho+\mathrm{const}$ as “smooth + non-smooth” and proposes the proximal gradient algorithm on the Wasserstein space: a gradient step on the potential, then the JKO step (6.14) of the entropy. It converges fast, but “the proximal map (6.14) is in general intractable” (p. 190). The proximal sampler (StatOT §6.2.3, pp. 190–191) is a Gibbs sampler that keeps the spirit of the proximal step; its rates “exactly replicate the rates for the proximal point method from convex optimization”. Grids. On images or volumes, an entropic JKO step — $W_2^2$ replaced by its entropic version of Lesson 7 — is solved by generalised Sinkhorn iterations: “An entropic JKO step fixes the first marginal to the previous iterate and puts the energy on the second marginal” (OT4ML §8.6, p. 155, and Prop. 8.26).

Note on the sources — CompOT’s clock

CompOT Remark 9.10 (p. 153) uses the step $\argmin_\al W_2^2(\al,\al^{(\ell)})+\tau F(\al)$ and says that $\al^{(\ell)}$ approximates the flow at “the time $t=\tau\ell$”. For $p=2$ this step is the JKO step of size $\tau/2$, so the right time is $\tau\ell/2$. Check on one Dirac with $F(\al)=\int\frac{x^2}2\dd\al$: the step is $x\mapsto x/(1+\tau/2)$; with $\tau=0.01$ and $100$ steps, $x_{100}/x_0=1.005^{-100}=0.6073$, close to $e^{-1/2}=0.6065$ and far from $e^{-1}=0.3679$. The limit PDE (9.28) is right; only the clock is off by a factor 2.

Reflexes
  • JKO step from a Dirac and a potential energy → the Euclidean implicit step, and the mass stays a Dirac.
  • JKO step with Gaussian data → restrict to Gaussians (when Gaussians are preserved) and minimise over $(m,\sigma)$: $W_2^2$ is Euclidean in $(m,\sigma)$ on the line.
  • JKO step on the line → write it in quantiles: $W_2^2=\int(Q-Q_\ell)^2$, entropy $=-\int\log Q'$.
  • Compare normalisations before comparing clocks: $\frac1{2\tau}W_2^2+f$ (step $\tau$) versus $W_2^2+\tau F$ (step $\tau/2$).
