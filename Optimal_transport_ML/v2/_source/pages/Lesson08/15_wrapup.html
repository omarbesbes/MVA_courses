14. Summary of the lecture

The lecture on one screen
  1. The model (§1): $\dot x=-\nabla h(x)$; explicit Euler $x_{k+1}=x_k-\tau\nabla h(x_k)$ (stable only for $\tau<2/L$), implicit Euler $x_{k+1}=\argmin_x\frac1{2\tau}\norm{x-x_k}^2+h(x)$ (always stable, defined for non-smooth $h$: the proximal step).
  2. JKO (§2): replace $\norm{\cdot}$ by $W_2$: $\al_{k+1}\in\argmin_\al\frac1{2\tau}W_2^2(\al,\al_k)+f(\al)$. For the entropy it gives the heat equation; on the line, with quantiles, it is a convex problem.
  3. Derivatives (§3): first variation $\frac{\dd}{\dd\eps}f(\al+\eps\chi)=\int\delta f(\al)\dd\chi$ (perturb by mixing); Wasserstein gradient $\nabla_Wf=\nabla\delta f$ (perturb by moving); table: $\nabla V$, $\nabla\log\rho$, $\nabla W*\al$, $2(\Id-T)$.
  4. Limit PDE (§4): $\partial_t\al=\dv(\al\nabla\delta f(\al))$, with dissipation $\frac{\dd}{\dd t}f=-\int\norm{\nabla\delta f}^2\dd\al$: transport, heat, aggregation, Fokker–Planck.
  5. Particles (§5): the flow moves particles by $\dot x=-\nabla\delta f(\al_t)(x)$; for empirical measures this is gradient descent on the positions (time $\times n$); entropy needs densities or noise.
  6. Langevin (§6): $\dd X=-\nabla V\dd t+\sqrt2\dd B$ has laws following the Fokker–Planck flow of $\KL(\cdot|e^{-V})$; Euler–Maruyama is biased ($\frac1{1-h/2}$ for the variance of the Gaussian case).
  7. Rates (§7): $\lambda$-convexity along geodesics gives contraction $e^{-\lambda t}$ and exponential decay of $f$; PL/log-Sobolev gives $e^{-2\lambda t}$ without convexity; LSI ⇒ Talagrand ⇒ HWI chain; plain convexity gives $O(1/t)$.
  8. Discrepancy flows (§8): MMD = interaction (repulsion) + potential (attraction to data); energy distance has no bandwidth; $W_2$ and Sinkhorn give matching-based motions.
  9. Gaussians (§9): affine velocities keep Gaussians Gaussian; the flow becomes ODEs on $(m,\Sigma)$ — Bures–Wasserstein flows, Gaussian VI; SVGD replaces the score by a kernel average.
  10. Two-layer networks (§10): neurons are particles, the risk is a quadratic interaction plus a potential; global optimality when the neurons can explore every direction (Chizat–Bach, Mei–Montanari–Nguyen).
  11. Transformers (§11): residual attention = Euler step; Gaussian attention = mean shift $\eps\nabla\log\rho_\eps$; Markov averaging contracts the cloud by the Dobrushin coefficient $\le\tanh(D^2/(4\eps))$: consensus, after a long phase of clusters; softmax attention is not a Wasserstein gradient flow.
  12. Cells (§12): couplings between snapshots give soft ancestries and fates (entropic, unbalanced in practice); a gradient-flow model (landscape $V$ + diffusion) gives a dynamical law.

The reflexes to acquire

  • An energy of a measure → perturb by mixing for $\delta f$, take $\nabla$, write $\partial_t\al=\dv(\al\nabla\delta f)$.
  • $\al$ appears twice (interaction, MMD, risk of a network) → factor $2$, or both slots if the kernel is not symmetric.
  • A step size question → explicit Euler needs $\tau<2/L$; implicit (JKO, proximal) is always stable.
  • Particles or density? → particles for potentials and interactions; entropy needs a density, or noise (Langevin).
  • A velocity affine in $x$ → Gaussians stay Gaussian: write the ODEs for $(m,\Sigma)$.
  • A rate → is the energy $\lambda$-convex along geodesics? If not, is there a PL / log-Sobolev inequality? Only convex → $O(1/t)$.
  • Does the flow converge to a global minimiser? → convexity along mixtures plus full support (Prop. 15.51), or a certificate $g\equiv0$.
  • A dynamics that is not a gradient flow (softmax attention) → no energy to dissipate; look for a contraction (Dobrushin) instead.
  • Any formula of the lesson → test it on a Dirac, on two points, and on a Gaussian.

Notes on the sources (things that could confuse you)
  • CompOT’s clock (Rem. 9.10, p. 153): the step $\argmin W_2^2+\tau F$ is a JKO step of size $\tau/2$, so its iterates follow the flow at time $\tau\ell/2$, not $\tau\ell$. Check: $1.005^{-100}=0.6073\approx e^{-1/2}$, not $e^{-1}$ (§2).
  • CompOT (9.29), p. 154: $H(\al)=-\int\rho(\log\rho-1)$ is called “$\KL(\al|\mathcal L_{\R^d})$”; it is minus that relative entropy, $\KL(\al|\mathcal L)=\int\rho(\log\rho-1)+1$ (generalised KL for positive measures). Check: for $\N(0,s^2)$, $H=\frac12\log(2\pi es^2)+1$ increases as the Gaussian spreads, while a divergence to Lebesgue measure should decrease. The text then uses $F=-H$, the right energy for the heat flow. On the same page, the $L^2$ flow of the Dirichlet energy $\int\norm{\nabla\rho}^2$ is $\partial_t\rho=2\Delta\rho$ (the heat equation needs $\frac12\int\norm{\nabla\rho}^2$, as in OT4ML; check in Exercise 2).
  • StatOT p. 185: a stray “+” in a Taylor expansion (§6). StatOT p. 206: “maximizers” of the attention energy should be “minimizers”, and the unnormalised attention flow is that of $\frac12\mathcal F$ (§11). StatOT Ex. 5.10-11: the time change is reversed (Exercise 11).
  • Compact notes: Prop. 16.14 announces a table that is not printed (it is OT4ML Prop. 16.23, p. 433; §9); Example 15.10 (single cells), Example 15.13 (entropy gives the heat equation), Prop. 16.19 and Thm 16.20 (Dobrushin consensus) are only in the book.
  • The sheet: Exercise 2 has a sign slip ($\partial_t\rho=-\delta E$) and a missing factor $\rho$ in the Fisher information; the solution of Exercise 5 calls a degree-2 kernel “bilinear”; the solution of Exercise 6 drops the factor $\frac1{1-s}$ of the geodesic velocity (its final ODE is right). Checks in §13.
  • Naming. OT4ML calls the scheme “JKO” without citing the 1998 paper of Jordan, Kinderlehrer and Otto; StatOT cites it ([JKO98], p. 183).
  • Two conventions that change factors of 2: $\iint k\,\dd\al\dd\al$ versus $\frac12\iint W\dd\al\dd\al$ (§3), and the sheet’s $f=W_2^2$ versus StatOT’s $\frac12W_2^2$ (Exercise 6 vs Exercise 11): the curves are the same, the clocks differ by a factor $2$.
