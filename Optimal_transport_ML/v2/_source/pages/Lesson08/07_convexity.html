7. Displacement convexity and rates of convergence OT4ML §15.2–15.3, pp. 367–386

For Wasserstein flows the right notion of convexity is convexity along $W_2$ geodesics (McCann’s displacement convexity): convex potentials, convex interactions, the entropy and the KL to a log-concave target have it, data-fitting losses usually do not; it gives an $O(1/t)$ rate, $\lambda$-convexity gives $e^{-2\lambda t}$, and for the KL the underlying inequality is the logarithmic Sobolev inequality.

Which convexity?

In §1, convexity of $h$ gave rates. For energies on measures there are two candidate notions, and the obvious one is useless. Convexity along mixtures ($f((1-t)\al_0+t\al_1)\le(1-t)f(\al_0)+tf(\al_1)$) holds for every potential energy $\int V\dd\al$, since it is linear in $\al$ — even when $V$ has many local minima, where the flow (each particle sliding down $V$) obviously gets stuck. The flow does not travel along mixtures, it travels along transport paths, so convexity must be tested along $W_2$ geodesics, the McCann interpolations of Lesson 3. Between two Diracs the geodesic is $\delta_{(1-t)x+ty}$, on which $\int V\dd\al=V((1-t)x+ty)$: geodesic convexity of $\int V\dd\al$ is exactly convexity of $V$.

In the course OT4ML Def. 15.16, p. 367

Geodesics and convexity. A constant-speed $W_2$ geodesic between $\al_0$ and $\al_1$ is obtained […] from any optimal coupling $\pi^\star\in\U(\al_0,\al_1)$ by the McCann interpolation $\al_t=\pf{((1-t)P_0+tP_1)}{\pi^\star}$, $t\in[0,1]$, where $P_0(x,y)=x$ and $P_1(x,y)=y$. If the optimal plan is induced by a Brenier map $T$, this reduces to $\pf{((1-t)\Id+tT)}{\al_0}$.

Definition 15.16 (Geodesic convexity). A functional $f$ on $\Pc_2(\R^d)$ is geodesically convex if for every $W_2$ geodesic $(\al_t)_t$, $f(\al_t)\le(1-t)f(\al_0)+tf(\al_1)$. It is $\lambda$-geodesically convex if the right-hand side is improved by $-\frac\lambda2t(1-t)W_2^2(\al_0,\al_1)$.

$\al_t$
The displacement interpolation: every unit of mass travels on a straight line from $x$ to its partner $y$ (Lesson 3, §6). When there are several optimal plans there are several geodesics, and the definition asks for all of them.
$\lambda$-geodesically convex
$t\mapsto f(\al_t)$ lies below the chord by at least $\frac\lambda2t(1-t)W_2^2(\al_0,\al_1)$: it curves upwards like $\frac\lambda2\norm{x}^2$ along a segment of length $W_2(\al_0,\al_1)$. $\lambda>0$ is the analogue of strong convexity; $\lambda<0$ allows some concavity.
other names
Displacement convexity (McCann, 1997); “convexity along generalised geodesics” is a stronger variant (StatOT Exercise 5.10-9, p. 171).

Which energies are geodesically convex

In the course OT4ML Prop. 15.17, pp. 367–368

Proposition 15.17 (Geodesic convexity of linear and quadratic energies). 1. If $h$ is convex, then the linear energy $\al\mapsto\int h\,\dd\al$ is geodesically convex; if $h$ is $\lambda$-strongly convex, it is $\lambda$-geodesically convex. Conversely, if this linear energy is geodesically convex for all Dirac endpoints, then $h$ is convex. 2. […] In particular, the quadratic interaction $\al\mapsto\frac12\iint W(x,y)\dd\al(x)\dd\al(y)$ is geodesically convex when $W$ is convex on $\R^d\times\R^d$, and it is $\lambda$-geodesically convex when $W$ is $\lambda$-strongly convex there.

Proof of part 1 (OT4ML p. 368)
  1. Let $(X_0,X_1)\sim\pi^\star$ (optimal coupling of $\al_0,\al_1$) and $X_t=(1-t)X_0+tX_1$. Then $X_t\sim\al_t$.This is the definition of the McCann interpolation, read with random variables.
  2. $h(X_t)\le(1-t)h(X_0)+th(X_1)-\frac\lambda2t(1-t)\norm{X_0-X_1}^2$.$\lambda$-strong convexity of $h$ along the segment $[X_0,X_1]$ ($\lambda=0$ for plain convexity).
  3. $\int h\,\dd\al_t\le(1-t)\int h\,\dd\al_0+t\int h\,\dd\al_1-\frac\lambda2t(1-t)W_2^2(\al_0,\al_1)$.Take expectations; $\E\norm{X_0-X_1}^2=W_2^2(\al_0,\al_1)$ because the coupling is optimal.
  4. Conversely, the geodesic between $\delta_x$ and $\delta_y$ is $\delta_{(1-t)x+ty}$, on which the inequality reads $h((1-t)x+ty)\le(1-t)h(x)+th(y)$.Test the definition on Dirac endpoints.

Part 2 is the same argument with two independent copies of the optimal coupling, one for each variable of $W$.

Internal energies need another mechanism. Along a geodesic the density is not an average of the end densities: mass is compressed or stretched by the Jacobian of the map. McCann’s theorem says when this works in our favour.

In the course OT4ML Thm. 15.19, pp. 369–370

Theorem 15.19 (McCann displacement convexity for internal energies). Let $g:[0,+\infty)\to\R\cup\{+\infty\}$ be convex with $g(0)=0$, and define $\psi(r)=r^dg(r^{-d})$, $r>0$. If $\psi$ is convex and nonincreasing on $(0,+\infty)$, then the internal energy $\mathcal U_g(\al)=\int g(\rho(x))\dd x$ if $\al=\rho\dd x$ ($+\infty$ otherwise) is geodesically convex on $\Pc_2(\R^d)$.

[…] The theorem applies to $g(s)=s\log s$, since then $\psi(r)=-d\log r$, to porous-medium powers $g(s)=s^m/(m-1)$, $m>1$, and more generally to the fast-diffusion range $m\ge1-1/d$ […].

Idea of the proof

Write the energy at time $t$ as an integral over the starting points: $\int g(\rho_t)=\int\rho_0(x)\,\psi\big(s_t(x)\big)\dd x$ where $s_t(x)^d$ is the volume occupied, per unit mass, by the mass that started at $x$. Volumes grow at least linearly along the interpolation ($\det^{1/d}$ is concave on positive semidefinite matrices), and $\psi$ is non-increasing and convex.

Full proof (smooth case, OT4ML pp. 369–370)
  1. Let $T=\nabla\varphi$ be the Brenier map from $\al_0=\rho_0\dd x$ to $\al_1=\rho_1\dd x$, $T_t=(1-t)\Id+tT$, $J_t(x)=\det\big((1-t)\Id+t\nabla T(x)\big)$. Then $\rho_t(T_t(x))J_t(x)=\rho_0(x)$.Change of variables for $\al_t=\pf{(T_t)}{\al_0}$ (the Monge–Ampère equation along the interpolation).
  2. $J_t(x)^{1/d}\ge(1-t)+t\det(\nabla T(x))^{1/d}$.$\nabla T=\nabla^2\varphi$ is symmetric positive semidefinite, and $M\mapsto\det(M)^{1/d}$ is concave on such matrices (Minkowski’s determinant inequality).
  3. With $s_t(x)=\big(J_t(x)/\rho_0(x)\big)^{1/d}$: $s_t(x)\ge(1-t)\rho_0(x)^{-1/d}+t\rho_1(T(x))^{-1/d}$.Divide line 2 by $\rho_0^{1/d}$ and use $\rho_1(T(x))\det\nabla T(x)=\rho_0(x)$ (line 1 at $t=1$).
  4. $\int g(\rho_t(y))\dd y=\int\rho_0(x)\,\psi(s_t(x))\,\dd x$.Change of variables $y=T_t(x)$, $\dd y=J_t\dd x$, and $g(\rho_t)J_t=\rho_0\,\frac{J_t}{\rho_0}g\big(\frac{\rho_0}{J_t}\big)=\rho_0\,\psi(s_t)$ by definition of $\psi$.
  5. $\psi(s_t)\le\psi\big((1-t)\rho_0^{-1/d}+t\rho_1(T)^{-1/d}\big)\le(1-t)\psi(\rho_0^{-1/d})+t\psi(\rho_1(T)^{-1/d})$.$\psi$ non-increasing and line 3, then $\psi$ convex.
  6. $\mathcal U_g(\al_t)\le(1-t)\,\mathcal U_g(\al_0)+t\,\mathcal U_g(\al_1)$. $\square$Multiply line 5 by $\rho_0$, integrate, and read line 4 at $t=0$ and $t=1$ (at $t=1$, change variables with $T$).

For $g=s\log s$: $\psi(r)=r^d\cdot r^{-d}\log r^{-d}=-d\log r$, convex and decreasing. ✓

In the course OT4ML Prop. 15.20 (3), p. 370

In the KL case $m=1$, if $V$ is $\lambda$-strongly convex, then $\al\mapsto\KL(\al|\be)$ [with $\be=Z^{-1}e^{-V}\dd x$] is $\lambda$-geodesically convex. […] Finally, for $m=1$, $\KL(\al|\be)=\int\rho\log\rho\,\dd x+\int V\dd\al+\log Z$. The entropy term is 0-geodesically convex by Theorem 15.19. So: the KL divergence to a $\lambda$-strongly log-concave target is $\lambda$-geodesically convex (StatOT Cor. 6.4, p. 176). For $\N(0,1)$, $V=\frac12x^2$ and $\lambda=1$.

What is not geodesically convex

In the course OT4ML Ex. 15.23, Rem. 15.24, p. 372

Example 15.23 (Squared Wasserstein distance need not be geodesically convex). […] In $\Pc_2(\R^d)$, $d\ge2$, take $\be=\frac12\delta_{(-1/2,-1/2)}+\frac12\delta_{(1/2,1/2)}$, $\al_0=\frac12\delta_{(-1,0)}+\frac12\delta_{(1,0)}$, $\al_1=\frac12\delta_{(0,-1)}+\frac12\delta_{(0,1)}$. One optimal coupling between $\al_0$ and $\al_1$ matches $(-1,0)$ with $(0,1)$ and $(1,0)$ with $(0,-1)$. The associated geodesic has midpoint $\al_{1/2}=\frac12\delta_{(-1/2,1/2)}+\frac12\delta_{(1/2,-1/2)}$. Then $W_2^2(\al_0,\be)=W_2^2(\al_1,\be)=\frac12$, $W_2^2(\al_{1/2},\be)=1$, which violates geodesic convexity.

Remark 15.24 (Target discrepancies are usually semiconcave). […] Losses of the form $\al\mapsto D(\al,\be)$, where $\be$ is a fixed target distribution, are rarely geodesically convex in the transport geometry. […] The squared Wasserstein loss has the opposite one-sided curvature: it is 2-geodesically semiconcave: $W_2^2(\al_t,\be)\ge(1-t)W_2^2(\al_0,\be)+tW_2^2(\al_1,\be)-t(1-t)W_2^2(\al_0,\al_1)$.

Checks with POT: both matchings between $\al_0$ and $\al_1$ cost $2$ (all four distances are $\sqrt2$), so $W_2^2(\al_0,\al_1)=2$ and there are two geodesics; along the other one the midpoint is $\be$ itself. The semiconcavity bound at $t=\frac12$ is $\frac12\cdot\frac12+\frac12\cdot\frac12-\frac14\cdot2=0\le1$. ✓ On the line, by contrast, $W_2^2(\cdot,\be)=\int(Q-Q_\be)^2$ is convex along geodesics, which are linear in the quantile function. The same failure happens for $\mathrm{MMD}^2$ with Gaussian or Laplace kernels (OT4ML p. 368), and for the risk of a neural network as a function of its neurons (§10): the losses of machine learning are generally not displacement convex, which is why §10 needs other arguments.

Rates: $O(1/t)$, Polyak–Łojasiewicz, $e^{-2\lambda t}$

In the course OT4ML Prop. 15.26, p. 374

Proposition 15.26 (Energy decay for convex Wasserstein flows). Assume formally that $f$ is geodesically convex, admits a smooth first variation, and has a minimizer $\al^\star$. Let $(\al_t)_t$ be a smooth solution of the Wasserstein gradient flow $\partial_t\al_t+\dv(\al_tv_t)=0$, $v_t=-\nabla_Wf(\al_t)$. Then $\frac{\dd}{\dd t}f(\al_t)=-\int\norm{\nabla_Wf(\al_t)(x)}^2\dd\al_t(x)\le0$. If $T_t$ is the optimal map from $\al_t$ to $\al^\star$, then $f(\al_t)-f(\al^\star)\le-\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\frac12W_2^2(\al_t,\al^\star)$, and consequently

$$f(\al_t)-f(\al^\star)\le\frac{W_2^2(\al_0,\al^\star)}{2t}.$$
Idea

The proof of fact (2) of §1, with the segment $[x_t,x^\star]$ replaced by the geodesic from $\al_t$ to $\al^\star$: convexity along it bounds the energy gap by the derivative of $\frac12W_2^2(\al_t,\al^\star)$, which integrates.

Full proof (OT4ML p. 374)
  1. $f(\al^\star)-f(\al_t)\ge\int\ps{\nabla_Wf(\al_t)(x)}{T_t(x)-x}\dd\al_t(x)$.Convexity along the geodesic $\pf{((1-s)\Id+sT_t)}{\al_t}$, $s\in[0,1]$: the convex function $s\mapsto f(\al_s)$ lies above its tangent at $s=0$, whose slope is given by Proposition 15.7 with $v=T_t-\Id$.
  2. $f(\al_t)-f(\al^\star)\le\int\ps{v_t(x)}{T_t(x)-x}\dd\al_t(x)$.Line 1 with $v_t=-\nabla_Wf(\al_t)$.
  3. $\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\frac12W_2^2(\al_t,\al^\star)=\int\ps{x-T_t(x)}{v_t(x)}\dd\al_t(x)$.The first variation of $\frac12W_2^2(\cdot,\al^\star)$ has gradient $\Id-T_t$ (§3), and the chain rule along the flow (StatOT Ex. 5.10-3 proves the corresponding inequality).
  4. $f(\al_t)-f(\al^\star)\le-\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\frac12W_2^2(\al_t,\al^\star)$.Lines 2 and 3.
  5. $t\big(f(\al_t)-f^\star\big)\le\int_0^t\big(f(\al_s)-f^\star\big)\dd s\le\frac12W_2^2(\al_0,\al^\star)$.Integrate line 4 on $[0,t]$ (drop $-\frac12W_2^2(\al_t,\al^\star)\le0$), and use that $s\mapsto f(\al_s)$ is non-increasing.

Example. $f(\al)=\frac14\int x^4\dd\al$ on $\R$ is geodesically convex ($\frac14x^4$ is convex) but not $\lambda$-convex for any $\lambda>0$ ($\frac{\dd^2}{\dd x^2}\frac{x^4}4=3x^2$ vanishes at $0$). From $\delta_1$, the particle follows $\dot x=-x^3$, so $x(t)=(1+2t)^{-1/2}$ and $f(\al_t)=\frac1{4(1+2t)^2}$; the bound is $\frac{W_2^2(\delta_1,\delta_0)}{2t}=\frac1{2t}$. At $t=10$: $5.67\times10^{-4}\le0.05$. The true rate is $t^{-2}$ ($t^2f\to\frac1{16}$; $0.0619$ at $t=100$), the rate predicted by the Łojasiewicz exponent of OT4ML Remark 15.34 (p. 378).

In the course OT4ML Def. 15.27, Thm. 15.28, pp. 374–375

Definition 15.27 (Wasserstein–Polyak–Łojasiewicz inequality). Let $f_\star:=\inf f>-\infty$. The functional $f$ satisfies the Wasserstein–PL inequality with constant $\kappa>0$ if $\abs{\partial f}^2(\al)\ge2\kappa\big(f(\al)-f_\star\big)$ for every $\al$ in the domain of $f$. In the smooth Otto notation this reads $\int\norm{\nabla_Wf(\al)}^2\dd\al\ge2\kappa\big(f(\al)-f_\star\big)$.

Theorem 15.28 (Wasserstein–PL convergence). Assume that $f_\star>-\infty$, that $f$ satisfies the Wasserstein–PL inequality (15.24) with constant $\kappa>0$, and that $(\al_t)_{t\ge0}$ is a global Wasserstein gradient flow satisfying the full energy-dissipation identity $\frac{\dd}{\dd t}f(\al_t)=-\abs{\partial f}^2(\al_t)$. Set $E(t):=f(\al_t)-f_\star$. Then, for $0\le s\le t$, $E(t)\le e^{-2\kappa(t-s)}E(s)$ […]. If, in addition, $f$ is lower semicontinuous for $W_2$-convergence, then $\al_t$ converges in $W_2$ to a minimizer $\al_\infty$ […] and $W_2(\al_t,\al_\infty)\le\sqrt{2/\kappa}\,\sqrt{E(0)}\,e^{-\kappa t}$.

The energy part is two lines, exactly as in §1: $E'(t)=-\int\norm{\nabla_Wf}^2\dd\al_t\le-2\kappa E(t)$ (dissipation, then PL), and Grönwall gives $E(t)\le e^{-2\kappa t}E(0)$. The distance part integrates the speed: the length of the remaining path is at most $\int_t^\infty\frac{-E'}{\sqrt{2\kappa E}}=\sqrt{2/\kappa}\sqrt{E(t)}$ (OT4ML p. 375).

In the course OT4ML Prop. 15.29, p. 376

Proposition 15.29 (Strong geodesic convexity implies Wasserstein–PL). Assume that $f$ is $\lambda$-geodesically convex with $\lambda>0$, that $f_\star$ is attained at some $\al^\star$, and that the Wasserstein slope is finite at $\al$. Then $\abs{\partial f}^2(\al)\ge2\lambda\big(f(\al)-f_\star\big)$.

Proof (OT4ML p. 376)
  1. Let $g=f(\al)-f_\star$, $r=W_2(\al,\al^\star)$, $a=\abs{\partial f}(\al)=\norm{\nabla_Wf(\al)}_{L^2(\al)}$, and $(\al_s)$ a geodesic from $\al$ to $\al^\star$.Notation.
  2. $f(\al)-f(\al_s)\ge sg+\frac\lambda2s(1-s)r^2$.$\lambda$-convexity: $f(\al_s)\le(1-s)f(\al)+sf_\star-\frac\lambda2s(1-s)r^2$.
  3. $a\ge\frac gr+\frac\lambda2r$.Divide line 2 by $W_2(\al,\al_s)=sr$ and let $s\downarrow0$: the left side tends to (at most) the slope $a$.
  4. $g\le ar-\frac\lambda2r^2\le\max_{r\ge0}\big(ar-\frac\lambda2r^2\big)=\frac{a^2}{2\lambda}$.Rearrange line 3, then maximise the parabola in $r$ (at $r=a/\lambda$). This is the PL inequality with $\kappa=\lambda$.

Conclusion (OT4ML p. 376): “Proposition 15.29 and Theorem 15.28 recover the exponential energy rate $e^{-2\lambda t}$ for positively geodesically convex Wasserstein gradient flows, and also give the distance rate $e^{-\lambda t}$ toward the minimizer.”

Entropy, Fisher information and $W_2$: log-Sobolev, Talagrand, HWI

For $f=\KL(\cdot|\be)$, the squared slope is a famous quantity, the relative Fisher information, and the PL inequality becomes a famous inequality.

In the course OT4ML Def. 15.39, Rem. 15.41, pp. 380–381

Definition 15.39 (Relative Fisher information and logarithmic Sobolev inequality). Let $\be\in\Pc(\R^d)$ have a positive density. For a probability measure $\al=h\be$, define $\mathcal I(\al|\be):=4\int\norm{\nabla\sqrt h}^2\dd\be=\int\norm{\nabla\log h}^2\dd\al$ […]. The measure $\be$ satisfies a logarithmic Sobolev inequality with constant $\lambda>0$ if $\KL(\al|\be)\le\frac1{2\lambda}\mathcal I(\al|\be)$ for every probability measure $\al$. (15.27)

Remark 15.41 (Logarithmic Sobolev as Wasserstein–PL). […] for $f(\al)=\KL(\al|\be)$ the Wasserstein gradient is $\nabla_Wf(\al)=\nabla\log\frac{\dd\al}{\dd\be}$, and its squared Wasserstein slope is precisely the relative Fisher information, $\abs{\partial f}^2(\al)=\mathcal I(\al|\be)$. Since the unique minimizer is $\be$ and $f(\be)=0$, (15.27) is exactly $\abs{\partial f}^2(\al)\ge2\lambda\big(f(\al)-f(\be)\big)$, that is, the Wasserstein–PL inequality of Definition 15.27.

In the course OT4ML Props. 15.40, 15.42, 15.49, 15.50

Proposition 15.40 (p. 381). Let $\be=Z^{-1}e^{-V}\dd x$ be a smooth probability measure on $\R^d$. If $\nabla^2V\succeq\lambda\Id$ for some $\lambda>0$, then $\be$ satisfies (15.27) with constant $\lambda$.

Proposition 15.42 (p. 381). Let $\be=e^{-V}\dd x/Z$ satisfy the logarithmic Sobolev inequality (15.27) with constant $\lambda>0$. Let $\al_t$ solve the Wasserstein gradient flow of $f(\al)=\KL(\al|\be)$, namely $\partial_t\rho_t=\nabla\cdot(\rho_t\nabla V)+\Delta\rho_t$. If $\KL(\al_0|\be)<+\infty$, then $\KL(\al_t|\be)\le e^{-2\lambda t}\KL(\al_0|\be)$. Moreover, if $\al_0,\be\in\Pc_2(\R^d)$, then $W_2(\al_t,\be)\le\sqrt{\frac2\lambda\KL(\al_0|\be)}\,e^{-\lambda t}$.

Proposition 15.49 (Gaussian $T_2$ inequality, p. 385). For every $\al\in\Pc_2(\R^d)$, $\frac12W_2^2(\al,\gamma_d)\le\KL(\al|\gamma_d)$.

Proposition 15.50 (Otto–Villani HWI inequality, p. 385). Let $\be=\rho_\be\dd x=e^{-V}\dd x/Z$ […] and assume that $\nabla^2V\succeq\lambda\Id$ for some $\lambda\in\R$. […] Then $\KL(\al|\be)\le W_2(\al,\be)\sqrt{\mathcal I(\al|\be)}-\frac\lambda2W_2^2(\al,\be)$.

$H,\ W,\ I$
Entropy $\KL(\al|\be)$, distance $W_2(\al,\be)$, Fisher information $\mathcal I(\al|\be)$: the energy, the distance to the minimiser, and the squared slope — the three quantities of every convergence proof.
log-Sobolev (LSI)
$H\le\frac I{2\lambda}$: the PL inequality of the KL. It gives the exponential decay of the entropy along Fokker–Planck (and Langevin) flows, at rate $2\lambda$.
Talagrand ($T_2$)
$\frac12W^2\le H$ for the standard Gaussian $\gamma_d$: the entropy controls the transport distance (“quadratic growth”). In general LSI with constant $\lambda$ implies $W_2^2\le\frac2\lambda\KL$ (Otto–Villani, p. 385).
HWI
One inequality linking the three letters; it contains LSI as a corollary.
Proof of HWI, and LSI from it (OT4ML p. 386)
  1. Let $T$ be the Brenier map from $\al$ to $\be$, $v=T-\Id$, $\al_t=\pf{((1-t)\Id+tT)}{\al}$, $e(t)=\KL(\al_t|\be)$.The geodesic from $\al$ to the minimiser.
  2. $0=e(1)\ge e(0)+e'(0)+\frac\lambda2W_2^2(\al,\be)$.$\KL(\cdot|\be)$ is $\lambda$-geodesically convex (Prop. 15.20), so $e$ is $\lambda W_2^2$-convex on $[0,1]$: it lies above its tangent plus the quadratic term; $e(1)=\KL(\be|\be)=0$.
  3. $e'(0)=\int\ps{\nabla\log\frac{\rho_\al}{\rho_\be}}{T-\Id}\dd\al\ge-\sqrt{\mathcal I(\al|\be)}\;W_2(\al,\be)$.Proposition 15.7 with velocity $v=T-\Id$; Cauchy–Schwarz in $L^2(\al)$ with $\int\norm{T-\Id}^2\dd\al=W_2^2(\al,\be)$.
  4. $\KL(\al|\be)=e(0)\le W\sqrt I-\frac\lambda2W^2$.Lines 2–3. This is HWI.
  5. For $\lambda>0$: $W\sqrt I-\frac\lambda2W^2\le\max_{r\ge0}\big(r\sqrt I-\frac\lambda2r^2\big)=\frac I{2\lambda}$.Maximise the parabola (at $r=\sqrt I/\lambda$). This is LSI: Proposition 15.40 via HWI.
  6. $\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\KL(\al_t|\be)=-\mathcal I(\al_t|\be)\le-2\lambda\KL(\al_t|\be)$, hence $\KL(\al_t|\be)\le e^{-2\lambda t}\KL(\al_0|\be)$.Dissipation along the Fokker–Planck flow (§4), LSI, Grönwall: Proposition 15.42.

In machine learning — when sampling and variational inference provably converge

For Bayesian logistic regression with labels $y_i\in\{\pm1\}$, features $z_i$ and a Gaussian prior $\N(0,s^2\Id)$, the negative log-posterior is $V(\theta)=\sum_i\log\big(1+e^{-y_i\ps{\theta}{z_i}}\big)+\frac{\norm{\theta}^2}{2s^2}$; the logistic terms are convex, so $\nabla^2V\succeq\frac1{s^2}\Id$. The posterior is $\frac1{s^2}$-strongly log-concave, satisfies LSI with $\lambda=\frac1{s^2}$, and the Langevin diffusion converges in KL like $e^{-2t/s^2}$ (Proposition 15.42, StatOT Cor. 6.14), whatever the data. The same constant governs Gaussian variational inference (StatOT Cor. 6.6, §9). Conversely, multimodal posteriors (mixture models, neural networks) have tiny LSI constants: the double well of the Langevin widget (§6) already needs $t\approx4$–$8$ to balance its two wells, and the time grows exponentially with the barrier height.

Pitfall — convex along mixtures is not convex along geodesics

$\mathrm{MMD}^2(\cdot,\be)$ and the risk of a two-layer network are convex along mixtures (they are quadratic in $\al$ with a positive kernel), but not along $W_2$ geodesics; conversely the entropy is convex along both. The rates of this section need the second notion. Mixture convexity is still useful: it is the key to the global optimality results of §10.

Reflexes
  • Geodesic convexity of $\int V$ ⇔ convexity of $V$ (test on Diracs); of $\frac12\iint W$ ⇐ convexity of $W$; entropy: always (McCann); KL to $e^{-V}$: $\lambda$-convex if $\nabla^2V\succeq\lambda\Id$.
  • Rate for a Wasserstein flow → $O(1/t)$ from convexity, $e^{-2\lambda t}$ from $\lambda$-convexity via PL; for KL, PL is log-Sobolev.
  • Gaussian test case → $W$, $H$, $I$ are explicit in $(m,s)$: check every inequality numerically.

Is $f(\al)=\frac12\iint\norm{x-y}^2\dd\al\dd\al$ $\lambda$-geodesically convex for some $\lambda>0$?

Answer

It is geodesically convex ($W(x,y)=\norm{x-y}^2$ is convex on $\R^d\times\R^d$), but not $\lambda$-convex for any $\lambda>0$: translating a measure along $\pf{(\Id+tv)}{\al}$ is a geodesic of length $\norm v$ along which $f$ is constant (it only depends on differences $x-y$). Strong convexity would force a strict increase above the chord. (StatOT Exercise 6.8-1, p. 209.) Its flow still converges exponentially, but to a Dirac at the mean — the minimisers form a whole family, one per mean.
