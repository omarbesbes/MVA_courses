11. Summary of the lesson

The lesson on one screen
  1. Discrete dual (§1): $\mathrm L_C(a,b)=\max\{\ps{f}{a}+\ps{g}{b}:\ f_i+g_j\le C_{ij}\}$. Weak duality is one line (marginals + $P\ge0$); prices are a lower bound, plans an upper bound. Complementary slackness: optimal plans only use contacts $f_i+g_j=C_{ij}$. Prices are defined up to a gauge, unique for non-degenerate plans, and they are the gradient of $a\mapsto\mathrm L_C(a,b)$.
  2. General duality (§2): $\Lc(\al,\be)=\max_{f\oplus g\le c}\int f\,\dd\al+\int g\,\dd\be$ on compact spaces (separation + $c$-transforms + Arzelà–Ascoli). Optimal plans live in the zero set of the slack $c-f\oplus g$. On the line $f'=2(x-T(x))$; for Gaussians the slack is $(y-T(x))^2/\sigma$.
  3. $c$-transforms (§3): $f^c(y)=\inf_xc(x,y)-f(x)$, the best response, a lower envelope; one potential is enough (semi-dual, concave, unconstrained); transforms inherit the modulus of $c$; $f\le f'\Rightarrow f^c\ge f'^c$, $f^{c\bar c}\ge f$, $f^{c\bar cc}=f^c$, so alternating stalls after one round.
  4. Brenier (§4): for $c=\frac12\norm{x-y}^2$, $f^c=\frac12\norm{y}^2-\varphi^*$ with $\varphi=\frac12\norm{x}^2-f$; optimal potentials give a convex $\varphi$ and plans on $\{y\in\partial\varphi(x)\}$; a density for $\al$ makes $\partial\varphi=\{\nabla\varphi\}$ a.e.: unique optimal plan $\pf{(\Id,\nabla\varphi)}{\al}$.
  5. $W_1$ (§5): for $c=d$, $c$-concave $=$ 1-Lipschitz and $f^c=-f$, so $W_1=\max_{\Lip f\le1}\int f\,\dd(\al-\be)$, a norm. On the line $W_1=\int\abs{C_\al-C_\be}$ with witness slope $-\mathrm{sign}(C_\al-C_\be)$; on graphs a min-cost flow (Beckmann). Compactness of the Lipschitz ball completes “$W_p$ metrizes weak convergence”.
  6. Divergences (§6): dual norms ($\TV$, $W_1$, flat, MMD) vs $\varphi$-divergences (KL, JS, TV). KL is infinite and JS, TV saturate on disjoint supports; Pinsker $\norm{\cdot}_\TV^2\le2\KL$; $W_1\le\frac{\diam}2\norm{\cdot}_\TV$. GANs learn the test function: vanilla ≈ $2\mathrm{JS}^2$, WGAN = Kantorovich–Rubinstein with a critic (clipping is only a proxy), MMD GANs = RKHS ball.
  7. Semi-discrete (§7): for $\be=\sum_jb_j\delta_{y_j}$, maximise the concave $\mathcal E_0(g)=\int\min_j(c(x,y_j)-g_j)\,\dd\al+\ps{g}{b}$; gradient $b_j-\al(\mathrm L_j(g))$ (Laguerre cells); stochastic gradient $b-e_{j(x)}$. Free masses: Voronoi cells; Lloyd = k-means, monotone but local; rate $m^{-1/d}$; on the line placement ($m^{-2}$) beats sampling ($m^{-1}$) for $W_2^2$.
  8. Mixtures (§8): maximum likelihood $\to\KL(\text{data}|\text{model})+h$ (mass-covering), VI minimises $\KL(q|\pi)$ (mode-seeking), $W_2$ fits quantiles; KL is hyperbolic on Gaussians, $W_2$ Euclidean. $\mathrm{MW}_2$ transports components with cost $W_2^2$ between Gaussians and bounds $W_2$ from above.

The reflexes to acquire

  • Upper bound on a transport cost → a plan. Lower bound → admissible prices (or a 1-Lipschitz witness for $W_1$).
  • Certify optimality → plan + prices with zero gap (the plan only uses contacts).
  • Prices of a non-degenerate vertex → solve $f_i+g_j=C_{ij}$ along the tree of the plan.
  • One potential known → the other is its $c$-transform (column minima, lower envelope).
  • Need regular or compact potentials → replace by $(f^{c\bar c},f^c)$.
  • Quadratic cost → write $\varphi=\frac12\norm{x}^2-f$ (check the normalisation!) and think convex conjugates.
  • $W_1$ on the line → area between CDFs; on a graph → a flow on edges.
  • Loss between a model and samples → a dual norm or a transport cost, not a density ratio.
  • One discrete measure → semi-dual in $\R^m$, gradient = mass mismatch, stochastic ascent from samples.
  • Free masses → Voronoi and k-means; prescribed masses → Laguerre cells.
  • Which KL? Data first (maximum likelihood, mass-covering) or model first (VI, mode-seeking).
  • Gaussian mixtures with meaningful components → $\mathrm{MW}_2$, a small LP.

What comes next

  • Lesson 5 (sliced OT). Averages $W_p$ over one-dimensional projections, each computed by the 1D formulas used here ($W_1=\int\abs{C_\al-C_\be}$, quantiles).
  • Lesson 6 (dynamic OT and flow matching). Beckmann’s flux plus time gives the Benamou–Brenier formulation of $W_2$; the optimal map of §4 becomes a velocity field.
  • Lesson 7 (entropic regularisation). The hard minimum of the $c$-transform becomes a soft minimum; alternating soft transforms is Sinkhorn’s algorithm, which — unlike §3 — keeps improving. The semi-discrete problem has an entropic version too.
  • Lesson 8 (gradient flows). Kantorovich potentials are the first variations of $W_2^2$ (the gradient of $\frac12W_2^2(\cdot,\be)$ at $\al$ is $x-T(x)$); variational inference becomes a Wasserstein gradient flow of the KL.

Notes on the sources (things that could confuse you), each with a check
  • Sign of the stochastic semi-dual gradient. CompOT §5.4 (p. 96) and the 2019 notes (p. 26) print $E(g,x)=g^{\bar c}(x)-\ps{g}{b}$ and $\nabla_gE=(\ind_{\mathrm L_j(g)}(x)-b_j)_j$; the correct signs are $+\ps{g}{b}$ and $(b_j-\ind_{\mathrm L_j(g)}(x))_j$, as in their own (5.7)–(5.8) and OT4ML (6.18). Check: with the printed gradient the ascent drives the dual value of the line example to $-0.112$, $-2.47$, $-9.96$ after 30, 300, 3000 samples (§7).
  • “Convex” semi-dual. The 2019 notes (p. 26) call $\mathcal E$ “this convex function”; it is concave (a minimum of affine functions of $g$) and it is maximised. Check: on the line example, $\mathcal E_0$ at the midpoint of $g=(-0.2,0.2)$ and $g=(0.1,-0.1)$ is $0.033012$, above the average $0.000869$ of the two values.
  • Voronoi cells reversed. The 2019 notes (p. 27) define $\mathrm V_j(Y)=\{x:\ \forall j',\ c(x,y_{j'})\le c(x,y_j)\}$: the points for which $y_j$ is the farthest codepoint. Check: with $y_1=0$, $y_2=1$ on $\R$, the printed $\mathrm V_1$ (the cell of $y_1=0$) is $[\frac12,+\infty)$, the points closer to $1$. OT4ML Prop. 6.13 has the correct inequality.
  • 1D quantization. The 2019 notes (p. 27) state that the optimal 1D sampling is $y_j=C_\al^{-1}(j/m)$ (with “ToDo: see where this is proved”). Check: on $\mathcal U[0,1]$ this gives $W_2^2=\frac1{3m^2}$, four times the optimum $\frac1{12m^2}$ (bin averages, OT4ML Prop. 6.14), for $m=2,4,8$.
  • The limit of the likelihood. The 2019 notes (p. 40) and CompOT (p. 159) write $-\sum_i\log\rho_\theta(x_i)\to\KL(\al|\bar\be)$; the correct statement is $-\frac1n\sum_i\log\rho_\theta(x_i)\to\KL(\bar\be|\al_\theta)+h(\bar\be)$. Check (§8): $1.7373$ against $1.7371$ (correct) and $2.2258$ (printed order), with $n=10^6$.
  • KL between Gaussians. CompOT (8.6), p. 122, has $\abs{m_\al-m_\be}$ instead of $(m_\al-m_\be)^2$. Check: $\KL(\N(0,1)|\N(2,1))=2$ by quadrature; the printed formula gives $1$.
  • Jensen–Shannon generator. CompOT Example 8.4 (p. 124) gives $\varphi(s)=t\log(t)-(t+1)\log(t+1)$, which mixes the variables and does not vanish at $1$ ($\varphi(1)=-2\log2$); the generator of $\mathrm{JS}^2$ is $\frac12\big(s\log s-(s+1)\log\frac{s+1}2\big)$ (OT4ML p. 132). Check: for $\N(0,1)$ and $\N(1,1)$, $\mathrm{JS}^2=0.1114$, OT4ML’s generator gives $0.1114$, the printed one $-1.1635=2\,\mathrm{JS}^2-2\log2$.
  • The $W_1$ dual is a linear program. CompOT (6.2), p. 102, and the 2019 notes (56), p. 29, call it “a finite-dimensional convex program with quadratic-cone constraints”; its constraints $\abs{f_k-f_\ell}\le d(z_k,z_\ell)$ are pairs of linear inequalities (OT4ML (6.25): “finite-dimensional linear program”). Check: the example of §5 was solved by an LP solver.
  • “$W_p$ is the dual of $p$-Hölder functions”. CompOT Remark 6.1 (p. 102), for $0
  • Weight clipping. The 2019 notes (p. 33) say that clipping $\norm{\xi}_\infty\le1$ is done “in order to ensure $f_\xi\in B=\{f;\ \Lip(f)\le1\}$”; it does not (OT4ML Remark 7.26). Check: a one-hidden-layer ReLU network with $K$ units and all weights equal to $1$ has Lipschitz constant $K$ (§6).
  • Proof of the algebra of $c$-transforms. The 2019 notes (Prop. 15, p. 25) write “to have $f^c\ge f^{c\bar c}$” where $f^c\ge f^{c\bar cc}$ is meant, and (49) writes $f^{cc}$, $f^{ccc}$ for $f^{c\bar c}$, $f^{c\bar cc}$. Their p. 24 says “Keeping a dual potential $g$ fixed, one can try to minimize” (it is $f$ that is fixed, and $g$ is maximised, as corrected in OT4ML p. 99) and writes $h^*(y)=\sup_x\ps{x}{y}-h(y)$ for $h(x)$. Check: the page images.
  • Complementary slackness in CompOT. Prop. 3.2 (p. 45) prints $P^\star_{i,j}(C_{i,j}-f^\star_i+g^\star_j)=0$ and refers to the primal as (2.24) and the dual as (2.11) (swapped). Check: in the shipping example with the LP prices $f=(0,2,-1)$, $g=(4,5,2)$, the used route $(1,1)$ has $C_{11}-f_1-g_1=0$ but $C_{11}-f_1+g_1=8$.
  • Normalisation in Brenier’s theorem. CompOT Thm. 2.1 (p. 31) states $\varphi(x)=\frac{\norm{x}^2}2-f(x)$ for the cost $\norm{x-y}^2$; the factor is $\frac f2$ for this cost (or the cost should be $\frac12\norm{x-y}^2$). Check: on the Gaussian example of §2, $\frac{x^2}2-f$ has derivative $3x+2\ne T(x)=2x+1$ (§4).
  • Announced proof of Brenier. The 2019 notes (p. 14) refer to a proof “detailed in Section 5.3”; §5.3 (pp. 24–25) contains the $c$-transform calculus only. The proof is OT4ML Remark 5.13 (p. 102), which the compact notes do not reproduce (they keep Prop. 5.11). Check: the pages themselves.
  • Spelling and references. “Ciszár divergence” (OT4ML p. 130, 2019 notes p. 32, CompOT §8.1) is Csiszár’s; OT4ML Example 7.19 (p. 131) says KL “was already introduced in (8.13) and (8.7)”, which come later, in Chapter 8.
  • The exercise sheet. No mathematical error; see the notes after Exercise 3 (identities that are inequalities, $\bar c$ not defined, $p\ge1$ not needed in Exercise 1).
