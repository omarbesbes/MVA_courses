5. $W_1$: a single Lipschitz price (Kantorovich–Rubinstein) OT4ML §6.5, pp. 120–124

When the cost is a distance, the $c$-concave potentials are exactly the 1-Lipschitz functions and the second potential is minus the first: $W_1(\al,\be)=\max_{\Lip(f)\le1}\int f\,\dd(\al-\be)$ — a norm of $\al-\be$, the area between the CDFs on the line, a minimal flow on a graph, and the missing piece of Lesson 3.

Why the distance cost is special

Take $c=d$, a distance. A unit that starts at $x$ and ends at $y$ can be billed $f(x)$ at collection and $-f(y)$ at delivery: think of $f$ as a potential energy, the bill being the drop of potential $f(x)-f(y)$. The vendor’s check “no route is billed above its length” becomes $f(x)-f(y)\le d(x,y)$ for all $x,y$ — and, exchanging $x$ and $y$, $\abs{f(x)-f(y)}\le d(x,y)$: $f$ is 1-Lipschitz. The proposition below says that this one-price scheme loses nothing.

In the course OT4ML Def. 6.16, Prop. 6.17, p. 120

Definition 6.16 (Lipschitz constant). For a function $f:\X\to\R$ on a metric space $(\X,d)$, its Lipschitz constant is $\Lip(f):=\sup\Big\{\frac{\abs{f(x)-f(y)}}{d(x,y)}\ ;\ x\ne y\Big\}$. The function is 1-Lipschitz when $\Lip(f)\le1$.

Proposition 6.17 ($c$-transforms and 1-Lipschitz functions). Suppose $\X=\Y$ and $c(x,y)=d(x,y)$. Then there exists $g$ such that $f=g^c$ if and only if $\Lip(f)\le1$. Furthermore, if $\Lip(f)\le1$, then $f^c=-f$.

“there exists $g$ with $f=g^c$”
$f$ is $c$-concave (§3): a best response to some price. For the distance cost these are exactly the 1-Lipschitz functions.
$f^c=-f$
The best target price against a 1-Lipschitz $f$ is $-f$. So the closed pairs $(f^{c\bar c},f^c)$ of §3 are all of the form $(f,-f)$: one function instead of two.
Full proof

(⇒) Suppose $f=g^c$.

  1. $$\abs{f(x)-f(y)}=\Big|\inf_z\big[d(x,z)-g(z)\big]-\inf_z\big[d(y,z)-g(z)\big]\Big|$$Definition of $g^c$ (with $c=d$ symmetric, the variable of the infimum is called $z$).
  2. $$\le\sup_z\abs{d(x,z)-d(y,z)}\le d(x,y).$$$\abs{\inf A-\inf B}\le\sup\abs{A-B}$ (§3); then the reverse triangle inequality $\abs{d(x,z)-d(y,z)}\le d(x,y)$.

(⇐) and $f^c=-f$. Suppose $\Lip(f)\le1$.

  1. For all $x,y$: $d(x,y)-f(x)\ge-f(y)$, hence $f^c(y)=\inf_x\big(d(x,y)-f(x)\big)\ge-f(y)$.1-Lipschitz: $f(x)\le f(y)+d(x,y)$.
  2. Taking $x=y$ in the infimum: $f^c(y)\le d(y,y)-f(y)=-f(y)$.An infimum is below each term; $d(y,y)=0$.
  3. So $f^c=-f$. Applied to the 1-Lipschitz function $-f$: $(-f)^c=f$, so $f$ is the $c$-transform of $g=-f$.Lines 1–2 with $-f$ in place of $f$.

The Kantorovich–Rubinstein formula

In the course OT4ML (6.24)–(6.25), p. 121

By Proposition 6.17, a closed dual pair has the form $(f,-f)$ with $\Lip(f)\le1$. The Kantorovich dual therefore becomes the Kantorovich–Rubinstein formula

$$W_1(\al,\be)=\max_f\Big\{\int_\X f\,\dd(\al-\be)\ ;\ \Lip(f)\le1\Big\}=:\norm{\al-\be}_{W_1}.\qquad(6.24)$$

This expression depends only on the signed measure $\xi=\al-\be$. On compact $\X$, it therefore extends to every finite signed measure $\xi$ with $\xi(\X)=0$ by the same supremum and defines the Kantorovich–Rubinstein norm $\norm{\cdot}_{W_1}$. […] For a discrete signed measure $\al-\be=\sum_{k=1}^Nr_k\delta_{z_k}$ on distinct sites, with $\sum_kr_k=0$, (6.24) becomes the finite-dimensional linear program

$$W_1(\al,\be)=\max_{(f_k)_k}\Big\{\sum_kf_kr_k\ ;\ \forall k,\ell,\ \abs{f_k-f_\ell}\le d(z_k,z_\ell)\Big\}.\qquad(6.25)$$

[…] When $d(x,y)=\abs{x-y}$ on $\R$, ordering the support points $z_1\le z_2\le\cdots$ reduces the constraints to neighboring pairs […]. In one dimension this is equivalent to the cumulative formula (2.12).

$\int f\,\dd(\al-\be)$
$\int f\,\dd\al-\int f\,\dd\be=\E[f(X)]-\E[f(Y)]$ for $X\sim\al$, $Y\sim\be$: how differently the two laws average the test function $f$.
$\max$ over $\Lip(f)\le1$
The best 1-Lipschitz witness: the test function that separates $\al$ from $\be$ the most, with slope at most $1$. It is defined up to an additive constant (constants integrate to $0$ against $\al-\be$).
$\norm{\xi}_{W_1}$
A norm on signed measures of total mass $0$: homogeneous and subadditive because it is a supremum of linear functions of $\xi$. The total mass must be $0$, otherwise adding constants to $f$ makes the supremum $+\infty$.
(6.25)
For discrete measures: a linear program in the values $f_k=f(z_k)$, with the Lipschitz constraint between every pair of sites ($O(N^2)$ constraints), or only between neighbours on the line.

The same formula: compact notes (6.24), p. 29; 2019 notes (55), p. 28; CompOT (6.1), p. 101; StatOT Thm. 1.21, pp. 46–47 (for $\Pc_1(\R^d)$).

Worked example — a plan, a witness, a linear program

$\al=\delta_0$ and $\be=\frac12\delta_1+\frac12\delta_3$ on $\R$.

  • Upper bound by a plan. The only coupling sends half the mass to $1$ and half to $3$: $W_1=\frac12\cdot1+\frac12\cdot3=2$.
  • Lower bound by a witness. $f(x)=-x$ is 1-Lipschitz and $\int f\,\dd(\al-\be)=f(0)-\frac12f(1)-\frac12f(3)=0+\frac12+\frac32=2$. So $W_1\ge2$: the witness certifies the plan.
  • Why no witness does better. For any 1-Lipschitz $f$: $f(0)-\frac12f(1)-\frac12f(3)=\frac12\big(f(0)-f(1)\big)+\frac12\big(f(0)-f(3)\big)\le\frac12\cdot1+\frac12\cdot3=2$.
  • The LP (6.25) on the line. Sites $z=(0,1,3)$, masses $r=(1,-\frac12,-\frac12)$, constraints $\abs{f_2-f_1}\le1$, $\abs{f_3-f_2}\le2$: an LP solver returns $f=(0,-1,-3)$ (with the gauge $f_1=0$) and the value $2$.

Back to Lesson 3: $W_p$ metrizes weak convergence

Lesson 3 proved half of “$W_p(\al_n,\al)\to0\iff\al_n\wto\al$” on a compact space and announced that the other half (weak $\Rightarrow W_p\to0$) needs the Kantorovich–Rubinstein formula: see Lesson 3, §4, where the proof “weak $\Rightarrow W_p\to0$ (uses Lesson 4)” is written out. Here is the general mechanism, from the book.

In the course OT4ML Prop. 7.5, Cor. 7.6, pp. 126–127

Proposition 7.5 (Metrization by dual norms). Assume that $\X$ is compact, that $B=-B$, and that the measures considered are probability measures.

  1. If every function in $\mathcal C(\X)$ can be uniformly approximated by elements of $\mathrm{Span}(B)$, then $\norm{\al_n-\al}_B\to0$ implies $\al_n\wto\al$.
  2. If $B\subset\mathcal C(\X)$ is compact for $\norm{\cdot}_\infty$, then $\al_n\wto\al$ implies $\norm{\al_n-\al}_B\to0$.

Corollary 7.6 (Wasserstein metrizes weak convergence). On a compact metric space, $W_p$ metrizes weak convergence on probability measures for every $1\le p<+\infty$.

$\norm{\xi}_B$
$\sup_{f\in B}\int f\,\dd\xi$, the dual norm of a class $B$ of test functions (§6). For $B=\{\Lip(f)\le1\}$ it is $W_1$.
condition 1
$B$ is rich enough: its linear combinations approximate every continuous function. Then controlling $\int f\,\dd(\al_n-\al)$ for $f\in B$ controls it for every continuous $f$.
condition 2
$B$ is small enough (compact): weak convergence, which is pointwise in $f$, becomes uniform over $f\in B$.
Idea of the proof for $W_1$

Lipschitz functions are dense in $\mathcal C(\X)$ (condition 1). The 1-Lipschitz functions with $f(x_0)=0$ are bounded by $\diam(\X)$ and equicontinuous, hence compact by Arzelà–Ascoli (condition 2); normalising $f(x_0)=0$ changes nothing because $\al_n-\al$ has mass $0$. Then compare $W_p$ with $W_1$ (Lesson 3, §3).

Proof of “weak ⇒ $W_1\to0$” (OT4ML p. 126)
  1. Let $B=\{f:\ \Lip(f)\le1,\ f(x_0)=0\}$. Then $W_1(\al_n,\al)=\sup_{f\in B}\int f\,\dd(\al_n-\al)$.Kantorovich–Rubinstein, and subtracting $f(x_0)$ does not change $\int f\,\dd(\al_n-\al)$ (zero total mass).
  2. $B$ is compact in $(\mathcal C(\X),\norm{\cdot}_\infty)$.$\abs{f(x)}=\abs{f(x)-f(x_0)}\le\diam(\X)$ and all $f\in B$ are 1-Lipschitz: Arzelà–Ascoli (refresher); $B$ is closed under uniform limits.
  3. Take a subsequence with $W_1(\al_{n_k},\al)\to\limsup_nW_1(\al_n,\al)$ and maximisers $f_{n_k}\in B$; extract again so that $f_{n_k}\to f\in B$ uniformly.The supremum over a compact set of a continuous function of $f$ is attained; compactness again for the extraction.
  4. $$\int f_{n_k}\,\dd(\al_{n_k}-\al)=\int f\,\dd(\al_{n_k}-\al)+\int(f_{n_k}-f)\,\dd\al_{n_k}-\int(f_{n_k}-f)\,\dd\al\to0.$$The first term tends to $0$ by weak convergence ($f$ is fixed and continuous); the other two are at most $\norm{f_{n_k}-f}_\infty\to0$ (probability measures).
  5. So $\limsup_nW_1(\al_n,\al)=0$; then $W_p\le\diam(\X)^{(p-1)/p}W_1^{1/p}\to0$.The comparison of Lesson 3, §3 with exponents $(1,p)$.

The book adds a warning about the endpoint $p=\infty$: $W_\infty$ (the largest displacement) does not metrize weak convergence — on $[0,1]$, $\al_n=(1-\frac1n)\delta_0+\frac1n\delta_1\wto\delta_0$ while $W_\infty(\al_n,\delta_0)=1$ for every $n$ (OT4ML p. 127).

On the line: the area between the CDFs

On $\R$ the best witness can be written down. Let $G=C_\al-C_\be$ (the CDF of the signed measure $\al-\be$; it vanishes at $\pm\infty$). For a 1-Lipschitz $f$ (differentiable almost everywhere with $\abs{f'}\le1$), integrate by parts:

  1. $$\int f\,\dd(\al-\be)=\Big[fG\Big]_{-\infty}^{+\infty}-\int_{\R}f'(x)\,G(x)\,\dd x=-\int_{\R}f'(x)\big(C_\al(x)-C_\be(x)\big)\,\dd x.$$$\dd(\al-\be)=\dd G$; the boundary term vanishes because $G\to0$ at $\pm\infty$ fast enough when the first moments are finite (for compact supports, $G=0$ outside a bounded interval).
  2. $$\le\int_{\R}\abs{f'(x)}\,\abs{C_\al(x)-C_\be(x)}\,\dd x\le\int_{\R}\abs{C_\al(x)-C_\be(x)}\,\dd x .$$$\abs{f'}\le1$.
  3. Equality holds for $f'(x)=-\mathrm{sign}\big(C_\al(x)-C_\be(x)\big)$, so $$W_1(\al,\be)=\int_{\R}\abs{C_\al(x)-C_\be(x)}\,\dd x.$$This $f$ is 1-Lipschitz (slopes $\pm1$) and makes both inequalities equalities. It is the formula of Lesson 1 (OT4ML (2.12)), now with its witness.

Worked example — a tent-shaped witness

$\al=\frac12\delta_0+\frac12\delta_3$ and $\be=\mathcal U[1,2]$. Then $C_\al-C_\be=\frac12$ on $[0,1)$, $\frac12-(x-1)=\frac32-x$ on $[1,2)$, $-\frac12$ on $[2,3)$, and $0$ elsewhere. So

$$W_1=\int_0^1\tfrac12\,\dd x+\int_1^2\abs{\tfrac32-x}\,\dd x+\int_2^3\tfrac12\,\dd x=\tfrac12+\tfrac14+\tfrac12=\tfrac54 .$$

The sign changes at $x=1.5$: the witness is the tent $f(x)=-x$ on $[0,1.5]$ and $f(x)=x-3$ on $[1.5,3]$. Check: $\int f\,\dd\al=\frac12f(0)+\frac12f(3)=0$ and $\int f\,\dd\be=\int_1^{1.5}(-x)\,\dd x+\int_{1.5}^2(x-3)\,\dd x=-0.625-0.625=-1.25$, so $\int f\,\dd(\al-\be)=1.25$. ✓ The monotone plan sends the atom at $0$ to $[1,1.5]$ and the atom at $3$ to $[1.5,2]$: cost $\int_0^{1/2}(1+u)\,\dd u+\int_{1/2}^1(2-u)\,\dd u=0.625+0.625=1.25$. ✓ (Checked numerically.)

Beckmann: transport as a flow

The dual of $W_1$ has one function; dualising its Lipschitz constraint once more gives a third formulation, where the unknown is a flow: how much mass crosses each place, in which direction — like traffic on a road network, without saying who goes where.

In the course OT4ML Def. 6.20–Prop. 6.23, pp. 122–123

Definition 6.20 (Beckmann problem). For $\al,\be\in\M(\R^d)$, define $\mathrm{Beck}(\al,\be):=\inf_{s\in\mathcal M(\R^d;\R^d)}\big\{\norm{s}_\TV\ ;\ \dv s=\al-\be\big\}$.

Proposition 6.21 (Euclidean Beckmann formula). For $\al,\be\in\M(\R^d)$ with finite first moments, $W_1(\al,\be)=\mathrm{Beck}(\al,\be)$. (6.27)

Proposition 6.23 ($W_1$ and Beckmann flow on a graph). Let $G=(V,E)$ be a connected finite graph with positive edge lengths $(\ell_e)_{e\in E}$ and graph geodesic distance $d_G$. For two probability vectors $a,b$ on $V$, set $r=a-b$ and orient each edge $e=(i,j)$. […] a positive flow on the oriented edge $i\to j$ has positive divergence at $i$ and negative divergence at $j$. With this convention,

$$W_{1,G}(a,b)=\max_f\Big\{\sum_{i\in V}f_ir_i\ ;\ \abs{f_i-f_j}\le\ell_e\ \forall e=(i,j)\Big\}=\min_m\Big\{\sum_{e\in E}\ell_e\abs{m_e}\ ;\ \dv_Gm=r\Big\}.$$

The vector $m_e$ is an oriented edge flow, and the constraint $\dv_Gm=r$ is conservation of mass at each vertex.

$s\in\mathcal M(\R^d;\R^d)$
A vector-valued measure: a flux of mass with a direction. It must be allowed to be singular — “transporting one Dirac mass to another produces a flux concentrated on a segment” (OT4ML p. 121).
$\norm{s}_\TV$
Its total amount, counting direction-free: $\int\norm{w(x)}\,\dd x$ when $s=w\,\dd x$ (Remark 6.19). Mass × distance travelled, summed.
$\dv s=\al-\be$
Conservation: the net outflow at each place is what $\al$ supplies minus what $\be$ demands; away from both, flux in = flux out. (In the sense of distributions: $\ps{\dv s}{\varphi}=-\int\ps{\nabla\varphi}{\dd s}$.)
on a graph
$m_e$ = mass flowing along edge $e$ (sign = direction); $(\dv_Gm)_i$ = net outflow at vertex $i$. Lipschitz constraints are only needed on edges: summing them along paths gives $\abs{f_i-f_j}\le d_G(i,j)$ for all pairs.
Idea of the graph proof

The first equality is Kantorovich–Rubinstein on the metric space $(V,d_G)$, with constraints on edges only. For the second, dualise “$\dv_Gm=r$” with a multiplier $f$: minimising $\ell_e\abs{m_e}+m_e(f_j-f_i)$ over each scalar $m_e$ gives $0$ if $\abs{f_i-f_j}\le\ell_e$ and $-\infty$ otherwise — exactly the Lipschitz constraint. Strong duality of linear programs closes the argument.

Worked example — a flow on a small grid

Unit-length edges on a $3\times2$ grid with vertices $(x,y)$, $x\in\{0,1,2\}$, $y\in\{0,1\}$. Supply $a=\delta_{(0,0)}$, demand $b=\frac12\delta_{(2,0)}+\frac12\delta_{(1,1)}$. An optimal flow (from an LP solver): $1$ along $(0,0)\to(1,0)$, then $\frac12$ along $(1,0)\to(2,0)$ and $\frac12$ along $(1,0)\to(1,1)$. Its cost is $1+\frac12+\frac12=2$. A witness: $f=-d_G\big((0,0),\cdot\big)$, i.e. $f=0,-1,-2$ on the bottom row and $-1,-2,-3$ on the top row; it is 1-Lipschitz along every edge and $\sum_if_ir_i=0+\frac12\cdot2+\frac12\cdot2=2$. Flow cost = witness value: both optimal, $W_{1,G}=2$. (The optimal flow is not unique: the mass for $(1,1)$ could also go through $(0,1)$.)

Intuition — Beckmann is transport without time

The flux says how much mass crosses each place, not when, nor which unit goes where. Adding time and replacing $\norm{s}$ by the kinetic energy turns it into the Benamou–Brenier formulation of $W_2$ (Lesson 6, dynamic OT and flow matching).

In machine learning — comparing documents and images with $W_1$

Word Mover’s Distance (Kusner, Sun, Kolkin and Weinberger, ICML 2015). A document is a histogram over its words (normalised word counts, stop words removed); each word is a point in an embedding space (word2vec vectors). The distance between two documents is $W_1$ between the two histograms, with ground distance the Euclidean distance between embeddings: “Obama speaks to the media in Illinois” and “The President greets the press in Chicago” share no word but are close, because each word travels a short way. Used with a $k$-nearest-neighbour classifier, it gave low test errors on document classification. Cheap lower bounds prune the search: dropping one marginal constraint (each word goes to its nearest word in the other document) gives $\sum_ia_i\min_j\norm{x_i-y_j}$ — the free-mass bound of §7 (Prop. 6.13). The same $W_1$ (“earth mover’s distance”) was used earlier for image retrieval with colour and texture histograms (Rubner et al., 2000; CompOT Remark 2.16, p. 25).

Pitfalls
  • Total mass zero. $\sup_{\Lip(f)\le1}\int f\,\dd\xi=+\infty$ if $\xi(\X)\ne0$ (add a large constant to $f$). Kantorovich–Rubinstein compares probability measures, or measures of equal mass.
  • Only $p=1$. For $c=d^p$ with $p>1$, $f^c\ne-f$ in general and no single-potential formula holds: $W_2$ is not a supremum of $\int f\,\dd(\al-\be)$ over a class of test functions. The duality of §2 (two potentials) is what remains.
  • It is a linear program. (6.25) has linear constraints $f_k-f_\ell\le d(z_k,z_\ell)$; quadratic (second-order cone) constraints only appear when one discretises $\norm{\nabla f(x)}_2\le1$ in $\R^d$ (see the notes on the sources).
Reflexes
  • Lower bound on $W_1$ (hence on every $W_p$, $p\ge1$) → choose a 1-Lipschitz test function: $f(x)=x$ (compares means), $f(x)=\abs{x}$, a distance to a set, a tent.
  • $W_1$ on the line → $\int\abs{C_\al-C_\be}$; the witness has slope $-\mathrm{sign}(C_\al-C_\be)$.
  • $W_1$ on a graph → a min-cost flow on the edges, not an $N\times N$ coupling.

Compute $W_1(\delta_0,\mathcal U[0,1])$ by the CDF formula and give an optimal witness.

Answer

$C_\al-C_\be=1-x\ge0$ on $[0,1]$, so $W_1=\int_0^1(1-x)\,\dd x=\frac12$ and the witness has slope $-1$: $f(x)=-x$. Check: $\int f\,\dd(\al-\be)=0-\int_0^1(-x)\,\dd x=\frac12$. ✓

Show that $W_1(\al,\be)\ge\abs{\E_\al[X]-\E_\be[Y]}$ on $\R$. When is it an equality?

Answer

$f(x)=x$ and $f(x)=-x$ are 1-Lipschitz, giving $\pm(\E_\al X-\E_\be Y)$. Equality iff the witness can be taken affine of slope $\pm1$, i.e. iff $C_\al-C_\be$ does not change sign (one CDF stays above the other: stochastic order), as in the quiz above.
