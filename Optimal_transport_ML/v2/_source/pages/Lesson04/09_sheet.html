Exercise 1 — Total variation and the 0/1 cost

In the course sheet, Exercise 1

Exercise 1 (Total variation and 0/1 cost). Let $d(x,y)=1_{x\ne y}$ be the discrete 0/1 distance. We wish to show that for any $p\ge1$, and probability measures $\al$ and $\be$,

$$W_p^p(\al,\be)=\frac12\norm{\al-\be}_\TV .$$

Start with two discrete probability measures $\al=\sum_ia_i\delta_{x_i}$ and $\be=\sum_ib_i\delta_{x_i}$ on the same finite set $\{x_i\}_i$ with weights $a=(a_i)$ and $b=(b_i)$ on the same support.

Reminder — the solution of Lesson 3

Since $d^p=d$, $W_p^p$ is the transport cost for $\ind_{x\ne y}$: the mass moved. At most $\min(a_i,b_i)$ can stay at $x_i$, and the plan $P^\star=\mathrm{diag}(\min(a,b))+\frac{(a-\min(a,b))(b-\min(a,b))\T}{r}$, $r=1-\sum_i\min(a_i,b_i)$, keeps exactly that; so $W_p^p=1-\sum_i\min(a_i,b_i)=\frac12\sum_i\abs{a_i-b_i}$. Full solution: Lesson 3, §5.

Hint for a dual proof

The plan of Lesson 3 gives $W_p^p\le\frac12\norm{a-b}_1$. For the reverse inequality you need a price. With the 0/1 distance, which functions are 1-Lipschitz? Try the indicator of $A=\{i:\ a_i>b_i\}$.

Solution (second proof, by duality)
  1. $W_p^p(\al,\be)=\mathcal L_{d^p}(\al,\be)=\mathcal L_d(\al,\be)=W_1(\al,\be)$ for the 0/1 distance.$d$ takes only the values $0$ and $1$, so $d^p=d$ for every $p>0$.
  2. For the 0/1 distance, $f$ is 1-Lipschitz iff $\abs{f(x)-f(y)}\le1$ for all $x\ne y$, i.e. iff the values of $f$ lie in an interval of length $1$.Definition 6.16 with $d(x,y)=1$ for $x\ne y$.
  3. Let $A=\{i:\ a_i>b_i\}$ and $f=\ind_A$ (values in $\{0,1\}$, hence 1-Lipschitz). Then $$W_1(\al,\be)\ge\int f\,\dd(\al-\be)=\sum_{i\in A}(a_i-b_i).$$Kantorovich–Rubinstein (§5): every 1-Lipschitz function gives a lower bound (weak duality with the pair $(f,-f)$).
  4. $$\sum_{i\in A}(a_i-b_i)=\frac12\sum_i\abs{a_i-b_i}=\frac12\norm{\al-\be}_\TV .$$$\sum_i(a_i-b_i)=0$, so the positive part $\sum_{A}(a_i-b_i)$ equals the negative part $\sum_{A^c}(b_i-a_i)$, and their sum is $\sum_i\abs{a_i-b_i}$.
  5. With the plan of Lesson 3 (upper bound $\frac12\norm{\al-\be}_\TV$): $W_p^p(\al,\be)=\frac12\norm{\al-\be}_\TV$, and both the plan and the price are optimal. $\square$An upper bound by a plan and a lower bound by a price that coincide.

Complementary slackness, as a check. The plan moves mass only from points of $A$ (excess) to points of $A^c$ (deficit): on those routes $f(x)-f(y)=1-0=1=d(x,y)$ — contacts; mass that stays has $f(x)-f(x)=0=d(x,x)$. Every used route is a contact, as Prop. 5.3 requires.

Numbers. $a=(0.5,0.3,0.2)$, $b=(0.2,0.3,0.5)$: $A=\{1\}$, $f=(1,0,0)$, $\int f\,\dd(\al-\be)=0.3=\frac12\cdot0.6$, and the plan of Lesson 3 costs $0.3$.

General measures. The same two objects work: the plan keeps the common part $\al\wedge\be$ in place, and the price is $\ind_A$ for a set $A$ where $\al-\be\ge0$ and $\al-\be\le0$ outside (a Hahn set, as in the proof of Pinsker’s inequality, §6).

Exercise 2 — Equivalence of topologies on a finite metric space

In the course sheet, Exercise 2

Exercise 2 (Equivalence of topologies on a finite discrete metric space). Let $(X,d)$ be a finite discrete metric space. Define $d_{\min}:=\min_{x\ne y}d(x,y)$, $d_{\max}:=\max_{x,y}d(x,y)$. Show that for all probability measures $\al,\be$ on $X$,

$$\frac{d_{\min}}2\norm{\al-\be}_\TV\ \le\ W_1(\al,\be)\ \le\ \frac{d_{\max}}2\norm{\al-\be}_\TV .$$

Conclude that the $W_1$-topology and the total variation topology coincide on $\mathcal P(X)$.

Reminder — the solution of Lesson 3

Compare the costs pointwise: $d_{\min}\ind_{x\ne y}\le d(x,y)\le d_{\max}\ind_{x\ne y}$, integrate against couplings, and use Exercise 1 for the 0/1 cost. Full solution and an example on $\{0,1,3\}$: Lesson 3, §5.

Hint for a dual proof

Lower bound: is $d_{\min}\ind_A$ 1-Lipschitz for $d$? Upper bound: a 1-Lipschitz $f$ takes values in an interval of length at most $d_{\max}$; shift it so that $\abs{f}\le\frac{d_{\max}}2$, and use $\abs{\int f\,\dd\xi}\le\norm{f}_\infty\norm{\xi}_\TV$.

Solution (second proof, by duality)

Lower bound.

  1. Let $A=\{x:\ \al(\{x\})>\be(\{x\})\}$ and $f=d_{\min}\ind_A$. For $x\ne y$: $\abs{f(x)-f(y)}\le d_{\min}\le d(x,y)$; so $f$ is 1-Lipschitz.$f$ takes the values $0$ and $d_{\min}$; and $d_{\min}$ is the smallest distance between distinct points.
  2. $$W_1(\al,\be)\ge\int f\,\dd(\al-\be)=d_{\min}\sum_{x\in A}\big(\al(\{x\})-\be(\{x\})\big)=\frac{d_{\min}}2\norm{\al-\be}_\TV .$$Kantorovich–Rubinstein, then step 4 of Exercise 1 (positive part = half of the $\ell^1$ norm).

Upper bound.

  1. Let $f$ be 1-Lipschitz. Then $\max f-\min f\le d_{\max}$.$f(x)-f(y)\le d(x,y)\le d_{\max}$ for the points where the max and the min are attained ($X$ is finite).
  2. Let $\tilde f=f-\frac{\max f+\min f}2$. Then $\abs{\tilde f}\le\frac{d_{\max}}2$ and $\int\tilde f\,\dd(\al-\be)=\int f\,\dd(\al-\be)$.Centering puts the values in $[-\frac{d_{\max}}2,\frac{d_{\max}}2]$; constants integrate to $0$ against $\al-\be$ (total mass $0$).
  3. $$\int f\,\dd(\al-\be)=\sum_x\tilde f(x)\big(\al(\{x\})-\be(\{x\})\big)\le\frac{d_{\max}}2\sum_x\abs{\al(\{x\})-\be(\{x\})}=\frac{d_{\max}}2\norm{\al-\be}_\TV .$$$\abs{\tilde f(x)}\le\frac{d_{\max}}2$ term by term.
  4. Taking the supremum over 1-Lipschitz $f$: $W_1(\al,\be)\le\frac{d_{\max}}2\norm{\al-\be}_\TV$.Kantorovich–Rubinstein (§5). This is OT4ML Remark 7.22 with $\diam(X)=d_{\max}$.

Conclusion. $d_{\min}>0$ because $X$ is finite (with at least two points). The two metrics bound each other up to the constants $\frac{d_{\min}}2$ and $\frac{d_{\max}}2$: a sequence converges for one iff it converges for the other, so the topologies coincide. On an infinite space, $d_{\min}$ can be $0$ and the lower bound is empty — this is how $\delta_{x_n}\to\delta_x$ in $W_1$ but not in total variation (Lesson 3).

Exercise 3 — $c$-transforms

In the course sheet, Exercise 3

Exercise 3 (c-transforms). Show the following identities

  • $f\le f'\to f^c\ge f'^c$
  • $f^{c\bar c}\ge f$
  • $g^{\bar cc}\ge g$
  • $f^{c\bar cc}=f^c$

What is assumed. The sheet does not recall the definitions; they are those of §3 (OT4ML Def. 5.8): a cost $c:\X\times\Y\to\R$, and for $f:\X\to\R\cup\{-\infty\}$ and $g:\Y\to\R\cup\{-\infty\}$ (not identically $-\infty$),

$$f^c(y)=\inf_{x\in\X}\ c(x,y)-f(x),\qquad g^{\bar c}(x)=\inf_{y\in\Y}\ c(x,y)-g(y),\qquad f^{c\bar c}=(f^c)^{\bar c},\ \ f^{c\bar cc}=\big((f^c)^{\bar c}\big)^c .$$

All inequalities are pointwise (for every $x$, or every $y$), “$\to$” means “implies”, and, as the book says, the identities hold “whenever the transforms are well defined” (each intermediate transform not identically $-\infty$). No continuity, compactness or structure of $c$ is needed: only the definition of an infimum. Two facts about infima are used:

  • (Inf1) an infimum is below each of its terms: $\inf_xA_x\le A_{x_0}$ for every $x_0$;
  • (Inf2) an infimum is monotone: if $A_x\ge B_x$ for every $x$, then $\inf_xA_x\ge\inf_xB_x$ (because $\inf B\le B_x\le A_x$ for every $x$, so $\inf B$ is a lower bound of the $A_x$, hence $\le\inf A$).
Hint 1

(i) is (Inf2). For (ii), start from (Inf1) applied to the infimum that defines $f^c(y)$, with the term $x$ itself, and move things across the inequality.

Hint 2

For (iv), prove two inequalities. One is (ii) followed by (i). The other is (iii) applied to the function $g=f^c$.

Full solution

(i) $f\le f'\Rightarrow f^c\ge f'^c$.

  1. Assume $f(x)\le f'(x)$ for every $x$, and fix $y\in\Y$. Then $c(x,y)-f(x)\ge c(x,y)-f'(x)$ for every $x$.Subtracting a larger number gives a smaller result (with $c(x,y)-(-\infty)=+\infty$, the inequality stays true when values are $-\infty$).
  2. $f^c(y)=\inf_x\big(c(x,y)-f(x)\big)\ge\inf_x\big(c(x,y)-f'(x)\big)=f'^c(y)$.(Inf2). As $y$ was arbitrary, $f^c\ge f'^c$: the transform reverses the order.

(ii) $f^{c\bar c}\ge f$.

  1. Fix $x\in\X$; if $f(x)=-\infty$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, for every $y$: $f^c(y)=\inf_{x'}\big(c(x',y)-f(x')\big)\le c(x,y)-f(x)$.(Inf1) with the term $x'=x$.
  2. Hence $c(x,y)-f^c(y)\ge f(x)$ for every $y$.Rearrange (add $f(x)-f^c(y)$ to both sides); if $f^c(y)=-\infty$, the left side is $+\infty$ and the inequality holds trivially.
  3. $f^{c\bar c}(x)=\inf_y\big(c(x,y)-f^c(y)\big)\ge f(x)$.$f(x)$ is a lower bound of all the terms, hence below their infimum.

(iii) $g^{\bar cc}\ge g$.

  1. Fix $y\in\Y$ with $g(y)>-\infty$. For every $x$: $g^{\bar c}(x)=\inf_{y'}\big(c(x,y')-g(y')\big)\le c(x,y)-g(y)$, so $c(x,y)-g^{\bar c}(x)\ge g(y)$.(Inf1) with $y'=y$, then rearrange — the proof of (ii) with the roles of $\X$ and $\Y$ exchanged.
  2. $g^{\bar cc}(y)=\inf_x\big(c(x,y)-g^{\bar c}(x)\big)\ge g(y)$.Same reason as in (ii). (Here $(g^{\bar c})^c$ is the $c$-transform of the function $g^{\bar c}$ on $\X$.)

(iv) $f^{c\bar cc}=f^c$.

  1. $f^{c\bar cc}\le f^c$.By (ii), $f\le f^{c\bar c}$. Apply (i) with $f'=f^{c\bar c}$: $f^c\ge(f^{c\bar c})^c=f^{c\bar cc}$.
  2. $f^{c\bar cc}\ge f^c$.Apply (iii) to the function $g:=f^c$ on $\Y$: $g^{\bar cc}\ge g$, i.e. $(f^c)^{\bar cc}=f^{c\bar cc}\ge f^c$.
  3. Hence $f^{c\bar cc}=f^c$. $\square$Two opposite inequalities between the same functions.

Worked check. On $\{0,1,2\}$ with $c=\abs{x-y}$ and $f=(0,2,1)$ (§3): $f^c=(-1,-2,-1)$, $f^{c\bar c}=(1,2,1)\ge f$ (ii), and $f^{c\bar cc}=(-1,-2,-1)=f^c$ (iv). For (i), take $f'=f^{c\bar c}=(1,2,1)\ge f$: $f'^c=(-1,-2,-1)\le f^c$ — here with equality. On 500 random cost matrices of sizes $2$ to $8$ with random $f\le f'$ and $g$, the four statements hold, with $\max\abs{f^{c\bar cc}-f^c}=1.8\times10^{-15}$ (rounding).

What it means. By (iv), $f^{c\bar c}$ is $\bar c$-concave and $(f^{c\bar c})^{c\bar c}=f^{c\bar c}$: transforming twice is a projection onto the $\bar c$-concave functions. It is the smallest $\bar c$-concave function above $f$: if $h=k^{\bar c}$ and $h\ge f$, then $h^c\le f^c$ (i), so $h^{c\bar c}\ge f^{c\bar c}$ ((i) for $\bar c$), and $h^{c\bar c}=k^{\bar cc\bar c}=k^{\bar c}=h$ ((iv) with the roles exchanged); hence $h\ge f^{c\bar c}$. For the quadratic cost this is the convex envelope of §4 ($\varphi^{**}$), for $c=d$ the smallest 1-Lipschitz majorant on the relevant points (the example: $(1,2,1)$). And (iv) is why alternating $c$-transforms stops after one round (§3).

Notes on the sheet
  • No mathematical error in the three statements. Exercise 3 calls them “identities”, though three of the four are (pointwise) inequalities; “$\to$” is to be read “$\Rightarrow$”; and the sheet uses $\bar c$ and the transforms without defining them (the definitions are OT4ML Def. 5.8 and the 2019 notes (48)).
  • In Exercise 1 the equality holds for every $p>0$, since $d^p=d$; the condition $p\ge1$ only matters for $W_p$ to be a distance (Lesson 3).
  • The 2019 notes prove the same algebra (Prop. 15, p. 25) with a slip in (iv): they write “to have $f^c\ge f^{c\bar c}$” where the inequality obtained is $f^c\ge f^{c\bar cc}$ (step 1 of (iv) above); and their alternation (49) writes $f^{cc}$, $f^{ccc}$ for $f^{c\bar c}$, $f^{c\bar cc}$. OT4ML Prop. 5.14 (p. 102) has the correct proof.
