9. The optimal transport facts every lesson reuses

Five results are used everywhere: Monge’s problem and Brenier’s theorem, Kantorovich’s relaxation as a linear program, the Wasserstein distances, duality with $c$-transforms, and the closed forms on the line and for Gaussians. Here they are in one place, each with a check on numbers and a link to the lesson that proves it.

Monge’s problem and Brenier’s theorem

Proved in Lesson 1 (statement, 1D and Gaussian cases) and Lesson 4 (proof through duality). Refresher in Lesson 3.

The facts OT4ML Def. 2.14, p. 25; Thm. 2.23, p. 29
  • Monge: $\displaystyle\min_{T:\ \pf{T}{\al}=\be}\int c\big(x,T(x)\big)\,\dd\al(x)$ — move each point to one destination. The constraint set can be empty: no map sends $\delta_0$ to $\frac12\delta_{-1}+\frac12\delta_1$ (a map cannot split a Dirac, §1).
  • Brenier: on $\R^d$ with $c(x,y)=\norm{x-y}^2$, if $\al$ has a density and both measures have finite second moments, there is an optimal map, unique $\al$-almost everywhere, and it is the gradient of a convex function: $T=\nabla\varphi$. The optimal plan is $\pf{(\Id,T)}{\al}$.
  • In 1D, “gradient of a convex function” means “non-decreasing”: $T=C_\be^{-1}\circ C_\al$ (§2). Between Gaussians, $T$ is affine with a symmetric positive matrix (§3).
Check

$\al=\N(0,1)$, $\be=\N(2,9)$ on $\R$: $T(x)=2+3x=\varphi'(x)$ with $\varphi(x)=\frac32x^2+2x$ convex, and $\pf{T}{\al}=\N(2,9)$. Cost: $\E\big[(X-T(X))^2\big]=\E\big[(2+2X)^2\big]=4+4=8=(0-2)^2+(1-3)^2$.

Kantorovich’s problem: couplings and the linear program

Proved in Lesson 2 (discrete case, linear programming, Birkhoff) and Lesson 3 (general measures).

The facts OT4ML Def. 3.31, p. 66; Prop. 3.34, p. 66; Cor. 3.18, p. 58
  • $\displaystyle\Lc(\al,\be)=\min_{\pi\in\U(\al,\be)}\int c\,\dd\pi=\min_{X\sim\al,\ Y\sim\be}\E\big[c(X,Y)\big]$: optimise over couplings (§1) — mass may be split. The set of couplings is never empty ($\al\otimes\be$) and the minimum is attained for lower semi-continuous costs.
  • Discrete case: the linear program $\min\{\ps{C}{P}:\ P\ge0,\ P\ind=a,\ P\T\ind=b\}$ (§5); there is an optimal plan with at most $n+m-1$ nonzero entries; for $n=m$ and uniform weights, an optimal permutation (§3), so Monge and Kantorovich agree.
  • When Brenier applies, the Kantorovich optimum is the Monge map: $\pi=\pf{(\Id,\nabla\varphi)}{\al}$.
Check

$\delta_0$ to $\frac12\delta_{-1}+\frac12\delta_1$ with $\abs{x-y}^2$: Monge is impossible, but the only coupling sends half of the mass to each point, at cost $\frac12+\frac12=1$. The $2\times2$ example of §5 has optimal cost $\frac14$ with the plan $\begin{pmatrix}1/4&1/4\\0&1/2\end{pmatrix}$.

Wasserstein distances

Proved in Lesson 3 (distance, comparison, convergence, geodesics, barycenters); Lesson 4 (the dual formula of $W_1$).

The facts OT4ML Def. 4.6 and Prop. 4.7, p. 76; Prop. 4.17, p. 80; Prop. 4.25, p. 83
  • $W_p(\al,\be)=\big(\min_{\pi\in\U(\al,\be)}\int d(x,y)^p\,\dd\pi\big)^{1/p}$, $p\ge1$, is a distance on $\Pc_p(\X)$; the triangle inequality comes from gluing plans and Minkowski (§4).
  • $W_p(\delta_x,\delta_y)=d(x,y)$; $W_p\le W_q$ for $p\le q$; $W_p$-convergence is weak convergence plus convergence of $p$-th moments (§1).
  • $W_2$ has constant-speed geodesics (displacement interpolation, a continuity equation, §4) and barycenters. $W_1(\al,\be)=\sup_{\Lip(f)\le1}\int f\,\dd(\al-\be)$ (Kantorovich–Rubinstein, §4).
Check

Moving a mass $\eps$ by a distance $1$: $W_p\big(\delta_0,(1-\eps)\delta_0+\eps\delta_1\big)=\eps^{1/p}$, so $W_1=\eps$ but $W_2=\sqrt\eps$ (Lesson 3). Versus total variation (§6): $\norm{\delta_0-\delta_\eps}_\TV=2$ while $W_p=\eps$.

Duality and $c$-transforms

Proved in Lesson 4; the discrete version is LP duality (§5), used in Lesson 2; the entropic version is the subject of Lesson 7.

The facts OT4ML Prop. 5.5, p. 96; Def. 5.8 and Prop. 5.9, p. 99; Prop. 5.12, p. 101
  • $\displaystyle\Lc(\al,\be)=\max_{f(x)+g(y)\le c(x,y)}\int f\,\dd\al+\int g\,\dd\be$ (compact spaces, continuous cost). The potentials $f,g$ are prices; at the optimum $f(x)+g(y)=c(x,y)$ wherever the optimal plan puts mass (complementary slackness).
  • $c$-transform: $f^c(y)=\inf_x\big(c(x,y)-f(x)\big)$ is the best $g$ for a given $f$; it inherits the regularity of $c$ (§4).
  • Quadratic cost $\frac12\norm{x-y}^2$: with $\varphi=\frac12\norm\cdot^2-f$, $f^c=\frac12\norm\cdot^2-\varphi^*$ — the Legendre transform (§5); this is how Brenier’s theorem is proved. For $c=d$: $g=-f$ with $f$ $1$-Lipschitz, giving the $W_1$ formula.
Check

In the $2\times2$ example of §5, $f=(0,-1)$ and $g=(0,1)$: $g$ is the $c$-transform of $f$, since $\min_i(C_{i1}-f_i)=\min(0,2)=0=g_1$ and $\min_i(C_{i2}-f_i)=\min(1,1)=1=g_2$. Dual value $\frac14$, equal to the primal one.

Closed forms: the line and Gaussians

Proved in Lesson 1; used in Lesson 3 (barycenters), Lesson 5 (sliced OT: the 1D formula on every projection), Lesson 7 (entropic Gaussians).

The facts OT4ML Prop. 2.44, p. 38–39; Prop. 2.56, p. 46
  • On the line, for $p\ge1$: $\displaystyle W_p^p(\al,\be)=\int_0^1\abs{C_\al^{-1}(t)-C_\be^{-1}(t)}^p\,\dd t$, and $W_1(\al,\be)=\int_\R\abs{C_\al(x)-C_\be(x)}\,\dd x$ (§2). For point clouds of equal size: sort and match (§2).
  • Gaussians: $W_2^2\big(\N(m_\al,\Sigma_\al),\N(m_\be,\Sigma_\be)\big)=\norm{m_\al-m_\be}^2+\B(\Sigma_\al,\Sigma_\be)^2$ with the Bures term of §3; in 1D, $(m_\al-m_\be)^2+(\sigma_\al-\sigma_\be)^2$.
Check

$\mathcal U[0,1]$ and $\mathcal U[a,1+a]$: the quantile functions are $t$ and $t+a$, so $W_p=a$ for every $p$. $W_2^2\big(\N(0,\mathrm I),\N(0,S)\big)=6-2(\sqrt3+1)\approx0.536$ for $S=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}$ (§3). Point clouds $(0,1,3)$ and $(1,2,5)$: $W_2^2=2$ (§2).

Compute $W_2\big(\mathcal U[0,1],\delta_{1/2}\big)$ with the quantile formula.

Answer

The quantile functions are $t$ and the constant $\frac12$, so $W_2^2=\int_0^1\big(t-\frac12\big)^2\dd t=\frac1{12}$ and $W_2=\frac1{2\sqrt3}\approx0.289$ — the standard deviation of the uniform law, as it must be: the only coupling with a Dirac sends everything to it.
