6. Comparing $SW_p$ with $W_p$ notes Prop. 1 and Remark 4, pp. 3–4

Projecting an optimal plan gives a plan for every slice, so slicing never increases the cost: $SW_p^p\le\kappa_{d,p}W_p^p$ with $\kappa_{d,2}=\frac1d$; the reverse comparison holds on bounded sets but only with a small power of $SW_p$ — the two distances have the same convergent sequences, not comparable values.

Slicing never increases the cost

In the course Nadjahi notes, Prop. 1 and its proof, p. 3

Proposition 1 ($\mathbf{SW}_p$ vs. $\mathbf W_p$). Let $p\in[1,+\infty)$. For any two distributions $\mu,\nu$ on $\R^d$ with finite moments of order $p$,

$$\mathbf{SW}_p(\mu,\nu)^p\le c_{d,p}\,\mathbf W_p(\mu,\nu)^p\qquad\textit{with}\qquad c_{d,p}=\frac1d\int_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}\norm{\theta}_p^p\,\dd\mathcal U_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}(\theta)\le1 .$$

Proof. Let $\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)$ be an optimal transport plan between $\mu$ and $\nu$. Then, $\big((p_\theta)\otimes(p_\theta)\big)_\sharp\gamma$ is a transport plan between $(p_\theta)_\sharp\mu$ and $(p_\theta)_\sharp\nu$ (rigorous justification is left as an exercise!). Therefore,

$$\begin{aligned}\mathbf W_p\big((p_\theta)_\sharp\mu,(p_\theta)_\sharp\nu\big)^p&\le\int_{\R\times\R}\abs{s-t}^p\,\dd\big[\big((p_\theta)\otimes(p_\theta)\big)_\sharp\gamma\big](s,t)&(13)\\&=\int_{\R^d\times\R^d}\norm{\ps{x-y}{\theta}}^p\,\dd\gamma(x,y)&(14)\end{aligned}$$

Moreover, for any $z\in\R^d$,

$$\begin{aligned}\int_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}\abs{\ps{z}{\theta}}^p\,\dd\mathcal U_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}(\theta)&\le\frac1d\norm{z}^p\int_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}\norm{\theta}_p^p\,\dd\mathcal U_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}&(15)\\&=c_{d,p}\norm{z}^p .&(16)\end{aligned}$$

Therefore,

$$\begin{aligned}\mathbf{SW}_p(\mu,\nu)^p&\le\int_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}\int_{\R^d\times\R^d}\abs{\ps{x-y}{\theta}}^p\,\dd\gamma(x,y)\,\dd\mathcal U_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}(\theta)&(17)\\&\le\int_{\R^d\times\R^d}\int_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}\abs{\ps{x-y}{\theta}}^p\,\dd\mathcal U_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}(\theta)\,\dd\gamma(x,y)&(18)\\&\le c_{d,p}\int_{\R^d\times\R^d}\norm{x-y}^p\,\dd\gamma(x,y)&(19)\\&\le c_{d,p}\,\mathbf W_p(\mu,\nu)^p .\ \square&(20)\end{aligned}$$
$\Gamma(\mu,\nu)$
The couplings of $\mu$ and $\nu$ ($\U(\al,\be)$ on this page).
$(p_\theta)\otimes(p_\theta)$
The map $(x,y)\mapsto\big(\ps{\theta}{x},\ps{\theta}{y}\big)$: project both ends of every transported unit of mass. Its push-forward of $\gamma$ is the projected plan $\gamma_\theta$: if $(X,Y)\sim\gamma$, then $\big(\ps{\theta}{X},\ps{\theta}{Y}\big)\sim\gamma_\theta$. That it is a coupling of the two slices is Exercise 2(a) of the sheet (§12).
$\norm{\theta}_p^p$
$\sum_{i=1}^d\abs{\theta_i}^p$, the $\ell^p$ norm of the direction to the power $p$ (not the Euclidean norm, which is $1$).
$c_{d,p}$
A number that depends only on the dimension and on $p$. We will see that $c_{d,p}=\E\abs{\theta_1}^p$, written $\kappa_{d,p}$ in OT4ML, and that $c_{d,2}=\frac1d$.
(13)
The projected plan is one coupling of the slices; the optimal one is cheaper.
(14)
Change of variables for the push-forward: $\abs{s-t}^p$ evaluated at $s=\ps{\theta}{x}$, $t=\ps{\theta}{y}$. The double bars $\norm{\cdot}$ around the number $\ps{x-y}{\theta}$ are an absolute value.
(18)
Exchange of the two integrals (Fubini–Tonelli, everything is non-negative): it is an equality.
Idea of the proof
  1. Take an optimal plan $\pi$ between $\al$ and $\be$, and project both ends of each arrow on $\theta$: you get a plan between the slices, so it bounds the slice cost from above.
  2. Average over $\theta$ and swap the integrals: for each arrow $z=x-y$, average $\abs{\ps{\theta}{z}}^p$ over the directions.
  3. By rotation invariance this average is $\kappa_{d,p}\norm{z}^p$: a fixed fraction of the full cost of the arrow. Summing over arrows gives $\kappa_{d,p}W_p^p$.
Proof, step by step
  1. Let $\pi$ be an optimal coupling of $\al$ and $\be$, and $(X,Y)\sim\pi$. For each $\theta$, $\big(\ps{\theta}{X},\ps{\theta}{Y}\big)$ is a coupling of $\al_\theta$ and $\be_\theta$.$\ps{\theta}{X}\sim\al_\theta$ because $X\sim\al$ (definition of the slice), and likewise for $Y$ (sheet, Exercise 2(a)).
  2. $$W_p^p(\al_\theta,\be_\theta)\le\E\big[\abs{\ps{\theta}{X-Y}}^p\big]\qquad\text{for every }\theta.$$Any coupling gives an upper bound (lines (13)–(14)).
  3. $$SW_p^p(\al,\be)\le\int_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}\E\big[\abs{\ps{\theta}{X-Y}}^p\big]\,\dd\sigma(\theta)=\E\Big[\int_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}\abs{\ps{\theta}{X-Y}}^p\,\dd\sigma(\theta)\Big].$$Integrate step 2 over $\theta$ (line (17)), then Fubini–Tonelli for a non-negative integrand (line (18)).
  4. $$\int_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}\abs{\ps{\theta}{z}}^p\,\dd\sigma(\theta)=\kappa_{d,p}\norm{z}^p\quad\text{for every }z\in\R^d,\qquad\kappa_{d,p}:=\int_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}\abs{\theta_1}^p\,\dd\sigma(\theta).$$For $z\ne0$, $\ps{\theta}{z}=\norm{z}\ps{\theta}{z/\norm z}$, and by rotation invariance $\ps{\theta}{e}$ has the law of $\theta_1$ for any unit vector $e$ (refresher). This is line (15)–(16), with an equality.
  5. $$SW_p^p(\al,\be)\le\kappa_{d,p}\,\E\norm{X-Y}^p=\kappa_{d,p}\,W_p^p(\al,\be).\qquad\square$$Step 4 with $z=X-Y$, then optimality of $\pi$ (lines (19)–(20)).

Why the notes’ $c_{d,p}$ is the same number. $\norm{\theta}_p^p=\sum_i\abs{\theta_i}^p$ and every coordinate $\theta_i$ has the law of $\theta_1$ (permuting coordinates is a rotation-type symmetry), so $c_{d,p}=\frac1d\sum_i\E\abs{\theta_i}^p=\E\abs{\theta_1}^p=\kappa_{d,p}$. (Checked by Monte Carlo in $d=10$: $0.2587$, $0.1000$, $0.0470$ for $p=1,2,3$, as the formula below.)

In the course OT4ML Prop. 11.24, p. 230 (the constant)

$$\mathcal{SW}_p(\al,\be)^p\le\kappa_{d,p}\,\mathcal W_p(\al,\be)^p,\qquad\kappa_{d,p}:=\int_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}\abs{\theta_1}^p\,\dd\sigma(\theta)=\frac{\Gamma(d/2)\,\Gamma((p+1)/2)}{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma((d+p)/2)}.$$ In particular, $\kappa_{d,2}=1/d$.

Worked example — values of $\kappa_{d,p}$
  • $p=2$: $\kappa_{d,2}=\E[\theta_1^2]=\frac1d$, by $\E[\theta\theta\T]=\frac1d\Id$. So $SW_2\le W_2/\sqrt d$.
  • $d=2$: $\kappa_{2,1}=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\abs{\cos\varphi}\,\dd\varphi=\frac2\pi\approx0.637$; $\kappa_{2,2}=\frac12$; $\kappa_{2,4}=\frac38$.
  • $d=3$ (Archimedes): the first coordinate of a uniform point of the sphere is uniform on $[-1,1]$, so $\kappa_{3,p}=\frac12\int_{-1}^1\abs{t}^p\,\dd t=\frac1{p+1}$: $\frac12,\frac13,\frac14,\dots$ The Gamma formula agrees: $\frac{\Gamma(3/2)\Gamma((p+1)/2)}{\sqrt\pi\,\frac{p+1}2\Gamma((p+1)/2)}=\frac1{p+1}$.
  • Large $d$: $\kappa_{d,1}\approx\sqrt{2/(\pi d)}$ ($0.2587$ for $d=10$, $0.0800$ for $d=100$), $\kappa_{d,2}=\frac1d$, and $\kappa_{d,p}$ decays like $d^{-p/2}$.
Worked example — when is the bound an equality?

For a translation $\be=\pf{\tau_z}{\al}$: the best plan moves every point by $z$, so $W_p=\norm{z}$ (Lesson 3, Exercise 2), and each slice is a translation by $\ps{\theta}{z}$, so $SW_p^p=\int\abs{\ps{\theta}{z}}^p\,\dd\sigma=\kappa_{d,p}\norm{z}^p$: equality. In high dimension this is striking: a unit translation in $\R^{100}$ has $W_2=1$ but $SW_2=0.1$, because only directions nearly aligned with $z$ see it. Remark 4 (Additional comments on Proposition 1).

  • As $p\ge2$, $c_{d,p}\le1/d$ (left as an exercise!)
  • $\mathbf W_p$ and $\mathbf{SW}_p$ are equivalent for distributions supported on $\mathcal B(\mathbf 0,R)\subset\R^d$ (closed Euclidean ball in $\R^d$ of center $\mathbf0$ and radius $R>0$). More precisely, there exists a constant $C_{d,p}>0$ such that, for any $\mu,\nu$ supported on $\mathcal B(\mathbf0,R)$, $$\mathbf{SW}_p(\mu,\nu)^p\le c_{d,p}\mathbf W_p(\mu,\nu)^p\le C_{d,p}R^{p-1/(d+1)}\,\mathbf{SW}_p(\mu,\nu)^{1/(d+1)}.\qquad(21)$$
first bullet
For $p\ge2$ the constant is even smaller than $\frac1d$: $\abs{\theta_i}^p\le\theta_i^2$ because $\abs{\theta_i}\le1$, so $\norm{\theta}_p^p\le\norm{\theta}_2^2=1$. Solved in Exercise 5 of §13 — together with the fact that it is false for $p=1$ ($\kappa_{d,1}>\frac1d$ for $d\ge2$).
“equivalent”
Here it means topologically equivalent (same convergent sequences), not “equal up to constants”: the right-hand side has the small power $\frac1{d+1}$ of $SW_p$.
(21), right inequality
On a ball, a small $SW_p$ forces a small $W_p$: $W_p\lesssim R^{1-\frac1{p(d+1)}}\,SW_p^{\frac1{p(d+1)}}$. In $\R^{10}$ with $p=1$, $SW_1=10^{-6}$ only guarantees $W_1\lesssim10^{-6/11}\approx0.28$ (times constants).
$R^{p-1/(d+1)}$
Makes the units right: $W_p^p$ scales like (length)$^p$, $SW_p^{1/(d+1)}$ like (length)$^{1/(d+1)}$.
In the course OT4ML Prop. 11.24, p. 230 (reverse estimates)

Conversely, if $d\ge2$, $R>0$, and $\al,\be\in\Pc_p(\R^d)$ are supported in $B_R$, then there is a constant $C_{d,p}$ depending only on $d$ and $p$ such that Bonnotte’s historical estimate reads $\ \mathcal W_p(\al,\be)^p\le C_{d,p}R^{p-\frac1{d+1}}\mathcal{SW}_p(\al,\be)^{\frac1{d+1}}$. The sharp reverse estimate for $p=1$ also gives, for every $p\ge1$, constants $\tilde C_{d,p},\tilde c_{d,p}>0$ such that

$$\mathcal W_p(\al,\be)\le\tilde C_{d,p}R^{1-\frac1{pd}}\mathcal{SW}_p(\al,\be)^{\frac1{pd}},\quad\text{equivalently}\quad\mathcal{SW}_p(\al,\be)\ge\tilde c_{d,p}R\Big(\frac{\mathcal W_p(\al,\be)}R\Big)^{pd}.$$

For $p=1$, the exponent $1/d$ in this last comparison is sharp.

Intuition — what slicing cannot see

A slice integrates the measure along hyperplanes. A perturbation that oscillates quickly inside the hyperplanes orthogonal to $\theta$ averages out in that slice. A rapidly oscillating perturbation is seen only by the few directions aligned with its oscillation, so $SW_p$ can be much smaller than $W_p$ — and the smaller the scale, the worse. This is why no inequality $W_p\le C\,SW_p$ can hold, even on a ball, and why the exponent in the reverse bound degrades with the dimension (Carlier, Figalli, Mérigot and Wang for the sharp exponent $1/d$, cited in OT4ML p. 231).

Going further — small deformations: $SW_2^2/W_2^2$ can be $\frac1d$, or smaller (OT4ML Prop. 11.27, Remarks 11.28–11.29, pp. 232–234)

Deform a measure by a small displacement field: $\al_t=\pf{(\Id+t\nabla\varphi)}{\al}$. For small $t$ this map is a Brenier map, so $W_2^2(\al,\al_t)=t^2\int\norm{\nabla\varphi}^2\,\dd\al$, and projecting the plan gives $SW_2^2(\al,\al_t)\le\frac{t^2}d\int\norm{\nabla\varphi}^2\,\dd\al$ (OT4ML Prop. 11.27). Two cases show that the ratio depends on the measure:

  • Atoms moving with fixed weights (Remark 11.28): for almost every direction, the projected order of the atoms does not change for small $t$, so each slice keeps the projected plan, and $SW_2^2/W_2^2\to\frac1d$.
  • A Gaussian sheared (Remark 11.29): with $\al=\N(0,\Id)$ and $\varphi(x)=\frac12x\T Ax$, $\ \lim_{t\to0}SW_2^2/t^2=\frac{(\tr A)^2+2\tr(A^2)}{d(d+2)}<\frac{\tr(A^2)}d=\lim_{t\to0}\frac{W_2^2}{d\,t^2}$ unless $A$ is a multiple of $\Id$. With $d=2$ and $A=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)$: $SW_2^2/t^2\to\frac{0+4}{8}=\frac12$ while $W_2^2/t^2=2$, a ratio $\frac14$ — half of $\frac1d$. (Checked by quadrature: the ratio is $0.2486$, $0.2498$, $0.2500$ for $t=0.3,0.1,0.01$.) The slices of a sheared Gaussian change less than the projected plan predicts: some of the motion happens “inside” the slices.
Pitfall — “$SW_p$ is a cheap approximation of $W_p$”

It is not an approximation of the value: for a translation in $\R^{d}$, $SW_2=W_2/\sqrt d$ exactly, and for other pairs the ratio can be anything between $0$ and $1/\sqrt d$. $SW_p$ is a different distance with the same convergent sequences. When a number close to $W_p$ is needed, use exact or entropic transport, or a variant that keeps more information (max-sliced, subspaces, §10).

Reflexes
  • Upper bound on a slice cost → project an optimal plan; average with $\int\abs{\ps{\theta}{z}}^p\,\dd\sigma=\kappa_{d,p}\norm z^p$.
  • $p=2$ → $\E[\theta\theta\T]=\frac1d\Id$, so $SW_2^2\le\frac1dW_2^2$, with equality for translations.
  • Reverse comparison → only on bounded sets, with a power $\frac1{d+1}$ (Bonnotte) or $\frac1d$ (sharp, $p=1$).
