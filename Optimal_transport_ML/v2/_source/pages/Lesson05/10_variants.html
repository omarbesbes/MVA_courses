10. Variants: the best direction, subspaces, lifted plans, other projections OT4ML Defs. 11.32–11.34, Props. 11.33–11.36

Averaging over random directions dilutes differences that live in few directions and produces no transport plan; the variants keep the most informative direction (max-sliced), project on subspaces (between $SW_p$ and $W_p$), lift a sorted matching back to a plan (Min-SW), or bend the projections (generalized slicing).

The notes stop at the Monte Carlo estimate; the slides’ references and the book list the main variants. This section is a guided tour: definitions, one example each, what they are used for.

Keep the best direction: max-sliced Wasserstein

In the course OT4ML Def. 11.32, p. 235 (case $k=1$)

Definition 11.32 ($L^q$-sliced and subspace-sliced Wasserstein). […] extend the definition to $q=\infty$ by $\ \mathcal{SW}_{p,\infty,k}(\al,\be):=\sup_{U\in\mathrm{St}(d,k)}\mathcal W_p\big((U\T)_\sharp\al,(U\T)_\sharp\be\big)$. […] $\mathrm{MaxSW}_p=\mathcal{SW}_{p,\infty}$. […] Quadratic max-slicing is useful when only a small set of directions carries the discrepancy, for instance in generative modeling.

For $k=1$, $\mathrm{St}(d,1)$ is the sphere and $U\T x=\ps{\theta}{x}$, so

$$\mathrm{MaxSW}_p(\al,\be)=\max_{\theta\in\mathbb S^{d-1}}W_p(\al_\theta,\be_\theta):$$

the largest one-dimensional distance over all directions (Deshpande et al. 2019, in the slides’ references). The maximum is attained (the sphere is compact and the profile is continuous, §5).

Properties (OT4ML Prop. 11.33, p. 235)

$\mathrm{MaxSW}_p$ is a distance with the same convergent sequences as $W_p$, and

$$SW_p\le\mathrm{MaxSW}_p\le W_p .$$
Why (three lines)
  1. $SW_p\le\mathrm{MaxSW}_p$.An average of the values $W_p^p(\al_\theta,\be_\theta)$ is at most their maximum; take $p$-th roots.
  2. $\mathrm{MaxSW}_p\le W_p$.For every $\theta$, $W_p(\al_\theta,\be_\theta)\le W_p(\al,\be)$: project an optimal plan, and $\abs{\ps{\theta}{x-y}}\le\norm{x-y}$ (§6).
  3. Triangle inequality: $W_p(\al_\theta,\ga_\theta)\le W_p(\al_\theta,\be_\theta)+W_p(\be_\theta,\ga_\theta)\le\mathrm{MaxSW}_p(\al,\be)+\mathrm{MaxSW}_p(\be,\ga)$; take the maximum over $\theta$ on the left. Separation: $\mathrm{MaxSW}_p=0$ makes every slice equal, and Cramér–Wold applies.No “almost every” subtlety here: a maximum equal to $0$ means all values are $0$.
Worked examples
  • Translation by $z$: the slice cost $\abs{\ps{\theta}{z}}^p$ is maximal for $\theta=z/\norm z$, so $\mathrm{MaxSW}_p=\norm z=W_p$: the max-sliced distance keeps the full size of a shift, which averaging divides by $\kappa_{d,p}^{1/p}$ ($\sqrt d$ for $p=2$).
  • The cross (§4): $\mathrm{MaxSW}_2^2=1$ (directions $e_1$, $e_2$), between $SW_2^2=0.363$ and $W_2^2=2$.
  • The two pairs $\al=\frac12(\delta_{(0,0)}+\delta_{(1,3)})$, $\be=\frac12(\delta_{(1,0)}+\delta_{(0,3)})$: $W_2^2=1$, $SW_2^2=0.364$, and $\mathrm{MaxSW}_2^2=0.9$, attained at $\theta=(3,1)/\sqrt{10}$ (angle $18.4°$), where both points of $\be$ project to $3/\sqrt{10}$ and $\al$ projects to $0$ and $6/\sqrt{10}$: $\frac12\big[(\frac3{\sqrt{10}})^2+(\frac3{\sqrt{10}})^2\big]=0.9$. (Checked on $36\,000$ angles.)
In machine learning — max-sliced Wasserstein GANs

When a generator of $64\times64$ images is trained with a Monte Carlo sliced loss, almost all random directions of $\R^{12\,288}$ are nearly orthogonal to the few directions in which generated and real images differ (§7, concentration): thousands of directions are needed, and most of the gradient is noise. Deshpande et al. (CVPR 2019, “Max-sliced Wasserstein distance and its use for GANs”, slides’ references) replace the average by the best direction, found by gradient ascent on $\theta\mapsto W_2^2(\al_\theta,\be_\theta)$, computed in a feature space learned by a discriminator network. One well-chosen direction then does the work of many random ones. The price: an inner maximization at each step, and a non-convex problem in $\theta$.

Project on subspaces: from $SW_p$ to $W_p$

Instead of lines, project on $k$-dimensional subspaces: $U\in\mathrm{St}(d,k)$ is a $d\times k$ matrix with orthonormal columns, $U\T x\in\R^k$, and $\mathcal{SW}_{p,p,k}$ averages $W_p^p\big((U\T)_\sharp\al,(U\T)_\sharp\be\big)$ over random subspaces (OT4ML Def. 11.32). For $k=1$ it is $SW_p$, for $k=d$ it is $W_p$; in between, OT4ML Prop. 11.33 gives $\mathcal{SW}_{2,2,k}^2\le\frac kdW_2^2$ — the constant $\frac1d$ becomes $\frac kd$. The price: each projected problem is now a $k$-dimensional transport problem, no longer a sort.

In the course OT4ML Def. 11.48, p. 247

Definition 11.48 (Projection- and subspace-robust Wasserstein). For $1\le k\le d$, let $\mathcal P_k=\{P:\ P^2=P=P\T,\ \tr(P)=k\}$. The Paty–Cuturi projection-robust and subspace-robust Wasserstein distances are respectively the max–min and min–max quantities

$$\mathrm{PRW}_{2,k}(\al,\be):=\sup_{U\T U=\Id_k}\mathcal W_2\big((U\T)_\sharp\al,(U\T)_\sharp\be\big),\qquad\mathrm{SRW}_{2,k}(\al,\be)^2:=\inf_{\pi\in\U(\al,\be)}\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_k}\tr(PM_\pi).$$
$\mathcal P_k$
The orthogonal projections of rank $k$ (onto $k$-dimensional subspaces).
$\mathrm{PRW}_{2,k}$
The max-sliced idea with subspaces: the most discriminating $k$-dimensional subspace ($\mathrm{MaxSW}_2$ for $k=1$).
$M_\pi$
The second-moment matrix of the displacements, $\int(x-y)(x-y)\T\,\dd\pi$; $\tr(PM_\pi)$ is the cost of $\pi$ measured only in the subspace of $P$.
$\mathrm{SRW}_{2,k}$
“Choose the plan first, then the adversary chooses the subspace”: a convex relaxation of PRW (min–max instead of max–min), computable by alternating a transport step and an eigenvalue step (Paty and Cuturi, ICML 2019).

From a number to a plan: lifted plans and Min-SW

$SW_p$ is a number; each slice has its own matching (§4), so no transport plan comes out of it. Yet a plan is often what we want (to move points, to match data). A simple idea: sort along one direction and use that matching in the original space.

In the course OT4ML (11.36), p. 237

Consider equal-weight empirical measures $\al=n^{-1}\sum_i\delta_{x_i}$ and $\be=n^{-1}\sum_i\delta_{y_i}$. For a direction $\theta$ along which both projected point families have distinct coordinates, let $\sigma_\theta,\tau_\theta\in\mathfrak S_n$ be their sorting permutations, $\ps{x_{\sigma_\theta(1)}}{\theta}<\dots<\ps{x_{\sigma_\theta(n)}}{\theta}$, $\ps{y_{\tau_\theta(1)}}{\theta}<\dots<\ps{y_{\tau_\theta(n)}}{\theta}$. Lifting the one-dimensional monotone matching gives

$$\pi_\theta=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{(x_{\sigma_\theta(i)},\,y_{\tau_\theta(i)})}\in\U(\al,\be).\qquad(11.36)$$

The cost of (11.36) is an upper bound on $\mathcal W_2(\al,\be)^2$, and Min-SW minimizes this upper bound over $\theta$. This inexpensive feasible-plan construction was introduced by Mahey, Chapel, Gasso, Bonet and Courty.

In the course OT4ML Props. 11.35–11.36, pp. 238–239

Proposition 11.35 (Min-SW lower bound, topology and metric status). For all $\al,\be\in\Pc_2(\R^d)$, $\ \mathcal W_2(\al,\be)\le\text{Min-SW}_2(\al,\be)$. Consequently, convergence for $\text{Min-SW}_2$ implies convergence for $\mathcal W_2$. The converse fails in every dimension $d\ge2$ […]. The function $\text{Min-SW}_2$ is non-negative, symmetric and separates measures, but it does not satisfy the triangle inequality in general. […] In dimension one, by contrast, $\text{Min-SW}_2=\mathcal W_2$.

Proposition 11.36 (Min-SW between nondegenerate Gaussians). Let $\al=\N(m_\al,\Sigma_\al)$ and $\be=\N(m_\be,\Sigma_\be)$, where $\Sigma_\al,\Sigma_\be\succ0$. Then $\text{Min-SW}_2(\al,\be)^2=\mathcal W_2(\al,\be)^2$.

$\mathfrak S_n$
The permutations of $\{1,\dots,n\}$.
lifted plan $\pi_\theta$
The $i$-th point of $\al$ along $\theta$ is sent to the $i$-th point of $\be$ along $\theta$ — but the cost is measured in $\R^d$, $\frac1n\sum_i\norm{x_{\sigma_\theta(i)}-y_{\tau_\theta(i)}}^2$, not along $\theta$. It is a genuine coupling, so its cost is at least $W_2^2$.
$\text{Min-SW}_2$
The cheapest lifted plan over all directions: $\text{Min-SW}_2^2=\min_\theta\frac1n\sum_i\norm{x_{\sigma_\theta(i)}-y_{\tau_\theta(i)}}^2$ for clouds without ties (Def. 11.34 handles ties by optimizing over all compatible lifts).
Gaussians
Sorting along an eigenvector $\theta$ of the matrix $A$ of the Brenier map $x\mapsto m_\be+A(x-m_\al)$ gives back that map: $\ps{\theta}{T(x)-m_\be}=\lambda\ps{\theta}{x-m_\al}$ with $\lambda>0$, an increasing function of the projection.
Worked example — Min-SW is not a distance (OT4ML, p. 238)

Let $\al_X,\al_Y,\al_Z$ be the uniform measures on the rows of

$$X=\begin{pmatrix}-4&-4\\0&3\\1&-2\\4&-4\end{pmatrix},\qquad Z=\begin{pmatrix}-3&2\\2&2\\0&0\\2&-4\end{pmatrix},\qquad Y=\frac{X+Z}2 .$$

The book finds $\text{Min-SW}_2^2(\al_X,\al_Y)=\text{Min-SW}_2^2(\al_Y,\al_Z)=\frac{51}{16}$ and $\text{Min-SW}_2^2(\al_X,\al_Z)=\frac{63}4$, so

$$\text{Min-SW}_2(\al_X,\al_Z)=\frac{\sqrt{63}}2\approx3.969\ >\ \frac{\sqrt{51}}4+\frac{\sqrt{51}}4=\frac{\sqrt{51}}2\approx3.571:$$

the triangle inequality fails. We recomputed all three values by sweeping $20\,000$ directions and checked them against the exact $W_2^2$ ($\frac{51}{16}$, $\frac{51}{16}$ and $12.75$ by enumerating the $4!$ assignments): for $(X,Z)$, the best lifted plan costs $15.75$, more than the optimal $12.75$ — no direction of the plane sorts $X$ and $Z$ consistently with the optimal assignment.

Other projections: generalized slicing

Slicing integrates a measure along hyperplanes $\{\ps{\theta}{x}=s\}$. Generalized sliced Wasserstein distances (Kolouri et al., NeurIPS 2019, in the slides’ references) replace the linear function $\ps{\theta}{x}$ by a nonlinear one $g(x,\theta)$ — for instance an odd polynomial, or a small neural network — and integrate along the hypersurfaces $\{g(x,\theta)=s\}$ (a generalized Radon transform). Each slice is still a measure on the line, solved by sorting; when the generalized Radon transform is injective, the result is still a distance. Other variants change the law of the directions instead of the projections: distributional and energy-based sliced distances put more weight on directions where the slices differ (Nguyen et al. 2021, Nguyen and Ho 2023, slides’ references); spherical and manifold versions slice data that live on a sphere or a curved space (Bonet et al. 2023, 2025).

In machine learning — choosing a variant
  • A loss for a generative model in high dimension: max-sliced or a learned/distributional law of directions, so that the informative directions are found rather than sampled.
  • A kernel or an embedding of distributions: plain $SW_2$, whose Hilbert embedding (§5) is exactly what a kernel method needs.
  • A matching or a transport map between two clouds (registration, colour transfer with correspondences): lifted plans and Min-SW give a feasible plan at the cost of a few sorts; the sliced flow of §11 gives another.
  • A value close to $W_2$: subspace projections ($k>1$), or abandon slicing for entropic transport (Lesson 7).
Pitfall — “Min-SW” is not a minimum of sliced distances

Despite its name, $\text{Min-SW}_2$ does not minimize $W_2(\al_\theta,\be_\theta)$ over $\theta$ (that minimum is often $0$: in the two pairs, the slices along $e_1$ coincide). It minimizes the full cost of a lifted plan, an upper bound on $W_2^2$. It lies above $W_2$, while $SW_2$ and $\mathrm{MaxSW}_2$ lie below.

Order the five numbers $SW_2$, $\mathrm{MaxSW}_2$, $W_2$, $\text{Min-SW}_2$, $W_2/\sqrt d$ for a general pair of clouds in $\R^d$.

Answer

$SW_2\le W_2/\sqrt d\le W_2\le\text{Min-SW}_2$ and $SW_2\le\mathrm{MaxSW}_2\le W_2$. $\mathrm{MaxSW}_2$ and $W_2/\sqrt d$ are not ordered in general: for a translation $\mathrm{MaxSW}_2=W_2>W_2/\sqrt d$; for the isotropic Gaussians of §7, every direction sees $\abs{s-t}$, so $\mathrm{MaxSW}_2=SW_2=W_2/\sqrt d$.
