14. Summary of the lecture

The lecture on one screen
  1. Why (§1): exact transport between $n$-point clouds is a linear program, $O(n^3)$ per evaluation ($\sim10^7$ for a mini-batch of $256$); from samples it converges like $n^{-1/d}$ (for $W_1$; $d>2p$ needed for $W_p$).
  2. The line (§2): sort both clouds and match by rank; $W_p^p=\frac1n\sum_i\abs{x_{(i)}-y_{(i)}}^p=\int_0^1\abs{F_\al^{-1}-F_\be^{-1}}^p$; $O(n\log n)$; rate $n^{-1/2}$.
  3. Slices (§3): $\al_\theta=\pf{(P_\theta)}{\al}$, $P_\theta(x)=\ps{\theta}{x}$; $\int g\,\dd\al_\theta=\int g(\ps{\theta}{x})\,\dd\al$; all slices = Radon transform; Fourier slice identity $\mathcal F\al(\omega\theta)=\mathcal F(\al_\theta)(\omega)$.
  4. Definition (§4): $SW_p^p(\al,\be)=\int_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}W_p^p(\al_\theta,\be_\theta)\,\dd\sigma(\theta)$ with the uniform probability $\sigma$; the sphere removes the redundant (and divergent) radial factor.
  5. A distance (§5): triangle inequality slice by slice + Minkowski; separation by continuity in $\theta$, Fourier slice identity and injectivity (Cramér–Wold); same convergent sequences as $W_p$; $SW_2$ is Hilbertian.
  6. Comparison (§6): $SW_p^p\le\kappa_{d,p}W_p^p$, $\kappa_{d,2}=\frac1d$, equality for translations; on a ball, $W_p^p\le C_{d,p}R^{p-\frac1{d+1}}SW_p^{\frac1{d+1}}$ — same topology, not comparable values.
  7. Gaussians (§7): $SW_2^2=\frac1d\norm{\Delta m}^2+\int(\sqrt{\theta\T\Sigma_\al\theta}-\sqrt{\theta\T\Sigma_\be\theta})^2\,\dd\sigma$; isotropic: $SW_2=\abs{s-t}$ while $W_2=\sqrt d\abs{s-t}$.
  8. Monte Carlo (§8): $K$ directions $\theta=G/\norm G$; cost $O(Kdn+Kn\log n)$; $\widehat{SW}_p^p$ unbiased, error $\le\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}/K}$ (not $\mathrm{Var}/K$).
  9. Statistics (§9): $\E\abs{SW_1(\hat\al_n,\hat\be_m)-SW_1(\al,\be)}\le R(n^{-1/2}+m^{-1/2})$ in every dimension; but differences living in few directions are divided by $\sqrt d$.
  10. Variants and uses (§10–§11): max-sliced, subspaces, lifted plans and Min-SW (not a distance), generalized slicing; the sliced flow (IDT, colour transfer, sampling) and Radon barycenters.

The reflexes to acquire

  • Transport on the line → sort and match by rank; quantile functions for general measures.
  • A property of $SW_p$ → prove it slice by slice, then integrate over the sphere (Minkowski for triangle inequalities).
  • An upper bound on a slice cost → project an optimal plan; average $\abs{\ps{\theta}{z}}^p$ with rotation invariance.
  • $p=2$ → $\E[\theta\theta\T]=\frac1d\Id$: means and translations are divided by $d$.
  • “The slices determine the measure” → Fourier slice identity + injectivity, and a continuity argument for “almost every direction”.
  • Random directions → normalized Gaussian vectors; never a normalized cube, never uniform angles in $d\ge3$.
  • Monte Carlo → error $\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}/K}$; average $p$-th powers, take the root at the end.
  • Rate of a sliced statistic → the one-dimensional rate, in every dimension; but watch the $1/\sqrt d$ shrinking of low-dimensional differences.
  • Need a plan, not a number → lifted plan (upper bound on $W_2^2$) or sliced flow; neither gives the optimal map in general.
  • Test any claim → dimension one ($SW_p=W_p$), Diracs and translations (equality cases), the cross and the two diagonals of a square.

What comes next

Notes on the sources (things that could confuse you)
  • Slides, frame 12/12: a missing square root. The Monte Carlo error is bounded by $\big(\mathrm{Var}_\theta/k\big)^{1/2}$, not $\frac1k\mathrm{Var}_\theta$ (that is the mean squared error). Checks: scaling (the error scales like $\lambda^p$, $\mathrm{Var}$ like $\lambda^{2p}$), and measured errors $0.274$ ($k=1$) to $0.0077$ ($k=1024$) against $\mathrm{Var}/k=0.095$ to $0.00009$ on the cross (§8). OT4ML Remark 10.17 has the correct $R/\sqrt L$.
  • Slides, frame 7/12: “$d>2$” is for $W_1$. For $W_p$ the rate $n^{-1/d}$ needs $d>2p$ (StatOT (2.21), p. 89); two atoms give $n^{-1/4}$ for $W_2$ in $\R^3$, slower than $n^{-1/3}$ (§1, §13 Exercise 11, exact binomial sums).
  • Slides, frame 11/12: $n^{-1/(2p)}$ is a worst case. It is attained by measures with gaps (two atoms, Exercise 11); for the Gaussians of the slide’s own plot the observed rate is about $n^{-1/2}$ (§9).
  • Notes, Theorem 1 (b). The second line should be “$\le$”, not “$=$” (check with $\delta_{e_1},\delta_{e_2},\delta_{-e_1}$: $\sqrt2$ against $1.81$, §5).
  • Notes, Theorem 1 (c). In (9) the integral is over $\R$, not $\R^d$; and passing from “almost every $\theta$” (7) to every $\theta$ needs the continuity of $\theta\mapsto(p_\theta)_\sharp\mu$ (StatOT, proof of Thm. 2.25; OT4ML, proof of Prop. 11.24). Without it, a pair like the two diagonals of a square shows what finitely many directions miss (§5).
  • Notes, Proposition 1. In (14) the double bars around the number $\ps{x-y}{\theta}$ mean an absolute value; (15) and (18) are equalities (rotation invariance; Fubini). The notes’ $c_{d,p}$ equals OT4ML’s $\kappa_{d,p}$ (§6, §13 Exercise 5). The result is unchanged.
  • Notes, Remark 4. “Equivalent” means “same convergent sequences”: the reverse bound (21) involves the power $\frac1{d+1}$ of $SW_p$, so the two distances are not equivalent up to constants.
  • Two meanings of $\sigma$. In the notes (Remark 1, Proposition 2), $\sigma$ is the surface measure of the sphere (mass $\mathcal A(\mathbb S^{d-1})$); in OT4ML, StatOT and on this page, $\sigma$ is the uniform probability. On the sheet, $\sigma,\tau$ are standard deviations.
  • CompOT (10.13). The unit sphere of $\R^d$ is $\mathbb S^{d-1}$ (written $\mathbb S^d$ there); $\dd\theta$ is the surface measure, so (10.13) is $\abs{\mathbb S^{d-1}}$ times the $SW_2^2$ of the notes; and its formula for $n$-point clouds drops the weights $\frac1n$, a further factor $n$. Only the step size of (10.15) is affected.
  • Exercise sheet, Exercise 3 (a). “For any $\theta\sim\mathcal U_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}$” is to be read “for any $\theta\in\mathbb S^{d-1}$” (§12).
  • Slides, frame 4/12. $\mu=\frac1n\sum_ix_i$ should read $\frac1n\sum_i\delta_{x_i}$ (typographic). Notes (1): an extra parenthesis in $\dd((p_u)_\sharp)\mu)(s)$.
  • Dates. The paper that introduced the sliced distance (Rabin, Peyré, Delon, Bernot, texture mixing, SSVM) is dated 2011 on the slides (6/12 and references) and in OT4ML and CompOT, 2012 in the notes; slide 9/12 writes “2012: Introduction of Sliced-Wasserstein distance (Bernot et al.)” — OT4ML (p. 230) credits the idea to Marc Bernot and its first publication to Rabin et al. Same paper; no mathematical consequence.
  • OT4ML, p. 207. It announces that §11.2 studies the “Monte-Carlo approximation” of $SW$; the only Monte Carlo analysis is Remark 10.17 on the same page.
