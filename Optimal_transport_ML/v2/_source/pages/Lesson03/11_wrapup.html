11. Summary of the lecture

The lecture on one screen
  1. Kantorovich (§1): $\Lc(\al,\be)=\min_{\pi\in\U(\al,\be)}\int c\,\dd\pi=\min_{X\sim\al,Y\sim\be}\E[c(X,Y)]$; for $\norm{x-y}^2$ and $\al$ with a density, the optimal plan is a map $T=\nabla\varphi$ (Brenier).
  2. $W_p$ is a distance (§2): $W_p=\mathcal L_{d^p}^{1/p}$, $p\ge1$. Symmetry and positivity from $d$; $W_p=0\Rightarrow$ an optimal coupling with $X=Y$ a.s. $\Rightarrow\al=\be$; triangle inequality by gluing $S_{ijk}=\pi_{\al\be}(i,j)\pi_{\be\ga}(j,k)/b_j$, then triangle inequality of $d$, then Minkowski.
  3. Comparison (§3): on a compact space, $W_p\le W_q\le\diam^{\frac{q-p}q}W_p^{p/q}$ ($p\le q$). Same convergent sequences, different values and rates ($W_1=\eps$, $W_2=\sqrt\eps$).
  4. Weak convergence (§4): test against $C_b$ (weak), $C_0$ (weak-*), $C_c$ (vague); equivalent for probability limits; one notion on compact spaces. $W_p$ metrizes it on compact spaces; on $\R^d$, $W_p$-convergence = weak + convergence of $p$-th moments (escaping mass).
  5. Total variation (§5): $\norm{\al-\be}_\TV=\sup_{\norm{f}_\infty\le1}\int f\,\dd(\al-\be)$ = total mass that differs; for the 0/1 distance, $W_p^p=\frac12\norm{\al-\be}_\TV$. Diracs and Riemann sums never converge in TV ($=2$), they do in $W_p$; on finite spaces the two topologies coincide.
  6. Geodesics (§6): $\al_t=\pf{P_t}{\pi}$, $P_t(x,y)=(1-t)x+ty$, moves mass along straight lines; $W_2(\al_s,\al_t)=\abs{t-s}W_2(\al,\be)$; $(\Pc_2(\R^d),W_2)$ is a geodesic space; the mixture is not a geodesic. $\al_t$ minimises $(1-t)W_2^2(\al,\rho)+tW_2^2(\be,\rho)$.
  7. Barycenters (§7): $\min_\al\sum_k\lambda_k\Lc(\al,\be_k)$, the Fréchet mean of distributions. Diracs → Dirac at the mean; fixed grid → LP; on $\R^d$ with $W_2$: exists, unique if one input with positive weight has a density (Agueh–Carlier).
  8. Closed forms (§8): Gaussians → $\N\big(\sum\lambda_km_k,\Sigma^\star\big)$ with $\Sigma^\star=\Psi(\Sigma^\star)$, $\Psi(\Sigma)=\sum_k\lambda_k(\Sigma^{1/2}\Sigma_k\Sigma^{1/2})^{1/2}$; 1D → $C_\al^{-1}=\sum_k\lambda_kC_{\be_k}^{-1}$ (average quantiles).
  9. Algorithm (§9): $\al^{(\ell+1)}=\pf{\big(\sum_k\lambda_kT_k\big)}{\al^{(\ell)}}$; it decreases the cost by at least $W_2^2(\al^{(\ell)},\al^{(\ell+1)})$; barycenters satisfy $\sum_k\lambda_kT_k=\Id$. Exact in one step in 1D; on Gaussians it is the Álvarez-Esteban covariance update.

The reflexes to acquire

  • Upper bound on a transport cost → exhibit one coupling.
  • Coupling of $\al$ and $\ga$ from couplings through $\be$ → glue (conditionally independent given the middle).
  • Any new claim about $W_p$ → test it on Diracs first.
  • $p\ge1$ and a $p$-th root → Minkowski. A power $s^r$, $r\ge1$, of an average → Jensen.
  • Two transport costs with comparable ground costs → compare the integrands under a good coupling.
  • “$\al_n\to\al$” → against which test functions? On $\R^d$, check the moments (escaping mass).
  • Total variation → “mass that differs”; it ignores distances; mutually singular measures are at distance $2$.
  • Interpolate distributions → move positions along an optimal plan, do not mix weights.
  • Average distributions → barycenter = Fréchet mean; on the line, average quantiles; for Gaussians, means and covariances separate.
  • Barycenter condition → the average of the optimal maps from the barycenter is the identity.

Notes on the sources (things that could confuse you)
  • Exponent in the comparison of the $W_p$. CourseOT.pdf, Prop. 8, prints $W_p^{q/p}$; the correct exponent is $p/q$, as in your notes (§3).
  • Factor $\frac12$ for the 0/1 cost. Peyré–Cuturi, Remark 2.26, states $L_C(a,b)=\norm{a-b}_1$; the correct value is $\frac12\norm{a-b}_1$, as on the board (§5). Some books call $\frac12\norm{\al-\be}_\TV$ “the total variation distance”: check the convention.
  • “To check” on the board. $\norm{\delta_{x_n}-\delta_x}_\TV=2$ for $x_n\ne x$: checked three ways in §5.
  • The Gaussian iteration. The board’s $\Sigma\leftarrow\Psi(\Sigma)$ converges in practice (Peyré–Cuturi, Remark 9.5; and in all our tests) but no general proof is given there; the Álvarez-Esteban update $\Sigma\leftarrow\Sigma^{-1/2}\Psi(\Sigma)^2\Sigma^{-1/2}$ is proved to converge and is what the general algorithm of the board does on Gaussians (§8–§9).
  • Sheet “Continuous Kantorovich formulation”, Exercise 5. The boxed optimal cross-covariance should end with $\Sigma_\al^{-1/2}$, not $\Sigma_\al^{1/2}$, and the trace maximum is attained at $M=VU\T$, not $UV\T$ (checks in §10).
  • Agueh–Carlier “2010”. The paper appeared in 2011 (SIAM J. Math. Anal.); 2010 is the preprint.
  • Two meanings of $P_1$. In §1, $P_1,P_2$ are the first and second projections; in §6, $P_0,P_1$ are the endpoints of $P_t$ (time $0$ and $1$). Same letters, different indexing.
  • Two meanings of $\ga$. A third measure in the triangle inequality (§2), a path in the definition of a geodesic space (§6).
  • Other letters with two meanings on the board. $P$: the number of inputs, and the plans $P_k$ of the LP (§7). $B(x_1,\dots,x_P)$: the barycenter (§7); $\B$: the Bures distance (§8). $\varphi$: a test function (§4), a convex potential (Brenier), Jensen’s convex function (§3). $c$: the cost, and $c_k$ the weights of $\ga$ (§2). $f$: a test function, and the density of $\al-\be$ in the TV example (§5).
  • Two small slips in the notes, corrected on this page. In the comparison of the $W_p$ (§3) the powers $q$ are missing on $W_p$ and $W_q$ in the Jensen line; the uniqueness of the Gaussian fixed point (§8) holds among positive definite matrices ($\Psi(0)=0$).
