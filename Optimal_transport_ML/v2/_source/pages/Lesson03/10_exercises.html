10. Exercises

The two exercises of the sheet that belong to this lecture (TV and 0/1 cost; finite spaces) are solved in full in §5: Exercise 1 and Exercise 2. (Exercise 3 of the sheet, on $c$-transforms, belongs to the next lecture.) Here are more, graded from ★ (direct application) to ★★★ (needs an idea). Try before opening the hint; open the hint before the solution.

Exercise 1 — Diracs and atoms ★

On $\R$, compute (a) $W_p\big(\delta_0,\frac12\delta_{-1}+\frac12\delta_1\big)$ for every $p\ge1$; (b) $W_2\big(\frac12\delta_0+\frac12\delta_1,\ \frac12\delta_2+\frac12\delta_3\big)$.

Hint

(a) How many couplings are there with a Dirac? (b) On the line, the optimal plan is monotone.

Solution

(a) The only coupling sends half of the mass to $-1$ and half to $1$, both at distance $1$: $W_p^p=\frac12\cdot1+\frac12\cdot1=1$, so $W_p=1$ for every $p$. (b) Monotone plan: $0\to2$, $1\to3$, each with mass $\frac12$ and displacement $2$: $W_2^2=\frac12\cdot4+\frac12\cdot4=4$, $W_2=2$. (The crossed plan $0\to3$, $1\to2$ would cost $\frac12(9+1)=5$.)

Exercise 2 — Translations ★

Let $\al\in\Pc_p(\R^d)$, $v\in\R^d$ and $\tau_v(x)=x+v$. Show that $W_p\big(\al,\pf{\tau_v}{\al}\big)=\norm{v}$.

Hint

Upper bound: one coupling. Lower bound: $W_p\ge W_1$ (§3) and, for every coupling, $\norm{\E[X-Y]}\le\E\norm{X-Y}$.

Solution

Upper bound. With $X\sim\al$, the pair $(X,X+v)$ is a coupling of $\al$ and $\pf{\tau_v}{\al}$, of cost $\E\norm{v}^p=\norm{v}^p$. So $W_p\le\norm{v}$.

Lower bound. For any coupling $(X,Y)$ of the two measures, $\E[Y]-\E[X]=v$ (the means differ by $v$), so $\norm{v}=\norm{\E[Y-X]}\le\E\norm{Y-X}$ (triangle inequality for integrals). Minimising over couplings: $\norm{v}\le W_1\le W_p$. Hence $W_p=\norm{v}$: translating a distribution moves it, in Wasserstein distance, by exactly the length of the translation — whatever its shape.

Exercise 3 — The mechanics of gluing ★★

Let $\al=\frac13(\delta_0+\delta_1+\delta_2)$, $\be=\frac12(\delta_0+\delta_2)$, $\ga=\frac12(\delta_0+\delta_2)$ on $\R$, with the plans

$$P=\begin{pmatrix}1/3&0\\1/6&1/6\\0&1/3\end{pmatrix}\in\U(\al,\be),\qquad Q=\begin{pmatrix}1/4&1/4\\1/4&1/4\end{pmatrix}\in\U(\be,\ga).$$

Compute the glued tensor $S$ and the glued plan $R\in\U(\al,\ga)$. What do you notice? Is $Q$ optimal?

Hint

Compute $Q_{jk}/b_j$ first: it is the same for all $j,k$.

Solution

$b=(\frac12,\frac12)$ and $Q_{jk}/b_j=\frac{1/4}{1/2}=\frac12$ for all $j,k$. So $S_{ijk}=\frac12P_{ij}$ and $R_{ik}=\sum_jS_{ijk}=\frac12\sum_jP_{ij}=\frac12a_i=\frac16$ for all $i,k$: $R=a\,c\T$ is the independent plan. Gluing with an independent second plan forgets everything about the first one: “$Z$ is independent of $Y$, hence of $X$”. $Q$ is not optimal (the diagonal plan $\mathrm{diag}(\frac12,\frac12)$ costs $0$, since $\be=\ga$); the gluing construction works with any plans, but the triangle inequality only follows when they are optimal.

Exercise 4 — For $W_1$, the mixture is a geodesic ★

Let $m_t=(1-t)\delta_x+t\delta_y$ on $\R$ with $x\ne y$. Compute $W_1(m_s,m_t)$ and $W_2(m_s,m_t)$ for $s\le t$. Is $t\mapsto m_t$ a constant-speed geodesic for $W_1$? For $W_2$?

Hint

Keep the common part in place; how much mass must move, and how far?

Solution

The mass $t-s$ must move from $x$ to $y$, everything else stays: $W_1(m_s,m_t)=(t-s)\abs{x-y}$ and $W_2(m_s,m_t)=\sqrt{t-s}\,\abs{x-y}$. For $W_1$: $W_1(m_s,m_t)=(t-s)W_1(m_0,m_1)$, the definition of a constant-speed geodesic: yes. For $W_2$: no — $\sqrt{t-s}\ne t-s$, and the path has infinite length (§6). Geodesics of $W_1$ are not unique (the displacement $\delta_{(1-t)x+ty}$ is another one); this is one reason why $W_2$ is preferred for interpolation.

Exercise 5 — Two geodesics between the same two measures ★★

In $\R^2$, let $\al=\frac12\big(\delta_{(-1,0)}+\delta_{(1,0)}\big)$ and $\be=\frac12\big(\delta_{(0,-2)}+\delta_{(0,2)}\big)$. Find all optimal plans for $W_2$, and the corresponding displacement interpolations at $t=\frac12$.

Hint

Compute the four squared distances between the points of $\al$ and of $\be$.

Solution

All four squared distances equal $1+4=5$, so every coupling costs $5$: all couplings are optimal and $W_2^2=5$. With rows $(-1,0),(1,0)$ and columns $(0,-2),(0,2)$, the couplings are $P_s=\begin{pmatrix}s&\frac12-s\\\frac12-s&s\end{pmatrix}$, $s\in[0,\frac12]$: infinitely many optimal plans, and as many geodesics. At $t=\frac12$,

$$\al_{1/2}=s\big(\delta_{(-\frac12,-1)}+\delta_{(\frac12,1)}\big)+\big(\tfrac12-s\big)\big(\delta_{(-\frac12,1)}+\delta_{(\frac12,-1)}\big).$$

The two pairings ($s=\frac12$ and $s=0$) give two-atom midpoints; the independent plan ($s=\frac14$) gives the uniform law on the four points $(\pm\frac12,\pm1)$. Geodesics in the Wasserstein space need not be unique when the optimal plan is not (here, no density). Compare with §7: same phenomenon for barycenters.

Exercise 6 — The comparison of §3 is sharp for every pair $p\le q$ ★★

On $\X=[0,1]$, let $\al=\delta_0$ and $\be=(1-\eps)\delta_0+\eps\delta_1$. Show that $W_q(\al,\be)=W_p(\al,\be)^{p/q}$ for all $1\le p\le q$. Deduce that no inequality $W_q\le C\,W_p^{r}$ with $r>p/q$ can hold on $[0,1]$.

Solution

$W_p=\eps^{1/p}$, so $W_p^{p/q}=\eps^{1/q}=W_q$: equality in the right inequality (with $\diam=1$). If $W_q\le C\,W_p^r$ held with $r>p/q$, then $\eps^{1/q}\le C\eps^{r/p}$, i.e. $\eps^{1/q-r/p}\le C$ for all small $\eps$; but $\frac1q-\frac rp<0$, so the left side blows up as $\eps\to0$. Contradiction.

Exercise 7 — Shifting a density versus shifting a Dirac ★★

Compute $\norm{\N(0,1)-\N(\eps,1)}_\TV$ and $W_1\big(\N(0,1),\N(\eps,1)\big)$, and compare with $\delta_0$ versus $\delta_\eps$.

Hint

The two densities cross at $x=\eps/2$. Write $\Phi$ for the standard Gaussian CDF.

Solution

For $x<\eps/2$ the first density is larger, after it the second one. So $\norm{\cdot}_\TV=2\int_{-\infty}^{\eps/2}\big(\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-\eps)\big)\dd x=2\big(\Phi(\tfrac\eps2)-\Phi(-\tfrac\eps2)\big)=2\big(2\Phi(\tfrac\eps2)-1\big)\approx2\eps\,\varphi(0)\approx0.80\,\eps$ (for $\eps=0.1$: $0.0798$). By Exercise 2, $W_1=\eps$. Both go to $0$ linearly: for smooth densities, small shifts are small in TV too. For Diracs, $\norm{\delta_0-\delta_\eps}_\TV=2$ while $W_1=\eps$: the difference between the two topologies comes from singular (atomic) measures.

Exercise 8 — Moments through $W_2$ ★

Show that $W_2(\al,\delta_0)^2=\E\norm{X}^2$ for $X\sim\al$, and deduce $\big\lvert\sqrt{\E\norm{X}^2}-\sqrt{\E\norm{Y}^2}\big\rvert\le W_2(\al,\be)$ for $X\sim\al$, $Y\sim\be$.

Solution

The only coupling of $\al$ with $\delta_0$ sends $x$ to $0$: $W_2^2(\al,\delta_0)=\int\norm{x}^2\dd\al$. The reverse triangle inequality for the distance $W_2$ gives $\abs{W_2(\al,\delta_0)-W_2(\be,\delta_0)}\le W_2(\al,\be)$. This is the argument of §4 that $W_p$-convergence implies convergence of the $p$-th moments.

Exercise 9 — Gaussian barycenter with commuting covariances ★★

Compute the $W_2$ barycenter of $\N\big(0,\mathrm{diag}(1,4)\big)$ and $\N\big(0,\mathrm{diag}(9,1)\big)$ with weights $\lambda=(\frac14,\frac34)$, and check the fixed-point equation $\Psi(\Sigma^\star)=\Sigma^\star$.

Hint

For diagonal matrices everything happens coordinate by coordinate, as in 1D: average the standard deviations.

Solution

Coordinate 1: standard deviations $1$ and $3$, average $\frac14+\frac94=2.5$. Coordinate 2: $2$ and $1$, average $\frac12+\frac34=1.25$. So $\Sigma^\star=\mathrm{diag}(6.25,\ 1.5625)$ and the barycenter is $\N(0,\Sigma^\star)$. Check: for commuting matrices $(\Sigma^{1/2}\Sigma_k\Sigma^{1/2})^{1/2}=\Sigma^{1/2}\Sigma_k^{1/2}$, so $\Psi(\Sigma^\star)=\Sigma^{\star1/2}\big(\frac14\Sigma_1^{1/2}+\frac34\Sigma_2^{1/2}\big)=\mathrm{diag}(2.5,1.25)\,\mathrm{diag}(2.5,1.25)=\Sigma^\star$. ✓ (Also checked numerically with the general iteration.)

Exercise 10 — The commuting case in general ★★★

Assume that $\Sigma_1,\dots,\Sigma_P$ are positive definite and commute. Show that $\Sigma^\star=\big(\sum_k\lambda_k\Sigma_k^{1/2}\big)^2$ satisfies $\Psi(\Sigma^\star)=\Sigma^\star$, hence is the barycenter covariance.

Hint

Commuting symmetric matrices can be diagonalised in a common orthonormal basis; then all square roots are taken eigenvalue by eigenvalue.

Solution

Write $\Sigma_k=UD_kU\T$ with a common orthogonal $U$ and diagonal $D_k>0$. Then $S:=\sum_k\lambda_k\Sigma_k^{1/2}=U\big(\sum_k\lambda_kD_k^{1/2}\big)U\T$ and $\Sigma^\star=S^2$, so $(\Sigma^\star)^{1/2}=S$, which commutes with every $\Sigma_k$. Hence $(\Sigma^{\star1/2}\Sigma_k\Sigma^{\star1/2})^{1/2}=(S^2\Sigma_k)^{1/2}=S\,\Sigma_k^{1/2}$ (all diagonal in the basis $U$, with positive entries). Therefore $\Psi(\Sigma^\star)=\sum_k\lambda_kS\,\Sigma_k^{1/2}=S\cdot S=\Sigma^\star$. By the board’s proposition (uniqueness of the positive definite fixed point), $\Sigma^\star$ is the barycenter covariance.

Exercise 11 — A 1D barycenter by quantiles ★★

Compute the $W_2$ barycenter of $\be_1=\mathcal U_{[0,2]}$ and $\be_2=\frac12(\delta_0+\delta_2)$ with weights $\frac12,\frac12$. Draw it.

Hint

$C_{\be_1}^{-1}(t)=2t$; $C_{\be_2}^{-1}(t)=0$ for $t\le\frac12$ and $2$ for $t>\frac12$.

Solution

The average quantile function is $t$ for $t\le\frac12$ and $t+1$ for $t>\frac12$. It increases with slope $1$ on two intervals of $t$ of length $\frac12$, with a jump in between: the barycenter is the uniform density $1$ on $[0,\frac12]\cup[\frac32,2]$ (two blocks of mass $\frac12$). The Dirac at $0$ “pulls” the left half of the uniform halfway towards it and shrinks it by half; the same on the right. A mixture would have given the uniform plus two atoms.

Exercise 12 — One run of the fixed-point algorithm by hand ★★★

In $\R^2$, with weights $\frac12,\frac12$: $\be_1=\frac12\big(\delta_{(0,1)}+\delta_{(1,1)}\big)$, $\be_2=\frac12\big(\delta_{(0,-1)}+\delta_{(2,-1)}\big)$, and the starting guess $\al^{(0)}=\frac12\big(\delta_{(0,0)}+\delta_{(1,0)}\big)$. Run the point-cloud version of the algorithm (§9) until it stops. Check the descent inequality, and show that the result is the true barycenter.

Hint

For each input, compare the two possible assignments. For the last question, use the lower bound $t(1-t)W_2^2(\be_1,\be_2)$ of §6.

Solution

Step 1. To $\be_1$: $(0,0)\to(0,1)$ and $(1,0)\to(1,1)$ (cost $1+1$, against $2+2$ for the crossed assignment). To $\be_2$: $(0,0)\to(0,-1)$ and $(1,0)\to(2,-1)$ (cost $1+2$, against $5+2$). Averaging the partners: $(0,0)\mapsto(0,0)$, $(1,0)\mapsto\frac12(1,1)+\frac12(2,-1)=(1.5,0)$. So $\al^{(1)}=\frac12\big(\delta_{(0,0)}+\delta_{(1.5,0)}\big)$.

Costs. $F(\al^{(0)})=\frac12\cdot1+\frac12\cdot1.5=1.25$; $F(\al^{(1)})=\frac12\cdot1.125+\frac12\cdot1.125=1.125$; squared step length $\frac12\cdot0.5^2=0.125$. Descent: $1.125\le1.25-0.125$ (equality here). ✓

Step 2. The assignments do not change, and the averages are again $(0,0)$ and $(1.5,0)$: fixed point, the algorithm stops.

Optimality. Between $\be_1$ and $\be_2$ the optimal plan pairs $(0,1)\leftrightarrow(0,-1)$ (cost $4$) and $(1,1)\leftrightarrow(2,-1)$ (cost $5$): $W_2^2(\be_1,\be_2)=4.5$. By §6, every candidate has $F\ge\frac12\cdot\frac12\cdot4.5=1.125$. $\al^{(1)}$ attains it, so it is a barycenter (it is the midpoint of the geodesic).

Exercise 13 — Barycenters for $W_1$ are very non-unique ★★

On $\R$, find all the minimisers of $\al\mapsto\frac12W_1(\al,\delta_0)+\frac12W_1(\al,\delta_1)$.

Solution

By the Dirac computation of §7 the objective is $\int F\,\dd\al$ with $F(x)=\frac12\abs{x}+\frac12\abs{x-1}$. $F=\frac12$ on $[0,1]$ and $F>\frac12$ outside. So the minimisers are all probability measures carried by $[0,1]$: $\delta_{1/2}$, the mixture $\frac12(\delta_0+\delta_1)$, $\mathcal U_{[0,1]}$, … The quadratic cost ($W_2^2$) selects a unique answer, $\delta_{1/2}$. This is the median-versus-mean phenomenon of §7, lifted to distributions.
