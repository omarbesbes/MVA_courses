0. Before you start

Part III turns the observations of Part II (activations interpolate between concepts) into one hypothesis: an activation is a sum of convex combinations of archetypes, one convex combination per active tile. Everything here is geometry of convex sets; the reminders are on the spot.

How to read this page

Boxes marked In the paper (green-edged, “❝”) quote the paper with its page. Boxes marked “Our reading” (⚑) are our remarks, kept short; page 6 develops them with experiments. A remark box next to a proof marks a place where the paper's argument needed a correction. Proofs are in folds: read the idea box first, open the fold when you want every step. Page numbers are those of the arXiv v3 PDF.

The story in seven steps

  1. Why a geometry. A method sees only what its assumed geometry can express (§1).
  2. The definition. Archetypes grouped in tiles; each tile is a polytope; an activation is a sum of one point per active tile (§2).
  3. Why attention gives it. A softmax head outputs a convex combination of its values; the output projection is affine; the heads are added (§3).
  4. Evidence. Straight paths leave the data, per-image archetypal analysis reconstructs well, co-activation is block-structured (§4).
  5. Implications. Concepts are points and regions; steering saturates at an archetype; the decomposition is not identifiable from one layer (§5).
  6. Limits. A working hypothesis, observational, one model (§6).
  7. Summary and exercises (§7–8).
