2. The definition

Archetypes are grouped into tiles; each tile spans a polytope; an activation is the sum of one point per active tile, each point being a convex combination of the archetypes of its tile.

In the paper — Definition 2, Minkowski Representation Hypothesis §7 · p. 18 (App. J, Def. 3 · p. 46)

Let $\X\subset\R^d$ be a layer's activation space and $\vx\in\X$. Let $\Ac=(\va_1,\dots,\va_c)\in\R^{c\times d}$ be an overcomplete dictionary of archetypes ($c\gg d$). We partition the archetypes into $m$ disjoint tiles $\{\Tc_i\}_{i=1}^m$, $\Tc_i\subset\{1,\dots,c\}$, and define the tile polytopes $\Pc_i=\conv(\Ac_{\Tc_i})$ with $\Ac_{\Tc_i}=\{\va_j:j\in\Tc_i\}$. Then $\X$ satisfies MRH if

$$\begin{cases}\text{(i) Minkowski sum:} & \X=\bigmsum_{i=1}^m\Pc_i,\\[2pt] \text{(ii) Block-convex coding:} & \vx=\sum_{i\in S}\vz_i\Ac_{\Tc_i},\quad \vz_i\in\simplex^{|\Tc_i|},\quad |S|\ll m,\end{cases}$$

where $\simplex^k=\{\vz\ge0:\mathbf 1\T\vz=1\}$.

The text adds: “MRH says that a point $\vx$ is a sparse composition of concept regions, with only a few tiles active $|S|\ll m$, and each active tile contributing a convex combination of its archetypes.” And: “the activation set is generated by Minkowski addition of distinct tile polytopes, with a union over attention patterns when different tiles are selected across inputs, rather than a single global linear space.”

Decoding, symbol by symbol

$\Ac\in\R^{c\times d}$
$c$ points of $\R^d$, stored as rows: the archetypes, the candidate vertices. They are points, not directions.
tiles $\Tc_i$
a partition of the $c$ row indices into $m$ groups. $\Ac_{\Tc_i}$ is the sub-matrix of the rows of group $i$. The paper's picture: one tile for position, one for depth, one for object category.
$\Pc_i=\conv(\Ac_{\Tc_i})$
everything one can reach by mixing the archetypes of tile $i$ with non-negative weights summing to one: a polytope whose vertices are (some of) the archetypes of the tile.
(i) $\X=\bigmsum_i\Pc_i$
the set of all vectors $\vp_1+\dots+\vp_m$ with $\vp_i\in\Pc_i$: one point from every tile, added.
(ii) $\vx=\sum_{i\in S}\vz_i\Ac_{\Tc_i}$
$\vz_i$ is a row vector of length $|\Tc_i|$, so $\vz_i\Ac_{\Tc_i}\in\R^d$ is the point of $\Pc_i$ with barycentric coordinates $\vz_i$. Only the tiles in the active set $S$ contribute.
$\vz_i\in\simplex^{|\Tc_i|}$
non-negative coordinates summing to one inside each active block. The code vector $\vz=(\vz_1|\dots|\vz_m)$ has $m$ blocks, each block is either absent or a probability vector.
$|S|\ll m$
block sparsity: few tiles at a time. This is the sparsity of MRH, the counterpart of $|\supp\vz|\ll c$ in the LRH.
Reminder — Minkowski sum and simplex

$A\oplus B=\{\va+\vb:\va\in A,\vb\in B\}$. For polytopes, $\conv(A)\oplus\conv(B)=\conv(A\oplus B)$, so the vertices of the sum are among the pairwise sums of vertices. A point of $\simplex^k$ is a vector of weights; $\vz\Ac_{\Tc}$ is the weighted average of the archetypes. Minkowski sum, simplex and barycentric coordinates.

Worked example in the plane

Two tiles in $\R^2$: a triangle and a segment

$d=2$. Tile 1 has three archetypes $(0,0),(4,0),(0,2)$: $\Pc_1$ is a triangle. Tile 2 has two archetypes $(0,0),(1,2)$: $\Pc_2$ is a segment. Both tiles are active ($|S|=m=2$).

The paper's example: a rabbit token

In the paper §7 · p. 18

“A token representing a rabbit might combine: (1) a convex mix from an ‘animal category’ tile (capturing rabbit-like features), (2) a convex mix from a ‘spatial position’ tile (left/center/right), and (3) a convex mix from a ‘depth’ tile (foreground/background). The final activation is the sum of these convex contributions.”

In the notation: $m$ tiles in all, $S=\{\text{animal},\text{position},\text{depth}\}$, $\vz_{\rm animal}$ puts most weight on a rabbit-like archetype, $\vz_{\rm position}$ is, say, $(0.7,0.3,0)$ on (left, centre, right), $\vz_{\rm depth}$ is close to the foreground vertex. The tile “position” is the one Part II observed (a 2-D sheet, page 4); the others are the paper's guess (“it is natural to imagine one hull reflecting token position, another depth, another object or part category”). The paper does not measure these tiles.

Archetypal analysis is the one-tile case

Take $m=1$ (or $|S|=1$): $\X=\Pc_1=\conv(\Ac)$ and $\vx=\vz\Ac$ with $\vz\in\simplex^c$. This is archetypal analysis: every data point is a convex combination of archetypes (and archetypes are themselves convex combinations of data points, reminder). The paper uses this to test item (ii) (§4). MRH adds the sum over tiles and the block-sparse activation.

Our reading — (a) LRH is a special case of MRH: box against simplex

A bounded sparse non-negative linear code $\vx=\sum_{j\in S}z_j\vd_j$, $0\le z_j\le M$, is the MRH with segment tiles $\Tc_j=\{\mathbf 0,M\vd_j\}$: the block of tile $j$ is $(1-z_j/M,\,z_j/M)\in\simplex^2$. The two hypotheses are therefore nested, and what MRH adds is only the joint constraint inside a tile with three or more archetypes (the weights of one tile compete, they sum to one: a simplex instead of independent coordinates: a box). Developed in page 6.

Our reading — (b) item (i) and item (ii) describe different sets

The sum over all $m$ tiles in (i) is a convex set. Item (ii) uses few tiles, so the set actually described is the union $\bigcup_{|S|\ll m}\bigmsum_{i\in S}\Pc_i$: not convex in general (three segments $[0,\ve_j]\subset\R^3$ with $|S|\le1$ give three segments, whereas the sum of the three is the cube). This is what the paper's sentence “with a union over attention patterns” says; the guide reads (i) in that sense whenever it matters. Page 6.

Our reading — (c) a criterion (iii) that the definition does not number

Definition 2 has two items, but §7.2 refers to “criterion (iii)”, “the block-sparse pattern” and “the Tiles assumption”. It is the existence of the partition into tiles with block-wise activation: the part of (ii) that says $|S|\ll m$. Read “(iii)” as “the tile structure”.
