4. Empirical evidence: Figure 17

Three observations are offered as compatible with MRH: straight paths between tokens leave the data while graph paths do not, per-image archetypal analysis reconstructs tokens better than the 32k SAE with about ten archetypes, and the archetype co-activation matrix is block-structured. The authors call this “compatible evidence, not proof”.

In the paper — the caveat before the evidence §7.2 · p. 20

“We now provide preliminary support for each criterion. The results below should be read as compatible evidence, not proof: multiple mechanisms can mimic the same surface phenomena. Unless stated otherwise we use ImageNet-1k validation tokens from the last DINOv2-B layer; cosine distance and $k$-NN graphs with standard symmetrization.” And after the figure: “these observations — piece-wise linear on-manifold trajectories, efficient archetypal reconstructions, and block-structured co-activation — represent preliminary evidence rather than definitive proof. Multiple geometric hypotheses could generate similar surface patterns.” (The same text is repeated in App. J, “Limitations & Positioning”, p. 51.)

Left panel: straight chords leave the data, graph paths do not

How to read this panel
  • What was done. Take two tokens. Path 1: the straight segment between them, cut into 100 steps. Path 2: the shortest path between them in the $k$-nearest-neighbour graph of the tokens (symmetrised), a polygonal line through other tokens, also resampled into 100 steps. At each step, measure the distance to the nearest token of the data set. Curves: mean with a band (dark = linear, light blue = graph).
  • Axes. $x$: interpolation step, 0 to 100. $y$: distance to the nearest sample, 0 to about 14. Both curves are $0$ at the ends (the endpoints are samples).
  • Numbers. Linear: a triangle rising to about $13$ at step 50 (band roughly 12–14), back to $0$. Graph: rises to about $5$ by step 15, plateau around $4$–$4.5$, peaks again near $5$ at steps 15 and 85. The plot's distance is Euclidean (the text says cosine, whose maximum is 2: a mismatch of the paper).
  • What MRH predicts, according to the authors. “Feasible displacements arise as sums of small face-walks within head polytopes, which are piecewise linear in barycentric coordinates yet appear curved in the ambient space.” They read the graph paths as such face-walks and conclude that the curved on-support geodesics support criterion (i).
  • What it does not show. It does not show that the straight segment leaves the support: only that its midpoint is far from every sample. The authors' own caveat applies: other geometries give the same surface pattern.
Our reading — chords leave the sample, not the support

In high dimension the midpoint of two samples is far from every sample even when the support is convex (a Gaussian, the uniform distribution on a simplex), and the graph path is within half an edge of a sample by construction. Besides, a Minkowski sum of convex sets is convex, so “straight lines leave the data” cannot support (i). What separates the tokens from a Gaussian with the same covariance is local density (the nearest neighbour is at 0.35 chord against 0.80): the tokens are clustered. The chord does leave the ellipsoid of normalised tokens, but only slightly (10% of the half-chord), ten times less than the peak of the signature, which is a sampling effect. Checked on toy data and on DINOv2 in page 6.

Middle panel: archetypal analysis against the SAE

In the paper §7.2 · p. 20

“To test the convex coding assumption (ii), we compare Archetypal Analysis (AA) with SAE for token reconstruction. Note that AA is precisely the single-tile case of MRH ($|S|=1$), making this a direct test of our geometric assumptions. AA imposes dramatically stronger constraints: it forces all reconstructions to lie within the convex hull of observed tokens and requires archetypes to be actual data combinations. Despite these restrictive assumptions, AA matches SAE performance with remarkably few archetypes (10 archetypes per image)”; the fact that tokens are reconstructed from about 10 archetypes “indicates that embeddings concentrate on low-dimensional polytopes embedded within the ambient space”, as the probability that a random point lies in the hull of a small set of samples “decays exponentially with dimension”.

Footnote: “Archetypal Analysis seeks $\mZ=\mX\mB$ where archetypes are convex combinations of data (so $\mZ\subset\conv(\mX)$), and $\mX\approx\mZ\mA$ where data are convex combinations of archetypes. Both $\mA$ and $\mB$ have simplex constraints (columns sum to 1, non-negative).”

Reminder — decoding the footnote

In the footnote, $\mX$ is the data matrix (tokens as columns), $\mB$ writes each archetype as a convex combination of data points ($\mZ=\mX\mB$, so the archetypes are in the hull of the data), $\mA$ writes each data point as a convex combination of archetypes ($\mX\approx\mZ\mA$). Notation clash: here $\mZ$ is the archetypes and $\mA$ the coefficients, whereas in the main text $\mZ$ is the code matrix and $\mA$ the activations; and $\mX$ is the data here, the image batch elsewhere. The problem is $\min\|\mX-\mX\mB\mA\|_F^2$ over simplex-constrained $\mA,\mB$. More on AA.

How to read this panel
  • What was done. Per image, run AA with $K$ archetypes on its tokens and measure the mean squared reconstruction error; compare with the error of the SAE (dashed line).
  • Axes. $x$: number of archetypes $K\in\{3,5,10,20,30\}$. $y$: mean squared error.
  • Numbers. AA: $0.32$ ($K=3$), $0.26$ (5), $0.19$ (10), $0.138$ (20), $0.116$ (30). SAE: $0.227$. The curve crosses the dashed line between $K=5$ and $K=10$.
  • What MRH predicts. If activations are convex combinations of few archetypes, a convex model with few archetypes should reconstruct them well.
  • What the authors conclude. AA “matches SAE performance” with about ten archetypes per image although it is more constrained: preliminary evidence that the single-tile case captures structure.
  • What it does not show. The paper does not say how the SAE error is normalised, and the two models differ: a dictionary fitted to each image against one global dictionary of 32,000 atoms with eight active codes per token.
Our reading — AA is sandwiched between PCA and k-means

For $K$ archetypes the per-image AA error is bounded below by that of PCA with $K-1$ components (the reconstructions lie in a $(K-1)$-dimensional affine subspace) and above by that of $k$-means with $K$ clusters (the centroids are convex combinations of the data, so they are feasible). The informative question is where AA sits in this sandwich, compared with null data of the same covariance; the comparison with the global SAE is not like for like. Our errors reproduce the paper's up to ten archetypes (0.32, 0.25, 0.18 against 0.32, 0.26, 0.19) and are about 20% lower at $K=20$ and $30$. The lemma is about optimal fits; in our runs $k$-means initialises AA. Page 6.

Right panel: block-structured co-activation

How to read this panel
  • What was done. Fit archetypal analysis with about $1000$ archetypes; take the coefficient matrix $\mZ$ (one row per token, one column per archetype); form $\mZ\T\mZ$, whose entry $(i,j)=\sum_n z_{n,i}z_{n,j}$ is large when archetypes $i$ and $j$ are used by the same tokens (co-activation). Reorder archetypes by clustering. The paper does not say on which tokens the AA is fitted, nor which clustering is used.
  • Axes. Both axes: archetype index $0$ to about $1000$. Colour: the matrix entry (dark = 0, bright = large).
  • What to see. Bright blocks along the diagonal (from about $20$ to about $150$ archetypes wide, smaller towards the end), and dark between them; in particular two large dark rectangles away from the diagonal (indices up to about $650$ against indices above $650$, and the transpose): the first $650$ archetypes and the last $350$ are almost never co-activated.
  • What MRH predicts, according to the authors. Tiles imply block-structured decompositions: “even without knowledge of tile boundaries, archetypes naturally organize into co-activating groups”. They say this supports the Tiles assumption (called criterion (iii) in the text).
  • What it does not show. That the groups are simultaneously active tiles that add; the matrix also fits tokens that each use a single group (clusters).
Our reading — blocks mean clusters, not sums

A block-diagonal $\mZ\T\mZ$ for an AA with one simplex per token says that a token uses archetypes of one group: a union of polytopes. A Minkowski sum of simultaneously active tiles predicts the opposite: groups co-activate, with a Kronecker-like structure $\mG_1\otimes\mG_2$ across them (the sum admits the product code $\alpha_i\beta_j$; codes are not unique, but numerically 97% of the pairs co-activate). On DINOv2 the blocks largely follow the classes: this supports the hypothesis in its weakest form, one active group per token, and gives no sign of tiles that add up. Page 6.
