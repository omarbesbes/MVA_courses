1. Start here

The paper looks inside DINOv2 with a 32,000-atom dictionary, studies what tasks use and how concepts are arranged, then proposes that activations are sums of convex polytopes. This guide lets you explain all of it alone.

The paper in three sentences

The paper takes DINOv2 and extracts concepts from its activations with a sparse dictionary. It asks what the model uses for classification, segmentation and depth (Part I), what the statistics and geometry of the concepts look like (Part II), and finally argues that a better picture than a sparse set of directions is a sum of convex polytopes, the Minkowski Representation Hypothesis (Part III). Part III is presented as a working hypothesis.

2. The paper in one page

A question, a tool, three parts, and a story in eight steps.

The question. What does a large vision model such as DINOv2 represent in its last layer, which of these representations does each task use, how are they arranged in space, and is “a sparse set of directions” the right picture of that arrangement?

The working hypothesis and its tool. The Linear Representation Hypothesis (LRH, Def. 1): an activation is a sparse non-negative combination of many quasi-orthogonal directions, $\va=\vz\mD$ with $\mD\in\R^{c\times d}$, $c\gg d$ (superposition). To work with it the authors train a sparse dictionary of $c=32{,}000$ atoms on the $d=768$ activations of DINOv2 ViT-B with registers, with at most $k=8$ active codes per token: an archetypal sparse autoencoder whose atoms lie in the convex hull of the data. The codes $\mZ$ give the statistics, the atoms $\mD$ the geometry.

Part I (§3–4). Concept importance for a probe is $\E[Z_i]W'_{ij}$. Classification uses many scattered concepts, segmentation and depth few aligned ones (borders; three families of depth cues); “elsewhere” concepts fire off the object; register and cls tokens carry global scene properties.

Part II (§5–6). Concepts are not uniformly spread: a few dense positional concepts, a spectrum of atoms far from a Grassmannian frame, antipodal pairs, weak link between co-activation and geometry. Token clouds look connected and position is a low-dimensional sheet.

Part III (§7–8). The Minkowski Representation Hypothesis: activations are sums of points of convex polytopes built on archetypes. It follows from attention, and the evidence is “compatible”, not conclusive.

The story

  1. DINOv2 is a vision transformer whose tokens (cls, registers, patches) are 768-dimensional vectors; its features are used by linear probes for several tasks (page 1).
  2. The LRH says activations are sparse combinations of many nearly orthogonal directions; an archetypal SAE with 32,000 atoms and 8 active codes per token makes it testable (LRH setup).
  3. Composing the probe with the dictionary gives each concept an importance for each output, $\E[Z_i]W'_{ij}$ (importance).
  4. The three tasks use the dictionary very differently, with segmentation relying on border concepts and some concepts firing away from the object they depend on (tasks, elsewhere, borders, depth).
  5. Concepts differ by token type: footprints show position-specific concepts and register concepts about global scene properties (token types).
  6. The usage and the geometry of the dictionary are not those of a random or a Grassmannian frame: dense positional concepts, anisotropy, antipodal pairs (occurrence, geometry, antipodal).
  7. Inside an image, tokens form smooth, connected clouds, and position takes a low-dimensional share of them (PCA maps, position).
  8. Softmax attention outputs convex combinations of values; with several heads the output is a Minkowski sum of polytopes, which motivates the MRH (definition, theory).
  9. Three observations are reported as compatible with the MRH, and the authors state its limits (evidence, limits); how far they discriminate is our question (diagnostics).

Our reading — the overall verdict

Parts I and II are mostly measurements, solid as measurements, with visual evidence selected by the authors. Part III is the interesting and the weakest part: a clean mathematical statement about attention, and diagnostics that several simpler pictures would also produce. One result is positive: the position code is a genuine Minkowski sum of a row set and a column set (R² = 0.974), whose summands are arcs, not simplices. Page 6 gives the detail and how LRH sits inside MRH.

6. Notation

One table for the whole paper, with the letters the paper reuses.

The paper uses the row-vector convention: an activation is a row $\va\in\R^d$, the dictionary $\mD\in\R^{c\times d}$ has the atoms as rows, a code is a row $\vz\in\R^c$ and $\va=\vz\mD$; for all tokens, $\mA\approx\mZ\mD$.
