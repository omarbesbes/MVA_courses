1. The verdict, claim by claim

Parts I and II are measurements and are not contested. In Part III, the mathematics is correct where it is a theorem, the arguments for the hypothesis do not separate it from simpler pictures, and one consequence fails exactly after the last normalisation.

What the report does not contest

Parts I and II of the paper: which atoms each task uses, the Elsewhere and border concepts, the token-type footprints, the geometry of the dictionary (dense positional atoms, anisotropy, antipodal pairs, the smooth token clouds). They are measurements of a trained dictionary on a trained model, with figures one can inspect; the report only uses them. It also does not contest the mathematics of Lemma 1 and Proposition 1, and it agrees that the hypothesis is a legitimate and fruitful one, since it asks whether features compete inside groups, a question the linear hypothesis cannot even state.

What the report contests

Four things. (1) The definition of the hypothesis, which needs a repair, and its relation to the linear hypothesis, which it contains. (2) The inference from “attention outputs convex combinations” to “the representation is a Minkowski sum of polytopes fixed across images”. (3) The three empirical signatures, which data without archetypes also produce. (4) The identifiability consequence, whose proof has a gap and whose weaker true statement is about supports only. Each is a statement about the argument; none is a proof that tokens are not sums of convex combinations of something.

Reflex

When you see “evidence for the hypothesis” → ask which simpler picture produces the same figure, and whether the authors ran it. Most of this page is that question, asked three times.
