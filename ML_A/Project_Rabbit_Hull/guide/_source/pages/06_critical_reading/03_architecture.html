3. What the attention argument shows

“A head outputs a convex combination of its values, and heads add up” is a correct statement about softmax. It does not say that the representation is a Minkowski sum of fixed polytopes, for four reasons, each with a measured number.

In the paper Lemma 1, Prop. 1 · p. 19, App. J · p. 47–48

A head with attention weights $\val\in\simplex^t$ and values $\vv_1,\dots,\vv_t$ outputs $\sum_j\alpha_j\vv_j\in\conv\{\vv_j\}$; with $H$ heads and output projections $\mW_O^{(h)}$, the layer's output lies in $\bigmsum_{h=1}^{H}\mW_O^{(h)}\conv\{\vv_{h,j}\}_j$, because linear maps send hulls to hulls. The inclusion is an equality if the logits of each head can reach every vector of $\R^t$ up to a constant. Full statements and proofs: page 5.

We take this statement as correct (it is: softmax weights are positive and sum to one). The report asks what it implies for the tokens that the dictionary is trained on. Four observations delimit it. The measurements are on 100 Imagenette images with the trained model and its random-weights twin; the script is r4_attention_anatomy.py, the results r4_attention_anatomy.json (section 8).

1. The polytopes are rebuilt for every image

The values of head $h$ are $\vv_{h,j}=\mW_V^{(h)}\LN(\vx_j)$, where $\vx_1,\dots,\vx_t$ are the tokens of the current image at the previous layer. The polytope $\conv\{\vv_{h,j}\}_j$ is a function of the image. The statement says that the update of a token is a mixture of the other tokens of its image, seen through $H$ linear maps. It holds verbatim for a network with random weights and for an input of pure noise: it cannot by itself explain anything that DINOv2 has learned. The hypothesis is a stronger claim, that of a fixed dictionary of archetypes shared by all images; it would require the values of all images to concentrate near a common finite set, which the statement does not address.

2. Equality is out of reach

The logits of a head are $\mK\vq$, where the rows of $\mK\in\R^{t\times d_k}$ are the keys and $\vq\in\R^{d_k}$ the query. They span a space of dimension at most $d_k=64$ (65 with the softmax shift), while equality asks for $\rank\mK+1\ge t=261$. In addition the query of a token is tied to that token, so an image realises exactly $t$ points of each polytope.

Attention is also far from the sharp regime in which outputs sit on vertices (low temperature): a patch token attends on average to the equivalent of 33 to 78 tokens out of 261, and puts 10% to 49% of its mass, depending on the head, on the 5 special tokens (class and registers), which hold 5 of 261 tokens, about 2%.

What was measured, and what it does and does not show
  • Rank. Numerical rank of the key matrix of each head of the last block (singular values above $s_{\max}\cdot261\cdot\varepsilon_{\text{float32}}$), with and without a column of ones: 64 and 65 in every case. It shows that the condition of Lemma 1 fails by a factor 4; it does not show that the outputs are far from the polytope's interior, only that they cannot fill it.
  • Spread. For each head and token, the entropy of its attention row, exponentiated (“effective number of tokens”), averaged over images and patch tokens. 33 to 78 in the last block; 1.5 to 210 over all 144 heads. It shows that the outputs are averages over many values, not vertices.
  • Special-token mass. Share of the row on cls and the four registers: 10% to 49% in the last block. It shows that a large part of every output is the same few tokens; it does not show these tokens carry no information.

3. An update is not a token

The representation that is analysed is the residual stream, $\vx^{(L)}=\vx^{(0)}+\sum_{\ell}\va^{(\ell)}+\sum_\ell\vm^{(\ell)}$, where $\va^{(\ell)}$ are attention updates and $\vm^{(\ell)}$ outputs of MLPs, to which the attention statement does not apply. We verified the identity numerically (largest absolute error $3\times10^{-5}$ on entries of size up to 176) and projected each term on the final token.

The share of a term is $\langle\text{term},\vx^{(L)}\rangle/\|\vx^{(L)}\|^2$, pooled over tokens; the three shares sum to 1 exactly. Removing the mean token of each image is what isolates the geometry within an image, which is what the signatures study. More than half of that structure is therefore produced by maps that are not convex combinations. The last block alone carries 82% of the token (70% after centring).

What this does and does not show

It shows that “attention builds the geometry” is not the whole story for DINOv2-B: the MLPs contribute a larger share of the within-image variation than attention does. It does not show that MLPs destroy convex structure, and the projection is a linear accounting, not a causal one: the share of a term is not what would be lost by removing it.

4. All heads are active, always

With tiles equal to heads, every tile is active for every token: $|S|=H$, so there is no block sparsity (the hypothesis asks $|S|\ll m$). Across the 144 heads of the model, the participation ratio of the per-head update norms of a token is between 5 and 11 heads out of 12 in each block (vi_heads_per_token), and a large share of heads exceeds 10% of the largest update. The sparsity of the hypothesis would have to come from elsewhere.

Our reading — what survives

The attention statement gives a natural candidate for the “codes” of a decomposition: the weights $\alpha$. That makes the per-head analysis recommended by the paper a sensible place to look for tiles, with two caveats from this section: all heads are active, and the polytopes move with the image.

A colleague says: “attention outputs convex combinations, so the last layer is a Minkowski sum.” Give two independent objections.

Answer

(1) The polytopes are made of the current image's own tokens, so they change with every image and the statement holds for random weights; it does not give a fixed dictionary. (2) Tokens are residual sums of attention and MLP updates and then normalised; the within-image structure is 54% MLP, and the final LayerNorm is not affine. (Also: rank 64 against 261, so the polytope cannot be filled.)
