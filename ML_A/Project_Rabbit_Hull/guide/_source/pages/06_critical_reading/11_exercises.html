11. Exercises

Exercise 1 — Nested hypotheses with signed codes ★

Let $\vx=\sum_{j\in S}z_j\vd_j$ with $|z_j|\le M$. (a) Prove that $\vx$ is block-convex with tiles $\Tc_j=\{-M\vd_j,M\vd_j\}$. (b) Take $M=2$, $z_1=-0.5$, $\vd_1=(1,0)$: give the code of the tile. (c) Why is the tile $\{0,M\vd_j\}$ not enough for signed codes?

Hint

Look for $\lambda\in[0,1]$ with $(1-\lambda)(-M\vd)+\lambda M\vd=z\vd$.

Solution

(a) Put $\lambda_j=(z_j+M)/(2M)$, in $[0,1]$ because $-M\le z_j\le M$. Then $(1-\lambda_j)(-M\vd_j)+\lambda_j M\vd_j=(2\lambda_j-1)M\vd_j=z_j\vd_j$, a point of the tile with code $(1-\lambda_j,\lambda_j)$ in $\simplex^2$. Summing over $S$ gives block-convex coding with the same active set.

(b) $\lambda_1=(-0.5+2)/4=0.375$, code $(0.625,0.375)$: $0.625\,(-2,0)+0.375\,(2,0)=(-0.5,0)$.

(c) The segment from $0$ to $M\vd_j$ only contains non-negative multiples of $\vd_j$; a negative $z_j$ is outside it.

Exercise 2 — Depth of a chord ★

Tokens after layer normalisation in $d=768$ have $\|\vu\|=\sqrt d$. Two tokens subtend $\theta=90^\circ$ in $\vu$-space. (a) Compute the depth of the midpoint of their chord below the sphere, the half-chord, and their ratio. (b) Same for $\theta=56^\circ$ (the mean of tokens of one image). (c) Why is the measured exact depth on the real ellipsoid smaller than (b)'s ratio?

Hint

The midpoint has norm $\sqrt d\cos(\theta/2)$; the half-chord is $\sqrt d\sin(\theta/2)$.

Solution

(a) $\sqrt{768}=27.71$. Depth $=(1-\cos45^\circ)\cdot27.71=8.12$; half-chord $=27.71\sin45^\circ=19.60$; ratio $\tan22.5^\circ=0.414$.

(b) Depth $=(1-\cos28^\circ)\cdot27.71=3.24$; half-chord $=27.71\sin28^\circ=13.01$; ratio $\tan14^\circ=0.249$. (The measured ray-based ratio on DINOv2 is $0.231$, matching.)

(c) The real set is an ellipsoid with axis ratio 78; the nearest point of an elongated surface to the midpoint is nearer than the radial projection, so the exact depth is $10\%$ of the half-chord, not $23$–$25\%$.

Exercise 3 — The sandwich on a tiny data set ★★

Data: $(0,0),(2,0),(4,0),(1,1.5),(3,1.5)$, $K=2$ (errors are sums of squares). (a) Compute the error of PCA with one component and of the best $k$-means with two centroids. (b) Show that the pair of archetypes $(0.5,0.5)$, $(3.5,0.5)$ is feasible for archetypal analysis and compute its error. (c) Check the order of the lemma.

Hint

PCA: the mean is $(2,0.6)$; the first singular direction is the $x$ axis up to a small tilt (use a numerical SVD or compare with the value in the solution). Archetypes must lie in the convex hull of the data; reconstruct each point by its projection on the segment between the archetypes.

Solution

(a) PCA$_1$: $2.70$ (from the singular values of the centred matrix). $k$-means: clusters $\{(0,0),(2,0),(1,1.5)\}$ and $\{(4,0),(3,1.5)\}$, centroids $(1,0.5)$ and $(3.5,0.75)$, error $3.5+1.625=5.125$.

(b) The hull has the left edge $x=\tfrac23y$ and the right edge $x=4-\tfrac23y$; at height $0.5$ it spans $x\in[0.33,3.67]$, so $(0.5,0.5)$ and $(3.5,0.5)$ are inside. Projections on the segment $y=0.5$, $x\in[0.5,3.5]$: $(0,0)\to(0.5,0.5)$, error $0.5$; $(2,0)\to(2,0.5)$, $0.25$; $(4,0)\to(3.5,0.5)$, $0.5$; $(1,1.5)$ and $(3,1.5)$ project straight down: $1$ each. Total $3.25$.

(c) $2.70\le3.25\le5.125$. Better archetypes (a numerical search finds about $2.97$) still satisfy $2.70\le2.97$.

Exercise 4 — A sum gives a dense matrix, a union gives blocks ★★

Two tiles are segments $[\va_1,\va_2]$ and $[\vb_1,\vb_2]$. A token has codes $\val=(0.5,0.5)$ and $\vbe=(0.25,0.75)$. (a) Give its code over the vertex sums $\va_i+\vb_j$ and show that its co-activation matrix has no zero. (b) A union token uses only the first tile with code $(0.5,0.5)$ over $(\va_1,\va_2,\vb_1,\vb_2)$: give its matrix. (c) Show that for the sum the matrix is a Kronecker product.

Hint

One admissible code over the sums is $\val\otimes\vbe$; the co-activation of one token is its code times its transpose.

Solution

(a) $\val\otimes\vbe=(0.125,0.375,0.125,0.375)$, which sums to 1. Its outer product has entries $w_aw_b>0$ for every pair: no zero (the smallest is $0.0156$).

(b) The code is $(0.5,0.5,0,0)$ and the matrix is $\begin{pmatrix}0.25&0.25\\0.25&0.25\end{pmatrix}$ in the top-left block, zero elsewhere.

(c) $(\val\otimes\vbe)(\val\otimes\vbe)\T=(\val\val\T)\otimes(\vbe\vbe\T)$ by the mixed-product property of the Kronecker product.

Exercise 5 — Another segment violates subadditivity ★★

$\X$ is the unit equilateral triangle of the text, $Q=\{0\}\times[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]$ (vertical), $s=h_Q$, $f=h_\X-s$. Take $\vu=(\cos30^\circ,\sin30^\circ)$ and $\vv=(0,-1)$. Compute $f(\vu)$, $f(\vv)$, $f(\vu+\vv)$ and the violation of subadditivity. Does it depend on $\varepsilon$?

Hint

$s(\vw)=\varepsilon|w_2|$. The vertices are $(\pm\tfrac12,-0.2887)$ and $(0,0.5774)$.

Solution

$h_\X(\vu)=0.2887$ (vertices $(0.5,-0.2887)$ and $(0,0.5774)$ both give $0.2887$), $h_\X(\vv)=0.2887$, $\vu+\vv=(0.866,-0.5)$ and $h_\X(\vu+\vv)=0.5774$ at the vertex $(0.5,-0.2887)$. With $s(\vu)=0.5\varepsilon$, $s(\vv)=\varepsilon$, $s(\vu+\vv)=0.5\varepsilon$: $f(\vu)=0.2887-0.5\varepsilon$, $f(\vv)=0.2887-\varepsilon$, $f(\vu+\vv)=0.5774-0.5\varepsilon$. So $f(\vu+\vv)-f(\vu)-f(\vv)=\varepsilon>0$ for every $\varepsilon>0$ (for $\varepsilon=0.1$: $0.527$ against $0.428$). Subadditivity fails, so $s$ is not a summand. The violation equals $\varepsilon$, as for the horizontal segment.

Exercise 6 — Stable rank against explained variance, and the null of the additive fit ★★★

(a) Show that a centred matrix whose top-2 principal components explain a share $R^2$ of the variance has stable rank at most $2/R^2$; apply it to $R^2=0.99$ and to the reported stable rank 2.6–2.8 of Fig. 14. (b) A $16\times16$ table has $255$ degrees of freedom after centring and the additive model has $30$: what $R^2$ does an unstructured table get on average, and why? (c) Explain why the held-out $R^2$ of the additive fit of the position table is compared with a ceiling.

Hint

(a) $\sigma_1\ge\sigma_2$. (b) The additive fit is the orthogonal projection on a subspace of dimension 30 of a space of dimension 255; for an isotropic random table the projected share of squared norm is the ratio of dimensions.

Solution

(a) $\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2=R^2\|\mP\|_F^2$ and $\sigma_1^2\ge\tfrac12(\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2)$, so stable rank $=\|\mP\|_F^2/\sigma_1^2\le2/R^2$. For $R^2=0.99$: $2.02$. A value of $2.6$–$2.8$ contradicts it, so the two panels cannot describe the same centred matrix.

(b) $30/255=11.8\%$: for a table with isotropic random entries, the projection onto a fixed 30-dimensional subspace keeps on average $30/255$ of the squared norm. So $97.4\%$ is far above chance.

(c) The table of each half of the images is a noisy estimate of the true table. Even the exact table of one half explains only part of the other's variance ($95.2\%$): this is the best any model can do. The additive fit reaches $94.7\%$, so it is within $0.5$ point of the maximum.
