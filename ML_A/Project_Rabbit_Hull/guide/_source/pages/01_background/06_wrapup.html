6. Summary and reflexes

The setting in ten lines
  1. DINOv2 ViT-B/14 with 4 registers turns a 224×224 image into $t=261$ tokens of dimension $d=768$ (256 patches + cls + 4 registers).
  2. One attention head outputs $\mY_i=\sum_j\alpha_{ij}\mV_j$, a weighted average of values; 12 heads of dimension 64 add up through $\mW_O$.
  3. A block adds an attention update and an MLP update to the residual stream, each after LayerNorm (pre-norm), with LayerScale.
  4. DINOv2 is trained by self-distillation (DINO + iBOT) with a KoLeo regulariser; features are used frozen with linear probes.
  5. Neurons are polysemantic; features are stored in superposition as nearly orthogonal directions.
  6. LRH: $\va=\vz\mD$, $c\gg d$, small coherence, sparse $\vz$.
  7. Dictionary learning: $\mA\approx\mZ\mD$; k-means, PCA, NMF, AA, SAE differ by constraint.
  8. SAE: encoder + TopK/BatchTopK; quality is $R^2$.
  9. Archetypal SAE: $\mD=\mS\mC$, atoms in the convex hull of 128,000 centroids; $c=32{,}000$, $k=8$, 1.4M images, 50 epochs, $R^2>88\%$.
  10. Heat maps show where, feature visualisation shows an exciting image; DinoVision gives both.
  • When you see $\softmax(\cdot)\mV$ → a point of the convex hull of the values.
  • When you see "a concept fires here" → ask whether that says where the concept depends on.
  • When you see $\mA\approx\mZ\mD$ → rows of $\mD$ are concepts, rows of $\mZ$ say which fire on each token.
  • When you see an SAE → ask which sparsity rule, which constraint on $\mD$, which $R^2$.
  • When you see an external fact about DINOv2 → check whether it is from the paper or from the DINOv2 files.
