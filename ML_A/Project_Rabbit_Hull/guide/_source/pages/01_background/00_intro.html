Before you start

The paper (§1–2) states in a few lines what this page unfolds: a model (DINOv2), a hypothesis (linear representation), a tool (an archetypal sparse autoencoder) and a way to look at the result (heat maps, visualisations). Nothing on this page is a result of the paper; it is the setting of all of them.

How to read this page

Sections 1–2 describe the network. Section 3 explains why one looks for concepts. Section 4 explains how they are extracted. Section 5 shows how to look at them. Facts about other papers were checked against their primary sources; where the paper is the only source, the text says "according to the paper". Every number not in the paper is computed by checks/check_01.py.

The story of the page in six steps

  1. Tokens. An image becomes a sequence of 261 vectors of dimension 768 (§1).
  2. Layers. Each block adds an attention update and an MLP update to every token; the sum is the residual stream (§1.3).
  3. Training. DINOv2 learns by self-distillation; its features are used frozen, with linear probes (§2).
  4. Concepts. Neurons mix many things; a representation can hold more features than dimensions if they are sparse (§3).
  5. Dictionary. Factorise the activations as $\mA\approx\mZ\mD$ with sparse $\mZ$; the paper uses an archetypal SAE (§4).
  6. Looking. Heat maps, visualisations, and the DinoVision demo (§5).
