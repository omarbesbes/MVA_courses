7. Token-type-specific concepts

Where on the 261 tokens does a concept fire, on average? Most concepts are spread out; a tail is localised (a position, the cls token, or the four registers), and the register-only ones encode global scene properties.

The problem

A ViT has three kinds of token (tokens): 256 patches tied to places, one cls token and four registers that read and write global information. Are some concepts reserved for one kind?

The footprint and its entropy

Footprint §4 · p. 9 and App. G · p. 39

Reshape $\mZ$ as a tensor $\R^{n\times t\times c}$. For concept $i$ the footprint is $$\vom_i=\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mZ_{n,:,i}\in\R^{t}.$$ "$\mZ_{n,:,i}$ is the concept map (a vector of 261 scalars) of concept $i$ on input $n$." The paper then measures "the entropy of its empirical footprint distribution": low entropy, localised; high entropy, "spatially uniform".

$\mZ_{n,:,i}$
the 261 codes of concept $i$ on image $n$: 1 for cls, 4 for registers, 256 for patches.
$\vom_i$
their average over $N$ images: the mean activation of the concept at each token position (a $16\times16$ map plus 5 special values).
entropy
To be an entropy, $\vom_i$ must be turned into a probability vector, $p_l=\omega_{i,l}/\sum_{l'}\omega_{i,l'}$, then $H=-\sum_l p_l\ln p_l$ (recall). The paper does not write this normalisation or the logarithm base; the range of Fig. 7 (up to about 5.5) fits natural logarithms, $\ln261=5.565$.

Reference values, computed by checks/check_03.py for footprints that are uniform on a set of $m$ tokens (then $H=\ln m$):

The maximum $\ln261=5.565$ is hardly smaller than $\ln256=5.545$: the entropy hardly sees the five special tokens, a concept that is uniform on patches and absent on specials is at 5.545. $e^{H}$ can be read as an effective number of tokens.

Fig. 7: the histogram and the bar chart

How to read this figure
  • Histogram (x: entropy 0–5.5; y: number of concepts, log). A spike at 0 of about 110 concepts (single-token footprints), a sparse scatter of 1–10 concepts per bin between 0.3 and 3, then a rise to the bulk: at 4 about 30–100 per bin, at 5 about $10^3$, peak around 5.2–5.5 with several thousand per bin. Compare with the table above: 0 is a single token, 4–4.5 is a third of the image, the bulk is "nearly uniform".
  • Token maps ($16\times16$ patch grid, colour = mean activation). Low entropy: a single blob, a vertical or horizontal strip, a border. Medium: a region (centre, left, bottom). High: nearly flat, with texture.
  • Not shown: the special tokens (maps show patches only), and which entropy cut defines "low".
  • A footprint with all its mass on one token has $H=0$; on the four registers equally, $H=1.386$. So the spike at 0 means single-token concepts (for instance cls-only, or one fixed patch); register-only concepts, which spread over four tokens, would sit at or below 1.386, in the sparse part of the histogram. The paper does not say which bins hold them.

The three categories

Footprint types §4 · p. 9–10
  1. Position-specific concepts on narrow regions ("left-only", "bottom-only"): "residual positional encoding, biases in the training data, or local geometric primitives".
  2. A unique cls-only concept that fires "persistently on the cls token across all images", "closely tied to the positional embedding of the cls token", maybe an "ID" or "passport".
  3. reg-only concepts: a "much more diverse" group, whose variety "cannot be explained by position alone", encoding "non-spatial features".
Remark — "one" or "about 30"?

The text (§4, App. G, caption of Fig. 21) says a single concept is cls-only. The bar chart, on a log axis, shows a cls-only bar at about 30, a reg-only bar at about 150 and a spatial-only bar at about $3\times10^4$ ($\approx$ 31.8k reading the figure). Both cannot be true with the same definition of "only". The paper does not define the threshold for "exclusively". The order of magnitude of "hundreds" for registers is roughly matched (about 150). We cannot resolve this from the paper.

Fig. 8: what register-only concepts are about

How to read this figure

Each concept is shown by images that maximally activate it; there is no token map, since the concept is not about a place. What they have in common is global: motion blur, lighting and shadows, caustic reflections, wide-angle warping or depth of field, and artistic or rendering style. Authors: registers act as "a conduit for abstract scene-level information". Not shown: how these concepts were chosen among the hundreds, and whether they matter for any task of section 2. Link with the registers' purpose: registers were added to ViTs to hold global computation instead of letting patch tokens be hijacked for it; here the concepts they host are global properties, consistent with that purpose.

Our reading

Two readings of the register-only concepts are compatible with the figure: registers hold global scene information (the paper's reading) or they are a place where the model stores attributes of the whole image that patch tokens also see weakly. The paper tests neither. See page 6.
