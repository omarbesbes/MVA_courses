Before you start

Part I of the paper contains no theorem about geometry. It builds a dictionary of 32,000 concepts, asks which ones three tasks use, and looks at where concepts fire. Everything in parts II and III reuses this dictionary.

How to read this page

Each section starts with a one-line summary, then quotes the paper in a paper box (with the page of the arXiv v3 PDF) and decodes it. Figures of the paper are followed by a "How to read this figure" box. Our own remarks are in Our reading boxes and are developed on page 6. The story of the page in seven steps

  1. Hypothesis. A token activation is a sparse non-negative combination of a few unit "atoms" among 32,000 (§2, Def. 1).
  2. Dictionary. A stable (archetypal) sparse autoencoder gives codes $\mZ$ and atoms $\mD$ with $\mA\approx\mZ\mD$.
  3. Importance. A linear probe $\mW$ on $\mA$ becomes a probe $\mW'=\mD\mW\T$ on the codes; the score of concept $i$ for output $j$ is $\E[Z_i]W'_{ij}$.
  4. Tasks. Classification uses many concepts, segmentation and depth few; the top concepts of a task are mutually aligned and span a low-dimensional subspace (Figs. 2, 3).
  5. What they look like. "Elsewhere" concepts for classification, border concepts for segmentation, monocular cues for depth (Figs. 4–6).
  6. Token types. Some concepts fire only on one kind of token: position-specific, cls-only, register-only (Figs. 7, 8).
  7. Hand-over. The statistics of $\mZ$ and the geometry of $\mD$ are the subject of page 4.
