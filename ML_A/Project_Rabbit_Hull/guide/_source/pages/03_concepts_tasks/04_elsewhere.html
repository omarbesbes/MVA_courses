4. "Elsewhere" concepts

Some of the most important concepts for an ImageNet class fire on tokens that are not on the object, and only when the object is somewhere in the image: a concept map and a causal map of the same concept point at different places.

The problem

Concept heat maps colour the tokens where a concept is active, and readers take this to mean "the concept is about this region". That assumption is not guaranteed. In a ViT, a token's activation depends on all other tokens through attention (recall), so a token can carry information about something located elsewhere.

"Elsewhere" concepts §3 · p. 7 and App. C.2 · p. 34

"Across a wide range of ImageNet classes, the top few most important concepts typically include not only interpretable objects or object-parts, but also an 'Elsewhere' concept": these "activate broadly across the tokens, but crucially not on the object itself", yet "their firing depends on the object's presence, and vanish if the object is removed". They express "not the object, but the object exists". They warn that heat maps "violate the unspoken assumption: the concept is just about the token where the concept fires".

How to read this figure
  • Token map (middle row): colour where the concept's code $Z_i$ is large, per patch. For these concepts the colour is on the surroundings and avoids the animal.
  • RISE map (bottom row): colour where masking the image changes the concept's activation. It lights up the animal itself.
  • The two maps disagree: where the concept fires is not what it depends on. That is the whole point.
  • Not shown: how many concepts are of this type (the paper says "recurring" and "for several classes"), and no number quantifies the effect; the object-removal claim is visual.

The RISE formula, decoded

Causal attribution App. C.2 · p. 34
$$\Gamma^{(i)}_{\text{RISE}}(f,\vx)=\E_{\vm\sim\mathbb{P}_{\vm}}\big(f(\vx\odot\vm)\mid \vm_i=1\big)$$

$f$ is "the composition of the model and the SAE", with 8000 forward passes per explanation.

$\vm$
a random binary (or soft) mask of the image, drawn from a distribution $\mathbb{P}_{\vm}$; $\odot$ multiplies it pixel-wise with the image $\vx$, hiding the parts where $\vm=0$.
$f(\vx\odot\vm)$
here: the activation of the concept of interest on the masked image (the concept is the output of model + SAE).
$\vm_i=1$
conditioning on pixel (or region) $i$ being visible.
$\Gamma^{(i)}$
average concept activation over the masks that keep location $i$. If keeping $i$ is what the concept needs, the average is high. Recall RISE. Estimated with 8000 masks.

Reading the logic: the object region gets a high $\Gamma$, because masks that hide the object kill the concept even if they keep the places where it fires. The paper's conclusion: "the object or animal itself is the most causally important region responsible for concept firing, even though the concept manifests its activation in spatially disjoint locations".

Why this is possible and useful

  • Attention makes it possible. A patch token far from the object can read the object through attention and copy a summary of it into its own residual stream, so its activation encodes "the object is somewhere in the image".
  • Pooling makes it useful. The classifier takes cls and the mean of the patch tokens. A mean does not care where the evidence sits: class evidence spread over off-object tokens still reaches the logit. The paper adds that this can improve robustness to occlusion and viewpoint (App. C.2), a plausibility argument that it does not test.
  • Implication for heat maps. A concept's token map shows where it is expressed, not what it is about. The paper recommends combining localisation with causal perturbation.
Our reading — what is established

The figure shows a few examples from three classes. The paper's statements "implicitly learns a form of fuzzy spatial logic" and "fundamental computational strategy" go beyond that: no frequency of Elsewhere concepts, no random-concept control and no ablation of such a concept on the class score is given. The simplest alternative explanation of the token maps is a concept that reads global context from the registers or cls token. Page 6.

Reflex

When you see a concept heat map → ask where it fires and what it depends on; the two can be disjoint.
