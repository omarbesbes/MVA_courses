5. Segmentation uses border concepts

All of the 50 most important concepts for segmentation fire along object contours, whatever the object, and form one tight cluster in the UMAP of atoms.

Segmentation and border concepts §3 · p. 7–8

"For segmentation, we observe that all the concepts among the top-50 consistently localize along object contours or spatial boundaries ... the precise visual features vary across classes (animal ears, tower edges, sea urchin spines), the spatial footprints remain strikingly consistent. In the concept embedding space, these border concepts form a visibly tight cluster."

How to read this figure
  • Overlays: colour is the concept's activation per token, on the image. Look at where: along limbs, heads, domes and rooflines, i.e. at silhouettes.
  • Right panel: the UMAP of atoms; the border concepts sit together.
  • Numbers: top-50; cosine and spectrum statistics are in Fig. 3 (segmentation: cosine peak near 0.07, tail to 0.6; top eigenvalue about 0.1–0.16) and Fig. 19 (an arc).
  • Not shown: that the 51st concept is not a border concept, and how the visualised examples were selected. The statement "all top-50" rests on the authors' inspection.

Intuition (ours, not the paper's): a segmentation head must assign a label to every patch, and the hardest patches are at boundaries; a "narrow, outline-shaped" footprint needs the token to know it is next to something else. The paper concludes that DINOv2 "allocates a dedicated region of its representational geometry to encoding object boundaries". "Dedicated region" is supported by the cluster (UMAP, Fig. 5) and by the higher cosines and faster spectral decay (Fig. 3), both for the top-100 and not the top-50.
