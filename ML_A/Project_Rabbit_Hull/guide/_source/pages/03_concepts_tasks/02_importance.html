2. Concept importance for a task

A linear probe on activations is, in the concept basis, a linear probe on codes with weights $\mW'=\mD\mW\T$; the importance of concept $i$ for output $j$ is its mean code times its weight, $\E[Z_i]\,W'_{ij}$.

The problem

The model has been trained on tasks (classification, segmentation, depth) through simple heads. We have 32,000 concepts. Which of them does a given head use? The head is linear, the concepts are linear directions, so the question can be answered exactly by one matrix product, with no new training.

Derivation

Importance of concepts §3 · p. 6 and App. C.1 · p. 33

For a downstream prediction $\mY=\mA\mW\T$ with $\mW\in\R^{o\times d}$, $o$ being the number of outputs, one has $\mY=(\mZ\mD)\mW\T=\mZ\mW'$ with $\mW'=\mD\mW\T$. "This formulation provides a direct measure of each concept's importance through $\E(\mZ\mW')=\E(\mZ)\mW'$, which represents the expected concept activation weighted by task-output alignment." In linear regimes this is "the optimal importance measure under standard faithfulness criteria (Theorem 3 in Fel et al. 2023)".

  1. $\mY=\mA\mW\T$ the probe: each row of $\mA$ (one token) gives one row of $\mY$ ($o$ scores). Shapes: $(nt\times d)(d\times o)=nt\times o$.
  2. $\mA\approx\mZ\mD$ the dictionary reconstruction; $\mZ$ is $nt\times c$, $\mD$ is $c\times d$.
  3. $\mY\approx\mZ\mD\mW\T$ substitute. Here the approximation sign hides the reconstruction error $(\mA-\mZ\mD)\mW\T$, which the paper drops.
  4. $\mY\approx\mZ\,(\mD\mW\T)=\mZ\mW'$ associativity. $\mW'=\mD\mW\T$ has shape $(c\times d)(d\times o)=c\times o$.
  5. $Y_{n,j}=\sum_{i=1}^c Z_{n,i}\,W'_{ij}$ write the product entry by entry: output $j$ of token $n$ is a sum over concepts of (code) × (weight). Only $\le k=8$ terms are non-zero.
  6. $\E[Y_{\cdot,j}]=\sum_i \E[Z_i]\,W'_{ij}$ linearity of expectation (over tokens, and images). The $i$-th summand is the contribution of concept $i$ to the mean output $j$: this is the score.
$W'_{ij}=\mD_i\cdot\mW_j$
alignment of atom $i$ with the probe row $j$ (the direction in activation space that raises output $j$). Large and positive: using concept $i$ pushes output $j$ up.
$\E[Z_i]$
mean code of concept $i$: frequency × typical intensity (including zeros, since the code is 0 when the concept is off).
$\E[Z_i]W'_{ij}$
importance of concept $i$ for output $j$; a signed number.
Reminder — why "gradient × input"

For a function $f$ and an input $x$, gradient × input attributes to coordinate $i$ the number $x_i\,\partial f/\partial x_i$ (more). Here $f=Y_j$ is, as a function of the codes, $\sum_i z_iW'_{ij}$: linear, so $\partial Y_j/\partial z_i=W'_{ij}$ and gradient × input is $z_iW'_{ij}$. Averaging over tokens gives $\E[Z_i]W'_{ij}$. So the score is gradient × input in the concept basis, averaged. For a linear function this also equals the Integrated Gradients with a zero baseline and the occlusion score (replace $z_i$ by 0); the paper states this in App. C.1. RISE (more) averages over random masks and for a linear model is proportional to it.

The optimality claim

The paper claims (footnote, §3, and App. C.1) that in the linear case this score is the optimal attribution under three faithfulness criteria, citing Theorem 3 of Fel et al. 2023 ("A holistic approach to unifying automatic concept extraction and concept importance estimation"). We have not re-derived this theorem; the statement below is "according to the paper".

  • C-Deletion: remove concepts one by one, most important first, and track the drop of the output. A good importance score makes it fall fast.
  • C-Insertion: add concepts one by one, most important first, starting from an empty input. A good score makes it rise fast.
  • C-$\mu$Fidelity: correlation, over random subsets of concepts, between the sum of importances of the removed concepts and the actual change of the output.

The paper says that "in linear cases, these metrics coincide and share the same optimal attribution function". Intuition: if the output is $\sum_i z_iW'_{ij}$, removing a set $S$ changes it by exactly $\sum_{i\in S}z_iW'_{ij}$, so the score is exactly additive, and ordering by it is optimal.

A worked example

Example — three concepts, two outputs

Let $\E[\vz]=(0.5,\,0.2,\,0.05)$ and $$\mW'=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\0.5&1\\-3&4\end{pmatrix}\quad(\text{rows: concepts, columns: outputs}).$$ Scores $\E[Z_i]W'_{ij}$: concept 1: $(1.00,\,-0.50)$; concept 2: $(0.10,\,0.20)$; concept 3: $(-0.15,\,0.20)$. Sums over concepts: $(0.95,\,-0.10)$, which is the mean output $\E[\mY]$ (the 2-vector).

Output 1 is driven by concept 1 (1.00 of 0.95; the others sum to $-0.05$). Output 2 is a near-cancellation: $-0.5+0.2+0.2=-0.1$. Concept 3 has the largest weights ($-3,4$) but a small mean code (0.05), so it ends up equal to concept 2 in output 2 and weaker than concept 1 everywhere. Weight alone does not make a concept important; frequency × intensity matters. Play with it:

What the definition leaves unclear

Pitfalls
  • Two readings of $\phi$. App. C.1 defines $\vphi=\E(\mZ)\mW'\in\R^o$, "the concept-importance vector for the probe". Summed over concepts this is the mean output (a vector of length $o$), not a vector of 32,000 scores. The per-concept score is each summand $\E[Z_i]W'_{ij}$; the appendix says it "can be read from $\E(\mZ)$ together with the corresponding rows of $\mW'$".
  • Aggregation over outputs is not specified. The scores form a $c\times o$ matrix. To rank concepts for a task one needs one number per concept: the paper does not say whether it takes a sum, a max, an absolute value or the score of one class. (For a segmentation head with many classes, or depth with a regression output, the choices differ.)
  • Sign. With a signed score, "most important" may mean most positive or largest in absolute value. Not specified.
  • What is dropped. Reconstruction error $(\mA-\mZ\mD)\mW\T$ (about 12% of the variance) and the probe's bias term are left out.
  • Tokens vs images. The score is an expectation over tokens. The classifier head of DINOv2 takes the concatenation of the cls token and the mean of the patch tokens (App. C.2), so a class score is not a function of one token. The averaging makes the token-level linear decomposition valid for the mean-pooled part, but the cls branch is a separate input.
  • Expectation includes zeros. $\E[Z_i]$ small can mean "rare but strong" or "frequent but weak"; the score cannot tell them apart.
Our reading — importance is not necessity

$\E[Z_i]W'_{ij}$ measures a linear contribution to the logit; it says nothing about whether the model would still classify correctly without the concept, since other atoms are correlated with it. See page 6.

Reflex

When you see "importance of a concept for a linear probe" → push the probe through the dictionary, $\mW'=\mD\mW\T$, then weight by the mean code.
