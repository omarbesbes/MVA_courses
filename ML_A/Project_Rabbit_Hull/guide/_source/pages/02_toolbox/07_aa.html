7. Archetypal analysis

Archetypal analysis (AA) approximates every data point by a convex combination of $K$ archetypes, which are themselves convex combinations of data points. Its error always lies between that of the best $(K-1)$-dimensional affine subspace and that of $k$-means.

The Cutler–Breiman formulation

Definition (Cutler and Breiman, 1994)

Given $\mX\in\R^{N\times d}$ (rows = points) and $K$, solve $$\min_{\mA,\mB}\ \norm{\mX-\mA\mB\mX}_F^2\quad\text{s.t. the rows of }\mA\in\R^{N\times K}\text{ and of }\mB\in\R^{K\times N}\text{ lie in the simplex.}$$ The matrix $\mZ=\mB\mX\in\R^{K\times d}$ holds the archetypes: each is a convex combination of data points ($\mZ_k\in\conv(\mX)$); each data point is approximated by $\mA_i\mZ$, a convex combination of archetypes.

In the paper §7.2 and footnote · p. 20, Fig. 17 middle · p. 21, App. A

"Archetypal Analysis seeks $\bm Z=\bm X\bm B$ where archetypes are convex combinations of data (so $\bm Z\subset\conv(\bm X)$), and $\bm X\approx\bm Z\bm A$ where data are convex combinations of archetypes. Both have simplex constraints." AA "is precisely the single-tile case of MRH ($\abs S=1$)". Fig. 17 (middle): per-image AA reconstruction error (MSE) for $3,5,10,20,30$ archetypes: $0.32,0.26,0.19,0.138,0.116$, against $0.227$ for the SAE (dashed). The paper's footnote uses $\bm Z$ for archetypes and $\bm A$ for the coefficients, while its main text uses $\mZ$ for the codes and $\mA$ for the activations; we write $\mA,\mB$ as above.

$\mB$ (rows in $\simplex^{N-1}$)
how each archetype is built from data points.
$\mA$ (rows in $\simplex^{K-1}$)
the code of each point: convex weights of the $K$ archetypes (the analogue of the SAE code $\vz$, with sum 1 instead of sparsity).

Algorithm (alternating projected gradient). The objective is convex in $\mA$ for fixed $\mB$ and convex in $\mB$ for fixed $\mA$, but not jointly. Start from a $k$-means solution or random data points; alternate: a gradient step on $\mA$ then projection of each row on the simplex; same on $\mB$. The step is $1/L$ with $L$ the largest eigenvalue of the Hessian of the sub-problem. The objective never increases.

Projection onto the simplex. The Euclidean projection of $v\in\R^n$ onto $\simplex^{n-1}$ is $\max(v-\theta\mathbf1,0)$ where, with $u$ the sorted values in decreasing order, $\rho=\max\{j:u_j-\tfrac1j(\sum_{i\le j}u_i-1)>0\}$ and $\theta=\tfrac1\rho(\sum_{i\le\rho}u_i-1)$ (Held et al. 1974; Duchi et al. 2008).

Example — projecting $v=(0.8,0.6,-0.2)$
  1. sorted $u=(0.8,0.6,-0.2)$ already decreasing.
  2. $j=1$: $0.8-(0.8-1)=1.0>0$; $j=2$: $0.6-\tfrac{1.4-1}{2}=0.4>0$; $j=3$: $-0.2-\tfrac{1.2-1}{3}=-0.267<0$ so $\rho=2$.
  3. $\theta=\tfrac{1.4-1}{2}=0.2$, result $(0.6,0.4,0)$ non-negative, sums to 1, and the third coordinate is clipped to 0. Computed with rh/aa.py.
Pitfall

The problem is non-convex, so different initialisations reach different archetypes; the error is stable, the archetypes are not unique. AA is invariant under translation of the data (rows sum to 1) but not under general linear maps of the coordinates other than rotations/scalings, which preserve the objective up to a factor.

The sandwich: PCA ≤ AA ≤ k-means

Problem. What does an AA error of $0.12$ with $30$ archetypes (Fig. 17) mean? We need reference points: what would any method with $K$ prototypes give?

Sandwich

Let $E_{\mathrm{PCA}}(r)$ be the squared error of the best $r$-dimensional affine subspace, $E_{\mathrm{AA}}(K)$ the optimum of the AA problem and $E_{\mathrm{km}}(K)$ the $k$-means error with $K$ centres (the optimum, or any particular solution, for the upper bound). Then $$E_{\mathrm{PCA}}(K-1)\ \le\ E_{\mathrm{AA}}(K)\ \le\ E_{\mathrm{km}}(K).$$

Proof

Right. Take a $k$-means solution with clusters $C_1..C_K$ and centres $\mu_k$ (cluster means). Let $\mA$ be one-hot (row $i$ has a 1 in the column of its cluster) and let $\mB$ have row $k$ equal to $1/\abs{C_k}$ on the points of $C_k$. Both are row-stochastic, $\mB\mX=(\mu_k)$ and $\mA\mB\mX$ has row $i$ equal to the centre of its cluster. So the $k$-means error is the AA objective at a feasible point: the minimum is at most that.

Left. Row $i$ of $\mA\mB\mX$ is a convex combination of the $K$ archetypes, hence lies in the affine hull of $K$ points, an affine subspace of dimension at most $K-1$. The reconstruction $\mA\mB\mX$ therefore has all its rows in one affine subspace of dimension $\le K-1$ (the one spanned by the archetypes), and the best such subspace (PCA) has error at most this one.
