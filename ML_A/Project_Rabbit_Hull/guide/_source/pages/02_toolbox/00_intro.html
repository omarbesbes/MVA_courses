After this page you can
  • compute the coherence of a dictionary, state and prove the Welch bound, and say what a random dictionary of the paper's size achieves (0.23) against the bound (0.036);
  • compute a stable rank, a Hoyer score and an entropy, and say which value is "flat" and which is "concentrated";
  • work with convex hulls, simplices and barycentric coordinates, and say why a new sample is almost never in the hull of the others in high dimension;
  • build a Minkowski sum, use support functions (including the proof that $h_{A\msum B}=h_A+h_B$), and decide whether a polygon is decomposable;
  • prove the archetypal-analysis sandwich and the equality of gradient × input, occlusion and integrated gradients for a linear model.

Before you start

This page is a reference. The other pages link to one entry (02_toolbox.html#anchor) each time they use a tool; read it when you meet the link, not in advance.

How to read this page

Each entry says what the tool is, where the paper uses it, a worked example, the pitfalls, and (folded) a proof when something is stated. Every number is computed by checks/check_02.py. What comes from the paper is marked as such; the rest is standard mathematics, recalled, or our own reading (marked Our reading). A few statements about the literature are given only as far as we verified them, and say so.

The page in nine steps

  1. Frames — a dictionary of $c=32{,}000$ unit vectors in $\R^{768}$: how close to orthogonal can it be (Welch), what does a random one give, what do the singular values say.
  2. Scores — stable rank, Hoyer sparsity, entropy, cosine: the four numbers the paper computes on matrices and vectors.
  3. Projections — PCA (per-image colour maps, position basis) and UMAP (maps of the dictionary).
  4. Convex geometry — hulls, simplices, barycentric coordinates, faces; what happens in high dimension.
  5. Minkowski sums — the central object of the MRH: sums, zonotopes, support functions, decomposability.
  6. Softmax — the map from logits to a point of the open simplex.
  7. Archetypal analysis — convex coding, and the sandwich between PCA and k-means.
  8. Graphs — k-NN graphs and shortest paths as approximate geodesics.
  9. Attribution — gradient × input, occlusion, integrated gradients, RISE.
