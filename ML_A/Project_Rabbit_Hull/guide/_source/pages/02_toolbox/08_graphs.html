8. Graphs: k-NN and geodesics

A k-nearest-neighbour graph connects each point to its closest samples; shortest paths in it approximate distances along the data (the Isomap idea) and are, by construction, made of pieces that join sample points.

k-NN graph, shortest paths, Isomap

Problem. The straight line between two tokens leaves the region where tokens live. How does one move from one to the other along the data?

Definitions

The k-NN graph of points $P_1..P_N$ has an edge $\{i,j\}$ when $P_j$ is among the $k$ nearest points of $P_i$ or the converse, with weight $\norm{P_i-P_j}$. A shortest path from $i$ to $j$ minimises the sum of weights (Dijkstra's algorithm). The Isomap idea (Tenenbaum, de Silva, Langford 2000): when points sample a curved manifold densely enough, the shortest-path length approximates the geodesic distance along the manifold, much better than the Euclidean distance through the ambient space. The geodesic path is the polyline through the sample points.

In the paper §7.2, Fig. 17 left · p. 20–21

"Straight lines depart the data support quickly, whereas graph geodesics remain close throughout": the distance to the nearest sample along a path between two tokens, for a linear interpolation (a triangle peaking at about $13$) and for the shortest path in the k-NN graph (a plateau of about $4$–$5$).

The graph path stays near the sample by construction

Every point of a polyline $P_{i_0}P_{i_1}\dots P_{i_m}$ whose vertices are sample points is within $\tfrac12\max_l\norm{P_{i_l}-P_{i_{l+1}}}$ of a sample point (one of its two endpoints).

Proof

A point $(1-t)a+tb$ of the edge $[a,b]$ is at distance $t\norm{a-b}$ from $a$ and $(1-t)\norm{a-b}$ from $b$; the smaller is $\le\tfrac12\norm{a-b}$.

For $d\ge10$ the nearest sample to the midpoint of two samples is one of the endpoints: the straight line's worst distance is exactly half the chord, and the k-NN path (two or three long edges) can only improve on that by a bit. In low dimension (2) the path hugs the sample.

Our reading

The plateau of the graph path is the half-edge bound at work: it measures the length of the longest edge of the path, not whether the data are on a curved support; and a chord between two samples is, for i.i.d. samples in high dimension, far from every sample whether or not the support is convex. Developed on page 6.

Pitfall

The graph depends on $k$: too small and the graph is disconnected, too large and it short-circuits the manifold (edges through empty space). The shortest-path length is always $\ge$ the Euclidean distance (triangle inequality) and equals it when the straight segment is an edge.
