11. Exercises

Exercise 1 — The simplex frame ★

Compute the Welch bound for $c=d+1$ unit vectors in $\R^d$. For $d=3$ find four unit vectors attaining it.

Hint

Plug in $c=d+1$; for the example use the vertices of a regular tetrahedron centred at the origin.

Solution

$\sqrt{(c-d)/(d(c-1))}=\sqrt{1/d^2}=1/d$ ($d=768$: $0.0013$). The regular tetrahedron has pairwise cosines $-1/3$, so $\mu=1/3$ equals the bound: it is an equiangular tight frame.

Exercise 2 — Hoyer score of a $k$-hot vector ★

Show that a vector in $\R^n$ with $k$ equal non-zero entries has $H=(\sqrt n-\sqrt k)/(\sqrt n-1)$. Compute it for $(1,1,1,0)$ and for $n=768$, $k=8$.

Solution

$\norm x_1=ka$, $\norm x_2=a\sqrt k$, ratio $\sqrt k$. $(1,1,1,0)$: $(2-\sqrt3)/1=0.268$. $n=768$, $k=8$: $(27.713-2.828)/26.713=0.932$. So atoms with Hoyer $0.22$ are nothing like 8-hot vectors.

Exercise 3 — Entropy of a footprint ★

A concept puts half of its mass on the cls token and the rest uniformly on the 256 patches (nothing on the registers). Compute its entropy in nats and compare with $\ln261$.

Solution

$H=-\tfrac12\ln\tfrac12-256\cdot\tfrac1{512}\ln\tfrac1{512}=\tfrac12\ln2+\tfrac12\cdot9\ln2=5\ln2=3.466$, against $\ln261=5.565$. It sits between the spike at $0$ and the bulk at $5$–$5.5$ of Fig. 7: a few high-mass tokens lower the entropy a lot.

Exercise 4 — Stable rank ★★

(a) Compute the stable rank of a matrix with singular values $(3,2,1)$. (b) A centred matrix has stable rank $2.7$. Can its two leading principal components explain $100\%$ of the variance?

Solution

(a) $(9+4+1)/9=14/9=1.56$. (b) No: if two components explain everything, there are two non-zero singular values, so srank $=1+\sigma_2^2/\sigma_1^2\le2<2.7$. (This is the incompatibility noted for Fig. 14.)

Exercise 5 — Barycentric coordinates ★

(a) Barycentric coordinates of $p=(1,1)$ in the triangle $(0,0),(4,0),(0,2)$. (b) Give two different convex combinations of the vertices of the square $(0,0),(2,0),(2,2),(0,2)$ equal to $(1,1)$.

Solution

(a) $\lambda_2=x/4=0.25$, $\lambda_3=y/2=0.5$, $\lambda_1=1-0.75=0.25$; unique. (b) $\tfrac12(v_1+v_3)$ and $\tfrac12(v_2+v_4)$ (also the average of all four).

Exercise 6 — A Minkowski sum ★★

Compute $T\msum S$ with $T=\conv\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\}$ and $S=[(0,0),(1,1)]$: vertices, number of edges, area. Check against the bound $\abs{V(T)}+\abs{V(S)}$.

Hint

List the edge vectors of both (the segment contributes $(1,1)$ and $(-1,-1)$), sort by angle, start at the lowest vertex.

Solution

Edges of $T$: $(1,0)$ at $0^\circ$, $(-1,1)$ at $135^\circ$, $(0,-1)$ at $270^\circ$; of $S$: $(1,1)$ at $45^\circ$, $(-1,-1)$ at $225^\circ$. Sorted: $0^\circ,45^\circ,135^\circ,225^\circ,270^\circ$, from $(0,0)$: vertices $(0,0),(1,0),(2,1),(1,2),(0,1)$: a pentagon, $5\le3+2$. Area by the shoelace formula $=2.5$; check: $\operatorname{area}(T)+\text{length}(S)\cdot\text{width of }T\perp S=0.5+\sqrt2\cdot\sqrt2=2.5$.

Exercise 7 — Support functions ★★

$P=\conv\{(0,0),(2,0),(0,1)\}$, $Q=[0,1]^2$. Compute $h_P(1,1)$, $h_Q(1,1)$, $h_{P\msum Q}(1,1)$ and find the vertex of $P\msum Q$ touched by the supporting line.

Solution

$h_P(1,1)=\max(0,2,1)=2$, $h_Q(1,1)=2$, so $h_{P\msum Q}(1,1)=4$ by additivity. The sum has vertices $(0,0),(3,0),(3,1),(1,2),(0,2)$ and $\ps{(3,1)}{(1,1)}=4$; the touching vertex is $(3,1)=(2,0)+(1,1)$, the sum of the touching vertices of $P$ and $Q$.

Exercise 8 — How cold must attention be? ★★★

A softmax over $n=261$ tokens has one logit $1$ above all the others (which are equal). Using $p_j\le e^{-\Delta_j/\tau}$, find a temperature that guarantees at most $1\%$ of the mass off the winner.

Solution

The mass off the winner is $\le260\,e^{-1/\tau}\le0.01$ iff $1/\tau\ge\ln26000=10.17$, i.e. $\tau\le0.098$. The exact mass at $\tau=0.098$ is $0.0095$, so the bound is almost tight here. With many keys, "selects a vertex" (Lemma 5) needs a very low temperature.

Exercise 9 — Attribution of a product ★★

$f(x)=x_1x_2$ at $x=(2,3)$, baseline $0$. Compute gradient × input, occlusion and integrated gradients, and compare their sums with $f(x)-f(0)$.

Solution

Gradient × input: $(2\cdot3,\,3\cdot2)=(6,6)$, sum $12$. Occlusion: $f(x)-f(0,3)=6$ and $f(x)-f(2,0)=6$, sum $12$. IG: $x_i\int_0^1\alpha x_j\,d\alpha=x_1x_2/2=(3,3)$, sum $6=f(x)-f(0)$. Only IG satisfies completeness here; for a linear $f$ the three coincide.

Exercise 10 — What the JL footnote gives ★★

Using the existence bound $p\ge e^{d\eps^2/4}$ of the JL entry, which coherence can be guaranteed for $c=10^6$ unit vectors in $d=768$? For $c=32{,}000$ in $d=3072$?

Solution

$\eps\ge\sqrt{4\ln c/d}$: $c=10^6$, $d=768$: $\sqrt{55.26/768}=0.268$. $c=32{,}000$, $d=3072$: $0.116$. The guaranteed level improves like $1/\sqrt d$ and worsens like $\sqrt{\ln c}$.
