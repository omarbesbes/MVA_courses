6. Softmax

Softmax turns a vector of logits into a point of the open simplex; it ignores a common shift, can be inverted up to a constant, and at low temperature selects the largest logit. Attention weights are softmax outputs, which is why they are convex weights (Lemma 1).

Definition and properties

Definition

For $x\in\R^n$ and a temperature $\tau>0$: $\softmax(x/\tau)_i=\dfrac{e^{x_i/\tau}}{\sum_je^{x_j/\tau}}$; $\tau=1$ is the usual softmax.

In the paper Lemma 1 · p. 19, Lemma 5 · p. 49

Single head: $\mY=\mA\mV$ with $\mA=\softmax(\mQ\mK\T)$, rows in the simplex, so each output is a convex combination of the values: $\mY\subset\conv(\mV)$. Low temperature: the weights select one vertex, with the bound $\bigl\|\sum_j\alpha_jv_j-v_{j^*}\bigr\|\le\operatorname{diam}(V)\sum_{j\ne j^*}e^{-\Delta_j/\tau}$ ($\Delta_j$ the gap between the largest logit and the $j$-th).

Properties
  1. Simplex: $\softmax(x)\in\simplex^{n-1}$ and all entries are $>0$: the image is the open simplex $\{p:p_i>0,\sum p_i=1\}$. Outputs are never exactly $0$ (in floating point they underflow only below about $e^{-745}$).
  2. Shift invariance: $\softmax(x+s\mathbf 1)=\softmax(x)$ for every $s\in\R$. So the logits contain $n-1$ numbers of information.
  3. Inverse: if $p=\softmax(x)$, then $\ln p_i=x_i-\ln\sum_je^{x_j}$, i.e. $x=\ln p+s\mathbf 1$; every $p$ in the open simplex is attained (take $x=\ln p$). So softmax is a bijection from $\R^n/\R\mathbf 1$ onto the open simplex.
  4. Temperature: if the maximum $x_{j^*}$ is unique, $\softmax(x/\tau)\to e_{j^*}$ as $\tau\to0$; if the maximum is attained on a set $M$, the limit is the uniform distribution on $M$; as $\tau\to\infty$ it tends to the uniform distribution $\tfrac1n\mathbf 1$.
  5. Quantitative low-temperature bound: for $j\ne j^*$, $p_j\le e^{-\Delta_j/\tau}$; and for any points $v_j$, $\bigl\|\sum_jp_jv_j-v_{j^*}\bigr\|\le\operatorname{diam}\{v_j\}\sum_{j\ne j^*}e^{-\Delta_j/\tau}$.
Proofs

(1) exponentials are positive; the denominator is the sum of the numerators. (2) the factor $e^{s}$ cancels. (3) take logs of the definition. (4) divide numerator and denominator by $e^{x_{j^*}/\tau}$: $p_j=e^{-\Delta_j/\tau}/\bigl(1+\sum_{l\ne j^*}e^{-\Delta_l/\tau}\bigr)$ with $\Delta_l>0$ (unique maximum) tends to $0$ for $j\ne j^*$, so $p_{j^*}\to1$; with ties, the tied terms contribute $e^0=1$ each; $\tau\to\infty$: all $e^{x_j/\tau}\to1$. (5) from the same formula $p_j\le e^{-\Delta_j/\tau}$ since the denominator is $\ge1$; then $\sum_jp_jv_j-v_{j^*}=\sum_{j\ne j^*}p_j(v_j-v_{j^*})$ because $\sum p_j=1$, and $\norm{v_j-v_{j^*}}\le\operatorname{diam}$.

Numbers
  • $x=(2,1,0)$: $\softmax(x)=(0.665,0.245,0.090)$; shifted by $+10$: the same. $\ln p=(-0.408,-1.408,-2.408)$, and $\ln p+2.408=(2,1,0)$ recovers $x$ up to the constant.
  • Temperature: $\tau=2$: $(0.507,0.307,0.186)$; $\tau=0.5$: $(0.867,0.117,0.016)$; $\tau=0.1$: $(1.000,0.000,0.000)$ with leftover mass about $4.5\times10^{-5}$; $\tau=10$: $(0.367,0.332,0.301)$; $\tau=100$: almost uniform.
  • Two logits $(x,0)$: $\softmax=(\sigma(x),1-\sigma(x))$, the sigmoid ($x=1$: $0.731$).
  • Bound check at $\tau=0.5$: $\sum_{j\ne j^*}e^{-\Delta_j/\tau}=e^{-2}+e^{-4}=0.154$ against the true leftover mass $0.133$.

Pitfalls
  • Softmax output is never on the boundary: attention outputs are in the relative interior of $\conv(V)$ but never on a face; the paper's Lemma 1 proof "the attainable set is $\conv(V)$" is true only for its closure (see page 6).
  • Which set the logits can reach matters: attention logits $\mK q$ span at most the column space of $\mK$ (dimension $\le d_k$), so not all of the simplex is attainable for a given query-key set.
